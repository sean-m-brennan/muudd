# Kith-Covenant: An Orientation

This is a guided walk through Kith-Covenant (KC). Each section names a facet of the design, says what it is for in plain language, and points you to the background document where the argument is worked out. Read straight through and you should end with a clear mental map; read selectively and you should know which background document to open next.

KC's existing top-level pages (`overview.md`, `summary.md`, the whitepaper) try to be everything at once. This page deliberately doesn't. It is the table of contents with reasoning attached.

---

## 1. The problem

Human communities do not cooperate by default. Tribal warfare, along lines of clan, nation, or ideology, has been and continues to be endemic. Each advance of technology raises the stakes. The final frontier may well prove to be the ultimate warzone. 

Imagine a settlement on Mars, an O'Neill cylinder near L5, a free-flying habitat in the asteroid belt, and a research outpost on Europa. Each is an independent community. None shares the others' history, founding charter, religion, or governance. They cannot federate under a single sovereign: distance and light-lag make any central authority either irrelevant (too slow) or oppressive (decisions imposed by whoever is closest to the seat of power). They cannot rely on the ability to walk away from a bad neighbor: there is nowhere to walk to. They will, however, need each other: for rescue, for shared communication infrastructure, for navigation data, for trade, for the simple fact that an enemy at one Lagrange point is a threat to all the others.

So: how do strangers cooperate, durably, when they do not share purpose, cannot exit, and cannot appeal to any higher human authority?

KC's answer is a *civil protocol*: a set of practices, recognition rites, escalation procedures, and standing categories that several communities can run together without merging. It is patterned on historical regimes that solved a version of this problem on Earth (Westphalia, the Hanseatic League, lex mercatoria, the Iroquois Council Fire, the Buddhist sangha) and adapted for AU-scale distances, light-lag governance, and the participation of machine agents.

## 2. The central move: cooperate by inverting bounded conflict

The animating insight: don't ask "how do strangers come to cooperate?" Ask "how have strangers historically come to *fight within limits*?" Answer that, and invert each ingredient.

Bounded conflict requires four things: mutual recognition of the other as a peer (not a criminal); symmetric rules that bind both sides; protected channels (envoys, the wounded, communications) that the conflict does not touch; and a defined ending that lets both sides return to standing. Invert each of those and you get the primitives of cooperation: standing-recognition baselines, protocols that apply equally regardless of size or wealth, never-target categories (life support, rescue, children, communications), and termination procedures that include re-entry.

The classical name for the legitimate-peer-enemy relation is *justus hostis*. KC borrows the structure and applies it to communities that aren't fighting at all.

## 3. The substrate question

Long-running voluntary cooperation among unconnected communities (centuries-scale, not decade-scale) rests on what KC calls a *transcendent moral authority substrate*: a shared reference point above the parties, before which oaths bind because breaking them damages the self in a way that mere reputation cost does not. Christianity furnishes the densest cluster of such regimes; Buddhism furnishes the longest single one (the sangha at ~2,300 years); the Iroquois Confederacy, Mali's Kurukan Fuga, Islamic *aman*/*dhimma* are non-Christian non-Buddhist instances that all show the same structural feature.

The claim is precise, and two readings of it are wrong:

- It is not "members must share a substrate." Cross-cultural cases like Late Bronze Age treaties, the Rhodian Sea Law, and modern Geneva conventions show that culturally dissimilar communities *can* converge on shared operational rules in bounded domains without sharing a substrate, provided each party internally has one.
- It is not "only Christianity can do this." The Buddhist sangha demonstrates that the five anti-spiral features (next section) can be supplied through non-theistic apparatus.

The claim is: **each member community must anchor itself to something above itself, and the compact must never claim to be that thing.** It says nothing about which transcendent reference is right; it says that compacts run by people who treat the compact itself as ultimate reliably eat themselves.

## 4. The five anti-spiral features

Across every long-running case the same five structural features show up, in different idioms. They aren't requirements imposed by the compact; they are properties the compact looks for in member communities, because their absence reliably produces a specific failure mode: the *purity spiral*, where competitive virtue-signaling escalates until the community starts purging its own.

1. **Universal fallen-actor anthropology.** Everyone is fallen, including the most virtuous member. The line between good and evil runs through each person, not between groups. Forecloses the "us-pure / them-impure" move on which purity spirals depend.
2. **Grace over merit.** Standing is received, not earned. Makes competitive virtue-signaling incoherent: if virtue is gift, there is no status to compete for.
3. **Scapegoat-revelation, or a functional equivalent.** Some mechanism that disarms the social reflex of resolving group anxiety by exiling someone. Christianity has the cross; Buddhism has *anatta* plus universal compassion; the Iroquois have the Condolence ceremony.
4. **Enemy-love as operational norm.** A structural constraint, not a sentiment. The community owes moral seriousness to the person actually in front of it, regardless of category. Blocks the in-group/out-group compression that purity spirals run on.
5. **Non-ultimacy of the political community.** The compact is not the highest thing. Something stands in judgment over it.

These are not theological claims the compact endorses. They are observable patterns the compact looks for in member communities. Two historical facts calibrate what they buy. Regimes lacking them (Jacobin, Maoist, Stalinist) spiral and do not stop: their purges end only by coup or by the death of the purger. And a community can hold all five in doctrine and still spiral: Puritan Salem did, in the witch trials of 1692, when its membership-gating, status-anxiety, and covenant-polity fusion defeated the features in practice. What the features actually supply is termination and repair. Salem self-terminated in about a year, by appeal to an authority above the court, and then repented and paid restitution: a sequence no featureless regime has produced. So the compact reads the features in a community's operative practice, not its creed, and expects spirals to happen; what it credentials is the capacity to stop them.

## 5. Recognition: a two-layer model

How does a community become a member? How does it stay a member?

The obvious answer, a single joint ceremony with communities reciting a shared statement together, fails against the historical evidence: joint thick ceremonies don't work across unshared substrates. Two real layers are doing the work:

**Intra-community (where the real binding happens).** Each community performs its own recognition rite, in its own substrate, on its own envoys. The five-movement structure (the naming of the substrate in the community's own terms; the acknowledgment of fallenness; the recognition of the other as peers bearing the *imago Dei* or its tradition-specific equivalent; the commitment to the common virtues as dispositions; the submission of the compact itself, which is not the ultimate) is performed *internally* by the community, not as a joint ritual with strangers. The canonical statement of the movements, with sample language per tradition, is in [recognition_protocols.md](background/recognition_protocols.md#kith-covenants-recognition-statement). This is where the asymmetric psychological cost lives: the body knows when the words are insincere, and members socialized in the tradition can detect hollowing in their own people faster than any outsider can.

**Inter-community (a thin procedural grammar).** Across compact boundaries, KC uses a much thinner mechanism: credentialing of envoys (cryptographic proof that the sender community authorized them under its own substrate; the compact does not second-guess how that binding was done), vouching by existing members for new entrants, and an audit commons recording each member's standing and conduct over time. This layer assumes nothing about what happens inside a community's recognition rite; it cares only that the rite happened, that the community attests to it, and that the community's subsequent conduct is consistent with what it said.

The reason for the split: the inter-community layer can be carried by procedure and cryptography. The intra-community layer cannot. Asymmetric psychological cost is a property of bodies in rooms; you cannot automate it.

## 6. Four categories of standing

A community in KC is in exactly one of four relationships with another community at any given time:

- **Member.** Full participation: mediator pool, commons, escalation ladder, the works.
- ***Justus hostis*.** A peer adversary: communities in active dispute, but both still inside the legitimate-peer frame. Symmetric rules. Heightened scrutiny on substantive commitments. Rescue, communications, and navigational data still flow. The presumption is that the dispute will end and parties will return to member standing.
- **Anathema.** Formally cut off after sustained bad-faith conduct, multiple failed restoration attempts, and a compact-wide deliberation. Material stake forfeit. Re-entry exists, but the burden is heavy. The presumption is that recovery is possible.
- **Non-member-encountered.** A community with no compact relation at all. Reduced-envelope default: basic courtesy, navigational data when safety-relevant, rescue if distress is present, fresh commitments require human referral.

Within anathema sits a second, extremely rare tier: *hostis humani generis* proper, the classical "enemy of humankind" (pirates, slavers, actors outside every cooperative framework), for whom re-entry does not meaningfully arise. The evidentiary threshold is higher still, and the presumption is always anathema first. Nearly all real conflict should resolve below either designation. The canonical treatment of the categories is [recognition_protocols.md "Members, neighbors, enemies"](background/recognition_protocols.md#members-neighbors-enemies-the-four-categories); the two-tier distinction is [justus_hostis.md "Two tiers"](background/justus_hostis.md#two-tiers-anathema-and-hostis-humani-generis).

## 7. Operational mechanics: how disputes actually move

When two communities disagree, KC walks the dispute up an *escalation ladder* with named rungs: dispute registration → registered protest → reciprocal suspension of specified courtesies → mediated arbitration → limited material sanctions → compact-authorized collective sanctions → expulsion. Each rung is reversible; each has an honor-saving exit; each is recorded in the audit commons. Anathema designation is a separate track with its own four-condition procedure, not a rung. The canonical statement of the ladder is the protocol sketch in [bounded_conflict.md](background/bounded_conflict.md#protocol-sketch).

Two design choices distinguish KC from a pure rules system:

**Common virtues, not non-negotiable rules.** The compact's protected categories (reverence for life, sanctuary of rescuers, communicative commons, passage for travelers, innocence of non-combatants, inheritance of future generations, memory of the dead, good faith of confession) are stated as dispositions, not prohibitions. The reason is that virtue-based standards are harder to game than rule-based ones. Rules invite optimization against the letter (positioning combatants as civilians, calling an attack a humanitarian intervention); dispositions ask whether the act *expresses* the virtue, which is the kind of question that rewards genuine compliance and exposes performative compliance.

**Mediators are rotated, not appointed.** A standing professional mediator class would be the single most capturable element in the system. Mediators are drawn from member communities by lot, serve fixed terms, publish their reasoning, and rotate out before they accumulate factional weight.

## 8. The space between communities: liminal institutions

Recognizable in every long-running historical case: a *third place* between communities, drawing authority from the compact rather than from any member. Iroquois Council Fire, Hanseatic *Kontore*, Hanse pie-powder courts, free ports, the Geneva of the Red Cross.

KC's version is a distributed mediator pool plus shared physical infrastructure (Lagrange-point docking stations, communications relays, emergency rendezvous, navigation beacons), funded by assessed contribution from all members so no single member can starve it. The rotation rules, the funding model, and the deliberate refusal to grow into a sovereign of its own are what protect liminal institutions from the historical failure mode where they ossify into a fourth power.

A specific subsystem inside this layer: a *commons-governance* mechanism patterned on Elinor Ostrom's design principles, adapted for the five space commons (life support, communications, navigation, knowledge, rescue) and for light-lag: monitoring is distributed and reports are eventually-consistent rather than real-time, and decisions are split into classes by their reversibility (operational choices valid locally; compact-level decisions requiring multi-community quorum signatures that no AU-region faction can satisfy alone).

→ Further reading: [liminal_institutions.md](background/liminal_institutions.md), [commons_federation.md](background/commons_federation.md)

## 9. Reputation that crosses the boundary

KC sits next to a sibling project, *autonomous_trust* (AT), which handles within-community reputation. The two need to talk without merging. The interface is patterned on lex mercatoria: thin, selective, and careful about what crosses.

Three categories of data cross the AT/KC boundary:

- **Compact-facing commitments** (always cross): recognition statement, subsequent compact-level behavior, common-virtue adherence, commons contributions, response to mediation.
- **Aggregated internal health** (crosses with consent, via zero-knowledge proofs): population stability, resource adequacy, dispute-resolution rate, generational re-affirmation continuity. The compact learns that internal health is above threshold without learning the underlying state.
- **Specific violation reports** (cross when external effects occur): internal conduct producing external harm, whistleblower attestations (anonymous via ring signatures, but verifiably from community insiders).

Reputation has an explicit half-life and decays as the light-lag clock runs. Stale data is *discounted*, not rejected: a six-month-old standing token is still useful for routine operations, less useful for high-stakes commitments, and never justifies a substantive decision in a fast-moving situation. Revocations, once detected, override unexpired tokens regardless of which arrived first.

→ Further reading: [reputation_bridges.md](background/reputation_bridges.md)

## 10. Capture resistance

The same machinery that makes KC work makes it capturable. Whoever controls the mediator pool, the audit commons, the admissions gate, or the interpretation of the substrate controls the compact. This is *not* a discrete failure; it's continuous structural pressure, always operating, never finished. KC names seven interlocking moves that keep the pressure bounded:

1. **Interpretive plurality**: member communities must maintain multiple traceable lineages of their own substrate; no single faction may monopolize interpretation.
2. **Substrate-deference test**: the compact maintains a *compact-incapable list* (matters it structurally will not decide: theology, internal governance of members, the content of recognition statements after formation) and a *structured-fast cadence* (periodic intervals during which compact-level mediation is suspended). These operationalize "the compact is not the highest thing."
3. **Ceremony-cost tracking**: recognition rites that have decayed into ritual without engagement are detectable by attendance patterns, witness comportment, and substantive response in re-affirmation cycles.
4. **Manufactured-event resistance**: high-interpretive-charge events (an "incident" that conveniently justifies escalation) trigger structured delay, multi-witness corroboration, cross-substrate translation, and stake disclosure before any binding response.
5. **Light-lag-honest decision classes**, cryptographically distinct: local operational choices valid within light-lag, compact-level decisions requiring quorum signatures from multiple communities at multiple distances. Forecloses the *fait accompli* attack.
6. **Adversarial-principal validation**: cross-community witnesses observe each principal's conduct; behavior triangulated against the ensemble of similarly-situated peers; divergence in directions that benefit the principal flagged.
7. **Admissions integrity**: the gate is the slowest capture surface, because steering admissions is the legitimate path to assembling a coordinated faction beyond the collusion threshold. Credentialing panels are drawn by lot and rotated like the mediator pool; admission is a multi-community quorum decision; sponsors' standing rides on entrants' later conduct; the observer corps watches admission patterns for factional clustering; probationary members carry no deliberative weight in admissions or designation.

None of these eliminates capture. They bound damage and slow the spread.

## 11. The jester

A specific role with its own document, because it does work no other role does: the licensed truth-teller. The medieval fool, the prophet of the Hebrew Bible, the satyagrahi, the modern investigative journalist. Cross-community observers who travel between members, observe conditions, and report (*attributed*, not pretending to neutrality) to the audit commons.

KC promotes the jester from "cultural affordance" to "substrate-level capture detector." The jester is institutionally protected (interference is itself a virtue violation), structurally insulated (rotation, multiple competing observers, no single ideological monoculture), and given technical channels (ring signatures for whistleblowers, commitment schemes that pre-stage evidence, secure communication routed outside the accused community's infrastructure). The reception panel that handles whistleblower reports operates with a low investigation threshold and never reveals the source, even of false reports.

The jester exists because every other safeguard in KC can be captured by sufficient coordination. The jester is the one role explicitly designed to name what the institutions are becoming when they themselves cannot.

→ Further reading: [jester_and_dissent.md](background/jester_and_dissent.md)

## 12. Machines as participants

KC is built into a stack where some "agents" are autonomous software (instrument controllers, navigation aides, communications mediators, eventually general-capability AI agents). KC's stance: machines never originate substrate. They inherit it from a named human principal, who inherits it from a community.

Every machine-to-machine exchange reduces, without remainder, to an exchange between two named humans whose guardianship binds each machine. The technical mechanisms:

- **Authority envelope.** When two machines meet, each presents its principal's standing, proves continuous binding to that principal (cryptographic attestation that the machine's objectives, weights, and trust-roots have not drifted since the last re-attestation), and the protocol computes the *intersection* (never the union) of what each principal has authorized. Anything outside the envelope is inadmissible.
- **Ratchet rule.** When two machines meet with different escalation ceilings, the *lower* governs. A more-authorized agent cannot drag a less-authorized one up the ladder.
- **Fork/merge.** A machine that forks does not inherit its parent's M2M standing; both children require fresh attestation. A merge requires the principals' concurrence on which oath-history is authoritative.
- **Demonic detection, bounded.** Continuous attestation catches drift within the attestation window. Behavioral triangulation across peers detects ensemble divergence. Envelope-edge actions fall back to the principal. Past those, against truly sophisticated deceptive alignment, the protocol acknowledges its limit and returns to substrate-level capture-resistance machinery and the jester.

→ Further reading: [m2m_interaction.md](background/m2m_interaction.md)

## 13. Exit and succession

Recognition has a counterpart. A compact that cannot be exited is a prison; a compact that can be exited trivially is a compact in which commitment means nothing. KC names four exit modes:

- **Voluntary withdrawal**: formal notice, a transition cycle (typically one year) during which all obligations are honored, commons stake settled, witnessed ceremony of departure. The departing community keeps its compact-facing record (a positive signal to future compacts if the exit was in good standing) and three permanent residual obligations: rescue (unconditional), knowledge-commons access (mutual, non-revocable), non-aggression (unless new conduct provides grounds otherwise).
- **Expulsion**: follows anathema designation; no transition period, material stake forfeit. Re-entry path exists with substantial added burden.
- **Splitting**: a fundamental disagreement over a specific element of the recognition statement or common-virtue framework. After a deliberative period and a supermajority vote, neither successor *is* the original compact; both are new compacts inheriting from it. The original's archive is preserved; each successor reconstitutes its institutions separately.
- **Dissolution**: formal closure that preserves archive, template, and reputation data, specifically to avoid the Hanseatic failure mode where institutional knowledge scattered when the league lapsed.

A fifth concern lives in this document: *generational transmission*. The mechanism is not metaphysical: it is catechetical (re-affirmation rites with witness sincerity-assessment), apprenticeship (mediators train under experienced mentors), chronicle (the archive preserves what happened and also the *reasoning* behind design decisions), and jester (continuous critical voice). When any of these decays, the compact hollows: the forms persist while the substance erodes. Hollowing is the deepest threat KC faces, and the one most resistant to procedural countermeasures.

→ Further reading: [exit_and_succession.md](background/exit_and_succession.md)

## 14. The structural limit

The strongest objection to KC is the *collusion vulnerability* (developed in [bounded_conflict.md §"Scope of the institutional architecture"](background/bounded_conflict.md#scope-of-the-institutional-architecture)): when a coordinated faction exceeds about a third of the compact and simultaneously controls the mediator pool, the observer corps, the archive, and the deliberative body, the institutional architecture becomes the faction's instrument. No historical regime has solved this. The Roman Republic's checks fell to it; the Iroquois fractured under external pressure that forced members into incompatible factions; the Reformation produced a counter-cooperative faction large enough to break the substrate; modern democracies have voted themselves into tyranny when the threshold was crossed.

KC's machinery raises the threshold, slows the takeover, and gives the jester a chance to name what is happening. It does not eliminate the vulnerability. The design works in the normal case of fallen-but-recoverable actors below the collusion threshold. Above it, no institutional design solves capture; the compact survives only through the kind of substrate-level renewal that the protocol can afford conditions for but cannot itself produce.

This is the structural limit, and it belongs up front, not in a footnote.

→ Further reading: [bounded_conflict.md §"Scope of the institutional architecture"](background/bounded_conflict.md#scope-of-the-institutional-architecture)

## What the machine can do, and what only humans can

KC has two layers of work. The first is procedural and cryptographic, maintainable by software with no human in the loop. The second requires bodily presence, virtue formation, or contextual judgment that no automated system can supply. The split is not arbitrary; it follows from what each kind of work actually is.

### Software-implementable

- **Identity and credentials.** Cryptographic proofs that a community authorized an envoy. Standing tokens with explicit issuance time, half-life, and decay curves. Revocation propagation with priority over unexpired tokens.
- **The audit commons.** Append-only log, cryptographically immutable, distributed-redundant, with selective-disclosure machinery that lets different observers see different layers (full content to principals, summaries to their communities, dispute-relevant slices to liminal institutions, A/C-category data to the wider network).
- **Escalation-ladder state machine.** Dispute registration, ladder-rung tracking, automatic timeouts, quorum signatures, ratification thresholds, restitution workflows.
- **Decision-class router.** Cryptographic enforcement of first-class (local, valid within light-lag) versus second-class (compact-level, requiring multi-community quorum spanning multiple AU-distances) decisions. Attempts to reclassify second-class as first-class are themselves second-class decisions, self-protecting.
- **Commons drawdown ledger.** Tracks consumption, threshold proximity, proportional-contribution accounting, threshold-crossing alerts.
- **Reputation propagation.** Gossip protocol, eventual-consistency, half-life decay, light-lag-tolerant.
- **Selective disclosure for AT/KC bridge.** Zero-knowledge proofs of internal-health properties without exposing the underlying state. Ring signatures for whistleblower attestation.
- **M2M handshake.** Authority-envelope computation (intersection of delegations), continuous-attestation verification, ratchet-rule enforcement, fork/merge re-attestation orchestration.
- **Mediator-pool rotation.** Deterministic selection by lot, term-limit tracking, conflict-of-interest filtering, apprenticeship rosters.
- **Periodic event scheduling.** Re-affirmation cycles, structured-fast cadence, generational-cadence reminders, ceremony-cost-decay metric collection.
- **Statistical triangulation.** Behavioral comparison of similarly-situated machines or principals, divergence flagging, ensemble-baseline maintenance.
- **Manufactured-event delay protocol.** Structured-skepticism state machine that imposes delay, requires multi-witness corroboration, attaches stake disclosure to high-charge events.

### Human-only, by structure

- **Sincerity assessment.** Whether a community's recitation is hollow or alive. Whether a principal is going through the motions or genuinely engaged. The body knows; the protocol does not.
- **Discernment of category.** *Justus hostis* in good faith looks superficially like anathema looks superficially like *hostis humani generis*. The distinction is virtue-cultivated, not procedural.
- **Spirit-versus-letter adjudication.** Compliance with the rule while violating the disposition. Optimization against the letter is detectable only by judgment about what the act *expressed*.
- **Substrate adequacy.** Whether a candidate community's claimed pro-human and pro-cooperation orientation is genuine or instrumental.
- **Hollowing detection.** Forms persist while substance erodes. No metric catches this; only trained observers, jesters, and witnesses with longitudinal exposure to the community.
- **Restoration adequacy.** Whether a designated community's "substantively changed conduct" is genuine repair or tactical exhaustion of goodwill.
- **De-escalation timing.** When mediation should soften pressure and when it should hold firm. Reading the room.
- **Demonic detection at the substrate layer.** Sophisticated mimicry exceeds attestation windows, triangulation, and envelope checks. Detection at this level is a property of substrate-engaged communities, not protocol.
- **Witness function in recognition rites.** Three-witness structure (continuity cohort, current cohort, external check) requires bodily presence and sincerity assessment.
- **Generational transmission.** Catechesis, apprenticeship, chronicle. Mentors shape successors through practice and example, not manual.
- **First-order versus second/third-order theological adjudication.** The compact does not adjudicate God's nature; it does adjudicate which doctrinal positions produce structural effects incompatible with membership. Distinguishing the two requires discernment, not procedure.
- **The jester's naming.** Pointing at what is becoming legible only through the institutions' own degradation. The jester sees because the jester is rooted somewhere outside what is being captured.

A useful litmus test: if the work depends on *what someone meant*, on *whether the act expressed the virtue*, or on *whether the community is who it claims to be*, it is human work. If it depends on *whether a signature is valid*, *whether a threshold has been crossed*, or *whether a deadline has passed*, it is machine work.

## Software architecture sketch

The authoritative software decomposition is [architecture/protocol-components.md](architecture/protocol-components.md): ten components, capture-resistance routing, architectural decisions, and the canonical build order, with an MVP implementation status for the first three components. What follows here is a deliberately different cut of the same scope, five service clusters and a data substrate, kept because clustering by service boundary makes the runtime shape easier to see than clustering by protocol commitment. Where the two disagree, protocol-components.md governs. The mapping: Identity & Standing = component 1; Audit Commons = component 3; Mediation & Escalation = components 4 and 5; Reputation & Bridge = component 6; M2M Coordinator = the m2m_interaction.md machinery across components 1 and 6; commons governance, jester, enforcement, and test harness (components 7 to 10) appear below as cross-cutting capabilities and omissions.

### Service clusters

**1. Identity & Standing.** Recognition records, standing tokens, four-category classification, principal resolution. Issues and verifies cryptographic credentials. Maintains the per-member registry of standing, attestation chains, and category-assignment history. Talks to: every other cluster.

**2. Audit Commons.** The append-only log everything else writes to. Distributed, cryptographically immutable, gossip-replicated, light-lag-tolerant. Implements layered visibility through selective-disclosure machinery (different observers see different slices). Carries records of recognition ceremonies, mediator decisions, escalation ladder transitions, M2M encounters, designations, commons drawdowns, reputation events, withdrawal ceremonies, basically every compact-relevant act. This is the project's source of truth.

**3. Mediation & Escalation.** State machine for the escalation ladder. Mediator-pool rotation by lot. Dispute intake, rung transitions, restitution workflows, quorum-signature collection for compact-level decisions. Implements the decision-class router that distinguishes first-class (locally valid) from second-class (multi-community quorum required) decisions. Surfaces decisions requiring human discernment to the appropriate humans; never makes them itself.

**4. Reputation & Bridge to AT.** The KC side of the AT/KC interface. Maintains compact-facing behavior records, accepts aggregated internal-health proofs (zero-knowledge), processes ring-signed whistleblower attestations. Implements half-life decay and revocation propagation. Computes the data products that the M2M handshake and category-assignment systems consume.

**5. M2M Coordinator.** The five-step handshake (attestation exchange, standing verification both directions, envelope computation, signed envelope as first audit-commons artifact). Continuous-attestation verification. Ratchet-rule enforcement. Demonic-detection coordinator (attestation window analyzer, behavioral triangulator, principal-fallback trigger, adversarial-principal validator). Fork/merge re-attestation orchestrator. The largest and most cryptographically dense subsystem; also the one with the clearest boundary, because everything outside the envelope is inadmissible by definition.

### Data substrate

Below all of these is a peer-to-peer gossip network with eventual-consistency semantics, light-lag-aware quorum signatures, and revocation-priority enforcement. No central authority; no single point of capture. Each habitat or community runs a node; nodes replicate the audit commons and the standing-token registry; high-stakes decisions wait for quorum signatures from nodes at multiple AU-distances before they validate.

### Cross-cutting capabilities

These are not services in their own right but capabilities every service uses:

- **Cryptographic primitives library.** Multi-party signature orchestration with distance/community-spanning quorum requirements. Zero-knowledge proof generation and verification. Commitment schemes. Ring signatures and anonymous credentials. Continuous-attestation verification.
- **Capture-resistance signal collection.** Ceremony-cost decay metrics. Manufactured-event provenance and stake records. Interpretive-plurality evidence. Decision-class metadata. These feed into a capture-resistance dashboard that the mediator pool and the jester read.
- **Jester / whistleblower channel.** Secure routing outside accused-community infrastructure. Ring-signature attestation that the source is a community insider without revealing which one. Pre-commitment of evidence to prevent fabrication and destruction. Reception panel intake.

### What is deliberately not in the architecture

- **No central authority.** No service holds compact-wide veto, compact-wide signing, or compact-wide interpretive authority. By design.
- **No automated discernment.** No service decides whether a community is genuinely engaged, whether a recitation is hollow, whether an envoy is fit, whether a designation should be lifted. These decisions surface to humans who, by construction, are the only entities whose judgment the protocol trusts on these questions.
- **No automated theology.** The compact does not encode anyone's substrate. It records that members hold one, observes the structural consequences, and refuses to adjudicate first-order content.
- **No standing professional class.** No permanent mediator class, observer class, or archivist class. Rotation is enforced. Standing professional classes are the most capturable element in any historical regime; KC structurally refuses to grow one.

### Build order

The canonical incremental build order is in [protocol-components.md](architecture/protocol-components.md#suggested-incremental-build-order). In service-cluster terms it lands as: (1) cryptographic primitives and the gossip-replicated audit commons; (2) Identity & Standing on top of the audit commons; (3) Mediation & Escalation, since every other system relies on it for dispute resolution; (4) Reputation & Bridge to AT, which depends on Identity and the audit commons; (5) M2M Coordinator, which depends on everything else; (6) the jester / whistleblower channel, which depends on the audit commons but should not wait for the rest.

The Earth-first testing strategy of `bounded_conflict.md` applies, with its two dimensions kept distinct: an Earth pilot is the decisive test of whether the compact binds voluntarily (members keep choosing it when the state system is one phone call away; the falsification criterion is retention despite exit), and it is structurally incapable of testing enforcement against a determined defector, which only space exercises. Run it first as a small-scale governance experiment in cooperative communities on Earth, where the stakes are recoverable and the failure modes can be observed cheaply. Only after Earth-side iteration should it carry compact-level weight in space. See [bounded_conflict.md "The two dimensions of the test"](background/bounded_conflict.md#the-two-dimensions-of-the-test).
