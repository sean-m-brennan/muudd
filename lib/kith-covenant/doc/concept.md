# Cooperation without a sovereign

Two communities that share no history, no creed, and no ruler will nonetheless
need each other. That is the whole problem, and it is the oldest one on record.

The earliest surviving parity treaty between two foreign great powers, struck
between Hittites and Egyptians in 1259 BCE, already has its shape. How do you
make an agreement hold between sides with different gods, different languages,
and different loyalties? Every civilization since has taken a run at it.

Kith-Covenant is a civil protocol for that case: a set of practices, recognition
rites, escalation procedures, and standing categories that several communities
can run together without merging. It is patterned on the historical regimes that
solved a version of the problem, being Westphalia, the Hanseatic League, the
merchant law, the Iroquois Council Fire, and the Buddhist sangha, and adapted for
distances measured in light-minutes and for the participation of machines.

The record it is drawn from divides sharply, and the division is the first thing
worth knowing.

*Bounded functional cooperation converges across cultures without much trouble,
and always has.* Commerce across religious lines, maritime rescue, envoy
protection, and the treatment of wounded combatants have all worked
cross-culturally on a thin shared grammar. The Rhodian sea law ran for a thousand
years across four civilizations. The Red Cross and Red Crescent operate on the
same framework with different emblems.

*Full compacts, the ones governing a broad slice of community life rather than
one function, have a much worse record.* And they fail in two distinct ways.
Without a foundation above the parties, oaths do not bind and short-term interest
overrides them. With a foundation that becomes the object of devotion for the
compact itself, the system collapses into loyalty cascades that end in purges.
Robespierre and the Cultural Revolution are not accidents. They are the
predictable failure mode of virtue-oriented communities that lack specific
structural brakes.

The harder case is the one that calibrates everything. Puritan Salem held the
brakes in doctrine, saw them defeated in practice, and then had its spiral
terminated from within, followed by public repentance and restitution. That
sequence is what the protocol actually credentials for, and the next chapter
explains why.

## The central move

The animating insight is an inversion, and it is structural rather than
rhetorical.

Do not ask how strangers come to cooperate. The literatures that answer that
question, being iterated-game theory, repeated-interaction reputation, and
commons governance, all run into the same limit at the boundary between
communities.

Ask instead how strangers have historically come to *fight within limits*.
Answer that, and invert each ingredient.

Bounded conflict requires four things. Firstly, the parties recognize each other
as legitimate peers, rather than as criminals, heretics, or vermin. Second,
symmetric rules bind both sides. Third, protected channels for envoys, the
wounded, and communications stay outside the conflict. Lastly, a defined ending
lets both sides return to standing.

Invert each and the primitives of cooperation fall out: standing-recognition
baselines; procedures that apply equally regardless of size or wealth;
never-target categories, being life support, rescue, children, and
communications; and termination procedures that include re-entry.

The classical name for the legitimate-peer-enemy relation is *justus hostis*.
Kith-Covenant borrows the structure and applies it to communities that are not
fighting at all.

## Two rules and a filter

The answer rests on two rules and one filter.

Firstly, *each community anchors to whatever it holds above itself*, and the
compact stays neutral on the content. A Christian community names the Trinity, a
Muslim community names Allah, a Haudenosaunee community names
*Shöñgwa'ya'di:söñ*, and a Buddhist community names the Dharma. The compact
requires that each party *have* such a ground, not that they share one.

Second, *the compact itself never becomes the ultimate*. An oath sworn before an
authority the swearer treats as exceeding the compact costs something real in a
dimension that compact-internal enforcement cannot reach. The compact is
structurally forbidden the role of that authority.

Lastly comes the filter. *A community qualifies for full membership only if its own
tradition carries five features* that historically block the loyalty-cascade
failure. Members can fail, including the virtuous ones. Virtue is received rather
than earned. Victim-selection as communal hygiene is refused. Moral obligation
extends to strangers. And the political community is not the final thing. These
are present across mature religious traditions and absent in the ideological
systems that produced the purges.

## What this book covers

Part IV runs from the ground up.

The next chapter is the substrate finding and the five features, which is the
foundation everything else stands on. Then recognition, being how a community
becomes and stays a member, in two layers that do different work. Then the four
relations a community can stand in toward another, and what is owed to a party in
each. Then disputes, being the escalation ladder, the mediator pool drawn by lot,
and the third places between communities. Then the five commons that have to be
governed jointly. Then the protected role whose whole job is to say aloud what
the institutions are becoming. Then machines, which participate only as delegates
of named humans. Then capture resistance, being the seven moves that keep
continuous structural pressure bounded. And lastly the limits, being the work no
machine can do and the one hole no institutional design closes.

## What sits below

Kith-Covenant takes a community as its atomic actor and presumes that community
is already bounded, coherent, persistent, and able to act as a unit. It does not
build that entity, and it does not know how the entity was built.

Between it and the trust substrate sits the polity tier of Part III, which
produces exactly the party this one presumes. That tier hands over six artifacts
in vocabulary the compact already speaks, and the compact never learns it exists.
The compact depends on neither lower tier.

The division of anthropology between the tiers is worth restating, because it
looks like an inconsistency and is not. The trust substrate handles anonymous
reputation *inside* one community, where anonymity is a protection. The compact
handles named humans *across* a boundary neither side rules, where anonymity is
the failure mode rather than the protection, because what has to cross is not a
score but a person whose standing is at stake in what he commits.

## Where it gets tested

Although the design target is permanent habitation off Earth, the proving ground
is Earth, and the reason is a matter of what each setting can actually test.

An Earth pilot is the decisive test of whether the compact binds *voluntarily*.
Members keep choosing it when the state system is one phone call away, and the
falsification criterion is retention despite easy exit. It is also structurally
incapable of testing enforcement against a determined defector, which only the
space case supplies.

Space is the sharpening domain rather than the main test. Habitats separated by
light-lag can neither federate under a central authority, since a decision that
takes forty minutes to travel is not a decision anyone can be governed by, nor
walk away from a bad neighbor, since there is nowhere to walk to. Every escape
hatch is welded shut at once.

Earth is the harder test, with more communities, older wounds, and the capture of
institutions by nation-states already in progress. A compact that holds where
exit is cheap has demonstrated something a compact enforced by isolation never
could.
