# The ground each community stands on

This is the load-bearing chapter, and it makes a claim most engineering documents
would not make. Long-running voluntary cooperation among unconnected communities,
measured in centuries rather than decades, rests on each community answering to
something above itself.

The claim comes out of the record rather than out of a preference. Apply a
voluntary-peer filter, which excludes imperial arrangements underwritten by
sovereign power, and rank what remains by longevity. Every durable
cross-community full compact on record rested on a transcendent moral authority.
The Westphalian system, the Hanseatic League, the merchant law, the codes duello,
and the Peace of God all operated within Christendom. The Haudenosaunee
Confederacy rested on the mandate of the Peacemaker. The Buddhist sangha, which
is the single longest-running voluntary cross-community network in the set at
roughly twenty-three centuries, rests on the Dharma. The Mali federation rested
on *Maa Ngala*.

The content of the ultimate varies. The structure does not.

Two readings of that finding are wrong, and both are common enough to be worth
foreclosing before anything else.

It is *not* that members must share a substrate. Culturally dissimilar
communities converge on shared operational rules in bounded domains all the time,
provided each party internally has a ground of its own. The Late Bronze Age
treaty grammar, the Rhodian sea law across four civilizations, and the modern
Geneva framework are all real, well-documented, and centuries long.

It is *not* that only one tradition can do this. The Buddhist case demonstrates
that the five features below can be supplied through non-theistic apparatus, and
it does so over a longer run than any of the Christian cases.

The claim, precisely: **each member community must anchor itself to something
above itself, and the compact must never claim to be that thing.** It says
nothing about which transcendent reference is right. It says that compacts run by
people who treat the compact itself as ultimate reliably eat themselves.

## Why an oath before something higher costs more

The mechanism is worth stating rather than assuming, because it is what the whole
structure rests on.

An oath sworn before an authority the swearer acknowledges as exceeding the
compact costs something real in a dimension that compact-internal enforcement
cannot muster, whether or not the other party holds the same authority. Breaking
it damages the self in a way that reputation cost does not reach.

An oath sworn before no such authority costs only what enforcement can muster,
and enforcement is not reliably available across unconnected communities. That is
the whole difficulty. If enforcement were available, none of this would be
needed, because there would be a sovereign.

The Kadesh precedent is directly relevant here. The Hittite and Egyptian parties
did not submit to a shared substrate and did not pretend to. Each invoked its own
gods as witnesses of a shared *form*, and the binding force of the treaty rested
on the internal substrate of each side doing its internal work on that side, plus
a diplomatic grammar neither side had to abandon its ground to accept. The
recognition ceremony of this compact descends from that precedent rather than
from the Nicene one.

## The five anti-spiral features

Across every long-running case the same five structural features show up, in
different idioms. They are not requirements the compact imposes. They are
properties the compact looks for in member communities, because their absence
reliably produces one specific failure: the *purity spiral*, in which competitive
virtue-signaling escalates until the community starts purging its own.

Firstly, a *universal fallen-actor anthropology* holds that everyone is fallen,
including the most virtuous member. The line between good and evil runs through each
person rather than between groups. This forecloses the us-pure and them-impure
move that purity spirals depend on.

Second, *grace over merit* means that standing is received rather than earned. That makes
competitive virtue-signaling incoherent, since if virtue is a gift there is no
status to compete for.

Third, *scapegoat-revelation or a functional equivalent* supplies a mechanism
that disarms the social reflex of resolving group anxiety by exiling somebody.
Christianity has the cross. Buddhism has *anatta* together with universal
compassion. The Iroquois have the Condolence ceremony.

Fourth, *enemy-love as an operational norm* is a structural constraint rather
than a sentiment. The community owes moral seriousness to the person
actually in front of it, regardless of category, which blocks the in-group and
out-group compression that purity spirals run on.

Lastly, *non-ultimacy of the political community* means the compact is not the
highest thing, and that something stands in judgement over it.

These are not theological claims the compact endorses. They are observable
patterns it looks for.

## What the features actually buy

Two historical facts calibrate them, and the second is the important one.

Regimes lacking the features, being the Jacobin, the Maoist, and the Stalinist,
spiral and do not stop. Their purges end only by coup or by the death of the
purger.

And a community can hold all five in doctrine and still spiral. Puritan Salem did
in 1692, when its membership-gating, its status anxiety, and the fusion of
covenant with polity defeated the features in practice.

So what the features supply is not prevention. It is *termination and repair*.
Salem self-terminated in about a year, by appeal to an authority above the court,
and then repented and paid restitution: a sequence no featureless regime has
produced.

The compact therefore reads the features in the operative practice of a
community rather than in its creed, and it expects spirals to happen. What it
credentials is the capacity to stop them.

## The anthropology this assumes

Cooperation-protocol engineering has historically imported its anthropology from
economics, and the imported picture, in which actors are self-interested,
consistent in their preferences, and roughly rational, is wrong in a way that
matters for design.

Humans are not rational utility-maximizers. They are not angels. They are, to use
the word the historical exemplars used and that this project does not hide from,
sinners. That is a load-bearing claim about what the protocol must be robust
against rather than a pessimistic flourish.

> A protocol designed for angels fails at first contact; a protocol designed for
> rational actors fails slightly later; a protocol designed for sinners has a
> chance.

Four design implications follow directly. The line between good and evil cuts
through every participant, so the protocol plans for atrocity as a normal
contingency rather than a shocking aberration. Rivalry is structural, so the
compact must not rely on scapegoating while acknowledging the energy that
scapegoating releases. Ambition must counteract ambition, so no self-interest of
any participant is realizable except through protocol behavior that also serves
another. And virtue is a disposition rather than compliance, since the compact
cannot form character and can only protect the communities in which character is
formed.

## The forcing function

Even among parties who meet all of the above, cooperation needs a reason the
parties cannot simply walk away. Mutual vulnerability is that reason.

Every Geneva prohibition holds because every belligerent is a potential victim of
every prohibited act, and none of them want to poison the well they drink from.
Prohibitions grounded in universal vulnerability are far more stable than those
grounded in moral consensus, because moral consensus is revisable in ways physics
is not.

Although that is a strong mechanism, it is uneven, and the unevenness is where
the frontier problem lives. Communities inside the compact are mutually
vulnerable in ways the compact can assume. Communities outside it stand in an
asymmetric relation that the European public law notoriously exploited: those
powers respected legitimate-enemy standing among themselves while denying it to
their colonies. The failure was the failure of the substrate itself, since the
scriptural mandate about the neighbor and the actual face in front of you was
betrayed by its nominal carriers.

The compact must not recapitulate that, and its correction is structural rather
than aspirational, being the four standing categories of a later chapter.

One half of the problem is now closed rather than merely scoped. What the compact
owes a party at its frontier no longer waits on reciprocal exposure to justify
it, because the compact grounds that forbearance in a thin functional minimum it
has adopted, being the short list any viable order among vulnerable, roughly
equal, imperfectly altruistic parties has to contain. Mutual vulnerability
remains uneven, and the ground does not even it out. What changes is that the
asymmetry is no longer also a gap in the account the compact gives of why it owes
anything to a party it does not need.

## What this costs, stated plainly

The substrate requirement excludes pro-human, pro-cooperation communities that do
not acknowledge a Creator from full membership. That is deliberate, and it has a
real cost, and the cost is stated rather than minimized.

And the finding itself is provisional rather than proven. It is abductive, drawn
from a finite and selection-weighted case set, with live confounds that include
literacy, trade density, imperial backing, and geography, none yet controlled for
on the merits. It is held provisionally, with stated falsification criteria.

A protocol that pretended to have answered everything would be precisely the kind
of protocol the fallen-actor anthropology forbids.
