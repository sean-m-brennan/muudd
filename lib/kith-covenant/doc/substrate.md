# The ground each community stands on

This is the load-bearing chapter, and it makes a claim most engineering
documents would not make. Long-running voluntary cooperation among unconnected
communities, measured in centuries rather than decades, rests on each community
answering to something above itself.

We take the claim from the record rather than from a preference. Apply a
voluntary-peer filter, which excludes imperial arrangements underwritten by
sovereign power, and rank what remains by longevity. Every durable
cross-community full compact on record rested on a transcendent moral authority.
The Westphalian system, the Hanseatic League, the merchant law, the codes
duello, and the Peace of God all operated within Christendom. The Haudenosaunee
Confederacy rested on the mandate of the Peacemaker. The Buddhist sangha, which
is the single longest-running voluntary cross-community network in the set at
roughly twenty-three centuries, rests on the Dharma. The Mali federation rested
on *Maa Ngala*. The content of the ultimate varies from case to case, and the
structure does not vary at all.

Two readings of that finding are wrong, and both are common enough that we had
better foreclose them before anything else.

It is *not* that members must share a substrate. Culturally dissimilar
communities converge on shared operational rules in bounded domains all the
time, provided each party internally has a ground of its own. The Late Bronze
Age treaty grammar, the Rhodian sea law across four civilizations, and the
modern Geneva framework are all real, well-documented, and centuries long.

It is *not* that only one tradition can do this. The Buddhist case demonstrates
that the five features below can be supplied through non-theistic apparatus, and
it does so over a longer run than any of the Christian cases.

The claim, precisely, is that **each member community must anchor itself to
something above itself, and the compact must never claim to be that thing.** It
says nothing about which transcendent reference is right. It says that compacts
run by people who treat the compact itself as ultimate reliably eat themselves.

## Why an oath before something higher costs more

The mechanism is worth stating rather than assuming, because it is what the
whole structure rests on.

An oath sworn before an authority the swearer acknowledges as exceeding the
compact costs something real in a dimension that compact-internal enforcement
cannot muster, whether or not the other party holds the same authority. Breaking
it damages the self in a way that reputation cost never reaches.

An oath sworn before no such authority costs only what enforcement can muster,
and enforcement is not reliably available across unconnected communities. That
is the whole difficulty. If enforcement were available, none of this would be
needed, because there would be a sovereign.

The Kadesh precedent is directly relevant here. The Hittite and Egyptian parties
did not submit to a shared substrate and did not pretend to. Each invoked its
own gods as witnesses of a shared *form*, and the binding force of the treaty
rested on the internal substrate of each side doing its internal work on that
side, plus a diplomatic grammar neither side had to abandon its ground to
accept. Our recognition ceremony descends from that precedent rather than from
the Nicene one.

## The five anti-spiral features

Across every long-running case the same five structural features show up, in
different idioms. They are not requirements the compact imposes. They are
properties the compact looks for in member communities, because their absence
reliably produces one specific failure, being the *purity spiral*, in which
competitive virtue-signaling escalates until the community starts purging its
own.

Firstly, a *universal fallen-actor anthropology* holds that everyone is fallen,
including the most virtuous member. The line between good and evil runs through
each person rather than between groups, which forecloses the us-pure and
them-impure move that purity spirals depend on.

Second, *grace over merit* means that standing is received rather than earned.
That makes competitive virtue-signaling incoherent, since if virtue is a gift
there is no status to compete for.

Third, *scapegoat-revelation or a functional equivalent* supplies a mechanism
that disarms the social reflex of resolving group anxiety by exiling somebody.
Christianity has the cross. Buddhism has *anatta* together with universal
compassion. The Iroquois have the Condolence ceremony.

Fourth, *enemy-love as an operational norm* is a structural constraint rather
than a sentiment. The community owes moral seriousness to the person actually in
front of it, whatever category that person falls in, which blocks the in-group
and out-group compression that purity spirals run on.

Lastly, *non-ultimacy of the political community* means the compact is not the
highest thing, and that something stands in judgment over it.

These are not theological claims the compact endorses. They are observable
patterns it looks for.

## What the features actually buy

Two historical facts calibrate them, and the second is the important one.

Regimes lacking the features, being the Jacobin, the Maoist, and the Stalinist,
spiral and do not stop. Their purges end only by coup or by the death of the
purger.

And a community can hold all five in doctrine and still spiral. Puritan Salem
did in 1692, when its membership-gating, its status anxiety, and the fusion of
covenant with polity defeated the features in practice.

So what the features supply is not prevention but *termination and repair*.
Salem self-terminated in about a year, by appeal to an authority above the
court, and then repented and paid restitution, which is a sequence no
featureless regime has produced.

The compact therefore reads the features in the operative practice of a
community rather than in its creed, and it expects spirals to happen. What it
credentials is the capacity to stop them.

## The anthropology this assumes

Cooperation-protocol engineering has historically imported its anthropology from
economics, and the imported picture, in which actors are self-interested,
consistent in their preferences, and roughly rational, is wrong in a way that
matters for design.

Humans are not rational utility-maximizers. They are not angels. They are, to
use the word the historical exemplars used and that we do not hide from,
sinners. That is a load-bearing claim about what the protocol must be robust
against rather than a pessimistic flourish.

> A protocol designed for angels fails at first contact; a protocol designed for
> rational actors fails slightly later; a protocol designed for sinners has a
> chance.

Four design implications follow directly. The line between good and evil cuts
through every participant, so the protocol plans for atrocity as a normal
contingency rather than a shocking aberration. Rivalry is structural, so the
compact must not rely on scapegoating while acknowledging the energy that
scapegoating releases. Ambition must counteract ambition, so no self-interest of
any participant is realizable except through protocol behavior that also serves
another. And virtue is a disposition rather than compliance, since the compact
cannot form character and can only protect the communities in which character is
formed.

## The forcing function

Even among parties who meet all of the above, cooperation needs a reason the
parties cannot simply walk away. Mutual vulnerability is that reason.

Every Geneva prohibition holds because every belligerent is a potential victim
of every prohibited act, and none of them want to poison the well they drink
from. Prohibitions grounded in universal vulnerability are far more stable than
those grounded in moral consensus, because moral consensus is revisable in ways
physics is not.

That is a strong mechanism and it is an uneven one, and the unevenness is where
the frontier problem lives. Communities inside the compact are mutually
vulnerable in ways the compact can assume. Communities outside it stand in an
asymmetric relation that the European public law notoriously exploited, since
those powers respected legitimate-enemy standing among themselves while denying
it to their colonies. The failure was a failure of the substrate itself, because
the scriptural mandate about the neighbor and the actual face in front of you
was betrayed by its nominal carriers.

We must not recapitulate that, and our correction is structural rather than
aspirational, being the four standing categories of a later chapter.

One half of the problem is now closed rather than merely scoped. What the
compact owes a party at its frontier no longer waits on reciprocal exposure to
justify it, because the compact grounds that forbearance in a thin functional
minimum it has adopted, being the short list any viable order among vulnerable,
roughly equal, imperfectly altruistic parties has to contain. Mutual
vulnerability remains uneven, and the ground does not even it out. What changes
is that the asymmetry is no longer also a gap in the account the compact gives
of why it owes anything to a party it does not need.

## What this costs, stated plainly

The substrate requirement excludes pro-human, pro-cooperation communities that
do not acknowledge a Creator from full membership. That is deliberate, it has a
real cost, and we state the cost rather than minimize it.

And the finding itself is provisional rather than proven. It is abductive, drawn
from a finite and selection-weighted case set, with live confounds that include
literacy, trade density, imperial backing, and geography, none of them yet
controlled for on the merits. We hold it provisionally, with stated
falsification criteria, because a protocol that pretended to have answered
everything would be exactly the kind of protocol the fallen-actor anthropology
forbids.
