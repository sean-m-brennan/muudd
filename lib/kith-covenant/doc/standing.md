# Four categories of standing

At any moment, one community stands in exactly one of four relations to another.
Naming them is not bookkeeping. The category decides what is owed, what is
protected, what may be withheld, and whether the relation is expected to end.

We use four because the alternatives are worse in known ways. A binary of member
and non-member has no room for a peer you are in dispute with, so every dispute
reads as a break. A binary of friend and enemy has no room for a stranger you
have simply not met, which is the arrangement that let European powers respect
legitimate-enemy standing among themselves while denying it to their colonies.
So the four are these.

*Member.* Full participation, meaning the mediator pool, the commons, the
escalation ladder, and everything else.

*Justus hostis.* A peer adversary, being communities in active dispute who both
remain inside the legitimate-peer frame. Symmetric rules apply. Scrutiny on
substantive commitments is heightened. Rescue, communications, and navigational
data still flow. The presumption is that the dispute ends and the parties return
to member standing.

*Anathema.* Formally cut off after sustained bad-faith conduct, multiple failed
restoration attempts, and a compact-wide deliberation. Material stake is
forfeit. Re-entry exists and the burden is heavy. The presumption is that
recovery is possible.

*Non-member encountered.* A community with no compact relation at all. A
reduced-envelope default applies, being basic courtesy, navigational data when
it is safety-relevant, rescue where distress is present, and fresh commitments
requiring referral to a human.

## The second tier inside anathema

Within anathema sits a second and extremely rare tier, being *hostis humani
generis* proper, the classical enemy of humankind. It covers pirates, slavers,
and actors standing outside every cooperative framework.

Three things distinguish it. The evidentiary threshold is higher still. Re-entry
does not meaningfully arise. And the presumption is always anathema first.

Nearly all real conflict should resolve below either designation. A compact
whose designation machinery runs often is a compact whose designation machinery
has become something else.

This tier is also where the inversion of the previous chapters reaches its outer
boundary, and the corpus says so rather than papering over it. The
legitimate-enemy frame is exactly the thing that cannot be extended to a party
who cannot be given bounded-enemy standing, and that is a conceptual limit of
the frame rather than a gap in its implementation.

## What an outsider is owed, and on what ground

A community that never joined still gets basic courtesy, safety-relevant
navigational data, and rescue when it is in distress. And the small number of
actors the compact condemns outright are condemned by a standard they never
agreed to.

Both of those look like problems for a consent-based arrangement, because they
are the two places where consent has run out. So we state the ground the compact
stands on rather than leave it implicit.

That ground is a *thin functional minimum*. Any arrangement that lasts among
parties who can all be hurt, who are roughly matched, and who are only sometimes
generous, has to contain a short list of things nobody does to anybody. The
compact adopts that list as the standard for its own conduct.

Four properties bound what the adoption does, and the fourth is the one keeping
it honest.

Firstly, it is *adopted rather than derived*. The compact does not claim to have
proven it from first principles.

Second, it *restrains the compact* rather than granting the compact anything. It
is a limit on conduct and not a warrant.

Third, it is *thin*. It is the minimum content rather than a moral code, and it
decides very little.

Lastly, and most importantly, it *reaches the boundary of the compact and stops
there*. It grants no writ inside any member community.

That last property is why the minimum is not on the list of matters the compact
structurally refuses to decide. That list names matters belonging to members
that the compact will not touch, and the minimum decides nothing belonging to a
member. Because it is a restraint on the compact itself, contracting it carries
the same asymmetric higher bar as contracting the list, and the contraction is a
capture signal on the same terms.

## No verdict on a member's interior

This is the sharpest refusal in the whole design, and it is deliberate rather
than squeamish.

The compact passes no judgment on the internal morality of a member community
until that morality reaches the covenant itself. It reaches the covenant through
conduct toward another member, conduct at the boundary, conduct toward a party
that arrived through a compact channel, what envoys carry into compact business,
or a hollowing that re-affirmation cannot survive.

Two reasons, and both are structural.

A body that sets itself up to judge the inner lives of its members has claimed
to be better than them. That claim makes the compact the ultimate that the fifth
movement forecloses, and it rots cooperation faster than anything it would
catch.

And a compact with the standing to audit its members has handed whoever controls
the deliberative machinery a legitimate instrument for finding a member
deficient. The instrument does not need to be abused often to be decisive.

The cost is real and we do not hide it. The compact can carry a member whose
interior life it would not defend, and it does so knowingly, on the judgment
that a compact confident enough to audit the souls of its members fails sooner
and worse.

## Discernment, and why the categories cannot be automated

The categories are procedurally recorded and not procedurally decided.

A community acting in good faith while in dispute looks superficially like one
acting in bad faith, which looks superficially like one outside every framework.
The distinction is virtue-cultivated rather than procedural, and it is one of
the items on the list of work no machine does.

The corpus takes the same posture toward the neighbor, and the reasoning
generalizes. No definition of the neighbor ever holds. Every attempt to draw the
boundary is answered by a case that crosses it, and the refusal to settle the
definition is the working part rather than vagueness. The protocol takes three
things from that. Credentialing examines operative practice rather than
confessed creed, enemy-love is written as an operational norm rather than a
sentiment, and the four categories rest on encounter rather than on a roster.

Discernment within the encounter is the guardrail against the opposite failure,
which is a fantasy-universal love that recognizes nobody in particular.
