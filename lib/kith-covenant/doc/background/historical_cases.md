# Historical cases of bounded cooperation among strangers

## Thesis

the kith-covenant design is grounded in the inverse argument
([Justus hostis](justus_hostis.md)). The structural features that
historically allowed strangers to *bound their conflicts* yield, when
inverted, the primitives by which strangers can cooperate. This document
catalogs the cases that argument depends on. For each case it reports
the founding context, the mechanisms that actually did the work, what
the case accomplished while it functioned, the way it eventually decayed
or collapsed, and the design lesson kith-covenant should take from it.

The case selection is deliberately weighted toward systems that worked
*without* a single overarching sovereign and *across* communities that
did not share purpose, identity, or governance. Geneva-tradition cases
are referenced only briefly here because they receive operational
treatment in [Bounded conflict](bounded_conflict.md). The Iroquois
Confederacy receives extended treatment as the most important
non-Christian case in the comparison set. Its weight in the argument is
disproportionate to its size precisely because it shows that the
sacred-substrate function does not require Christianity in particular.

## Reading order

The cases are presented roughly in increasing distance from the
intuitions of the modern reader. Western institutional readers
will find Westphalia and lex mercatoria most legible. The Peace
of God and the Iroquois Kaianerekowa will require more
imaginative work. The patterns that recur across all of them are
summarized at the end.

## Westphalia and the jus publicum Europaeum

### Founding context

The Peace of Westphalia (1648) consists of two treaties. Münster
between Spain and the Dutch Republic and the Empire and France, and
Osnabrück between the Empire and Sweden, ending the Thirty Years' War
in central Europe and the Eighty Years' War in the Low Countries. The
Thirty Years' War had killed perhaps a third of the German-speaking
population. The treaties did not restore prewar conditions. They
established a new framework whose primary innovation was the
*depoliticization of religious adherence as grounds for war between
sovereigns*.

The Peace of Augsburg (1555) had already established *cuius regio,
eius religio* (each sovereign settles the religious question within
his own territory), but Augsburg recognized only Lutheran and Catholic;
Westphalia added Calvinist. More importantly, Westphalia generalized
the principle into what became the Westphalian system: territorial
integrity, sovereign equality, non-intervention in domestic affairs.

### Mechanism

The system worked through several tightly coupled institutions:

- **Sovereign equality.** Recognition, not capability, was the
  primary currency. Even though their material weights were
  utterly different, a small principality and a great power had
  formally equal standing in the procedure.
- **Resident embassies.** A permanent diplomatic corps emerged in the
  17th-18th centuries (earlier ad hoc legations gave way to standing
  embassies in major capitals) with diplomatic immunity, codified
  precedence, and a shared procedural language (largely French by the
  18th c.).
- **Treaty law as the operative idiom.** Disputes ended in written
  agreements with named witnesses, ratification procedures, and (often)
  guarantor powers. Treaty texts circulated in print. The public
  character of the agreements was load-bearing.
- **A working *jus in bello* distinct from *jus ad bellum*.** Wars
  could be fought lawfully even when their causes were dubious. The
  procedure was judged independently of the cause, which kept the
  procedure available across cause-shifts.

### Output

For roughly 250 years, European wars were largely *bounded*. They had
named beginnings and ends, identifiable belligerents, defined theaters,
exchanges of prisoners, and termination treaties that included the
defeated as participants rather than reducing them to subjects. This is
not an idealization of the period (it included serial partitions of
Poland, the Napoleonic disruption, colonial atrocities outside Europe);
it is the observation that European intra-system warfare worked very
differently from extra-system warfare and from the post-1914 era.

### Decay

The decay vectors are largely those discussed in
[Justus hostis](justus_hostis.md#how-the-jus-publicum-fell-apart),
recapped briefly:

- The *partitions of Poland* (1772, 1793, 1795) showed that great
  powers could simply *abolish a sovereign* by collusion when the
  Westphalian principle of recognition collided with their joint
  interest. The system already had a lethal vulnerability.
- The *Napoleonic disruption* (1796-1815) treated dynasties as
  interchangeable and redrew borders by conquest. The *Concert of
  Europe* (1815) was a partial restoration but introduced explicit
  Great Power oversight, undermining the formal equality of smaller
  sovereigns.
- The *colonial frontier* never fit the model; European powers
  did not grant *justus hostis* status to non-European opponents.
  The internal coherence of the system depended on having an
  outside in which its rules did not apply.
- The *nationalist principle* (consolidated at Versailles 1919) replaced
  recognition-of-sovereigns with recognition-of-peoples and made
  domestic ethnic composition a legitimate matter of international
  concern.
- *Universalist ideologies* (in 20th-c. forms: liberal, communist, and
  the universal-human-rights frame) finished the depoliticization in
  reverse: the opponent became a criminal or a heretic rather than a
  peer.

### Lesson for kith-covenant

The Westphalian system *worked* for an unusually long time and across
deep divisions, but it carried two latent vulnerabilities kith-covenant must
design out. It depended on an excluded outside (the colonies) where its
rules did not apply, and it had no defense against great-power
collusion against a smaller member. Both vulnerabilities are inversions
of the symmetric-rules requirement. The kith-covenant symmetric rules must apply
to *all* members regardless of size and must include explicit
provisions against insider collusion that targets a member.

## The Peace of God and Truce of God

### Founding context

The *Pax Dei* (Peace of God) and *Treuga Dei* (Truce of God) were
ecclesiastical movements emerging in late-10th- and 11th-century France
and spreading across western Europe. Carolingian central authority had
collapsed. Private lordly violence (feud, raiding, predation on
unarmed populations) was endemic. Local bishops, gathered in regional
councils (Charroux 989, Le Puy 990, Narbonne 1054), declared categories
of persons and places exempt from violence, with violators subject to
ecclesiastical anathema.

### Mechanism

Two distinguishable but overlapping movements:

- **The Peace of God** named *categories of person and place* protected
  at all times: clergy, monks, women, peasants, pilgrims, merchants.
  Churches, cemeteries, mills. Violators were excommunicated and, in
  more developed forms, faced the prospect of a "peace militia" (a
  league of laymen sworn to enforce the peace).
- **The Truce of God** named *times* during which all violence was
  forbidden: from Wednesday evening through Monday morning, on feast
  days, during Lent and Advent. The rationale was explicitly liturgical.
  These were the times of Christ's passion, resurrection, and the
  major mysteries.

Enforcement was anathema (cutting the violator off from sacraments and
salvation), supplemented by collective sworn militias in some regions.
Many participating bishops and lords swore oaths on relics to uphold
the peace.

### Output

Where it functioned, the movement substantially reduced predation on
non-combatants. More importantly for the kith-covenant purposes, it demonstrated
that *categories of inviolability could be sustained without state
authority*, by sacred substrate alone, in a period when no state was
strong enough to do the work. The Peace of God arguably trained the
moral grammar that later bounded warfare in *jus in bello* tradition.

### Decay

The Peace movements were largely absorbed into the rising territorial
monarchies and the Crusading project (which redirected aristocratic
violence outward) by the late 12th century. They did not "fail" so
much as get overtaken by stronger institutions that performed
overlapping functions, though the Crusading redirection is itself a
cautionary tale about how a peace movement can be repurposed into a
weapon by giving its adherents an external object.

### Lesson for kith-covenant

The Peace of God is the strongest pre-modern example of a
sacred- substrate-only enforcement regime working at scale
across communities that shared no common government. Its
mechanism was *anathema.* Exclusion from a shared sacred
reality. Although the closest contemporary analog available to
kith-covenant is *exclusion from the compact*, exclusion only
carries comparable weight if the compact itself is
sacred-equivalent, which (per
[Justus hostis](justus_hostis.md#substrate-affords-emergence-it-does-not-dictate-content))
kith-covenant cannot dictate but only afford. The Peace of God
also illustrates the redirection failure mode. A peace regime
that gives its adherents a sanctioned external object (Crusade)
becomes the engine of the violence it formed to bound.

## The Hanseatic League

### Founding context

The Hanse began as a loose association of north German merchants
trading in the Baltic and North Sea (12th-13th c.) and consolidated
into a league of cities around 1356 (Lübeck Hansetag). At its peak it
included between 70 and ~170 cities, with primary influence from
Lübeck, Hamburg, Bremen, Cologne, Danzig (Gdańsk), Riga, and many
others. It was never a state. It had no sovereign, no army, no
treasury beyond ad hoc contributions, and no permanent bureaucracy.

### Mechanism

- **The Hansetag** (Hanseatic Diet) was a consultative assembly at
  Lübeck, attended by member-city representatives. It made binding
  decisions on common policy (boycott declarations, joint negotiations
  with foreign powers, regulation of the *Kontore*).
- **The *Kontore*** were privileged trading posts in foreign
  jurisdictions: Steelyard (London), Tyske Bryggen (Bergen), Peterhof
  (Novgorod), Bruges. Each *Kontor* had its own internal governance,
  its own court for disputes among Hanseatic merchants, and negotiated
  privileges from the host sovereign.
- **Verhansung**, collective expulsion from the league, was the
  ultimate sanction against members who defected from agreed positions
  or violated league norms. Cities under Verhansung lost access to all
  league trading privileges across northern Europe simultaneously, a
  catastrophic commercial penalty.
- **Collective boycott** of foreign powers who violated Hanseatic
  privileges was the main external weapon of the league. The 1284
  boycott of Norway over privileges at Bergen ended after about a
  year with Norwegian capitulation. Many similar campaigns
  followed.

### Output

The Hanse dominated Baltic and North Sea commerce for ~300 years,
created the first sustained transregional commercial law in northern
Europe, and provided collective defense (occasional naval action) and
collective negotiation against rising territorial powers.

### Decay

Multiple converging pressures destroyed it across the 16th-17th
centuries:

- **Rise of strong territorial states** (Sweden, Denmark, England,
  Brandenburg-Prussia) with standing navies that could simply force
  open Baltic markets the Hanse had previously controlled. The league
  had no comparable military instrument.
- **Atlantic shift.** Discovery of the New World and the rise of
  Iberian, Dutch, and English Atlantic trade moved economic gravity
  away from the Baltic. The Hanse's geographical center became
  peripheral.
- **Reformation confessional split.** Member cities ended up on
  different sides of the Lutheran/Catholic split, and (after Augsburg
  1555) under rulers whose religious settlements diverged. It is
        tempting to describe this as a "substrate fracture," but
        the claim does not survive close inspection. The
        *substrate* in the kith-covenant sense (a transcendent
        moral authority before whom oaths bind) was the same on
        both sides of the confessional line. Both Lutheran and
        Catholic cities continued to swear before the God of the
        same scripture. What divided them was first-order
        theology (church authority, sacramental theology,
        ecclesiology), not the substrate. The Hanse's ~150 years
        of continued operation after Augsburg 1555 is therefore
        *consistent* with the substrate thesis, not a
        counterexample to it. The substrate persisted across the
        confessional divide, which is exactly why Hanseatic oaths
        continued to function. The confessional split did
        contribute to decline through secondary channels (the
        religious settlements of rulers constraining civic
        autonomy, confessional alliance politics overriding
        league loyalty, inter-city trust attrition at the
        first-order layer) but these were accelerants on top of
        the economic and political shifts already under way, not
        a primary substrate-level collapse.
- **Capture by host sovereigns.** As the home territorial rulers
  of the cities (Brandenburg, Sweden) consolidated power, league
  membership became subordinate to state policy, and cities could
  no longer collectively defy their sovereigns to honor Hanse
  positions.
- **Failure to adapt institutional form.** The Hanse never developed
  taxation, a standing fleet, a permanent secretariat, or any other
  durable apparatus. It remained a 13th-century institutional design
  in a 17th-century world.

The last Hansetag in 1669 attended by only nine cities is the symbolic
endpoint. The Hanse formally dissolved in stages thereafter.

### Lesson for kith-covenant

Three sharp lessons follow. First, a confederation without taxation, military
authority, or permanent staff is brittle to external coercion when
states arise that have all three. Kith-Covenant must answer what its
*permanent capacity* is. Not "central authority" (the Hanse's absence
was a feature) but some durable institutional mass that cannot be
casually overwhelmed. Second, the Hanse is informative for the
substrate/first-order distinction
([Justus hostis](justus_hostis.md#the-sacred-substrate-and-the-fallen-actor)).
Its ~150 years of continued operation across the Reformation
confessional divide shows that cooperation survives first-order
theological disagreement provided the underlying substrate (shared
transcendent moral authority, shared oath-binding) persists. What
ultimately ended the Hanse was external coercion, economic gravity
shifting Atlantic-ward, and institutional ossification, not substrate
collapse. Third, *Verhansung* was a working precedent for collective
sanction by a sovereign-less league. Expulsion from the compact
privileges is a real instrument provided the privileges are real.

## Lex Mercatoria

### Founding context

The medieval law merchant (*lex mercatoria*) developed organically
from the late 11th century onward, particularly around the Champagne
fairs (12th-13th c.), the Italian maritime cities, and the Hanseatic
North. It was customary law made by and for merchants, often without
state participation, applicable across jurisdictions because merchants
needed predictable rules at every node of their networks.

(Caveat: recent scholarship, Emily Kadens, Ron Harris, and others,
has questioned the older picture of a single unified "law merchant,"
arguing that practice was more local and varied than the traditional
account suggests. The discussion below uses the traditional picture
because the *function* the picture describes really did exist, even
if its institutional unity was patchier than once supposed.)

### Mechanism

- **Custom-based rules** evolving from merchant practice and recorded
  over time. The *Consolato del Mare* (Mediterranean, 14th c.), the
  *Lex Rhodia* (ancient maritime), the rolls of Oleron, and the laws
  of Wisby were significant compilations.
- **Pie powder courts** (*curia pedis pulverizati*, "dusty feet") at
  English fairs gave summary justice to traveling merchants while the
  fair lasted. Disputes were decided same-day so merchants could
  continue.
- **Fair courts and consular courts** more generally provided
  jurisdictions specific to merchants, often outside the regular royal
  or feudal courts.
- **Reputation and exclusion as enforcement.** A merchant who refused
  to honor an arbitral award lost access to the trading networks that
  recognized the award. The threat operated across jurisdictions
  because the networks did.

### Output

Cross-jurisdictional commerce at substantial scale, in an era when
royal courts were slow, expensive, and unpredictable. Merchants could
trade with strangers in distant lands with reasonable confidence that
disputes would be adjudicated by familiar standards.

### Decay

- **Absorption into national law.** Lord Mansfield's incorporation of
  the *Law Merchant* into English common law (mid-18th c.) and the
  Napoleonic Code (1804) substantially absorbed the substantive
  content into state-enforced commercial codes.
- **State-court jurisdictional assertion.** As territorial states
  grew, their courts asserted jurisdiction over commercial disputes,
  displacing the older merchant fora.
- **Standardization of contracts** moved from merchant custom to
  state regulation. The cooperative rule-making of merchant practice
  gave way to top-down statutory regimes.

Although the *new lex mercatoria* (since the 1960s: ICC
arbitration, UNIDROIT Principles of International Commercial
Contracts) is a partial revival, it operates within space the
states permit.

### Lesson for kith-covenant

The lex mercatoria shows that reputation-based, non-state
cross-jurisdictional cooperation is *possible* and was not an
historical accident. It also shows that the durability of the
regime depends on what reputation can travel on. The medieval
merchant network was the substrate. Once states centralized
communication, banking, and adjudication, the older substrate was
undercut and the regime was absorbed. Kith-Covenant must answer.
When most infrastructure is captured by state actors, what is
reputation portable on? This is the central question for
[Reputation bridges](reputation_bridges.md).

## Codes Duello

### Founding context

The personal duel as a formal institution emerges in late-medieval
Italy, spreads through 16th-17th-century European aristocracy, and
peaks in the 18th-19th centuries. Major codifications include the
Italian treatises (Muzio's *Il Duello*, 1550, and successors), the
Irish *Code Duello* (1777, formalized at Clonmel and adopted across
Britain and the United States), and various French and German codes.
The institution was specific to the aristocratic and gentry class,
later extended to military officers and bourgeois professionals. It
was *not* universal across society.

### Mechanism

- **The challenge**, written or verbal, converted a perceived
  offense into a formal procedure with named rungs and defined exit
  conditions at each rung. The challenge itself was a substitute for
  ambush and feud.
- **Seconds** (typically two per principal) were the central
  procedural innovation. Their duties included: receiving the
  challenge, negotiating with the seconds of the other side in
  attempts to resolve the dispute without a meeting, agreeing on
  weapons, time, ground, and rules of engagement, and ensuring
  the meeting (if it occurred) was conducted according to form.
  Seconds were active de-escalators, not just witnesses.
- **The meeting** (pistols at measured paces, swords on prepared
  ground) was conducted under strict procedural rules. Honor was
  satisfied by the meeting itself in many traditions. The *deloping*
  practice (deliberately firing wide) was common and often
  mutual.
- **Honor satisfied.** Once the meeting occurred according to form,
  the dispute was formally closed regardless of outcome. The principals
  resumed normal social relations.

### Output

Bounded the lethal violence of an armed aristocratic class. The
overwhelming majority of disputes were settled by the seconds before
ever reaching the field. The institution channeled what would
otherwise have been blood-feud predation on each other and on
non-combatants into a controlled, infrequent, ritualized form.

### Decay

- **Criminalization.** The British Army banned dueling in 1844;
  Prussia tightened enforcement through the late 19th c.; American
  states banned it during the 19th c.. Widespread post-1800
  criminalization across European states.
- **Public ridicule via mass journalism.** As the periodical press
  developed in the 19th c., dueling shifted from honorable to
  ridiculous in mass culture. The institution depended on its public
  meaning. Once that meaning curdled into mockery, the institution
  could not be maintained.
- **Democratization.** The honor code did not generalize beyond the
  aristocratic and officer class. As political power broadened and
  the aristocracy lost its position, the institution had no broader
  cultural carrier.
- **Lethality of firearms.** As firearms became more accurate and
  reliable, duels became almost certainly fatal, and the cost-benefit
  inverted. The institution had been bearable when the meeting was
  often a flesh wound or a face-saving exchange.
- **Industrial-scale war.** The World Wars made personal honor as a
  social arbiter incompatible with war as a fact of mass life.
  Surviving duelists came home from the trenches with no remaining
  use for the form.

The institution had effectively ceased by the mid-20th century.

### Lesson for kith-covenant

Codes duello are the cleanest historical example of a *graduated
escalation ladder with face-saving exits at every rung* (see
[Bounded conflict](bounded_conflict.md#escalation-ladders)) working
as designed. The third-party seconds-as-de-escalators role is the
direct precedent for the kith-covenant mediator architecture. The decay mode is
also instructive. A bounded-conflict regime that depends on the
specific cultural position of a specific class does not survive that
class losing position. Kith-Covenant cannot stake itself on any single
sociological substrate that is itself contingent on a particular
political configuration.

## The Iroquois Confederacy (Haudenosaunee Kaianerekowa)

### Founding context

The Haudenosaunee ("People of the Longhouse") Confederacy bound
originally five nations (Mohawk, Oneida, Onondaga, Cayuga, Seneca)
across what is now upstate New York and the surrounding region. The
Tuscarora joined ca. 1722, making it the Six Nations. The founding,
attributed to the Peacemaker (Deganawida) and his disciple and
spokesman Hiawatha, is dated by various scholars to a range from
ca. 1142 (some Haudenosaunee oral tradition and recent astronomical
analysis by Mann and Fields) to ca. 1450-1570 (older European
scholarship). The pre-confederation period was characterized by
intense intertribal blood-feuding. The founding narrative centers on
ending that feuding.

This is the most important non-Christian case in the kith-covenant comparison
set, but (as the discussion of substrate below makes clear) it is
*not* a secular case. Calling it a "non-Christian counterpoint"
leaves a wrong impression of secularity. Haudenosaunee
cosmology is explicitly theistic, with a Creator at the apex of its
order. Its weight in the argument is therefore not that the
sacred-substrate function can be performed *without* a sacred substrate,
but that it can be performed by a non-Christian one, and (as the
"transcendent moral authority pattern" section below develops) by one that shares with
Christianity the structural feature of pointing to a single ultimate
source.

### The substrate

Haudenosaunee cosmology recognizes a Creator at the apex of its order:
*Shöñgwa'ya'di:söñ* in Onondaga, *Sonkwaiatison* in Mohawk,
*Ha:wëni:yo:'* in Seneca. In modern Haudenosaunee English usage,
"the Creator" or "Sky Holder." The *Ohén:ton Karihwatékwen*
("Thanksgiving Address" or "the words that come before all else"),
recited at the opening of every council, ceremony, and gathering,
addresses thanks in ascending sequence through the levels of creation:
people, mother earth, waters, fish, plants, food plants, medicine
herbs, animals, trees, birds, four winds, thunderers, sun, grandmother
moon, stars, enlightened teachers, and finally the Creator who is the
source of all. The structure is explicitly hierarchical and
explicitly culminates in a single ultimate.

The Council Fire at Onondaga is not merely a physical fire but a
sacred object. Its continual burning is itself a religious act. Its
extinction would mark not only institutional dissolution but a kind
of cosmological rupture. The Peacemaker (Deganawida) is in the
tradition not a mere historical figure but a sacred one, sent or
inspired by the Creator to bring the Great Peace. The Great Law
itself is delivered with sacred authority, not merely customary
weight.

These details correct the impression (easy to absorb from European
secondary sources written without sufficient care) that the
Confederacy was somehow a "secular" or "rationalist" achievement.
It was not. It was a deeply religious settlement whose cosmology
shares with the Abrahamic traditions the structural feature of a
*single ultimate* at the apex, even where its surface theology is
otherwise quite different.

### Mechanism

The Kaianerekowa (Great Law of Peace) is preserved in oral tradition
and in wampum belts. Modern reconstructions exist (Arthur Parker's
1916 version, Paul Wallace's, and authoritative modern Haudenosaunee
sources such as Chief Jacob Thomas's recitation). Its central
institutional features:

- **The Council Fire at Onondaga.** The fifty hereditary chiefs
  (*hoyaneh*; often translated "lords" or "sachems") convened around
  a central fire kept by the Onondaga. As discussed under "The
  substrate" above, the fire was a sacred object, not merely a
  symbol. Its continual burning was a religious act and its
  extinction would mark a cosmological as well as institutional
  rupture.
- **Selection by clan mothers.** Chiefs were nominated by the senior
  women of each clan and could be removed (*dehorned*) by them. This
  inverts most early-modern European selection procedures and
  decoupled office from inheritance-by-male-line.
- **Consensus across nations, with structured deliberation.** The
  council deliberated by side: Mohawk and Seneca on one side
  ("Elder Brothers"), Oneida and Cayuga on the other ("Younger
  Brothers"), with Onondaga as fire-keepers and tie-breakers. Each
  side discussed internally, then the sides exchanged positions, then
  the Onondaga ratified or sent back for further discussion.
  Decisions required unanimity across the nations.
- **The Condolence ceremony.** Before any deliberation involving a
  bereaved party, a formal mourning was performed (the "Three Bare
  Words" wiping away tears, removing obstructions from the throat,
  cleansing from grief) to clear the minds of the bereaved before
  they were asked to deliberate. This is the most distinctive
  institutional innovation: *the recognition that a person consumed
  by grief or rage cannot deliberate, and that the community owes
  them ceremonial restoration before requiring them to participate*.
  No comparable institution existed in the European tradition.
- **Internal sovereignty preserved.** Each nation retained internal
  governance. The Confederacy operated only on matters affecting all
  members or external relations.
- **Wampum as record.** Sequenced shell beads recorded treaties, the
  Great Law itself, and historical events. The Hiawatha Belt (five
  figures linked around a central tree) symbolized the union.

### Output

For somewhere between three and seven centuries (depending on dating),
the Confederacy maintained internal peace among its constituent
nations and conducted unified foreign policy, including with the
Dutch, French, and English colonial presences. Coordinated diplomacy
allowed the Haudenosaunee to play colonial powers against each other
for substantial periods, preserving their territory and independence
longer than most surrounding peoples. The influence of the Great Law
on later American federalism is debated in scholarship. The connection
is real (Franklin and others knew of the Confederacy and cited it) but
its weight in the actual constitutional drafting is contested.

### Decay

- **Disease.** Smallpox and other introduced diseases drastically
  reduced Haudenosaunee population from the 17th century onward,
  shifting the demographic balance against them.
- **Economic dependence.** Trade goods, particularly firearms,
  metal tools, and cloth, created dependency on European supply
  chains, which gave colonial powers leverage that did not exist in
  the pre-contact period.
- **The American Revolution split the league.** Forced to choose
  sides in an external existential war, the Confederacy fractured.
  Mohawk, Cayuga, Onondaga, Seneca largely sided with the British;
  Oneida and Tuscarora largely with the Patriots. The first real
  break in the Confederacy came not from internal failure of the
  Great Law but from the impossibility of being neutral in an
  external war that defined survival in confessional terms.
- **The Sullivan Expedition (1779).** A deliberate American military
  campaign destroyed Haudenosaunee villages, agricultural
  infrastructure, and food stores in central New York, with explicit
  intent to break the Confederacy's material capacity.
- **Post-war displacement.** The Haldimand Grant (1784) established
  the Six Nations Reserve at Grand River in Canada for those who had
  sided with the British. Remaining nations were confined to
  reservations in New York. The Council Fire was re-established at
  both Onondaga (in NY) and Six Nations (in Canada), which in some
  Haudenosaunee accounts is described as a division of the fire
  rather than its extinguishment.

The Confederacy continues today as a living institution. The Council
exists, the Great Law is recited and taught, and the Haudenosaunee
issue their own passports and have engaged in international diplomacy
in the 20th and 21st centuries. The institution did not end. It
continues in dramatically altered form, alongside the political
structures imposed on it.

### Lesson for kith-covenant

Three lessons of unusual weight follow.

First, the *sacred-substrate function* does not require Christianity
specifically. The Kaianerekowa carried the same load that Christian
oath-culture carried in Europe, through entirely different surface
content. But the deeper finding, developed in the "transcendent moral authority pattern"
section below, is that the Iroquois case shares with Christianity the
specific structural feature of pointing to *a single ultimate* at the
apex of its cosmology. This is consistent with the substrate-affords-
emergence position
([Justus hostis](justus_hostis.md#substrate-affords-emergence-it-does-not-dictate-content)),
kith-covenant cannot dictate content, but it sharpens the constraint.
The substrate that emerges must have *transcendent moral authority / one-ultimate
structure*, even when the content of that ultimate differs across
communities.

Second, the *Condolence ceremony* is a procedural innovation kith-covenant
should study seriously. The recognition that a bereaved or enraged
party cannot deliberate well, and the institutional response of
*ceremonially clearing their minds before deliberation begins*, has
no European-tradition parallel and is exactly the kind of practice
that would address several of the kith-covenant hardest problems.
Post-violation reintegration (see protocol sketch in
[Bounded conflict](bounded_conflict.md#protocol-sketch)),
de-escalation when one party is in the grip of grief or rage,
restitution-and-return paths.

Third, and most uncomfortable, the Confederacy survived internal
disputes for centuries but could not survive *being forced to take
sides in an external existential war.* This is the failure mode
kith-covenant most needs to confront. A compact has no answer to the
situation in which an external conflict is structured such that
neutrality is not survivable for individual members. Its design
either includes a credible answer to that situation or it does not,
and the Iroquois case suggests there may not be a fully satisfactory
answer. Kith-Covenant should at minimum (a) actively work to avoid being
made an interlocutor in such conflicts, (b) preserve the compact
re-formation paths so that even if it is broken it can be
reconstituted, and (c) state in its founding documents that this
failure mode exists.

## Further non-Western cases. A brief survey

The Iroquois Confederacy is the most extensively treated non-Western
case above, but it is not the only one available, and looking at three
further non-Western cooperation regimes substantially strengthens the
pattern of substrate observed across all the cases. Each is presented
here in compact form (rather than with the full structure of the cases
above) because the goal is comparative, not comprehensive: do these
cases share the structural feature the Iroquois case displays?

### The Mali Empire and the Kurukan Fuga (ca. 1236)

After Sundiata Keita's victory at the Battle of Kirina (1235), a
great gathering at Kurukan Fuga proclaimed what is often
translated as the *Manden Charter*, a foundational compact of the
Mande Confederation that bound previously rival kingdoms into a
federated empire. The surviving recensions of the charter
(preserved in the oral tradition of the Mandinka griots and
inscribed by UNESCO as Intangible Cultural Heritage in 2009)
include provisions on division of powers, the rights of women and
children, environmental protection, food security, prohibitions
on maltreatment of foreigners and prisoners, and (in some
readings) the abolition of certain forms of slavery.

**Substrate.** Mande religion centers on *Maa Ngala*, the supreme
creator, with various lesser spirits and ancestral beings beneath. By
Sundiata's time Islam had penetrated the Mande world; Sundiata himself
was nominally Muslim, his successors more thoroughly so. The
substrate of the Charter is therefore a synthesis of Islamic
monotheism and the Maa Ngala-centered traditional religion, both of
which point to a single ultimate at the apex.

**Decay.** The Mali Empire weakened from the 15th century under
external pressure (Songhai expansion) and internal succession
disputes. The Charter as a binding compact eroded with the
political coherence of the empire. The cultural memory has been
preserved through the griot tradition and is in active revival in
West African constitutional discourse.

**Lesson.** A federated, multi-ethnic compact, sustained for several
centuries across communities that did not share day-to-day life, on a
substrate whose distinctive structural feature is, again, a single
ultimate. The synthesis of Islam with traditional Maa Ngala worship
is itself instructive. The two traditions could fuse in practice
precisely because they shared the transcendent moral authority structure, even where
their surface theologies differed.

### The Mongol Yasa and the Pax Mongolica (ca. 1206-1368)

*Note: The Mongol and Islamic cases below are **imperial** cooperation
regimes, cooperation imposed or structured by a dominant power, not
voluntary peer cooperation among communities of roughly equal standing.
They are included as secondary support for the transcendent moral authority pattern
(the substrate finding holds even in imperial contexts) but are not
primary evidence for the kith-covenant design, which assumes voluntary
peers. See the open-questions note on splitting these into a separate
appendix.*

Genghis Khan's *Yasa* (also Jasagh) was a body of law proclaimed at
the founding of the Mongol Empire and elaborated by his successors,
governing relations among Mongol clans and between the Mongols and the
peoples they ruled. The *Pax Mongolica* it sustained, for roughly a
century and a half across an empire stretching from Korea to Hungary,
enabled overland trade, religious travel, diplomatic exchange, and
the safe passage of figures like Marco Polo, Rabban Bar Sauma, and
Ibn Battuta across distances and political boundaries that had
previously been uncrossable.

**Substrate.** Mongol religion in this period was *Tengrism*, centered
on *Tengri* (the Eternal Blue Sky), the supreme deity from whom
political authority, including the Khan's own mandate, derived. The
structural parallel to the Chinese *Tian* (Heaven) and to other
transcendent moral authority systems is exact. Famously, Mongol rule was *strikingly
tolerant* of other religions: Christianity, Islam, Buddhism, Daoism
all received imperial protection, and the tolerance was grounded
explicitly in the Mongol view that worship of the high gods of other
peoples was at root worship of Tengri under another name. This is the
transcendent moral authority hypothesis (below) made explicit policy by a state.

**Decay.** The empire fragmented after Möngke's death (1259) into the
four khanates, which gradually adopted the religions of their subject
populations (Islam in the Ilkhanate and Golden Horde, Buddhism in the
Yuan), eroding the unifying Tengrist substrate. The Pax Mongolica
ended definitively with the Ming overthrow of the Yuan in 1368.

**Lesson.** The most consequential cooperation regime in pre-modern
Eurasia rested on an explicitly monotheistic substrate (Tengrism)
whose internal logic *recognized other monotheisms as variant
expressions of the same ultimate*. This is unusually direct evidence
for the transcendent moral authority hypothesis. Cooperation across radically different
cultures was sustained precisely by the belief that all the high gods
were one. When the substrate fractured into separate religious
communities, the cooperation regime fractured with it.

### Islamic frameworks: aman, dhimma, muwadaʿa

Classical Islamic law developed an unusually elaborated set of
institutions for cooperation across the boundary between the Muslim
community (*umma*) and non-Muslim peoples. Three of these are
particularly relevant:

- **Aman.** Safe-conduct for a non-Muslim entering Muslim territory.
  Could be granted by any Muslim and was binding on all Muslims. The
  recipient was inviolable for the duration. The institution is
  structurally close to the European tradition of safe conduct but
  was both more widespread and more legally elaborated.
- **Dhimma.** Covenant of protection extended to "People of the
  Book" (*Ahl al-Kitab.* Christians, Jews, sometimes Zoroastrians and
  others). *Dhimmis* retained their religious autonomy, internal law,
  and community institutions in exchange for paying *jizya* (a poll
  tax) and accepting certain restrictions. The system functioned
  across the caliphates and most of their successor states for over a
  millennium.
- **Muwadaʿa / hudna.** Formal truce or treaty with a non-Muslim
  political power. Bounded in time (classically a maximum of ten
  years) but renewable. The framework structured most pre-modern
  Islamic-Christian and Islamic-Hindu state relations.

**Substrate.** Islam is paradigmatically monotheistic, and its
internal categorization of other peoples is *explicitly structured by
their relationship to monotheism*. The *Ahl al-Kitab* (fellow
monotheists who received scripture from the same God under prior
covenants) receive the elevated *dhimma* status. The Constitution of
Medina (622 CE), often considered the first written constitution in
Islamic history, explicitly extends the protected-community framework
to the Jewish tribes of Medina, treating them as part of the *umma*
in the political sense while preserving their religious distinctness.
This is the transcendent moral authority pattern made into formal legal architecture.
Cooperation is structured around shared monotheism, with the
practical extensions to polytheists handled separately and
(historically) less stably.

**Decay.** The framework largely dissolved with the rise of the
modern nation-state, which absorbed the *dhimmi* communities into
state-citizenship law and recoded inter-state relations under
European-derived international law. The dissolution was institutional
rather than doctrinal. The underlying categories remain in classical
Islamic jurisprudence but are not enacted by modern Muslim states in
their classical form.

**Lesson.** The *most explicitly worked-out historical legal
architecture for cross-community cooperation*, running for over a
millennium across an enormous geographic and cultural range, is
built directly on the structural recognition of fellow
monotheists as standing in special relationship. The two-tier
character of the framework (*Ahl al-Kitab* vs. others) is the
transcendent moral authority hypothesis *operationalized as law*:
monotheists recognize each other across deep difference more
readily than they recognize polytheists.

### Brief notes on the harder cases

Several further cases deserve mention, most complicating or clarifying
the transcendent moral authority pattern, one set aside as not
probative, each in compressed form:

- **The Buddhist *sangha* across kingdoms.** Buddhism is non-theistic
  in its philosophical core (no creator deity, no high god in the
  Abrahamic or Tengrist sense), yet sustained extraordinary
  cross-kingdom cooperation through its monastic networks for over
  two millennia, from India through Central Asia to East Asia. This
  appears to be a counterexample to the transcendent moral authority
  hypothesis. On closer examination, Buddhist tradition substitutes a
  different *single ultimate*, the *Dharma* (cosmic moral and natural
  law), which functions structurally like a high god in providing a
  single reference point that makes oaths and recognition meaningful.
  Whether the Dharma is "really" a high god is a deep philosophical
  question. For purposes of the sociological pattern, it appears to
  play the same structural role. The full case analysis
  (treating the sangha as three distinct cross-polity regimes,
  Theravada, East Asian, Tibetan Vajrayana, on the same metrics the
  Christian cases above are evaluated on, and applying the five
  anti-spiral features test,
  [Justus hostis](justus_hostis.md#the-anti-spiral-features-why-anchor-above-self-is-necessary-but-not-sufficient))
  is in [The Buddhist sangha](buddhist_sangha.md). Its principal
  finding is that the sangha is a real case of long-running voluntary
  cross-community cooperation that supplies the anti-spiral features
  through non-Christian theoretical apparatus, but is structurally a
  *trans-polity religious community* (closer to the Roman Catholic
  monastic orders than to Westphalia) rather than a *cross-polity
  cooperation framework among separate sovereigns*. This distinction
  matters for what the sangha counterweight does to the
  Christian-particularist reading. See
  [The noticeable hand](noticeable_hand.md#counterweights) for
  the updated verdict.

- **Classical Greek amphictyonic leagues and Roman federations.**
  Both were polytheistic, but both centered cooperation on shared
  *sanctuaries* dedicated to a *primus inter pares* high god:
  Zeus at Olympia and Dodona, Apollo at Delphi, Jupiter Optimus
  Maximus at the Capitol. The formal treaties of the leagues were
  sworn before these high gods specifically. So the pattern holds
  even within polytheism, as long as a primary transcendent moral
  authority frame is available for binding oaths.

- **Modern secular international institutions** (UN, IMF, WTO, etc.)
  attempt cross-community cooperation on an explicitly *non-theistic*
  substrate. Their relative brittleness (their tendency to function
  while powerful states approve and to fail when those states defect)
  is consistent with the prediction the transcendent moral authority hypothesis makes.
  Cooperation regimes built without a transcendent moral authority substrate must be
  underwritten by state coercion to function, and so are not really
  cooperation among strangers but rather cooperation among states
  that find current cooperation convenient. This is not a
  *refutation* of the hypothesis. It is an instance of the failure
  mode the hypothesis predicts.

- **Anarchist cooperation experiments (set aside).** The Spanish
  Republican-era collectives and Rojava are too short-lived and too
  externally suppressed to bear on the durability question. The
  Zapatista *caracoles* are more durable but are not actually anarchist
  (parallel-state indigenous autonomy on a Mayan-Catholic syncretic
  substrate) and organize cooperation among culturally and religiously
  cohesive Mayan peoples, so they do not test the cross-substrate case
  that concerns kith-covenant. These are set aside as not probative
  rather than treated as evidence either way.

## Non-Western cases. Stress-testing the substrate thesis

The case set above is heavily weighted toward Western and
Western-adjacent examples. A skeptic rightly asks whether this
reflects evidential weight or selection bias. The following survey
examines the most significant non-Western cooperation regimes to
test the substrate thesis. Cases already treated above
(Iroquois, Mali Kurukan Fuga, Mongol Yasa, Islamic frameworks) are
not repeated. What follows is the additional case set.

### Nuer segmentary lineage system (Sudan)

The Nuer, documented by Evans-Pritchard (*The Nuer*, 1940; *Nuer
Religion*, 1956), maintained cooperation across independent,
acephalous segments (genuinely voluntary peers with no central
authority) for at least several centuries before colonial disruption.

**Substrate.** Evans-Pritchard identified *Kwoth* (Spirit/God) as
the supreme being in Nuer religion. Oaths were sworn by Kwoth, and
oath-breaking was believed to bring supernatural punishment. The
leopard-skin chief (*kuaar muon*) who mediated inter-segment disputes
derived authority from a sacred, not political, role. He could not
compel but could invoke spiritual sanctions.

**Mechanism.** Segmentary opposition (groups that feud at one level
unite against threats at a higher level), leopard-skin-chief
mediation, age-set systems cross-cutting lineage divisions, and
cattle-based compensation for homicide.

**Decay mode.** Disrupted by British colonial administration (which
imposed alien chiefs), then by Sudanese civil wars. The system was
designed for low-density pastoral ecology and was vulnerable to
state imposition.

**Assessment.** A genuine non-Western case of voluntary peer
cooperation with an explicit transcendent moral authority substrate. *Supports* the
thesis. (The methodological caveat: Evans-Pritchard, himself a
Catholic convert, has been accused of over-reading monotheism into
Nuer religion. The accusation is worth noting but the Kwoth concept
is well-attested in independent sources.)

### Tiv segmentary system (Nigeria)

The Tiv, documented by the Bohannans (*Tiv Economy*, 1968; *Justice
and Judgment Among the Tiv*, 1957), maintained large-scale acephalous
cooperation among over a million people. The Tiv famously resisted
any form of chieftainship.

**Substrate.** Tiv religion centered on *tsav* (a substance/power
associated with witchcraft and authority) and *akombo* (ritual
forces). There is no clear transcendent moral authority figure comparable to Nuer Kwoth.
Cooperation was maintained through fear of *tsav* (elders with tsav
could harm defectors) and through shared ritual obligations.

**Mechanism.** Segmentary opposition, rotating market networks
creating economic interdependence, age-grade organizations, and
public moots for dispute resolution.

**Decay mode.** British colonial imposition of "warrant chiefs"
disrupted the system. Post-colonial Nigerian state incorporation
continued the process.

**Assessment:** *Challenges* the strict transcendent moral authority thesis. The Tiv
achieved large-scale cooperation without a transcendent moral authority substrate, using
witchcraft beliefs as the enforcement mechanism. However, the
cooperation was arguably less stable than the Nuer case and more
prone to inter-segment warfare. The counter-argument: *tsav*-based
enforcement functions analogously to a transcendent moral authority (supernatural
consequences for norm violation) even without a unified deity.

### Igbo political organization (Nigeria)

The Igbo, comprising millions across southeastern Nigeria, maintained
autonomous village-groups with no paramount authority. Most Igbo
communities were genuinely self-governing.

**Substrate.** Igbo religion featured *Chukwu* (supreme being) and
*Chi* (personal spiritual essence), with the Earth goddess *Ala/Ani*
as moral enforcer. The Aro Oracle (*Ibini Ukpabi*) at Arochukwu
was a supreme court for inter-community disputes. Communities
brought cases to the oracle, whose judgments were considered divinely
authoritative.

**Mechanism.** Title societies (*Ozo*, *Nze*), age-grade systems
cross-cutting kinship, oracular authority, rotating market networks,
and masquerade/secret societies enforcing norms across communities.

**Decay mode.** British colonialism imposed warrant chiefs (provoking
the 1929 Women's War when Igbo women protested the alien authority).
The Aro Oracle was destroyed by British military expedition in
1901-1902.

**Assessment.** This is a mixed case. The Aro Oracle system involves a
sacred substrate (divine judgment) facilitating inter-community
cooperation, *supporting* the thesis. But the Aro were also
traders who profited from the authority of the oracle, and their
network had quasi-hegemonic features. The genuinely egalitarian
village-group system relied more on institutional mechanisms
(title societies, age-grades) with sacred dimensions but not a
singular transcendent moral authority covenant structure.

### Aboriginal Australian inter-group systems

Aboriginal Australian language groups and clans cooperated across
the entire continent through songlines (dreaming tracks), trade
routes, and ceremonial obligations. Groups were fully autonomous.
The duration is extraordinary, possibly tens of thousands of years,
making these by far the longest-lived cooperation systems known.

**Substrate.** The Dreaming (*Tjukurpa*, *Jukurrpa*) is a sacred
cosmology in which ancestral beings created the landscape and
established the Law. The Dreaming is not a transcendent moral authority system but a
*sacred-law* system. The rules of cooperation are embedded in the
landscape itself, maintained through ceremony, and transmitted
through initiation. Violation of Dreaming Law carried supernatural
and social sanctions.

**Mechanism.** Songlines defined routes along which trade goods,
ceremonies, and marriage partners flowed. Ceremonial gatherings
(corroborees) brought distant groups together for shared ritual,
dispute resolution, and exchange. Section and moiety systems
regulated inter-group marriage, creating durable alliance networks.
Message sticks formalized inter-group communication.

**Decay mode.** European colonization catastrophically disrupted
the systems through dispossession, massacres, and forced removal.
Some systems persist in modified form in remote areas.

**Assessment.** The most significant challenge to a *strict*
transcendent moral authority thesis. Continent-scale cooperation over millennia without
monotheism. The Dreaming is sacred and morally authoritative but not
theistic in the Western sense. However, and this is critical for
the kith-covenant design, the cooperation was *thin.* No binding
arbitration, no collective defense, no formal political federation,
no recognition-ceremony equivalent, no escalation-ladder equivalent.
The cooperation governed trade, marriage, ceremony, and passage, but
not the kind of formalized inter-community political architecture
that kith-covenant is building. The Aboriginal case demonstrates
that *some* forms of cooperation can be sustained on a sacred substrate without a transcendent moral authority. It does not demonstrate that *formalized political
federation* can be.

### Andean ayllu networks (pre-Inca)

Ayllus, kin-based communities in the Andean highlands, cooperated
through reciprocal labor (*ayni*, *minka*) and vertical-archipelago
resource-sharing (communities maintaining colonies at different
altitudes to access diverse ecological zones).

**Substrate.** Andean religion centered on *huacas* (sacred
places/objects), ancestor worship, and *Pachamama* (Earth Mother).
The transcendent moral authority figure *Viracocha* existed but was relatively remote.
The operative sacred forces were local huacas and ancestors.
Inter-ayllu cooperation was sanctified by shared ritual obligations
to landscape features (mountains, springs).

**Mechanism.** Reciprocal labor exchange, vertical-archipelago
resource complementarity, shared ritual calendars, and inter-ayllu
marriage networks. The mit'a (labor-turn) system predates the Inca
and was genuine peer reciprocity before the Inca co-opted it as
tribute.

**Decay mode.** The Inca Empire absorbed ayllu cooperation into an
imperial framework (converting voluntary reciprocity into tributary
obligation). The Spanish colonial *reducción* system disrupted the
vertical-archipelago pattern. But ayllu cooperation has shown
unusual persistence, with elements surviving to the present day in
Bolivian and Peruvian highland communities.

**Assessment.** Extraordinarily durable cooperation with a
landscape-based rather than transcendent moral authority sacred substrate. *Challenges*
the strict transcendent moral authority thesis but *supports* a broader sacred-substrate
thesis. The ayllu system survived two imperial conquests, evidence
of deep structural resilience. As with the Aboriginal case, the
cooperation governed commons and reciprocity, not formalized
political federation with binding arbitration.

### Pacific exchange networks: Kula ring and Hiri trade

The Kula ring linked approximately 18 island communities across the
Massim archipelago (Papua New Guinea). The Hiri trade connected
Motu coastal villages with Gulf of Papua communities. Participants
were independent communities.

**Substrate.** Kula exchange was saturated with magic and ritual:
canoe-building magic, beauty magic, taboos governing the handling of
valuables (*vaygu'a*). The sacred dimension was real but distributed:
no single transcendent moral authority, but a web of magical obligations binding partners
across communities. Hiri trade involved ritual partnerships and
ancestral obligations.

**Mechanism.** Formalized reciprocal exchange of shell valuables
(armshells clockwise, necklaces counter-clockwise) creating ongoing
obligations between partners. Alongside the ceremonial exchange,
mundane trade occurred (*gimwali*). Multi-generational
partner relationships.

**Decay mode.** The Kula ring has attenuated but not entirely
disappeared. Colonial and post-colonial economic integration
provided alternatives. The Hiri trade ended in the early 20th
century as colonial markets supplanted it.

**Assessment.** Genuine voluntary, multi-generational,
cross-community cooperation with a sacred but not transcendent moral authority substrate.
*Challenges* the strict transcendent moral authority thesis but the cooperation is
*narrow.* It governs exchange relationships, not the broader
political-legal cooperation that covenant systems produce.

### ASEAN (1967-present)

The Association of Southeast Asian Nations. Voluntary cooperation
among states with radically different religious substrates (Buddhist,
Muslim, Catholic, Confucian-secular).

**Substrate.** Explicitly secular. The "ASEAN Way" is based on
consensus, non-interference, and face-saving. Cultural norms rather
than sacred commitments. No shared sacred substrate.

**Mechanism.** Consensus decision-making, non-interference in
internal affairs, quiet diplomacy, economic integration, regular
summits. The cooperation is deliberately shallow by design. Deep
integration is avoided to preserve sovereignty.

**Assessment.** The most prominent modern case of cross-substrate
voluntary cooperation. Directly challenges the thesis that a shared
sacred substrate is *necessary*. However: 59 years is historically
short. The cooperation is notably shallow (ASEAN cannot enforce norms,
e.g., inability to address Myanmar's junta); and ASEAN free-rides
on the post-WWII liberal international order for security guarantees.
These are legitimate responses but risk becoming unfalsifiable. At
what point would ASEAN's continued existence *count* as evidence
against the thesis?

### Swiss Confederacy (1291-present)

Already treated as a Western case, but relevant here for its
*cross-substrate survival*. The Confederacy survived the Reformation
with both Catholic and Protestant cantons cooperating, the very
fracture that destroyed cooperation elsewhere in Europe.

**Assessment.** Supports the thesis at founding (the 1291 Bundesbrief
is explicitly sworn "in the name of God"), and, on a careful reading,
supports it post-Reformation as well. The confessional split between
Catholic and Protestant cantons was a *first-order* theological
division, not a substrate fracture. Both sides continued to swear oaths
before the same God of scripture, and the substrate-level commitment
that made Bundesbrief-style oaths binding remained intact. What was
contested was ecclesiology, sacramental theology, and church authority,
not the existence of a transcendent moral authority before whom
oaths bind. The Confederacy's post-Reformation persistence is therefore
consistent with the substrate thesis. It does, however, illustrate
that *first-order* disagreement within a shared substrate can be
survived, an important precedent for kith-covenant, which expects
its member communities to hold different first-order theologies while
sharing the substrate structure.

### The European Union and the postwar international order (1945-present)

The counter-case a skeptical reader reaches for first, and the one
this survey longest omitted: the European Union, and behind it the
postwar international order (UN system, Bretton Woods, Geneva
framework). Broad-envelope, voluntary, substrate-plural, eighty
years old. If modern secular institutions refute the substrate
thesis, this is where the refutation lives.

**Substrate.** Officially none. The EU's treaties invoke "the
cultural, religious and humanist inheritance of Europe" (a formula
adopted after the explicit *invocatio Dei* was fought over and
rejected for the 2004 constitutional treaty) and otherwise ground
themselves in human dignity, democracy, and the rule of law. The
founding generation was different: Schuman, Adenauer, and De Gasperi
were Christian-democratic statesmen who understood the project in
explicitly Christian-civilizational terms, and the founding
population still largely lived inside that inheritance. The
substrate question for the EU is therefore not "was one present at
founding" (it was) but "what happens as it recedes."

**Mechanism.** Progressive treaty integration, a supranational court
whose law overrides national law, a directly elected parliament, a
common currency for most members, and enforcement running through
binding adjudication and material sanction. Cooperation is deep,
daily, and legally coercive.

**Decay signs.** The rejection of the constitutional treaty
(2005); exit exercised at scale (Brexit, 2016-2020) with the
remaining members retained. Sustained inability to discipline
internal defection from its own foundational values (the Article
7 impasse over Hungary); chronic legitimacy strain ("democratic
deficit") wherever integration outruns the felt community that
would authorize it.

**Assessment.** Three findings, stated in order of weight.

First, the EU is substantially *out of class*. The kith-covenant case
set is voluntary, structurally sovereign-less compacts. The EU
solves the cooperation problem by the opposite move, building a
sovereign incrementally: supranational law with primacy, compulsory
adjudication, a currency, a nascent executive. That is not a
refutation of the substrate thesis. It is the *Westphalian
alternative* the thesis never claimed was impossible. States can
always cooperate by fusing sovereignty. The thesis concerns what
sustains cooperation when they will not or cannot.

Second, the postwar order outside the EU decomposes on inspection
into thin-functional domains: maritime rescue, aviation, postal
union, wounded-combatant treatment, diplomatic immunity. These
converge without shared substrate, exactly as the thin-domain
pattern (below, §"Convergence without shared culture") predicts,
and their durability is evidence *for* the two-tier scoping, not
against the full-compact claim. The UN's full-compact ambitions
(collective security, binding resolution) are precisely the parts
that strain.

Third, for the residue that is genuinely full-compact-shaped and
genuinely voluntary, the inheritance reading is the live one. The
EU was founded on the Christian-civilizational substrate of its
founders and has been spending that inheritance since. On this
reading its trajectory (bindingness migrating from oath to
economic entanglement and legal coercion as the substrate
recedes) is not a counterexample but a slow-motion confirmation
in progress. We state this as the prediction of the thesis rather
than as an established fact, because this is where the thesis
meets the modern world and can lose: **if the EU sustains
full-compact-depth voluntary cooperation through a
multi-generational span (three generations from founding, roughly
2100) without substrate renewal and without completing its
hardening into a federal sovereign, the substrate thesis is
substantially weakened**. The same criterion, mutatis mutandis,
applies to ASEAN (above). The thesis makes a falsifiable forecast
here, with a date attached.

### East Asian absence

East Asian political history (Chinese, Japanese, Korean) is
overwhelmingly imperial or feudal. Voluntary peer cooperation between
independent communities is genuinely rare in this tradition. The
Confucian emphasis on hierarchical order, the centralized-state model,
and the tributary-system framework all work against it. This is not
selection bias. It is a genuine structural feature of East Asian
political culture. The Japanese village *iriai* commons (already in
[Commons federation](commons_federation.md)) is local, not
inter-polity.

### Source quality and ideological bias

The non-Western cases rest on ethnographic fieldwork of varying
quality and age. The ideological-bias question (whether the
anti-theistic and anti-Western tendencies of modern scholarship
have skewed the interpretation of these cases) is real and must
be assessed case by case.

**Primary sources and their reliability:**

| Case | Primary source(s) | Date | Quality | Bias concern |
|------|-------------------|------|---------|-------------|
| Kula ring | Malinowski, *Argonauts of the Western Pacific* | 1922 | Excellent | Pre-ideological. Later confirmations (Weiner 1976, Leach 1983) |
| Nuer | Evans-Pritchard, *The Nuer* / *Nuer Religion* | 1940/1956 | Good | E-P converted to Catholicism 1944, between the two books. His identification of Kwoth as transcendent moral authority may reflect his conversion, but Kwoth independently attested (Hutchinson 1996) |
| Tiv | P. & L. Bohannan, *Tiv Economy* / *Justice and Judgment* | 1957/1968 | Excellent | Pre-ideological. Absence of transcendent moral authority is primary-data finding, not interpretation |
| Igbo | Meek (1937), Uchendu (1965, himself Igbo), Isichei (1976) | 1937-1976 | Good | Chukwu as supreme being attested by multiple independent sources incl. indigenous scholars. Question: pre-contact or missionary-influenced? |
| Andean ayllu | Murra, *Economic Organization of the Inca State* | 1980 | Good | Cooperation system uncontested; Viracocha-as-transcendent-moral-authority question contested (colonial chroniclers may have imposed monotheistic frame; MacCormack 1991) |
| Aboriginal | Spencer & Gillen (1899), Elkin (1938), Stanner (1979), Berndt & Berndt (1964) | 1899-1979 | Good | Primary ethnography strong and mostly pre-ideological. Transcendent moral authority question (Baiame, Rainbow Serpent) heavily contested |

**The ideological bias cuts in two directions, both relevant to the
substrate thesis:**

**Direction 1: Post-1970s anthropology has been suspicious of finding
transcendent moral authorities in non-Western cultures**. Earlier researchers who *did*
find transcendent moral authority elements (Andrew Lang, *The Making of Religion*, 1898,
arguing Aboriginal Australians had a primitive monotheism centered
on Baiame; Evans-Pritchard identifying Nuer Kwoth as a supreme
being) have been retroactively questioned by later scholars who
treat such findings as Christian projection onto indigenous cosmology.
Sometimes the questioning is methodologically fair
(Evans-Pritchard's Catholic conversion between his two Nuer books
is a legitimate concern). Sometimes it is ideological. A blanket
assumption that *any* finding of monotheistic structure in a
non-Western culture must be Western imposition rather than
indigenous reality.

The implication: **the non-Western cases may have more
transcendent moral authority structure than the post-1970s
scholarly consensus acknowledges**. The apparent counterexamples
to the substrate thesis may be artifacts of the anti-theistic
priors of later scholarship. The Aboriginal case is the sharpest
example. Some Aboriginal groups *do* have creator figures (Baiame
among the Kamilaroi/Gamilaraay, the Rainbow Serpent across many
groups). Whether these constitute "high gods" in the structurally
relevant sense is precisely where the ideological battle is
fought. A pre-ideological-turn scholar would more readily
classify Baiame as a transcendent moral authority. A post-1970s
scholar resists the classification as Western projection. The
data does not settle the question. The priors of the scholar do.

**Direction 2: Post-1970s anthropology has been eager to present
non-Western cooperation as successful and self-sufficient**, as a
corrective to colonial narratives of "primitive" peoples requiring
Western institutions. This corrective was needed. Earlier
scholarship often dismissed non-Western cooperation. But the
corrective risks overcorrecting. The *durability and effectiveness*
of non-Western cooperation systems may be overstated in recent
scholarship, while *internal conflicts and failures* may be
under-reported. The Tiv case is instructive. The Bohannans
(mid-century, pre-ideological) documented significant inter-segment
warfare alongside the cooperation. A post-1970s account might
foreground the cooperation and background the warfare.

**The methodological position for this research.** Rely primarily on
the earliest and most detailed ethnographic sources (which are
generally pre-ideological-turn and less susceptible to either
pro-theistic or anti-theistic bias), flag interpretive debates
explicitly, and avoid treating either the presence or absence of
transcendent moral authority structure as settled where the scholarship is genuinely
contested. Where the primary data is strong but the interpretation
is disputed (Aboriginal, Andean, Nuer), state the dispute rather
than resolving it by fiat.

The net effect on the substrate thesis: the source-quality assessment
*strengthens* rather than weakens the thesis. If the apparent
counterexamples (Aboriginal, Andean, Tiv) may have more transcendent moral authority
structure than post-1970s scholarship acknowledges, if the
exceptions to the pattern are less exceptional than they appear in
the recent literature, then the evidential base for the thesis is
broader than a naive reading of the modern secondary literature
suggests.

### What the expanded case set teaches

The non-Western survey produces a finding more precise than the
original transcendent moral authority hypothesis:

**Two kinds of durable cooperation exist, with different substrate
requirements:**

1. **Commons-and-exchange cooperation.** Trade networks, resource
   reciprocity, ceremonial exchange, marriage alliances, commons
   governance. This form of cooperation can be sustained on *any*
   sacred substrate (transcendent moral authority, animist, landscape-based, ancestral).
   The Aboriginal Australian, Andean ayllu, Pacific Kula, and Tiv
   cases demonstrate this. The cooperation is real, durable (sometimes
   extraordinarily so), and does not require a transcendent moral authority.

2. **Formalized political federation.** Binding arbitration,
   recognition ceremonies, escalation procedures, anathema/expulsion
   mechanisms, collective defense, covenant-level commitments among
   independent communities. This form of cooperation *does* appear to
   require a transcendent moral authority or equivalent substrate. The cases that
   achieved it (Westphalia, Hanse, Iroquois, Nuer leopard-skin-chief
   arbitration, Igbo Aro Oracle, the *jus publicum Europaeum*) all
   involve a transcendent moral authority before whom oaths bind.
   The cases that achieved durable cooperation without a transcendent moral authority
   (Aboriginal, Andean, Pacific, Tiv) did not produce this specific
   institutional form.

**Kith-covenant is building the second kind.** Its architecture (the
recognition ceremony, the escalation ladder, the anathema
procedure, the liminal institutions, the mediator pool) is
formalized political federation among independent communities. The
evidence suggests that this specific architecture requires the
transcendent moral authority substrate, not because other substrates are inferior for
all purposes, but because the *specific mechanisms* kith-covenant
uses (binding oaths, sacred witness, asymmetric-psychological-cost
ceremonies, substrate-grounded discernment) require a transcendent
moral authority to function.

This precision matters. The claim is not "transcendent moral authority substrates are
better." The Aboriginal Australian case, with its tens-of-thousands-
of-years duration, demolishes any such claim. The claim is:
**the specific kind of cooperation kith-covenant is designed to
produce has historically required a transcendent moral authority substrate, and the
evidence does not show it being produced without one**.

### The commons compact. A lighter-weight tier

The two-kinds finding suggests that the kith-covenant architecture
should make room for both. The full compact, with its recognition
ceremony, escalation ladder, anathema procedure, and the complete
institutional apparatus, is the ideal and requires the transcendent moral authority
substrate. But a **commons compact**, a lighter-weight cooperation
framework for communities that hold *some* sacred substrate (animist,
landscape-based, ancestral, or otherwise) but not the transcendent moral authority
structure the full compact requires, is both historically attested
and practically valuable.

The commons compact would support:
- **Trade and exchange.** Formalized reciprocity, market access,
  resource sharing, the economic cooperation that the Kula ring,
  the Hanse's commercial layer, and the Andean vertical archipelago
  demonstrate.
- **Commons governance.** Shared management of resources using
  Ostrom's design principles
  ([Commons federation](commons_federation.md)), which do not
  require a transcendent moral authority substrate.
- **Passage and hospitality.** Safe transit, emergency aid, the
  "passage for the traveler" virtue operating at a lighter
  commitment level.
- **Knowledge exchange.** Shared archives, scholarly cooperation,
  the information commons at the exchange level.

The commons compact would *not* support (or would support only in
attenuated form):
- **The full recognition ceremony**, which requires transcendent moral authority
  acknowledgment in Movement 1. A commons-compact equivalent might
  involve a lighter commitment (acknowledgment of a sacred order,
  without specifying its structure).
- **The anathema procedure**, which depends on substrate-grounded
  discernment. Commons-compact dispute resolution would be closer
  to the pie-powder-court model (rapid, pragmatic, reputation-based)
  than the full escalation ladder.
- **Binding arbitration**, which requires oaths sworn before a
  transcendent authority. Commons-compact arbitration would be
  advisory rather than binding, enforced by reputation and trade
  consequences rather than by sacred obligation.

**The relationship between the two tiers is hypothetically
developmental**. The commons compact is a *degraded form* of the
full compact, not in the pejorative sense, but in the precise
sense. It is what cooperation looks like when the substrate is not
strong enough (or not yet strong enough) to support the full
architecture. A community participating in a commons compact may
over time develop the substrate depth that makes full compact
membership possible, or it may not, and the commons-compact
relationship remains valuable on its own terms.

The historical precedent is the relationship of the lex
mercatoria to the broader Christendom framework. The lex
mercatoria operated at the commons-and-exchange level (trade,
dispute resolution, reputation) while the Christendom framework
operated at the political-federation level (Westphalia, the *jus
publicum*, the Church's mediating authority). The two were
nested. Although the lex mercatoria functioned within and was
supported by the broader Christendom substrate, it required less
of its participants than the full political framework did. A
Jewish or Muslim merchant could participate in the lex
mercatoria (commons compact) without being part of Christendom
(full compact).

The Noahide analogy from
[Justus hostis](justus_hostis.md) §"The Rawlsian objection and
the durability hypothesis" applies at the compact level. The commons
compact is the Noahide tier. Less is required, less is provided,
but the cooperation is real and the relationship is genuine. The full
compact is the Mosaic tier. More is required, more is provided, and
the institutional architecture is correspondingly deeper.

The design implication is that the kith-covenant founding documents should
include a commons-compact template alongside the full-compact
template, specifying the lighter substrate requirements, the
available mechanisms, and the pathway (if any) from commons compact
to full compact. Communities that cannot or will not meet the full
substrate requirements are not thereby excluded from all cooperation.
They are offered a genuine, historically attested, lighter-weight
alternative. The alternative is less than the ideal. It is not
nothing.

## Convergence without shared culture. The thin-functional-domain cases

The preceding case-set was assembled under the
voluntary-peer-longevity filter (established in
[The noticeable hand](noticeable_hand.md#a-methodological-refinement-voluntary-peer-cooperation-under-longevity))
and consists of *full cooperation regimes*, regimes that govern
a broad envelope of the interactions of a community with other
communities. That filter is methodologically defensible but it
excludes a different and equally real category of historical
evidence: **cases in which culturally dissimilar communities
converged on a shared operational framework in a bounded
functional domain without sharing substrate**. Although these
cases are not full compacts and do not refute the substrate
thesis at the compact scale, they do carry important information
about what can cross a cultural boundary and under what
conditions. Naming them explicitly keeps the filter from
silently excluding the counter-evidence.

The strongest cases, roughly in chronological order:

**The Late Bronze Age treaty grammar (c. 1500-1200 BCE)**. The
Hittite-Egyptian treaty of 1259 BCE (Ramesses II and Hattušili
III, commonly called the Treaty of Kadesh) is the earliest
surviving parity treaty between two great powers and survives in
parallel Egyptian hieroglyphic and Akkadian cuneiform copies that
agree on substance. The two courts had genuinely dissimilar
cosmologies (Egyptian divine kingship with a pantheon headed by
Amun-Re; Hittite Storm God and patron deities of cities), yet the
*form* of the treaty was one both parties recognized as binding:
preamble, historical prologue, stipulations, list of divine
witnesses, blessings and curses, instructions for deposition and
periodic reading. Each side invoked its *own* gods as witnesses.
Neither submitted to the substrate of the other. The same
structural form appears in Hittite suzerainty treaties, in
Mitannian and Assyrian diplomatic correspondence (Amarna letters,
mid-14th c.), and, the inheritance that matters for the later
substrate tradition, in the Sinai covenant, which adopts the
six-part suzerainty-treaty form for Israel's covenant with YHWH.
The significance for the present argument is that a diplomatic grammar
shared across radically different cosmologies operated reliably
for centuries before any of the substrates later discussed in
this document came into being. Convergence on the *form* did not
require convergence on the *ground*. (Primary: Beckman, *Hittite
Diplomatic Texts*, 2nd ed., 1999; Mendenhall, *Law and Covenant
in Israel and the Ancient Near East*, 1955.)

**Rhodian Sea Law and its transmission (c. 800 BCE to 1500 CE)**.
General-average and jettison rules (the principle that loss
deliberately incurred to save the rest of a venture is shared
proportionally among all whose property was at risk) are attested
for Rhodian maritime practice from the Hellenistic period and were
absorbed into Roman law as the *Lex Rhodia de iactu* (*Digest*
14.2), thence into the Byzantine *Nómos Rhodíōn Nautikós* (7th-8th
c. CE), thence into Islamic maritime law (the *Qanun al-Bahr* and
the Maliki-school treatment of the *qirad* maritime partnership),
thence into the Mediterranean *Consolat de Mar* (codified in
Barcelona, 14th c.) from which substantively-identical rules passed
into Genoese, Venetian, Pisan, Amalfitan, and eventually Atlantic
commercial practice. Four civilizations with incompatible theologies
held essentially the same rules on jettison apportionment, average
adjustment, and bottomry for over a millennium. Each justified the
rule in its own frame (Roman *ius*, Byzantine-Christian *nomos*,
shariah-compatible *qanun*, Christian-Catalan maritime custom), but
the rule itself did not change. The transmitted substance predated,
outlasted, and operated across every confessional divide in the
Mediterranean basin. (Primary: Ashburner, *The Rhodian Sea-Law*,
1909; Khalilieh, *Islamic Maritime Law*, 1998; Pryor, *Commerce,
Shipping and Naval Warfare in the Medieval Mediterranean*, 1988.)

**Mediterranean frontier norms across the Christian-Islamic
boundary (c. 1100-1700 CE)**. The Crusader period and the
centuries that followed produced a substantially overlapping set
of customs across what was nominally a hard religious frontier:
the Islamic *aman* (safe-conduct) and European *salvus
conductus*, the *hudna* and Christian *treva* (truce), ransom
practice for captives (codified on the Christian side through the
Trinitarians, founded 1198, and the Mercedarians, founded 1218;
on the Muslim side through corresponding redemption networks and
the zakat-funded ransoming of Muslim captives), the Venetian
*bailo* in Constantinople, the Ottoman *dragoman* corps, the
Genoese and Venetian *fondachi* in Alexandria and Acre, and
mutually-recognized parley-flag conventions at sea. These were
not single-community regimes. They operated reliably *across* the
Christian-Islamic boundary, through the Reconquista, through the
Ottoman expansion, through the Crusades, and through the Barbary
period. Each side justified its participation in its own terms
(shariah permitted *hudna* with non-Muslim polities; Christian
canon law permitted *treva* with Muslim powers) and the
frameworks did not require either to concede the substrate of the
other. (Primary: Friedman, *Encounter Between Enemies: Captivity
and Ransom in the Latin Kingdom of Jerusalem*, 2002; Khadduri,
*War and Peace in the Law of Islam*, 1955; Rodríguez, *Captives
and Their Saviors in the Medieval Crown of Aragon*, 2007.)

**Polycentric diplomatic immunity.** Inviolability of envoys is one
of the few near-universal norms that emerged independently in Greek,
Roman, Chinese, Persian, Byzantine, Islamic, Mongol, and Indian
civilizations and was mutually ratified on contact rather than
imposed by any one. The Greek *kērux* (herald, under Hermes'
protection), the Roman *fetiales* and later resident embassies,
Chinese imperial envoy protection codified across multiple dynasties,
the *aman* extended to diplomatic missions in Islamic practice, the
Mongol understanding that envoy-killing was a casus belli so severe
that Chinggis Khan's response to the Khwarezmshah's execution of his
envoys in 1219 triggered the campaign that destroyed Khwarezm.
All of these are independently-originated and mutually-recognized.
When the Ottomans and Europeans established permanent embassies in
each other's capitals in the 16th-17th centuries, they did not
invent the framework. They ratified what both sides already
practiced. The Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations (1961) is
the modern codification of a pattern that was polycentric for at
least three millennia. (Primary: Mattingly, *Renaissance Diplomacy*,
1955; Adair, *The Exterritoriality of Ambassadors in the Sixteenth
and Seventeenth Centuries*, 1929; Fletcher, *The Mongols*, in the
*Cambridge History of China* vol. 6, 1994.)

**Red Crescent, Red Crystal, and the Geneva framework**. The 1864
Geneva Convention adopted a single protective symbol (the red
cross on white, a heraldic inversion of the Swiss flag chosen for
visual simplicity, not theological content). The 1876
Russo-Turkish War saw the Ottoman Empire declare that it would
use the red crescent in place of the cross for its own medical
services. The 1929 Geneva Convention formally recognized both
emblems; Iran later added the Red Lion and Sun (used until 1980);
the 2005 Third Additional Protocol adopted the Red Crystal as a
third denominationally-neutral emblem, resolving the dispute over
Israel's Magen David Adom. The underlying *protective framework*
(medical neutrality, immunity from attack, the distinctive emblem
as a protective marker rather than a religious claim) has
remained unchanged throughout. This is the cleanest modern case
of a single framework with *culturally-pluralized symbols.* One
protocol, many marks. It is also the closest modern analog to
what the kith-covenant recognition ceremony aspires to
structurally. The same substantive commitment, entered in the
terms of each community itself.

### What the thin-domain cases have in common

Across the five cases, the preconditions for framework-convergence
across genuinely dissimilar cultures look consistent:

1. **The domain is functional, not metaphysical.** Commerce,
   maritime salvage, envoy protection, combatant and wounded
   treatment. Domains in which both parties have aligned interests
   in predictability and where the cost of defection is legible.
   Thick moral domains (marriage, succession, criminal guilt,
   blasphemy, inheritance of office) essentially never converge
   cross-culturally.
2. **The geography is frontier or interstitial.** Fairs, ports,
   embassies, treaty-ratification precincts, the high seas, the
   battlefield under truce. Places where the full institutions of
   neither side reach and the envoys of both sides meet.
3. **Each side arrives already having something like the framework**.
   The convergence is not invented jointly. It is discovered on
   contact. Rhodian jettison rules were absorbed because Roman law
   already had something close; Islamic law already had the
   structural analogs; Mediterranean custom already recognized
   them. Diplomatic immunity is polycentric precisely because the
   framework emerged independently many times before any synthesis.
4. **Each party justifies the shared rule in its own substrate**.
   Hittite gods and Egyptian gods both witness Kadesh. The Rhodian
   rule is Roman *ius*, Byzantine *nomos*, shariah-compatible
   *qanun*, and Catalan custom simultaneously. Neither side is
   asked to abandon its own ground to accept the shared form.
5. **The cost of non-convergence is legible and high**. Piracy,
   shipwreck, executed envoys, unenforceable debts, wounded soldiers
   dying under the red cross. The alternative to convergence is
   visibly expensive to all parties.
6. **A mediator class lives across the boundary.** Merchants,
   dragomans, ambassadors, heralds, ransom orders, fair-court
   personnel, Red Cross delegates. Human carriers whose livelihood
   and calling depend on the framework holding, and who carry the
   norms back into their home communities.

### What this means for the substrate thesis

The thin-domain cases **do not refute** the substrate thesis in the
form in which that thesis is actually defensible. They do, however,
require the thesis to be stated more carefully than the preceding
round of this document stated it. Three corrections follow:

- **The substrate claim applies at the full-compact scale, not at
  every scale of cross-community cooperation**. The full-compact
  scale is the one at which the voluntary-peer-longevity filter is
  the right instrument. Below that scale, in bounded functional
  domains with the preconditions above, cooperation can converge
  without shared substrate, and the historical record shows that
  it repeatedly does.
- **What each case does require is that each party internally
  hold *something above itself* to which its participation is
  accountable.** Egyptian gods witness Kadesh for Egypt; Hittite
  gods witness for Hatti. The Trinitarians' ransom work was
  Christian-mandated. The work of their Muslim counterparts was
  shariah-mandated. The shared *form* holds because each party
  has a ground outside itself that binds its compliance. The
  compact itself is never the ultimate to which parties are
  accountable. The ultimate is always inside the participating
  community. This is the **narrower and stronger** form of the
  substrate claim. Not "parties must share a substrate" but
  "*each* party must have one, and the compact must not claim to
  be it."
- **The recognition mechanism for thin-domain cooperation is not
  a joint ceremony. It is the internal authorization by each
  community of its envoys/representatives under its own
  substrate, plus a thin shared grammar across the boundary**.
  Hittite kings did not submit to Egyptian theology to sign
  Kadesh. They authorized their own envoys under their own gods,
  and both sides shared a treaty form. Merchants at Champagne
  fairs did not recite shared creeds. Each was authorized by his
  own house and carried the reputation of his community, and all
  operated a shared procedural grammar. This pattern has direct
  consequences for the kith-covenant recognition-ceremony design,
  taken up in
  [Recognition protocols](recognition_protocols.md).

The thesis survives, narrowed: **each compact member community
must anchor itself to something above itself, and the compact must
not claim to be that thing**. The fifth movement of the recognition
ceremony, submission of the compact itself to what it was formed
to serve, was already the load-bearing element. The correction
elevates it from fifth-among-five to central.

## The transcendent moral authority pattern

Pulling the comparative evidence from the full-compact cases
together. Every cooperation regime that has sustained itself across
genuinely unconnected communities *as a full compact* for substantial
periods has rested on a substrate that points to a *single ultimate*
at the apex of its cosmology. The surface theology varies
enormously (the God of Israel and his successors in Christianity and
Islam; *Maa Ngala*; *Tengri*; *Shöñgwa'ya'di:söñ*. The *Dharma*. Even
the Greek *primus inter pares* high gods sworn before in formal oaths)
but the structural feature is consistent.

The hypothesis stated as design implication for kith-covenant:

> **the kith-covenant compact members are likely to be capable counterparties
> only insofar as their internal cosmology has a transcendent moral authority structure,
> a single ultimate to which oaths, virtue, and mutual recognition can
> ultimately refer. The content of the ultimate need not be shared
> across members. The *structural feature* of having one, apparently,
> must be.**

This is a stronger claim than "a shared sacred/cosmological frame
that makes oaths mean something"
([Justus hostis](justus_hostis.md#why-a-transcendent-moral-authority-was-structurally-necessary)):
*not just any sacred frame,
but one with transcendent moral authority / one-ultimate structure*. The historical
evidence assembled here is the basis for the stronger form.

Several careful consequences follow:

- **Kith-Covenant requires structure but does not dictate the
  Creator's nature**. The compact requires acknowledgment of a
  Creator, a pro-human orientation, and a pro-cooperation
  orientation
  ([Justus hostis](justus_hostis.md#what-the-compact-requires-what-it-does-not-and-why)).
  It cannot specify whose ultimate is right, or whether the
  ultimates are really one or really many, or what the ultimate
  is called. First-order theology is the business of each
  community itself.
- **But the kith-covenant expectations about candidate members can be
  realistic**. A community whose internal cosmology lacks a transcendent moral authority
  frame (a strict polytheism without a primary deity, a thoroughly
  materialist worldview, an ideological system that recognizes no
  ultimate beyond itself) may not be a stable counterparty for the
  long-term sacred-binding work, regardless of its present intentions.
  This is not a moral judgment. It is a structural observation about
  what substrates have historically sustained what cooperation.
- **The pattern is implicitly ecumenical, explicitly cross-traditional,
  and quietly incompatible with strict atheism as a *substrate*.**
  Strict atheism within a member community is a matter for that
  community. What the pattern observes is that no historical
  cooperation regime among unconnected communities has been sustained
  on a substrate that denies any ultimate altogether. This is data,
  not policy. Kith-Covenant should state what the data say without
  thereby legislating theology.
- **The Mongol case (Tengrist tolerance grounded in shared
  transcendent moral authority ontology) and the Islamic case
  (formal legal architecture for *Ahl al-Kitab*) are the closest
  historical analogs to what kith-covenant would need to design
  at compact founding.** Both *recognized in advance* that the
  high gods of other communities were in some sense the same as
  their own, and built cooperation on that recognition. The
  kith-covenant recognition protocols
  ([Recognition protocols](recognition_protocols.md)) should
  learn directly from these cases.

The hypothesis is open to refutation. A clear case of sustained
cross-community cooperation among strangers built on a substrate
without transcendent moral authority structure would substantially weaken it. Several
candidates have been considered (Buddhist *sangha*, classical Greek
leagues, modern secular institutions); each on examination either
confirms the pattern or fits the predicted failure mode. The
Buddhist *sangha* case, which at first presents as a counterexample
because of Buddhism's non-theistic philosophical core, on detailed
examination in [The Buddhist sangha](buddhist_sangha.md) confirms the
pattern through the Dharma as structural single-ultimate, and adds a
refinement. The sangha is a *trans-polity religious community*
rather than a *cross-polity cooperation framework among separate
sovereigns*, and the two are distinct forms of long-running voluntary
cross-community cooperation. Greek high-god oath practice confirms
the pattern within polytheism. Modern secular institutions fit the
predicted failure mode. The universe of cases is finite and
selection bias is possible. The hypothesis should be held
provisionally and tested against further evidence rather than
asserted as established.

One structural feature of the argument must be named because a
skeptical reader will name it if we do not. The evidence runs
through an **asymmetric filter**. Successes are credited to the
substrate. Failures among substrate-holding communities (the
colonial frontier, the Salem witch trials) are debited to
nominal carriers betraying the substrate. Although each
individual move is defensible on its own evidence, jointly they
make the thesis hard to lose, and a thesis that cannot lose is
not carrying information. The corrective is to state, in
advance, what counts against it:

- **A durable secular full compact.** A voluntary, structurally
  sovereign-less, broad-envelope compact sustained across three or
  more generations on a substrate that recognizes no ultimate. The
  EU (above) is the live candidate and the forecast has a date;
  ASEAN is the second candidate on a longer clock.
- **A features-present community that spirals without termination
  or repair**. After the Salem re-analysis ([justus_hostis.md
  "The Salem witch
  trials"](justus_hostis.md#the-salem-witch-trials-the-features-can-be-held-and-still-defeated)),
  the claimed function of the features is metabolism: termination
  and repair, not immunity. A community holding the five features
  in operative practice whose purity spiral runs to exhaustion or
  external suppression, with no substrate-internal termination
  and no repentance sequence, breaks that claim.
- **A featureless regime that self-terminates and repairs.** The
  Salem contrast depends on the ideological purges ending only by
  coup or death of the purger. A regime lacking the features that
  nonetheless produced Salem's sequence (internal termination by
  appeal above the political community, followed by institutional
  repentance and restitution) would erase the distinction the
  features are credited with.
- **Substrate-blind confound survival.** If the durability pattern
  survives controlling for the confounds (literacy, trade density,
  imperial backing, geography; discussed under "The circularity
  objection, confounds, and provisional confidence" below) *only* in
  the substrate-holding cases, that is confirmation. If the confounds
  alone reproduce the pattern, the substrate is riding on them.

These criteria are the exposure of the thesis. The pattern has
survived every case examined so far. It has not yet been run
against a world that outlives its inheritance, and that test is
underway.

### The circularity objection, confounds, and provisional confidence

Two methodological objections bear on how much weight the pattern can
carry, and both are conceded in part.

**Selection and circularity.** The case set is weighted toward
regimes that possess a transcendent moral authority, and some
counterexamples are absorbed by reading their structure
generously (the Buddhist *sangha*'s Dharma as a single-ultimate;
the *primus inter pares* high gods of the Greek leagues sworn
before in formal oaths). A reader can fairly ask whether the
pattern is found because it is looked for. The reasoning is not
circular in the vicious sense. It is *abductive*, an inference to
the best explanation from a finite case set, and the case set is
openly finite and openly weighted, which is why the non-Western
survey above exists to test the weighting. The modern-secular
counterpoint that the sovereign-less secular institutions of
today "may simply be young" cuts both ways. The pattern cannot
prove they will fail, and the objection cannot prove they will
last. The defensible position is that the evidence is suggestive,
not conclusive, and the three-reading structure of
[The noticeable hand](noticeable_hand.md) holds exactly that
degree of confidence.

**Confounds.** The durable cases also tend to share literacy, dense
trade networks, imperial backing, and favorable geography, any of which
could carry part of the load the substrate is credited with. This is
the fourth exposure criterion named just above. If the durability
pattern survives controlling for those confounds only among the
substrate-holding cases, that is confirmation. If the confounds alone
reproduce it, the substrate is riding on them. The controlled
comparison has not been done on the merits, and until it is, the
confounds remain a live alternative explanation rather than a defeated
one.

**Confidence.** The residual issue is neither the thesis nor its
falsifiability but the *confidence* with which it is stated. The
finding is held provisionally here, and the documents that build on it
([Justus hostis](justus_hostis.md#why-a-transcendent-moral-authority-was-structurally-necessary),
[Recognition protocols](recognition_protocols.md),
[Bounded conflict](bounded_conflict.md)) state it with the same
provisional confidence rather than as settled fact. They adopt the
one-ultimate structure as a *design requirement* independently of how
the empirical question ultimately resolves.

## Patterns across the cases

Six observations recur across the cases above. They are not the *only*
patterns and the cases differ in many particulars. They are the
patterns most directly relevant to the kith-covenant design.

1. **Every long-functioning case rested on a sacred substrate with
   transcendent moral authority / one-ultimate structure that was not legislated by the
   cooperation regime itself**. Christendom for the European cases;
   *Shöñgwa'ya'di:söñ* for the Haudenosaunee; *Maa Ngala* + Islam for
   Mali; Tengri for the Mongols; *Ahl al-Kitab* monotheism for Islamic
   inter-community law. The *Dharma* for the Buddhist *sangha*. The
   primus-inter-pares high gods for the Greek and Roman leagues. The
   cooperation regime presupposed the substrate. It did not create it.
   The strengthened claim, that the substrate must specifically have
   transcendent moral authority structure rather than merely being "shared cosmology," is
   developed in "The transcendent moral authority pattern" section above and is consistent
   with the substrate-affords-emergence position
   ([Justus hostis](justus_hostis.md#substrate-affords-emergence-it-does-not-dictate-content)).

2. **The third-party role (second, mediator, fire-keeper) was
   institutionally central in all of them**. Cooperation among two
   parties in dispute is structurally less stable than cooperation
   mediated by a recognized third. The kith-covenant mediator architecture
   should reflect this.

3. **None of the regimes had a single sovereign**. They were either
   federations of equals (Westphalia, Hanse, Iroquois) or
   non-state customary regimes (lex mercatoria, Peace of God, codes
   duello). This is encouraging for the kith-covenant design space, which
   explicitly excludes a sovereign over the compact members.

4. **Decay was driven by loss of substrate, by external coercion the
   regime could not match, or by being forced into existential
   external conflicts**. Internal failure was rarely the proximate
   cause. The kith-covenant threat models should weight external decay
   vectors heavily.

5. **All of them cultivated a procedural choreography (embassies,
   challenges, fairs, councils, ceremonies) that *substituted form
   for trust* among strangers.** Form is the universal device for
   cooperation among strangers. The kith-covenant protocol layer is exactly
   this kind of choreography.

6. **The cases that cultivated explicit practices of restoration (the
   Condolence ceremony, the *jus post bellum* tradition,
   ecclesiastical reconciliation) outlasted those that did not.**
   A regime built for fallen actors must include named paths back
   into good standing. This is incorporated in
   [Bounded conflict](bounded_conflict.md#protocol-sketch) as the
   restitution-and-return rung.

## Open questions

- The Haudenosaunee Condolence ceremony has no European-tradition
  analog and is a strong candidate for direct study by kith-covenant. What
  is the appropriate way for kith-covenant to engage with this: citation,
  inspiration, formal collaboration with Haudenosaunee scholars?
  This is a matter of intellectual seriousness as well as basic
  respect for living tradition.
- The transcendent moral authority hypothesis is the most consequential single claim in
  this document. It deserves dedicated stress-testing: are there
  cases of sustained cross-community cooperation among strangers
  built on substrates *without* transcendent moral authority structure that the present
  survey has missed? Candidates worth looking at specifically for this
  purpose include certain African stateless societies (e.g., the Tiv,
  the Nuer), pre-Inca Andean ayllu networks, and Pacific voyaging
  exchange systems.
- The realpolitik / voluntary-peer / longevity reweighting performed
  in [The noticeable hand](noticeable_hand.md) substantially changes
  which cases bear most directly on the kith-covenant design (filtering out
  Pax Mongolica, *dhimma*, and other imperial frameworks as not
  voluntary peer cooperation). The case treatments above should
  probably be revisited with that filter applied, likely splitting
  the imperial cases into a separate appendix labeled "cooperation
  regimes that are not what kith-covenant is, but bear instructive
  parallels," rather than presenting them alongside the voluntary
  peer cases as if they were comparable.
- The brief survey above did not treat the Chinese tributary
  system, the Indian *raja-dharma* tradition, or pre-Inca Andean
  cooperation. Even though none seems likely to refute the
  transcendent moral authority pattern, each may carry
  additional design lessons.
- The "external existential war" failure mode (the Iroquois case)
  appears unsolved. Has any historical regime survived this
  situation? If not, what does kith-covenant owe its members by way of
  disclosure?
