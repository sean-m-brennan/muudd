# Recognition Protocols: The Handshake That Does Real Work

## Thesis

Recognition is the load-bearing first act of any compact. Everything
else in the kith-covenant design (the escalation ladder, the common-virtue
commitments, the restoration paths, the handling of the *hostis
humani generis*) presupposes that the parties have already
recognized one another as
the kind of party with whom these procedures are even possible. Get
recognition wrong and nothing downstream works. A compact populated by
parties who cannot really be members will be hollowed out from within.
A compact that cannot recognize legitimate counterparties will fail to
form at all.

This document develops the kith-covenant recognition protocols. It takes
seriously two converging conclusions from the prior research:

- **The transcendent moral authority substrate finding** from
  [historical_cases.md](historical_cases.md#the-transcendent-moral-authority-pattern) and
  [noticeable_hand.md](noticeable_hand.md). The cooperation regimes
  that have endured across unconnected communities in the case set
  rested on substrates that point to a single ultimate. The finding is
  abductive and held provisionally (see
  [historical_cases.md §"The circularity objection, confounds, and
  provisional confidence"](historical_cases.md#the-circularity-objection-confounds-and-provisional-confidence)),
  but it is the
  consistent reading of the record, and kith-covenant adopts it as a
  design requirement for member communities rather than leaving it as
  an open question to be settled later. Recognition is where that
  requirement is enacted at compact-formation.
- **The anathema consideration** from
  [justus_hostis.md "Two tiers: anathema and *hostis humani generis*"](justus_hostis.md#two-tiers-anathema-and-hostis-humani-generis)
  and [bounded_conflict.md "Handling the anathema"](bounded_conflict.md#handling-the-anathema). The protocol must include a category for actors whose orientation
  toward the compact is destructive rather than competitive.
  Recognition is where the first filter against such actors is applied.
  Better to detect the misfit at the threshold than to discover it
  after the actor has been admitted to the compact interior.

The model the document leans on most heavily, *for the
intra-community layer of recognition*, is the Christian creed, a
historical institution that successfully filtered entryists and
heretics within the church through what looked like nothing more
than recitation of words. The creed worked at that layer.
Understanding *why* it worked gives kith-covenant its template
for an internal community rite of authorizing its own envoys,
the layer at which asymmetric psychological cost historically
carries load. The *inter*-community layer is a different problem
and is governed by the thin-functional-domain pattern documented
in [historical_cases.md "Convergence without shared
culture"](historical_cases.md#convergence-without-shared-culture-the-thin-functional-domain-cases).
Those cases are Kadesh, the Rhodian transmission, Mediterranean
frontier norms, polycentric diplomatic immunity, and the Red
Crescent. The two
layers are treated separately in the section that follows. The
creed analysis in the body of this document applies to the
intra-community layer.

## What recognition must accomplish

A working recognition protocol must do five things at once. The
demands are in tension. The difficulty of the design problem is in
satisfying all five simultaneously.

1. **Filter at the threshold.** Communities incapable of being
   members (because they lack the substrate, or because their
   orientation is destructive, or because they cannot in good
   conscience commit to the protocol terms) must be filtered out
   before they enter the compact interior. After entry, removing
   them is procedurally heavy and politically costly.
2. **Establish common knowledge.** All members must publicly know
   that all members have publicly committed to the same things. This
   is the technical sense of common knowledge from
   [tacit_coordination.md "Common knowledge"](tacit_coordination.md#common-knowledge-and-culture-relative-salience). Each member must know each other has committed, and must know
   that each other knows, recursively. Recognition is the mechanism
   that creates this.
3. **Honor particularity.** Each member community arrives with its
   own substrate: Christian, Buddhist, Haudenosaunee, Muslim,
   Mande, etc. The recognition protocol must not require any
   community to flatten its particularity, abandon its own theology,
   or pretend to a uniformity it does not share. The compact is for
   strangers, not converts.
4. **Make a commitment that means something.** A recognition that
   commits to nothing in particular is a recognition that does
   nothing. The commitment must be substantive enough that those who
   cannot really make it find themselves struggling. *Generic piety
   filters nothing.*
5. **Be re-affirmable across generations.** Recognition is not
   one-time. Member communities change over time. The substrate that
   was honestly affirmed by one generation may be hollowed out by
   the next. The protocol must be designed for periodic re-recitation
   that catches drift before it becomes terminal.

These five demands are the test against which any candidate
recognition protocol must be measured. Under the two-layers
framing developed in the next section, demands 1 and 4 ("filter
at the threshold" and "commitment that means something") are
primarily intra-community layer work. The rite of the community
itself filters its own members under its own substrate, and the
commitment means something because it is made on the substrate
of the community itself. Demand 2 ("establish common knowledge")
is inter-community layer work: the thin grammar, credentialing,
vouching, and audit commons together produce public knowledge of
who has committed to what. Demand 3 ("honor particularity") is
structurally guaranteed by the separation of layers. No
community is asked to adopt the substrate of another at either
layer. Demand 5 ("re-affirmable across generations") lives
primarily in the intra-community layer (the periodic rite of
each community itself) with inter-community credentialing
refreshed accordingly.

## Two layers. Intra-community authorization and inter-community grammar

A single joint ceremony (candidate member communities publicly
reciting the five-movement statement across the boundary, with
asymmetric psychological cost filtering entryists at the compact
threshold) would import the mechanism of the Christian creed and
apply it at the wrong layer. The creed historically attested
work is *intra*-community filtering (the church filtering its
own members), not cross-cultural entry-gating between
communities that do not share substrate. When the historical
record of actual cross-cultural cooperation is consulted
directly ([historical_cases.md "Convergence without shared
culture"](historical_cases.md#convergence-without-shared-culture-the-thin-functional-domain-cases)),
what is found is not joint thick ceremonies. The Hittites and
Egyptians did not recite a shared creed at Kadesh. Each invoked
its own gods as witnesses of a shared treaty form. Merchants at
Champagne fairs did not jointly recite. Each was credentialed by
his own house and carried the reputation of his community, under
a shared procedural grammar. The Red Crescent did not require
Ottoman adoption of the Red Cross's theological background. It
adjusted the symbol and kept the protective framework intact.

The recognition mechanism therefore runs in **two distinct
layers**. The rest of this document is largely about the
intra-community layer. That is where the
five-movement statement lives and where
asymmetric-psychological-cost work actually happens.

### The intra-community layer. Where the five movements live

Each candidate member community performs the five-movement rite
*on its own envoys and representative machines before they are
authorized to bind the community inside the compact*. The
ceremony, the creed model, the asymmetric-psychological-cost
mechanism developed below, the full community-internal apparatus
of catechumenate, sponsorship, reaffirmation, and anti-drift
disciplines. All of this is the work of the intra-community
layer, and all of it operates on the members of the community
itself, in the language its own substrate supplies. This is
where asymmetric psychological cost is historically attested to
carry load. The creed worked on people raised within the
tradition whose substrate was already making its claims on them.
The rite doing its work at this layer is exactly the pattern the
creed established.

The five movements carry their full content at this layer. The
addressee is the community itself, which addresses itself as it
commissions its own representatives, not the compact as a whole.

### The inter-community layer. Thin grammar and credentialing

What crosses the compact boundary is not joint recitation but a
thin procedural grammar of the kind Kadesh, lex mercatoria, and
the Geneva framework document. The inter-community layer of the
compact carries four elements:

1. **A shared form for compact entry and standing**. A
   treaty-grammar analogue specifying what a member community
   must declare, in what form, to whom, with what deposit and
   what periodic reaffirmation. The form is content-thin by
   design. It encodes the structural commitment ("we have
   something above ourselves that binds us; we do not treat the
   compact as that thing") without requiring any community to
   accept the substrate of another community. This is the
   compact-side analogue of the treaty form attested from Kadesh
   through Westphalia and into modern multilateral accession
   instruments.
2. **Credentialing of envoys and representative machines** whose
   intra-community authorization has been completed by the sending
   community. The inter-community layer sees credentials, not
   confessions. A credential carries proof that the envoy was
   authorized by a community whose own substrate has bound its
   authorization act. The compact does not second-guess how that
   binding was done, any more than Kadesh asked Egyptian priests
   to vet the Hittite pantheon.
3. **Vouching and sponsor structure.** Existing member communities
   stake standing on new entrants, analogous to the merchant houses
   that vouched for apprentices entering the Champagne fairs and
   to the sponsor system in the historical catechumenate. This is
   the catechumenate analogue *at the inter-community layer*, and
   it does what the catechumenate did internally. It moves the
   filter from the moment of admission to an extended period of
   observation and vouching.
4. **An audit commons** by which the history of the standing of
   each member and the conduct of each envoy is visible to the
   compact as a whole. This is the lex mercatoria reputation
   mechanism as developed in
   [`reputation_bridges.md`](reputation_bridges.md); the
   inter-community layer runs on observed conduct over time, not
   on the affect of a one-shot ceremony.
5. **Evidence of interpretive plurality within the candidate
   community**. Per [capture_resistance.md "Interpretive
   plurality"](capture_resistance.md#1-interpretive-plurality-as-a-structural-requirement-on-members),
   the candidate must demonstrate **multiple traceable
   interpretive lineages** of its own substrate, with the
   divergences visible in the audit commons. Although
   communities are not required to *agree* across their internal
   lineages, since disagreement is the condition that makes the
   lineages substantive, they must show that no single faction
   has cornered the interpretive function. The requirement is
   not that the candidate be internally conciliar in form. It is
   that the interpretive authority of the candidate not be
   monopolized by a single living individual or a single closed
   body whose deliberations are not visible to peers within the
   community. The Buddhist sangha's tolerance of multiple
   lineages and the Christian conciliar tradition both
   demonstrate that this is achievable without dissolving
   substrate coherence. Structurally unitary traditions satisfy
   the requirement through conciliar processes, polycentric
   jurisdictions, and the visible accommodation of theological
   schools within the tradition.

The inter-community layer does not attempt to filter entryists by
psychological-cost-at-threshold, because the historical record
does not show that mechanism working at that layer. It filters by
the longer and more reliable mechanisms that the historical record
does show: credential-carrying, vouching, observed conduct, the
mutual-vulnerability forcing function that makes defection visibly
expensive, and the structural-plurality check that makes
interpretive monopoly visible at threshold.

### Why this relocation matters

Three consequences follow for the rest of this document and the
rest of the corpus. First, the creed analysis is scoped to one
layer. Second, the substrate-coherence hole resolves at two
layers rather than one. Third, asymmetric psychological cost
keeps its load.

- **The creed analysis below is the intra-community layer
  mechanism and not the inter-community one**. The six
  mechanisms that make the creed work (surgical specificity,
  performative speech, asymmetric psychological cost, community
  detection of inauthenticity, self-shaping over time,
  repetition, and witnessed recording) all operate within a
  community that shares the substrate being invoked. They are
  load-bearing for intra-community filtering and do not
  straightforwardly transplant across substrate boundaries.
- **Hole #1 (substrate-coherence-under-pluralism) is resolved at
  two layers rather than one**. The substrate does not have to
  cohere across communities because the joint ceremony does not
  exist. The ceremony of each community is internal to it and
  filters its own members using its own substrate. What has to
  cohere is the thin inter-community grammar, and that grammar
  is content-thin by design.
- **Asymmetric psychological cost keeps its load, in its right
  place**. It filters the members of the community itself at the
  point where the community authorizes envoys to speak and bind
  for it. Nothing about the historical attestation of the
  mechanism is given up. Only the layer at which it is deployed
  is corrected.

The rest of this document treats the intra-community layer in
depth, because that is where the mechanism-level detail lives.
The inter-community thin grammar is developed further (as the
compact treaty-form, credentialing, vouching, and audit-commons
apparatus) in [`reputation_bridges.md`](reputation_bridges.md)
and [`liminal_institutions.md`](liminal_institutions.md) and
should be read alongside this one.

## The creed. A Christian historical case study

The Christian creeds were the early Church's solution to a
recognition problem closely analogous to that of kith-covenant.
The three creeds are the Apostles' (early form, 2nd-4th c..
Later codification), the Nicene (325, revised at Constantinople
381), and the Athanasian (5th-6th c.). The Church needed to
distinguish:

- True members from *entryists* (e.g., gnostic infiltrators who
  presented as Christians while teaching incompatible doctrines)
- Orthodox members from *heretics* who shared most of the vocabulary
  but rejected specific doctrinal commitments (Arians, Nestorians,
  Monophysites, etc.)
- Sincere converts from those seeking baptism for instrumental
  reasons (social position, marriage, and escape from
  persecution)

The creeds did this work. Empirically and across centuries, public
recitation of the creed substantially filtered out the categories
they were designed to filter. *And it worked even on people who did
not actually believe what they were reciting.* This last fact is the
puzzle worth understanding, because it points at the mechanisms that
make recognition protocols actually work.

### Why "just words" worked

Several mechanisms operate together. None is sufficient alone. Their
combination is what gives the creed its load-bearing character.

- **Surgical specificity.** The Nicene drafters chose the phrase
  *homoousios* ("consubstantial with the Father") precisely because
  Arians could not accept it. Other phrases were available that
  Arians could have stretched to fit; *homoousios* was the one they
  could not. The creed was not a generic statement of piety. It was
  a *precision instrument*, drafted to pin down the exact points
  where heresy diverged. Generic faith statements filter nothing
  because everyone can sign them. Surgically specific ones filter
  precisely because they cannot be signed by those who reject the
  specifics.
- **Performative speech.** A creed is not a description. Reciting it
  is a *speech act* in J.L. Austin's sense. Saying "I believe in
  one God, the Father almighty" is not reporting a belief, it is
  *committing to* it in the presence of witnesses. The reciter has,
  by the act of recitation, positioned themselves toward the
  community. The community now has standing to hold them to the
  position. This is structurally similar to "I do" in a wedding
  ceremony. The words make the marriage, not describe a marriage
  that exists prior.
- **Asymmetric psychological cost.** Most humans find it
  surprisingly difficult to publicly lie about deep commitments.
  Reciting in a community of those who mean what they say, in
  front of witnesses, with the words touching one's own most
  fundamental orientations, generates real internal cost even for
  those without theological commitment. Anthropologically. The
  shame mechanism is engaged by public commitment in a way that
  private lying does not engage. Heretics often refused to recite
  not because of fear of detection but because *they could not
  bring themselves to*. The cost was real even when no
  enforcement was present.
- **Detection of inauthenticity in community.** A community that has
  recited the creed authentically over years recognizes the marks
  of inauthenticity in others: tone, hesitation, willingness or
  unwillingness to discuss the words, response under questioning,
  body language, eye movement. The recitation is observed in
  context, and the context provides interpretation that the words
  alone do not.
- **Self-shaping over time.** Repeated recitation tends to form the
  reciter. People who began saying the creed for instrumental
  reasons (Constantine's conversion-by-policy, later social-Christian
  state Christianity) often came to mean it. This is the mechanism
  the church fathers actually relied on for the long-term shaping
  of nominal Christians into actual ones. Heretics intuited this
  shaping effect and avoided participation in it precisely because
  they did not wish to be shaped.
- **Repeatability.** The creed is recited *again and again*. Every
  liturgy. Every baptism. Every catechesis. Every council. The
  inauthentic reciter must maintain the act not once but
  perpetually. Over years and generations, this becomes harder, not
  easier. The repetition is itself a filter.
- **Witnessed and recorded.** The recitation is public. It is
  witnessed by the community and (later) recorded in registries.
  This creates a reference point for accountability: "you said this
  on the day of your reception. What you are now saying contradicts
  it." The creed is not merely a moment but a permanent commitment
  with documentary backing.

The convergence of these mechanisms explains the empirical success
of the creed as a recognition protocol, including its success on
unbelievers and entryists, who were filtered out by the *combination*
of specificity, performativity, psychological cost, community
detection, self-shaping, repetition, and recorded witness.

### Asymmetric psychological cost. A closer look

Of the mechanisms above, the asymmetric psychological cost of
insincere recitation is the one that most repays close examination.
It is also the most surprising. The first-pass intuition is that an
unbeliever could simply *say the words.* Sound is just sound.
Performance is performance. What does it cost to mouth a sentence?
The empirical answer, both from the historical record of creed
recitation and from contemporary cross-cultural and experimental
research, is that it costs a great deal, often more than the
unbeliever has. This subsection develops *why*, drawing on several
overlapping bodies of evidence.

#### The body knows

The first observation is that speech is a *bodily* act, and the body
participates in commitment in ways the conscious mind cannot fully
override. Vocalization requires breath, posture, vocal-cord tension,
facial muscle engagement, eye direction, hand position. A reciter
who does not mean the words generates *involuntary signaling* that
trained observers can detect: pitch shifts, breath irregularity,
micro-hesitations on key phrases, eye-contact patterns characteristic
of cognitive load, posture stiffening at points of greatest
disjunction between word and intent.

This is not folklore. The forensic-psychology literature on deception
detection finds that deception about *deeply held* matters generates
detectable involuntary signals across multiple modalities, even
when the deceiver is highly motivated to conceal them, even when the
words themselves are perfectly performed. The reliability of any
single signal is modest. The reliability of the *pattern* of signals
is substantial. A community that recites together, regularly, learns
to read the pattern. The reciter cannot easily lie to a community
that knows what authentic recitation looks like, because the body is
not under full conscious control during the act of speaking deeply
weighted commitments.

This is also why *written* commitment is generally weaker than
*spoken* commitment. Written words can be composed, edited, signed
without the involuntary signaling spoken words generate. The bodily
exposure of public recitation is part of what makes recitation
load-bearing in a way that signing a document is not. The kith-covenant
recognition ceremony preserves this by requiring spoken recitation
in person, not merely cryptographic signature. The signature is
necessary for the record. The recitation is necessary for the
filtering.

#### Self-perception and cognitive dissonance

The second observation is that human beings infer their own beliefs
partly from observing their own behavior. This is Daryl Bem's
self-perception theory (1967, developed across the 1970s), now
substantially supported by experimental literature. When asked
"what do you believe," people often reach for the answer by
considering what they have done, what they have said, what
positions they have publicly taken. The "I" that holds beliefs and
the "I" that performs actions are the same I. The actions update
the beliefs.

For an unbeliever publicly reciting a creed, this creates real
internal pressure. The act of recitation pulls the reciter
self-perception toward the recited content. Each performance is
not just an external display but an *update* to the reciter
sense of who they are. The unbeliever knows this, at least
intuitively, and feels the pull as a threat.

Festinger's cognitive-dissonance theory (1957) names what happens
when the unbeliever resists the pull. Holding "I have publicly
committed to X" alongside "I do not believe X" generates
psychological discomfort that the mind seeks to resolve. There are
only two stable resolutions: come to believe X (which is what the
reciting community wants and what the unbeliever fears), or refuse
to make the commitment (which removes the dissonance by removing
its cause).

The historical pattern of creed-resistance among heretics is
exactly what this predicts. Arians refused to recite the Nicene
Creed not because they feared exposure but because *they could
not make themselves do it*. The dissonance pressure was
unbearable. They withdrew rather than be shaped, and their
withdrawal was itself the filter. Although it looks like
cowardice or fear of discovery, the story is about the
structural cost of public commitment to what one does not hold.

#### Speech act theory. What is done in the saying

The third observation comes from speech-act theory (Austin,
*How to Do Things with Words*, 1962; Searle, *Speech Acts*, 1969).
Some utterances do not merely *describe* states of affairs. They
*constitute* them. "I do" in a marriage ceremony does not report a
marriage that exists prior to the utterance: the utterance, in the
right context, *makes* the marriage. "I promise" does not describe
a promise. It is the promise. "I bequeath" does not describe a
bequest. It is the bequest.

A creed recitation is structurally similar. "I believe in one God"
is not (only) a report on a prior internal belief state. In the
context of a recognition ceremony, it is the *act of becoming
committed*, what Searle calls a *commissive*. The reciter has done
something by saying the words, regardless of whether the prior
internal state matched. The community has standing to hold the
reciter to the commitment because the commitment is constituted by
the act, not predicated on a separable internal belief.

This explains why an unbeliever cannot honestly recite by
quietly holding back inside. The recitation is not a description
that can be falsified by an undisclosed inner state. It is an
act that has been performed. The unbeliever who recites has
*committed*, in the public sense that kith-covenant cares about,
even if their private state disagrees with the commitment. The
disjunction between commitment and private state is now their
problem and not that of the community. They must either bring
their private state into alignment with their public commitment
(which is what creed-recitation aims at) or live with a known
internal contradiction, which is psychologically costly to
maintain over time.

#### The special weight of sacred speech

The fourth observation is that speech invoking the sacred carries
*additional* weight beyond ordinary speech-acts. Across cultures
that have anything resembling a sacred category, false claims about
sacred matters (false oaths, false invocations, false claims of
divine commission) are treated more seriously than other forms of
deception. The penalties (legal, social, religious) are heavier.
The prohibitions are more emphatic. The language of warning is
stronger.

There is good reason to think this reflects a deep feature of
human cognition rather than an arbitrary cultural choice. Humans
appear to have a category for "things one cannot lie about without
incurring grave cost," and the contents of that category cluster
heavily around the sacred. Why this is so is a deep question. It
may reflect a genuine intuition that there is something against
which the lie is being committed, beyond merely the listener. It
may reflect group-evolutionary dynamics around honest signaling at
high stakes. It may reflect both. Whatever its origin, the
phenomenon is robust.

For the unbeliever reciting a creed, the sacred-speech weight
amplifies all the other costs. It is one thing to lie about
one's preferences. Another to lie about one's deepest
commitments. Another to lie about *what one has staked one's
soul on*. The creed is not merely a personal commitment but an
invocation of God. Reciting it falsely is, in the frame of the
believing community and (apparently) in the intuition of the
unbeliever as well, an act of particular gravity. Many would-be
heretics historically appear to have felt this even when they
explicitly rejected the framework that would account for it. The
*intuition* of sacred-speech weight seems to operate
independently of doctrinal acceptance of what makes the speech
sacred.

#### Community resonance. Reciting with believers

The fifth observation is that reciting *alone* is
psychologically different from reciting *with a community of
believers*. The collective experience generates what Émile
Durkheim called "collective effervescence," the heightened
emotional state of shared ritual participation, which can
produce experiences participants describe as transcendent,
unifying, and overwhelming. For an unbeliever attempting to
perform recitation in such a context, two things happen at once.
First, their own resistance is engaged by the sacred-speech
weight. Second, they are surrounded by people whose authentic
recitation is generating the collective state.

The combination is hard to bear. The unbeliever feels what the
believers feel (emotional contagion is real and largely
involuntary) without sharing the belief that would make the
feeling coherent. While they cannot deny the power of what they
are participating in, they cannot accept its ground. This
produces a particular kind of distress that the believers, who
are simply experiencing participation in a coherent whole, do
not feel.

Many converts in the historical record describe their conversion as
having begun in exactly this dynamic. They came to scoff or to
infiltrate. They recited. They experienced the collective
participation. They could not afterward maintain the unbelief that
had brought them in the first place. The community-resonance effect
is *the mechanism* of the Pascal-style "kneel and you will believe"
prediction. The kith-covenant recognition ceremony, conducted in the
presence of the assembled compact members, generates a smaller-scale
version of the same effect.

#### The repetition trap

The sixth observation is that repetition makes insincere recitation
*harder*, not easier. The first-pass intuition is the opposite.
Practice should make performance smoother. But repeated insincere
recitation generates accumulating dissonance, accumulating
self-perception updates, and accumulating awareness of one's own
internal contradiction. The unbeliever who recites once and walks
away has paid a single cost. The unbeliever who must recite weekly,
or monthly, or every Sunday for years, finds the cost compounding.

The historical pattern is exactly this. Heretics often joined,
attempted to maintain participation, and *then withdrew*, citing
their inability to continue rather than any specific external
event. The compound cost had become unbearable. By contrast,
genuine believers found repeated recitation deepening and
sustaining their belief. The same mechanism, with opposite
direction depending on initial state.

This is why the kith-covenant periodic re-affirmation cadence is not merely
an administrative event but a genuine filter. A community that has
hollowed out theologically over a generation will struggle to
re-recite authentically. The struggle will be observable. The
filter will operate. Communities that remain substantively oriented
toward their substrates will recite easily, perhaps with deepened
conviction, and the re-affirmation will be a strengthening event
rather than a stressful one.

#### The Pascal effect. Fear of being shaped

The seventh observation closes the circle. Pascal's wager (1670)
contained an often-overlooked subtlety: Pascal anticipated the
objection that one cannot believe by willing to. His response was
that one should *act as if* (kneel, take holy water, attend mass)
and "*cela vous abêtira*" ("this will make you stupider," in
Pascal's deliberately provocative phrase), meaning, more
charitably, that the practice will form the disposition, the
disposition will form the belief, and one will become a believer
through the practice.

This is empirically correct, and it is the deepest reason why
unbelievers resist creed recitation. Not fear of immediate exposure
(most communities are forgiving of nervous first recitations) but
*fear of being shaped*. The unbeliever who understands what
recitation does does not want to be done to. They do not want to
become the kind of person who means the words. The unbelief is, for
them, a possession they wish to retain. The practice threatens it.

This explains the most striking empirical pattern of all. The
*systematic* avoidance of recitation by entryists who would have
benefited materially from successful infiltration. They chose
ineffective overt opposition over effective covert participation
because they correctly understood that the participation would
unmake them. The creed worked not only by detection but by
*anticipated transformation*, a defense the would-be infiltrator
applied to themselves before the community had to.

#### Cross-cultural validation

The eighth observation is that this complex of mechanisms is not a
Christian particularity. Initiation rites, oath ceremonies,
naturalization rituals, professional admission practices, and
analogous events across cultures use the same toolkit. Public
recitation of substantive commitments, witnessed by community,
recorded for accountability, repeated over time, with sacred-speech
weight where available. The anthropological literature on initiation
(van Gennep's *Rites de Passage*, 1909; Turner's *The Ritual
Process*, 1969) documents the recurrence across cultures and
historical periods. The same asymmetric psychological cost dynamic
operates in each.

This is encouraging for the kith-covenant design. Kith-Covenant is not asking
member communities to do something foreign to human cognition or
to any particular tradition. It is asking them to do something
that human communities have done in many forms across many
contexts, with mechanisms that human cognition appears already
shaped to support.

It also explains why the kith-covenant recognition statement, designed to
work across radically different theological substrates, can rely on
the same mechanism that worked for the Christian creed within a
single tradition. The mechanism is *generic*. The content varies.
The five-movement structure is the kith-covenant instantiation, but the
underlying architecture is the same architecture that humans have
relied on whenever they have needed to bind strangers into a
community of substantive commitment.

#### Implications for the kith-covenant design

The deeper analysis points to several specific design choices in
the recognition protocol that should not be compromised:

- **Recitation must be spoken, not merely signed.** The body
  participation is part of the mechanism. Written signature alone
  forfeits the involuntary signaling that does the filtering work.
- **Recitation must be in person.** Virtual or remote recitation
  loses the community-resonance effect and compromises the body-
  knows mechanism (involuntary signals are harder to read remotely).
- **The content must be substantive.** Generic piety filters
  nothing because reciting it generates no dissonance. The
  five-movement structure includes commitments specific enough to
  trigger the asymmetric cost.
- **The community must be present and attentive.** The witnesses
  are not passive observers. They are part of the mechanism. Their
  authentic recitation generates the collective resonance that
  amplifies the cost of insincere participation.
- **Repetition must be required.** A one-time recitation is
  filterable but accumulating cost is what catches the slow drift
  and the long-term entryist.
- **The community must be allowed to learn the marks of
  authentic recitation**. This means kith-covenant cannot rotate
  witnesses out so rapidly that no one ever develops the
  discernment to read the pattern. Even while rotation prevents
  capture, some continuity in witnessing roles is necessary.
- **Kith-Covenant must not pretend the mechanism is rationally
  optional**. A community that asks "why must we recite *spoken*
  words *in person* in front of *witnesses* with *repetition*?"
  and demands written-only or remote-only or
  single-occasion-only alternatives has, knowingly or not, asked
  for the mechanism to be disabled. The legitimate response of
  the compact is that these are not arbitrary preferences but
  the load-bearing features of how recognition works on actual
  human cognition.

The asymmetric psychological cost is, in the end, the answer to the
puzzle. How can "just words" do real work? They are
not just words. They are bodily, performative, sacred-weighted,
community-amplified, repeated, and accountable utterances whose
asymmetric cost on insincere reciters is what gives them their
filtering power. The Christian creed empirically demonstrated this
for nearly two thousand years. Kith-covenant inherits the architecture and
adapts the content.

#### Limits of the mechanism

The arguments above state the mechanism at its strongest. A responsible
treatment must also state where it weakens, because the kith-covenant
design should not be over-promised.

- **One-shot deception detection is unreliable.** The empirical
  deception-detection literature (DePaulo et al., "Cues to Deception,"
  *Psychological Bulletin* 129 (2003); Bond and DePaulo, "Accuracy of
  Deception Judgments," *Personality and Social Psychology Review* 10
  (2006)) converges on roughly 54% accuracy for observers attempting
  to detect lies in single, brief interactions, barely above chance.
  The "body knows" argument cannot rest on humans being good lie
  detectors on short exposure. They are not. What the mechanism
  actually depends on is *accumulated* signal across many repetitions
  and *community* signal aggregated across many observers, neither of
  which the one-shot literature is designed to measure. This is a
  defensible position but it is weaker than the naive "the body
  signals" claim, and the docs should not overreach.
- **The scaling problem is real.** The historical success of the
  Christian creed rested on tight communities with frequent,
  local, repeated performance: weekly liturgy, confession,
  ongoing neighborly observation. A kith-covenant compact among
  communities that meet only at compact-formation and at
  generational re-affirmation has dramatically less frequency
  and proximity to work with. Whether the mechanism retains
  meaningful filtering power at that cadence is an open
  empirical question, and the historical evidence does not
  directly settle it. The re-affirmation cadence is one answer.
  Witness continuity is another. But both are hypotheses that
  this project has not yet tested.
- **Sophisticated insincerity is not reliably detectable even over
  time**. Marrano and crypto-Protestant precedents show that
  communities can sustain insincere recitation across generations when
  the stakes are survival under a coercive regime. Kith-Covenant
  operates in voluntary rather than coercive conditions, which reduces
  but does not eliminate this risk. Communities with sophisticated
  incentives to pass the recognition filter (trade access, rescue
  capacity, intelligence-gathering) can likely train successive
  generations to recite convincingly. The accumulating-cost argument
  weakens substantially in such cases.
- **The accumulating-cost claim is theoretically supported but
  empirically under-demonstrated**. Self-perception theory (Bem),
  cognitive dissonance (Festinger), and speech-act theory (Austin /
  Searle) give plausible grounds for believing repeated insincere
  recitation is costly to the reciter over time. But the size of that
  effect, whether it rises to the level of a reliable filter, and
  whether it survives communities that *culturally normalize* the
  insincerity, are not well measured in the experimental literature.

The conclusion. Asymmetric psychological cost is **one filter
among several** rather than a strong stand-alone filter. Its
work is done in combination with (not instead of) conduct
observation over time, material entanglement (trade, rescue,
commons), costly signaling (capacity commitments visible enough
that falsity eventually surfaces), the jester function
([jester_and_dissent.md](jester_and_dissent.md)), and the
anathema procedure for cases the earlier filters miss. The
recognition ceremony is the *first* filter. It should not be
asked to be the *only* filter. Where the other filters are
absent or weak, the filtering power of recognition should be
expected to weaken correspondingly. This is an actual weakness
of the design at low- contact, low-repetition, low-entanglement
scales, and future design work should weigh whether
kith-covenant is viable at such scales or whether those
conditions themselves fail the compact preconditions.

### What this teaches kith-covenant

The recognition protocol kith-covenant needs has the same structural
requirements:

- It must commit to **specifics** that those who cannot really be
  members will struggle to recite.
- It must be **performative.** Recitation must commit the
  community, not merely report on it.
- It must impose **psychological cost** that scales with
  inauthenticity.
- It must be **publicly witnessed** by other members and
  **recorded** with cryptographic permanence.
- It must be **repeated.** At compact-formation, at periodic
  re-affirmation, at re-entry after designation, and on other named
  occasions.
- It must be **substantive enough to do work** without dictating
  doctrinal content kith-covenant has no business dictating.

The challenge is that kith-covenant cannot be specifically Christian (that
would exclude Buddhist, Iroquois, Muslim, and other members), cannot
be specifically theistic in a doctrinal sense (that would exclude
the *Dharma*-based Buddhist case), and cannot be specifically
sectarian within any tradition. The substantive commitment must be
*structural* (to the transcendent moral authority substrate finding)
rather than to any particular content of that substrate. The
following section develops what that statement looks like.

## the kith-covenant recognition statement

The proposal. At compact-formation, each candidate community publicly
recites a recognition statement consisting of five movements. Each
movement requires substantive commitment that hollows recitation will
struggle to satisfy. Together, the five movements perform the same
function the Christian creed performed for orthodoxy: filtering
entryists and heretics while admitting genuine members across
substantial theological diversity.

The five movements:

1. The naming of the substrate
2. The acknowledgment of fallenness
3. The recognition of the other
4. The commitment to the common virtues
5. The submission of the compact itself to the substrate

Each is developed below with sample language. The sample language is
a *template* for each community to fill in with its own particularity.
The structure is fixed, the content is theirs.

### Movement 1: The naming of the substrate

The community publicly names the transcendent moral authority to
which its own commitments refer, in the terms of its own
community.

> *"We, the [community name], affirm in the presence of these
> members of the compact that our commitments to this compact rest
> ultimately on [our community's named ultimate]. We acknowledge
> [this ultimate] as transcendent (beyond the authority of any
> compact, including this one) and as the source of the meaning
> our commitments here bear."*

The blank is filled in by each community in its own language and from
its own substrate:

- A Christian community: "the Holy Trinity, Father, Son, and Holy
  Spirit, one God"
- A Catholic community might add: "as confessed in the Nicene Creed"
- A Muslim community: "Allah, the One, the Sustainer"
- A Jewish community: "the God of Israel, the Holy One, blessed be He"
- A Haudenosaunee community: "*Shöñgwa'ya'di:söñ*, the Creator,
  Sky Holder"
- A Buddhist community: "the Dharma, the eternal moral and natural
  law"
- A Hindu community: "Brahman, the one ultimate beyond all names
  and forms"
- A Mande community: "*Maa Ngala*, the supreme creator"

This movement is the *filter*. A community whose internal
cosmology genuinely lacks a transcendent moral authority
structure cannot complete this movement honestly. They may try
to fake it (substituting "humanity" or "reason" or "the
planet"); the inauthenticity will be detectable to other members
in the same way creed-inauthenticity was detectable to the early
Church: through the marks of evasion, abstraction, or hollowness
in the recitation. Although a community whose internal substrate
is *open* about not naming an ultimate may be welcomed in
observer or restricted-member capacity, full membership requires
the substantive act this movement performs.

### Movement 2: The acknowledgment of fallenness

The community publicly acknowledges that its members are sinners:
fallen, capable of evil, in need of grace.

> *"We acknowledge that we are fallen. That our members will fail
> the commitments we make here, individually and collectively. That
> we will require restitution and return. That we cannot rely on our
> own virtue alone but require [the substrate named in Movement 1]
> to make our commitments hold. We will not pretend to a virtue
> we do not have."*

This movement is the **anthropological commitment** developed in
[justus_hostis.md "The anthropology"](justus_hostis.md#the-anthropology-the-substrate-must-assume).
It is hard to recite in good faith for any community whose self-
conception is one of unique righteousness, ideological purity, or
cleansed historical innocence. Universalist movements that present
themselves as the bringers of a perfected order, the failure mode
that destroyed the *jus publicum Europaeum* (see
[justus_hostis.md "How the jus publicum fell apart"](justus_hostis.md#how-the-jus-publicum-fell-apart)),
characteristically refuse to acknowledge their own fallenness.
Their inability to recite this movement honestly is a strong filter
against them.

### Movement 3: The recognition of the other

The community publicly recognizes the other compact members as
bearing what its own tradition recognizes as the divine image or
fundamental human dignity, regardless of their substrate surface
content.

> *"We recognize the other communities present here as bearing >
[what our tradition names as the imago Dei, the Buddha-nature,
the > children of the Creator, etc., according to the
formulation of each community itself >]. We recognize that their
commitments rest on their > own ultimates, which we may
understand differently from how they > understand them, but
which we accept as bearing the same > structural role in their
cosmology that our ultimate bears in > ours. We do not require
them to share our theology to be our > peers in this compact."*

This is the *justus hostis* commitment in its compact-formation
form. It performs the move that the Mongol Tengrist tolerance made
into policy and that the Christian catholic / perennialist tradition
endorses theologically (Justin Martyr's *logos spermatikos*; Acts 17;
Romans 2). It does not require any community to abandon its own
theology. A Christian community can recite this while continuing to
believe Christianity is the fullest revelation of God, a Muslim
community while continuing to hold Islam is the final revelation, a
Buddhist community while continuing to hold the Dharma is the deepest
truth. What it requires is the practical recognition that *for the
purpose of this compact* the other members are peers, not heretics
to be corrected.

A demonic actor, particularly an ideologically universalist one
whose orientation is to bring everyone to *its own* substrate,
will struggle to recite this in good faith. Their internal logic
requires that the other members be eventually corrected, converted,
or eliminated. The present tolerance is tactical. The asymmetric
psychological cost of recitation will detect this over time, even
when the immediate words are mouthed.

### Movement 4: The commitment to the common virtues

The community publicly commits to the indicative virtue list
developed in
[bounded_conflict.md "Common virtue, not non-negotiable rules"](bounded_conflict.md#common-virtue-not-non-negotiable-rules),
in the form of *refusals* concrete enough to mean something:

> *"We commit to the common virtues this compact affirms:
> reverence for life, including the life of those with whom we
> disagree. The sanctuary of the rescuer, regardless of whom is
> rescued. The protection of the communicative commons. The
> passage of the traveler. The innocence of the non-combatant and
> the child. The inheritance of the future, including the knowledge
> commons that connects the dead to the unborn. The memory of the
> dead. And the good faith of the confession.*
>
> *We affirm these as virtues we will cultivate, not merely rules
> we will follow. We will refuse the acts that flow from their
> violation, including the acts not yet enumerated that the
> virtues plainly cover."*

The *not merely rules* clause is where the gamification problem
identified in [bounded_conflict.md "Gamification and the
letter/spirit
divergence"](bounded_conflict.md#gamification-and-the-letterspirit-divergence)
is preempted. Although a community may intend to follow the
letter while gaming the spirit, it cannot honestly recite this.
The recitation commits them to a virtue-cultivation, not to
rule-compliance.

### Movement 5: The submission of the compact itself

The community publicly acknowledges that the compact is not
itself the ultimate. If it ceases to serve something beyond
itself, it may rightly be judged or even dissolved.

> *"We acknowledge that this compact is not itself the ultimate to
> which our commitments refer. The compact is in service to the
> common virtues, to the substrates of its member communities, and
> to the transcendent moral authority that each of our communities names
> in its own way. The compact authority is contingent on its
> continuing to so serve. If the compact ever ceases to honor what
> it was formed to honor (if it becomes the captured instrument of
> any one community, of any ideology, or of its own perpetuation),
> we are not bound to it. The higher commitments take precedence.
> We will not idolize this compact, nor allow it to idolize
> itself."*

This is the **antidote-to-entropy commitment**, and it is
essential. Without it, the compact has no Archimedean point
from which to evaluate its own decay. With it, every member has
explicitly committed to recognizing when the compact has become
something other than what it was formed to be, and to acting on
that recognition.

A demonic actor whose orientation is to capture the compact for its
own ends cannot honestly recite this movement. Their entire purpose
is to make the compact *idolize itself* (or them). The movement
forecloses that purpose at recognition.

This movement also forecloses the engineered-civil-religion failure
mode warned against in
[justus_hostis.md "Substrate affords emergence"](justus_hostis.md#substrate-affords-emergence-it-does-not-dictate-content).
The compact cannot become its own object of devotion because the
recognition statement explicitly subordinates it to something
beyond itself.

## The recognition ceremony. Sequential intra-community rites under cross-community witness

Under the two-layers framing established earlier in this
document, the ceremony at compact-formation is *not* a single
joint recitation of a shared creed. It is the **Kadesh
pattern.** Each founding community, in turn, performs its own
five-movement rite in its own terms and substrate, while the
other founding communities witness. No community is asked to
submit to the substrate of another. Each affirms its own
anchor-above-self, its own fallen-actor anthropology, and the
five anti-spiral features in the language the tradition of each
community supplies. The compact records what each community has
done and, by that record, the compact itself is constituted.
This is the inversion of the Nicene pattern (single shared
creed, joint recitation, common substrate) and is the pattern
the historical record of cross-cultural cooperation actually
shows (see [historical_cases.md §"Convergence without shared
culture"](historical_cases.md#convergence-without-shared-culture-the-thin-functional-domain-cases)).

The ceremony has the following characteristics:

- **The representative of each member community speaks the words
  of *that community's* five-movement rite.** The representative
  is a designated person of standing in the community, not a
  hired diplomat or external proxy. The recitation is by someone
  whose community has already internally authorized them under
  the substrate of that community itself. What the compact
  witnesses is an internal community rite being performed in
  public. What the compact does not witness is a joint creedal
  submission.
- **In the primary liturgical or formal language of the
  community itself, with translation provided for witness
  legibility**. Recitation in one's own language is what engages
  the asymmetric psychological cost mechanism, because that
  mechanism operates within a community that shares the
  substrate being invoked. Translation makes the recitation
  legible to witnesses whose substrates differ. It is not itself
  a second recitation.
- **Sequential, not simultaneous.** Each founding community
  performs its rite in turn, with the others witnessing. At
  Kadesh the Hittite gods witnessed the Egyptian oath for the
  Hittite side and the Egyptian gods witnessed the Hittite oath
  for the Egyptian side. Neither party pretended to share the
  witnesses of the other. Sequential recitation preserves this.
  The ordering is procedural and interchangeable.
- **In the presence of all founding members as witnesses**.
  Witness is constitutive. What is recited without witness is
  not a recognition. Compact members are not just observers but
  *parties to* the recognition. Even though the substrate being
  invoked is not shared, the rite of each community itself
  becomes load-bearing for the compact by the fact of its being
  witnessed across the boundary.
- **Examined against the five anti-spiral features.** After the
  recitation of each community, other communities may ask the
  recitant community to clarify how its own substrate supplies
  the five features (fallen-actor, grace,
  scapegoat-revelation-or-equivalent, enemy-love, non-ultimacy;
  see [justus_hostis.md "The anti-spiral
  features"](justus_hostis.md#the-anti-spiral-features-why-anchor-above-self-is-necessary-but-not-sufficient)
  and [overview.md §3b.i](../concept.md)). This is the
  *examination* function and it is the real filter at the
  inter-community layer. The compact is not checking whether the
  substrate is true in its own terms but whether the substrate
  supplies the structural features the compact requires of all
  members. The community answers in its own terms. Each
  witnessing community translates the answer into its own frame
  and verifies that the features are present.
- **Recorded with cryptographic signatures.** The recitation of
  each community is signed by its representative, by its own
  internal witnesses (fellow members of the reciting community),
  and by the other founding communities as external witnesses.
  The record is available to all members in perpetuity (subject
  to the inheritance-of-the-future virtue; the record is part of
  the knowledge commons). Cross-boundary signatures certify
  witness, not shared substrate.
- **No reciprocal joint re-recitation by existing members.**
  Earlier drafts specified that existing members would re-recite
  the five movements at each new reception, "to bind themselves
  anew and to demonstrate mutuality." Under the two-layers
  framing this is a category error. Existing members cannot
  re-recite a joint creed because there is no joint creed. The
  five movements of each existing community are its own. What
  *does* happen at each new reception is that each existing
  community may, at its option and in its own rite, re-perform
  its intra-community authorization (a renewal of the
  commitments of its own members under its own substrate) with
  the new admission as occasion. This is the generational
  re-affirmation pattern treated in
  [exit_and_succession.md](exit_and_succession.md); it is intra-
  community, not joint.

The ceremony is *expensive in time, attention, and human investment*.
This expense is itself part of the costly-signaling architecture
([tacit_coordination.md "Costly signaling"](tacit_coordination.md#costly-signaling)).
A community willing to undergo the ceremony has demonstrated
something a community unwilling has not.

## Periodic re-affirmation

The recognition statement is not recited once. The periodic
re-affirmation of the compact cadence (rung 7 of the protocol
sketch in
[bounded_conflict.md](bounded_conflict.md#protocol-sketch))
includes a full re-recitation of the five movements by every
current member community.

The cadence is generational: every 25 to 30 years, with the exact
period set at compact-formation. This timing has structural reasons:

- Generational cadence catches the substrate drift that occurs as
  one generation succeeds another. A community that has hollowed
  out theologically over a generation will struggle to re-recite
  authentically. A community that has remained substantively
  oriented toward its substrate will recite easily.
- Generational cadence is long enough that the re-recitation is
  *consequential.* Not a routine annual ritual that becomes
  empty, but a substantial occasion that engages the full
  attention of the community and creates fresh commitment.
- Generational cadence coincides with the *sacred-equivalent* events
  the compact time structure protects (per
  [justus_hostis.md "Substrate affords emergence"](justus_hostis.md#substrate-affords-emergence-it-does-not-dictate-content)).
  Re-affirmation is itself an occasion in which sacred-equivalent
  meaning can grow.

Re-affirmation is also triggered by specific events: the
reception of any member (per "The recognition ceremony" above),
any reversal of an anathema designation, and any major amendment
to the compact common-virtue list.

### Tracking ceremony cost across re-affirmation cycles

Recognition ceremonies are load-bearing because of the asymmetric
psychological cost they impose at recitation
([above](#asymmetric-psychological-cost-a-closer-look)), but
ceremonies tend to **ossify into credentials.** The cost decays as
the ceremony becomes routine, generations inherit the form without
the cost, and what was meant to bind has become procedural
formality. This is the third strengthening move identified in
[capture_resistance.md "Ceremony cost as tracked quantity"](capture_resistance.md#3-ceremony-cost-as-tracked-quantity)
and is incorporated into the re-affirmation cadence here.

Cost is not directly observable, but **proxies are.** Time and
attention given to preparation, public articulation by the
recitant of what they are committing to and what they are
forgoing by committing, witness reports of recitant comportment
during the rite, content of the anti-spiral-feature examination
exchange, and the substantive (rather than rote) responses of
the recitant to challenge questions. The audit commons records
the proxies. The compact surfaces them across cycles to detect
erosion.

When the proxies show consistent decay over multiple
re-affirmation cycles within a member community, the compact
response is not anathema (the community has not actively
repudiated the substrate) but **substantive re-engagement.** A
heavier re-affirmation cycle in which the compact witnesses
examine more demandingly, the recitant prepares longer, and the
cost is reasserted at the level the original compact-formation
required. This is a designated rung between ordinary
re-affirmation and anathema designation, and it has historical
precedent in the renewal practice of each major tradition. The
Christian *mission* preached by visiting clergy, the Buddhist
periodic ordination renewal, the Iroquois renewal of the chiefs'
titles at each new generation. Each tradition has independently
discovered that ceremony cost decays without reinforcement, and
has built reinforcement into ordinary practice. The
kith-covenant substantive re-engagement rung is the
inter-community analogue of that pattern.

## Recognition, withdrawal, and the anathema

The recognition protocol intersects with the anathema procedure in
two specific ways.

**Designation as anathema is, in effect, a finding that the community
has repudiated its recognition statement**. The compact finds that the
community is no longer committing what it once committed. That some
movement has been emptied (most often Movement 5, the submission of
the compact, when an actor seeks to capture it; or Movement 3, the
recognition of the other, when an actor turns universalist; or
Movement 4, the virtue commitment, when an actor reveals consistent
virtue-violation). The designation procedure (per
[bounded_conflict.md "Handling the anathema"](bounded_conflict.md#handling-the-anathema))
applies.

**Re-entry requires a fresh intra-community rite witnessed
afresh across the compact, with substantial scrutiny**. A
community that has been designated and now seeks to return must
perform its own five-movement rite anew on its own
representatives under its own substrate, with the full compact
present as witness and with explicit acknowledgment of the prior
designation and what has changed. The anti-spiral-feature
examination is repeated in the demanding form. The compact is
not accepting the community word that it has changed. It is
requiring demonstrable evidence under cross-boundary scrutiny.
The rite is examined more demandingly than at first reception.
What was acceptable at threshold is not necessarily acceptable
at re-entry, because the community now has a record of having
committed and then violated. The re-entry rite is itself a
costly signal. A community that cannot honestly perform it has
not actually changed, and the compact is right to refuse it.

## Members, neighbors, enemies. The four categories

Recognition sits at the center of a larger question the prior
documents addressed imprecisely: *who counts as a neighbor, and what
is owed to whom?* The colonial-Christian failure mode discussed in
[noticeable_hand.md "The colonial frontier"](noticeable_hand.md#counterweights)
was a failure to extend *neighbor* status to those encountered across
ethnic and religious boundaries, a failure to follow the parable of
the Good Samaritan (Luke 10:25-37), which Christ offered precisely in
answer to the lawyer's question "and who is my neighbor?"

Before laying out the four operational categories, the neighbor
concept itself requires precision. The parable does *not* extend
neighbor status to "all humanity in the abstract." That reading
produces either guilt-inducing impossibility (no finite body can
love eight billion strangers) or hollow sentimentalism
(universal love professed, nothing acted upon). The actual
structure of the parable is much tighter and more useful:

- **Neighbor is actualized by encounter, not by theoretical
  inclusion**. The Samaritan's neighbor was *the man he met on the
  road*. The priest and Levite who passed by had also encountered
  the wounded man (they had seen him) and their failure was
  failure to *act within encounter*, not failure to love all
  hypothetical travelers. The neighbor relation is brought into
  being by the fact of contact. It is not extended in the abstract
  to everyone one does not meet.
- **Discernment operates within encounter.** The Samaritan
  rescued the victim, not the robber, not the fellow travelers.
  Within the encounter he discerned the situation. Who was in
  need, what was the risk, what response was appropriate. A
  naive reading that says "treat everyone encountered the same"
  misses the most important operational feature of the parable.
  The Samaritan's mercy was *specific* and *discerned*.
- **Contact is the condition of moral responsibility.** Where the
  compact does not encounter a community, it does not have
  Samaritan-style obligations toward that community. This is not
  moral shrinking but moral honesty. Responsibility tracks contact
  because discernment requires contact. One cannot know what is
  owed without seeing what is there.

With this precision in place, the operational categories can be
named. What is implicitly a two-category scheme (member /
non-member) is better articulated as four, distinguished by the
nature of the relationship to the compact and of the encounter in
which that relationship operates.

### 1. Members (friends, peers, co-recitants)

Communities that have made the recognition statement, are in
good standing, and to whom the full protocol architecture of the
compact applies. Mutual recognition is active. Restoration paths
are available. The common virtues are commitments held *with*
them. This is the friend pole of Schmitt's friend/enemy
distinction in its proper place. Members are neighbors by virtue
of their ongoing, formally recognized encounter with one another
through the compact. The encounter is continuous, structured,
and bidirectional.

### 2. *Justus hostis* (legitimate adversaries)

Either members in active dispute (who remain peers despite the
dispute, per the entire architecture of
[bounded_conflict.md](bounded_conflict.md)) or non-members with
whom conflict exists but who are still treated within the
bounded-conflict frame. The *justus hostis* is the enemy who
*remains a neighbor within the encounter of conflict.* One who
can be fought, but who must be fought with discernment of what
they are (human, adversary, fellow-bearer of the *imago Dei* or
its functional equivalent) and what they are not (demonic, outside
the moral universe, raw material). Peace remains possible.
Restoration to non-conflict is structurally available.

### 3. Anathema (formally cut off from the compact)

Communities that have been formally designated through the heavy
procedure in [bounded_conflict.md "Handling the
anathema"](bounded_conflict.md#handling-the-anathema). The
ordinary restoration architecture is suspended. Engagement is
guarded. Active opposition is permitted; **but re-entry remains
available** through fresh recitation with heightened scrutiny.
The anathema is the primary response of the compact to actors
who have exceeded the *justus hostis* frame, a formal
cutting-off, not an ontological judgment. *And, this remains
critical, even the anathema retains the basic moral standing*
that prevents the compact from inflicting acts that violate the
common virtues against them. The category change permits
stronger response *within* the virtue frame. It does not permit
suspension of the frame. The anathema encountered is still a
human being encountered, and the compact conduct toward them is
still bounded by the virtues that apply to all its conduct. The
compact does not become demonic in its response.

In the extremely rare case where the orientation of an actor is
so fundamentally and constitutively destructive that the
anathema designation is insufficient (the classical *hostis
humani generis* proper, "enemy of humankind". Pirates, slavers,
actors outside every cooperative framework), the re-entry path
does not apply. This designation requires an even higher
evidentiary threshold and should be presumed almost never to
arise. The presumption is always anathema unless the evidence is
overwhelming. See [justus_hostis.md "Two
tiers"](justus_hostis.md#two-tiers-anathema-and-hostis-humani-generis).

### 4. Non-members encountered

This is the category most easily omitted in silence, and the one
that the neighbor-question requires sharp
thinking about. Non-members are communities not in the compact,
not designated anathema, not in active *justus hostis*-style
conflict. The compact encounters such communities
constantly: in trade, travel, rescue situations, disaster
response, academic exchange, diplomacy short of conflict, and
ordinary adjacency.

Within each such encounter, the encountered community is a
neighbor in the Samaritan sense. Within the encounter, the
common virtues of the compact govern its conduct, and the
compact must *discern.* What is this encounter, who is in need,
what is the risk, what response is appropriate?

Operationally:

- **the kith-covenant protocols do not apply to non-members in their full
  form**. No recitation, no restoration paths, no mediator
  authority over them. They are not parties to the compact
  procedures because they have not joined it. The compact does
  not govern them.
- **The common virtues of the compact apply within every actual
  encounter with a non-member**. Reverence for life, sanctuary
  of the rescuer, protection of the communicative commons, and
  the rest. These govern the compact conduct whenever it acts,
  regardless of the status of those it acts toward. The virtues
  are boundaries the compact draws on *its own behavior*, not
  limits it imposes on others.
- **Discernment is required within every encounter.** Not every
  encountered non-member is a victim needing rescue. Not every one
  is a candidate for membership. Not every one is in distress. Not
  every one is a threat. The Samaritan rescued the wounded man,
  did not attempt to apprehend the absent robber, did not pester
  the fellow travelers. The kith-covenant encounters likewise require
  specific, situation-discerned responses, not blanket applications
  of "treat everyone equally."
- **Non-members encountered are not raw material.** The compact
  does not view non-members as potential members-in-waiting whose
  cooperation can be coerced, as resources to be exploited, as
  populations to be governed without consent, or as obstacles to
  be removed. The colonial Christian failure was precisely the
  treatment of encountered non-members as falling into one or more
  of these categories. Kith-Covenant explicitly forbids it.
- **Non-members may become members through ordinary recognition
  procedures**. The recognition ceremony is open to any community
  capable of the five movements. The compact posture toward
  non-members should be one of *ready welcome without coercion.* Making membership available to those who seek it, but not
  pressing membership on those who do not.
- **Non-members may also become *justus hostis* without being
  designated anathema.** Even though the internal restoration
  paths of the compact do not apply to it, conflict with
  non-members is bounded by the same virtue frame as conflict
  among members. A dispute may resolve back to peaceful
  non-membership or (if it leads there) to a fresh recognition
  ceremony establishing full membership.

The ground of the self-binding is the thin functional minimum
the compact has adopted. The virtues the compact draws on its
own behavior at the frontier are not owed because the
encountered party has joined, has recited anything, or is in a
position to reciprocate, and they are not owed on the strength
of the substrate of any one member, which the compact does not
speak for. They are what any viable order among vulnerable,
approximately equal, imperfectly altruistic parties contains,
which is why the compact does not treat them as discretionary
where reciprocity is absent
([natural_law.md](natural_law.md#the-ground-the-compact-claims-for-itself)).
The same ground carries the *hostis humani generis* designation
at the other edge, and it stops at the boundary of the compact
itself. It says nothing about whom a member community counts as
within its own order.

What the compact does *not* have, on this framing, is responsibility
toward communities it does not encounter. This is not a moral
shrinkage (no compact can bear responsibility for all human
affairs) but the disciplined recognition that moral obligation
tracks contact because *discernment requires contact*. What kith-covenant
owes is not universal abstract love but full moral seriousness in
every actual encounter, conducted with the discernment the
Samaritan exemplified.

### Why the distinction matters

A compact that fails to maintain the four-category distinction
will collapse in one of two ways. *Either* it expands membership
indiscriminately (extending full member treatment to anyone
without recognition, dissolving the filter and inviting capture)
*or* it contracts the moral universe to members only, treating
encountered non-members as outside concern and recapitulating the
colonial failure. Both failures are well-documented historically.
The four categories are designed to forestall both.

The Schmittian instinct, collapsing all moral relation onto the
friend/enemy axis, is the deeper structural temptation toward
the second failure. It must be explicitly resisted. Although the
compact is *political* in Schmitt's sense, in that it has
friends and enemies and the distinction matters, it is *not
only* political. Within every actual encounter, the encountered
party is a neighbor before they are a friend or an enemy. The
political distinction operates within the prior moral reality,
not in its place. Where the compact discusses its enemies, the
discussion must proceed within an awareness that the enemies
encountered are still neighbors. Where the compact deals with
non-members, the dealings must proceed within an awareness that
encountered non-members are owed what neighbors encountered are
owed. No more (kith-covenant is not a crusading savior of all
humanity) and no less (kith-covenant does not treat the
encountered as raw material).

This is not sentimentality. It is the structural feature that
prevents the compact from becoming the very thing the *hostis
humani generis* category was designed to defend against (per
[bounded_conflict.md "The deepest constraint"](bounded_conflict.md#the-deepest-constraint)).

## Edge cases and failure modes

Several patterns are foreseeable. Each requires explicit handling.

### The hollow recitation

A community recites the five movements without meaning them.
Detection is challenging at first reception but becomes easier
over time. The actual conduct of the community will diverge from
its commitments. Re-affirmation cycles will catch the
inauthenticity. The asymmetric psychological cost mechanism
creates internal pressure that erodes the act. The compact
response. Increased scrutiny, slower extension of trust,
attentive observation through the first re-affirmation cycle,
and willingness to invoke the anathema procedure if the
divergence becomes pattern.

This is the failure mode the Christian creed encountered most often
and managed most successfully. Kith-Covenant should expect it as a normal
case, not an emergency.

### The sincere atheist (or strict materialist)

A community whose substrate is *honestly* materialist or strictly
non-theistic (not as a posture but as a considered worldview) may
find Movements 1 and 5 impossible to recite in good faith. This is
the hardest case and deserves nuance.

Two subcases:

- **Open materialism.** A community that holds materialist views
  while acknowledging the limits of materialism, willing to act *as
  if* there could be more, willing to honor the structural
  commitments even without the metaphysical ones. Such a community
  can probably recite Movements 2 to 4 honestly and may be admitted in
  a *restricted member* capacity that does not require Movements 1
  and 5 in their full theistic form. They commit to the virtues,
  acknowledge their own fallenness, and recognize the other. They
  do not name a transcendent moral authority they do not believe in. The compact accepts
  the asymmetry and names it as such. What binds such a community to the
  virtues it commits to is the adopted functional minimum rather than a
  substrate it has declined to name
  ([natural_law.md](natural_law.md#the-ground-the-compact-claims-for-itself)), which is
  what makes the category coherent and not a courtesy. The thinness of that
  ground is also why the category remains restricted. It supplies no
  anti-spiral features and cannot be enlarged to yield them.
- **Closed materialism.** A community that asserts materialism
  as the final word and holds that the theistic claims of other
  communities are simply false. Such a community cannot honestly
  recite Movement 3 (recognition of the other's substrate as
  bearing the same structural role) because they hold the
  substrate of the others to be illusion. They are not capable
  of being members and the compact should say so rather than
  soften the incompatibility.

The distinction matters because it preserves the substrate-affords-
emergence principle (kith-covenant does not legislate theology) while
stating that the substrate-finding has structural consequences
the design cannot wish away.

### The forced recitation

A community pressured by other members or by the compact leadership
into reciting against its actual position. This corrupts the
recognition mechanism itself and is forbidden. The recognition
ceremony includes provisions for the recitant to refuse or modify
any movement. Refusal does not preclude further conversation about
membership but does preclude the compact from treating the community
as having committed to what they did not actually commit to. Forced
recitation produces a hollow member who will weaken the compact.
Honest refusal preserves the substrate of the recognition act.

### The dissident faction within a member community

A member community recites the recognition statement. Some
faction within that community does not assent. Although the
compact recognizes the community as a whole through its
designated representatives, the existence of internal dissent is
not by itself a designation problem. What matters is whether the
community continues to perform the commitments. Internal dissent
that does not produce protocol violations is a matter for the
polity of the member community itself (which is the
autonomous_trust scope, not that of kith-covenant). Internal
dissent that *does* produce protocol violations triggers the
ordinary disputes- and-restoration process, with the anathema
procedure available if the violations are sustained.

### Civil-religion drift

Over time, the recognition statement becomes a hollow ritual that
everyone performs without meaning. This is the most insidious
failure mode and is what Movement 5 is specifically designed to
forestall: by including the *contingency* of the compact itself
in the recitation, the protocol resists becoming its own object of
devotion.

Detection is hard because hollow recitation looks like sincere
recitation from outside. The principal mechanism is the
cumulative effect of generational re-affirmation. A compact
whose recognition statement has become hollow will produce a
*generation* unable to recite it convincingly, and this is
detectable. Although kith-covenant cannot prevent civil-religion
drift entirely, it can detect it before terminal collapse.

### The capture attempt via recognition itself

A sophisticated adversary may attempt to capture the compact by
modifying the recognition statement during a re-affirmation cycle:
removing Movement 5, weakening Movement 3, etc. This is the worst
failure mode and is foreclosed by the rule that *the five movements
are not amendable*. New common-virtue items can be added (Movement 4
is the only movement whose content is in some part open); the
movements themselves are constitutive and not subject to revision.
A compact that allows the recognition statement to be revised has
allowed itself to be captured.

## Antidote to entropy

The framing is exact: the recognition statement, particularly
Movement 1 and Movement 5, is *the antidote to entropy*. Cooperation
regimes without a transcendent reference tend to drift toward
dissolution by their own internal logic. Every mechanism is gameable
by its own participants, and over time is liable to be gamed. The
transcendent reference is the Archimedean point from which the compact
can recognize when it
is itself drifting.

Three observations on this:

- **The acknowledgment is structural, not devotional.** The compact
  does not require its members to *worship* the transcendent moral authority they name.
  It requires them to *acknowledge* the transcendent moral authority as the source of
  the meaning their commitments bear. Worship is the proper work of
  the member communities in their own substrates. Acknowledgment is
  the work of the compact at its boundary.
- **The acknowledgment is in tension with what any member
  community holds as proper devotion**. The worship of the
  Trinity by a Christian member is not diminished by
  acknowledging that a Buddhist commitment of a Buddhist member
  to the Dharma plays the same structural role. The practice of
  the Eightfold Path by a Buddhist member is not diminished by
  acknowledging that a Christian commitment of a Christian
  member to grace plays the same structural role. The compact
  stays out of the disagreements between communities about whose
  substrate is *fullest* or *truest.* Those are matters for the
  communities to work out across time, in their own ways, beyond
  the compact authority.
- **The acknowledgment may, over time, contribute to the convergence
  the catholic / perennialist hypothesis suggests is possible**.
  Communities that work together in compacts whose foundational acts
  acknowledge a single ultimate may, in their own ways and at their
  own paces, come to recognize that the ultimates they name in their
  separate languages are perhaps not as separate as they once
  appeared. This is not the compact project (the compact stays
  out of theology) but it is a possible second-order effect that
  member communities may welcome or resist on their own terms.

The Christian creed worked in part because it pointed beyond the
Church to the God the Church served. The kith-covenant recognition statement
works in the same way. By pointing beyond the compact to the
substrate the compact serves.

## Implementation notes

Translation to the code layer under `lib/muudd/lib/kith-covenant/src/`:

- **The recognition statement template** is part of the compact
  founding documents, with the filled-in version of each member
  stored as a signed artifact.
- **The recitation event** is recorded as a signed protocol message
  with cryptographic timestamps, multi-party signatures (recitant +
  witnesses), and references to the witnessed text.
- **Periodic re-affirmation** is a scheduled protocol event with
  named participants, time bounds, and recorded outcomes. The
  schedule is set at compact-formation and is itself part of the
  compact founding commitment.
- **The five-movement structure** is enforced at the protocol layer.
  Recognition messages that do not include all five movements are
  rejected. Movement 4 (common virtues) accepts compact-specific
  extensions; Movements 1, 2, 3, and 5 are templated.
- **The examination function** (other members questioning the
  recitant during the ceremony) is procedurally provided for.
  Questions and clarifications are part of the recorded event.
- **The recitation language** can be any language. The translation is
  also recorded. Mismatches between recitation language and
  translation are themselves diagnostic.
- **Designation withdrawal and re-entry** are protocol events with
  fresh recitation requirements and substantially heavier examination
  than first reception.
- **Capture-resistance** is enforced at the protocol layer: amendments
  to Movements 1, 2, 3, or 5 are forbidden by the compact
  immutability rules. Attempts trigger compact-wide alarm.

The simulation work in sibling packages (per
[bounded_conflict.md "Protocol sketch"](bounded_conflict.md#protocol-sketch))
should include scenarios in which adversarial communities attempt to
recite the recognition statement while remaining structurally unable
to commit to it. The ability of the protocol to detect such cases is
itself a measurable property and should be tested explicitly.

## Open questions

- **The sincere-atheist case is the hardest.** Are restricted-member
  arrangements stable, or do they corrode the compact over time
  through asymmetric expectations? The Mongol case (Tengrist
  tolerance grounded in the view that other monotheisms were variants
  of Tengrism) is suggestive but not directly applicable. What binds a
  restricted member is settled (the adopted functional minimum, see
  [natural_law.md](natural_law.md#the-ground-the-compact-claims-for-itself)); whether the
  arrangement holds over generations is the part that remains open, and a
  coherent ground is not the same thing as a durable one.
- **Detection of inauthenticity at first reception** is a virtue, not
  a procedure. The compact mediator and recipient roles must
  cultivate this discernment, but how, and against what corrupting
  pressures? This is a shared open question with the *hostis humani
  generis* treatment in
  [justus_hostis.md](justus_hostis.md#open-questions) and
  [bounded_conflict.md](bounded_conflict.md#open-questions).
- **Movement 3 (recognition of the other)** asks each community
  to recognize the substrate of the other as bearing the same
  structural role. This is a strong claim. Some communities
  (strict exclusivists in any tradition) may find it doctrinally
  impossible. Are these communities filterable at first
  reception, or should the movement be weakened to "recognize
  that the other community holds an ultimate of structural
  significance to them" without endorsing it as ontologically
  equivalent? The latter is weaker and probably more inclusive
  but also less filtering.
- **The generational re-affirmation cadence** is set to 25 to 30 years
  by default. Is this calibrated correctly across communities with
  very different generational rhythms? Some communities (e.g.,
  monastic ones) have effective cadences much faster. Some
  agricultural communities much slower.
- **The five-movement structure** is presented here as fixed and
  non-amendable, with only Movement 4's content extensible. Is this
  the right balance, or are there cases where one of the other
  movements should be revised across compacts of different
  generations or different scopes?
- **Is there a meaningful first-test scenario**, a real
  earthbound case where the kith-covenant recognition protocol could be
  tested at small scale before being deployed to inter-habitat
  scope? Candidate: religious-cooperative networks, sister-city
  arrangements with substantive theological diversity, certain
  ecumenical or interfaith bodies that have already done parts of
  this work.
