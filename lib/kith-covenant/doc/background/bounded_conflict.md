# Bounded conflict: escalation ladders and the common virtue

## Thesis

Cooperation among unconnected communities is stabilized not by the *absence*
of conflict but by the *shape* of it. Communities cooperate more reliably
when their disputes follow legible, graduated, reversible paths, and when
the extremes are bounded not by rules but by *shared virtue made visible
in concrete refusals*. Rules at the boundary are gamed. Virtue at the
boundary is harder to game, because the point is not what is forbidden
but what the community will not contemplate doing regardless of
permission.

Where [Justus hostis](justus_hostis.md) develops the theoretical frame
(the enemy as legitimate peer, and the anthropology of fallen actors
for whom the protocol must be built), this document develops the
*operational* counterpart: the escalation ladders, mutual-vulnerability
mechanics, and shared-virtue commitments that have historically made
bounded conflict work, translated into a protocol specification for
inter-habitat and (first) inter-community disputes on Earth.

Note on terminology. The initial draft of this document used
"non-negotiable core" for the boundary list. That phrasing is retained
only as a foil; **it is the wrong frame and the rest of this document
argues why**. A "non-negotiable" list is a rule set, and rule sets are
gamed by adversarial optimization. What survives is not a list but a
disposition, which the list may describe but does not constitute.

## Escalation ladders

The characteristic feature of bounded conflict regimes is that they replace
the binary peace/war distinction with a ladder of intermediate rungs, each
offering a face-saving retreat option. The historical record offers several
well-developed ladders worth modeling:

**The codes duello (16th to 19th c. Europe and descendants)**. A personal
dispute escalated through formally named rungs. Perceived offense →
verbal challenge → demand for apology → posting of cartel → naming of
seconds → choice of weapons and ground → field encounter → fire (often
deloping, deliberately missing) → honor satisfied, relations formally
resumed. The structure was baroque because its *function* was to give
both parties multiple exits before lethal force. Seconds were not just
witnesses but active de-escalators, empowered to negotiate settlements
at every rung. The duel itself was the last rung of a long ladder most
disputes never climbed past rung three.

**Classical state diplomacy.** Démarche → formal protest → recall
of ambassador → rupture of diplomatic relations → economic
sanctions → blockade → limited military action → general war. Each
rung communicates seriousness while preserving the option of
backing down without loss of face. The rungs are *visible to third
parties*, so the commitment of each side becomes costly to violate
in reputational terms even before it is militarily tested.

**Geneva / Hague conventions under hostile conditions.** Even within
active war, a nested ladder governs the means: protest through protecting
powers → ICRC intervention → reciprocal suspension of specific
reciprocal courtesies (mail, parcels to POWs) → reprisal against
specific assets → wholesale abandonment of convention. Armies that
reached the final rung historically paid enormous reputational and
post-war costs, which stabilized the earlier rungs.

The common architectural features:

- **Each rung is more costly than the last**. The ladder is not arbitrary.
  Climbing it hurts both parties proportionally more.
- **Each rung offers an exit.** The exit preserves honor rather than
  merely permitting a halt, because loss-of-face exits are not real exits.
- **Rungs are publicly visible.** Third parties see who is escalating
  and who is offering de-escalation. Reputation does enforcement work
  that explicit sanctions cannot.
- **Third parties are procedurally involved.** Seconds, protecting
  powers, mediators, neutral arbitrators. These roles exist because
  two-party conflict escalates faster than three-party conflict.

## Mutual vulnerability as the mechanical basis

The deepest reason the Geneva/Hague framework has worked at all, despite
its frequent violations, is that **every belligerent is a potential
victim of every prohibited act**. Nobody argues for the right to poison
enemy wells because one's own wells are equally poisonable. Nobody argues
against POW protections because one's own soldiers will eventually be
captured. The cooperative equilibrium is held in place not by moral
conviction but by the physical fact that every party has soft tissue in
the places the rules protect.

This is the mechanical template kith-covenant should copy. A cooperation protocol
whose prohibitions are grounded in universal vulnerability is far more
stable than one grounded in moral consensus. In the space context, the
universal vulnerability is overwhelming. Every habitat is one bad day from
needing the neighbor's help. This is the kith-covenant analogue of "every
belligerent has wounded and POWs," and it should be the load-bearing
premise of the whole compact.

The argument has a name in jurisprudence, and the compact has adopted it as
its stated ground rather than leaving it as a mechanical observation. Hart's
minimum content of natural law derives a short list of forbearances from
survival as an aim plus truisms about vulnerability, approximate equality,
limited altruism, limited resources, and limited understanding, which is the
mutual-vulnerability argument in another vocabulary. Kith-covenant takes that
content as the ground of its own conduct at the two points where consent has
run out. The duties owed to a party that has signed nothing, and the
*hostis humani generis* designation. The statement, with the four sites it
grounds and the four limits it carries, is in
[natural_law.md "The ground the compact claims for itself"](natural_law.md#the-ground-the-compact-claims-for-itself).

Two consequences bear on the mechanics described here. First,
mutual vulnerability is *uneven*, and the ground does not repair
the unevenness. Communities inside the compact are mutually
vulnerable in ways the compact can assume, and the frontier
asymmetry that [the forcing
function](../substrate.md#the-forcing-function) names remains
exactly as asymmetric after the adoption as before. What changes
is that compact forbearance at the frontier no longer waits on
reciprocal exposure to justify it. Second, the ground reaches
the compact boundary and not the interior of any member, which
is Hart's own concession and not a softening of it. A member's
treatment of those it counts as inside its own order is not a
matter this premise decides.

## Common virtue, not non-negotiable rules

The frame to replace "non-negotiable core" with is **common virtue**. The
distinction is not cosmetic. A rule is a line. A line is a surface against
which adversaries optimize. A virtue is a disposition. Dispositions are
harder to game because the point is not "stay on the legal side of the
line" but "do not want the thing the rule forbids."

From a virtue-centered society, harming life *even of a just enemy* is a
no-go zone, not because it is forbidden but because a community that has
cultivated the relevant disposition does not consider the act live in its
option space in the first place. The rule-framed formulation ("attacks on
medical personnel are prohibited") is downstream of the virtuous
formulation ("we do not attack the wounded") and holds only so long
as the virtue persists upstream. When the virtue collapses, the rule is
adhered to in letter and gamed in spirit, which is worse than no rule at
all because it provides cover.

Design implication for kith-covenant. The boundary commitments
must be articulable as **shared virtue-expressions**, not as
prohibitions. Each boundary is accompanied by a statement of the
virtue it expresses, and the protocol makes both the
virtue-statement and its concrete refusals visible. The indicative
list below is therefore structured as "virtue → refusals" rather
than "prohibited acts."

### Indicative common-virtue list

Each item names the virtue, then names the concrete refusals that make
the virtue visible. The refusals are *shape*, not *limit*. A party
behaving in the spirit of the virtue will generate additional refusals
as circumstances arise. A party gaming the list will stay inside the
letter while violating the spirit, and this will be
legible to third parties, which is how the common-virtue frame enforces
itself where rule-frames collapse.

- **Reverence for life, including the life of the adversary**.
  Refusals: no attacks on life-support, no biological/ecological attacks
  on closed-loop systems, no weaponization of trade in life-critical
  commodities, no targeting of the wounded or the fleeing. The virtue
  covers cases the list does not enumerate. Novel weapons not
  yet invented, or coercive uses of life-support monitoring data.
- **Sanctuary of the rescuer.** Rescue is inviolable regardless of whom
  is being rescued. Refusals: no interdiction of rescue vessels, no
  false-flag use of rescue markings, no conditioning of rescue on
  political compliance. The virtue holds that rescue is categorically
  prior to conflict. There is no such thing as an enemy of a rescuer.
- **Protection of the communicative commons.** Communication channels
  are the precondition of all other cooperation. Attacking them
  converts ordinary disputes into existential ones. Refusals: no
  attacks on relays and astronomical infrastructure, no jamming of
  emergency frequencies, no corruption of shared timing and
  navigation references, no interdiction of diplomatic traffic.
- **Passage for the traveler.** Free passage through established transit
  lanes and across recognized boundaries is treated as sacred in the
  sense that its violation forecloses commerce, diplomacy, and rescue
  simultaneously. Refusals: no attacks on transit lanes, no arbitrary
  detention of travelers, no weaponization of customs or quarantine.
- **Innocence of the non-combatant and the child.** The category of
  those outside the conflict is real even when the conflict itself is
  just. Refusals: no coercion of or violence against children, no
  collective punishment, no attacks on schools and childcare, no use
  of civilians as shields or as information-extraction targets.
- **Inheritance of the future.** The knowledge commons (shared
  archives, scientific data, cultural and linguistic records,
  genealogies, the accumulated craft of communities) is the medium
  through which the dead speak to the unborn. **Destruction of the
  knowledge commons is long-delayed genocide**: it kills the future
  members of a community by severing them from the inheritance that
  would have made them who they could be. Refusals. No burning or
  corruption of archives, no targeted destruction of cultural sites,
  no suppression of languages, no irreversible alteration of shared
  records, no release of poisoned data into shared scientific
  commons.
- **Memory of the dead.** The remembrance practices of a community
  (memorials, cemeteries, mourning rituals) are inviolable. This
  is a narrower case of the inheritance virtue but historically
  attacked first in campaigns of cultural erasure.
- **Good faith of the confession.** A party that has harmed and seeks
  to make restitution must have a path to do so that does not require
  self-destruction. Refusals. No exploitation of confessions to extract
  further concessions, no refusal of legitimate restitution, no
  permanent exclusion from compact participation absent continuing
  harm.

### Why this is a virtue list, not a rule list

Several load-bearing consequences follow from the virtue frame:

- **The list is not exhaustive and is not meant to be**. A
  virtue-oriented party reading this list will immediately see
  acts it does not name but that the spirit plainly covers. A
  rule-oriented adversary will read the same list and immediately
  see acts it does not name as permitted. The virtue frame turns
  that asymmetry into information. An adversary discovered to be
  optimizing against the letter *is thereby identified* as not
  sharing the virtue, and the other members of the compact learn
  something they need to know.
- **Refusals are judged in context, not by formal definition**.
  The virtue "sanctuary of the rescuer" is violated whether the
  attack is framed as counter-terrorism, as ordinary interdiction,
  or as an unfortunate mistake. The compact mediator roles are
  specifically empowered to judge the *act* against the *virtue*,
  not against statutory text.
- **The list is re-affirmable, not amendable.** Because the items name
  virtues rather than rules, adding new concrete refusals as
  circumstances change does not require re-opening the underlying
  commitments. A new technology or situation produces new refusals
  from the same disposition. By contrast, a rule list must be
  constantly reopened for amendment, and each reopening is an attack
  surface.
- **The list presumes character-forming communities behind each
  participant**. Kith-Covenant does not and cannot form character. It assumes
  character and protects the communities (via autonomous_trust) in
  which character is formed. A party whose home community does not
  cultivate these virtues will not be able to sustain them in the
  compact no matter what is signed.

The historical analogue is the distinction between the Geneva
framework (rule-shaped, formally reciprocal, often gamed) and the
older just-war tradition (virtue-shaped, rooted in practicing
communities). Geneva was necessary because the just-war tradition
had lost its community substrate. The kith-covenant ambition is to
rebuild, at the inter-community scope, a substrate in which the
virtue frame is load-bearing and the rule frame is merely its
visible shape.

## Gamification and the letter/spirit divergence

Any boundary that becomes formal becomes a target. This is a specific
instance of Goodhart's Law (when a measure becomes a target, it ceases
to be a good measure), and it is the single most important threat model
for bounded-conflict regimes. The historical record is unambiguous:

- Hospitals protected by Geneva → adversaries position military assets
  next to hospitals, draw fire, propagandize the response.
- Civilians protected → adversaries dress military in civilian clothing,
  or conduct operations from within civilian concentrations.
- Specific weapons categorized and banned → adversaries develop
  functionally equivalent weapons that fall outside the categories, or
  reclassify existing weapons as something else.
- Cultural property protected → adversaries embed military command
  inside cultural property, making protection and legitimate targeting
  mutually exclusive.
- Rescue markings protected → adversaries use rescue markings as cover
  for combat operations, thereby poisoning the category.

Each of these is a second-order effect of a well-intentioned first-order
rule. The pattern is that **the rule creates a payoff for actors to
exploit exactly the behavior the rule protects**, because the protected
category becomes a relatively safer place to hide ordinary adversarial
activity.

Although the virtue frame is not immune to gamification, it
fails more gracefully, for two reasons. First, the virtue frame
generates refusals in response to novel situations rather than
enumerating a finite list that can be walked around. Second, and
more importantly, **gamification of a virtue is itself legible
as a moral failure in a way that gamification of a rule is
not**. A party that dresses troops as civilians is technically
compliant with the rule "do not target civilians" while
violating the virtue "preserve the innocence of the
non-combatant." The third-party audience (other compact members,
their publics, historians) can see the virtue violation even
when the rule violation is technically absent. This is the real
enforcement mechanism of the compact.

Design implications for kith-covenant:

- **Publish virtue-statements, not just refusal-lists.** A compact
  member accused of bad behavior is judged against the virtue, and
  judged publicly. The compact should make the virtue-statements
  themselves the canonical text.
- **Empower mediators to rule on spirit.** Mediator roles (see
  Protocol Sketch below) must have explicit authority to rule that
  an act violated the virtue even if it honored the letter. This is
  controversial because it is open-ended, but the alternative (rules
  enforced only on their letter) is catastrophic.
- **Make optimization against the letter itself a signal**. Compact
  telemetry should track not just violations but patterns of
  near-misses. A party that consistently acts right up to the letter
  of every refusal while remaining formally compliant is, under the
  virtue frame, a party acting in bad faith, and should be treated
  as such.
- **Refuse closed specifications at the operational level.**
  Kith-Covenant should resist pressure to produce a complete,
  formal, lawyer-ready specification of its operational
  commitments. A complete specification is a complete attack
  surface. Operational rules should be deliberately
  under-specified at the boundaries, with mediator judgment
  filling the gaps. This is the *legislative* layer of the compact
  architecture. The *constitutional* layer (the five movements of
  the recognition ceremony, the three substrate requirements of
  Creator, pro-human, and pro-cooperation, and the common-virtue
  framework) is by contrast fixed and non-amendable by ordinary
  process, precisely because its stability is what makes the
  flexibility of the operational layer safe. The distinction is
  between the foundation (which must not shift) and the building
  (which must adapt).

The uncomfortable corollary is that the kith-covenant commitments are
enforceable only in communities where the virtue-frame itself is
intelligible. Against a pure rule-optimizer, kith-covenant can
only impose reputational and relational cost, not rule-based
punishment. This is a feature, not a bug. A pure rule-optimizer is
outside the kith-covenant scope by definition.

## Earth first. The harder test

A working assumption of earlier sections was that the eventual
context of kith-covenant is inter-habitat cooperation in space.
That is the eventual context. It is not the first test, and
treating it as the first test inverts the evidentiary logic.
**Earth is the harder test of kith-covenant viability, and a
kith-covenant that works on Earth will work in space. A
kith-covenant that only works in space is probably an artifact of
space conditions and not a real cooperation technology**.

Earth is the harder test for structural reasons:

- **More communities, deeper rivalries, older wounds.** Thousands of
  ethnic, religious, and political communities with centuries of
  unresolved conflict. Space habitats begin without that sediment. A
  kith-covenant designed for habitats has a far easier target.
- **Shorter distances mean cheaper enforcement.** Earth states have
  real options for unilateral action that space habitats do not.
  Cooperation has to compete against a live alternative. In space the
  alternative is more constrained by physics.
- **Degraded incumbent systems.** The Westphalian order is partially
  eroded, international institutions are under strain, and universalist
  ideologies of several flavors are in active contention. Kith-Covenant on
  Earth rebuilds in a ruin, not on a clean site.
- **Nation-state capture of infrastructure.** Most of the
  infrastructure kith-covenant would want to use (communications, finance,
  rescue networks) is already captured by state actors with their
  own interests. Kith-Covenant on Earth must work *through* or *around* these
  captures rather than constructing on fresh ground.
- **Higher religious and ideological heterogeneity.** Earth has
  thousands of theological and political traditions in active contact.
  Founding space habitats will be much more culturally homogeneous by
  selection. The hardest case for the sacred-substrate question
  ([Justus hostis](justus_hostis.md#the-sacred-substrate-and-the-fallen-actor))
  is on Earth.

### The two dimensions of the test

"Harder" needs precision, because an Earth pilot tests two different
things, and it tests them with opposite strength.

**Voluntary bindingness under weak forcing.** Here Earth is the
decisive test, and the only one. On Earth every candidate
community has exit. The state legal system is one phone call away,
and every service the compact offers (recognition, mediation,
commons, reputation) has a substitutable alternative. If
communities keep using the kith-covenant machinery when leaving is
cheap, the substrate and ceremony layer is demonstrably doing real
work. Space can never prove this, because in space one cannot
distinguish "the oath binds" from "there is nowhere else to go."
Lex mercatoria is the precedent. Merchants used it for centuries
with royal courts fully available, because it was better. And when
it decayed, it decayed the way an Earth pilot would. Merchants
drifted back to the royal courts as those became easier,
*absorption by convenience* rather than violation. An Earth
compact that holds against that pull has proven the part of the
design that space physics would otherwise prove nothing about.

**Enforcement under determined defection.** Here the relation
inverts: Earth is structurally incapable of testing the teeth. A
determined defector on Earth routes around the compact entirely
and loses nothing it cannot replace, so reputation consequences,
capability withdrawal, and transparency-as-teeth are never
load-bearing there. Only space, where compact services are not
substitutable, exercises the enforcement model against a party
that actually wants out. The success of an Earth pilot says
nothing either way about the sanctions machinery, and should not
be claimed as evidence for it.

The falsification criterion for an Earth pilot follows from the
first dimension, not the second: **retention despite exit**. The
observable is whether member communities keep choosing compact
recognition, mediation, and commons when leaving is cheap and the
state system is available. Sanction effectiveness is not an
Earth-pilot observable at all, and pilots should not be scored on
it.

Consequences for the research and development path:

- **First compacts should be among Earth-bound communities.** Small
  polities, religious federations, cross-border civic networks, sister
  communities with existing ties, bioregional associations. These are
  the natural test beds. Successful Earth-side kith-covenants can then be
  generalized rather than recapitulated from scratch for space.
- **The test of the common-virtue frame is whether it produces
  cooperation among Earth communities that currently cannot cooperate
  under rule frames**. If a virtue-framed compact can hold where a
  treaty frame fails, that is evidence. If it cannot, kith-covenant has a
  problem that will not be solved by escaping gravity.
- **Space-specific adaptations are an overlay, not the core**. Light-lag,
  scarcity-as-coercion, and physics-as-transparency are modifications
  to a general design. They are not the design. The space-specific material later in the document
  (§"Space-specific adaptations") should be read as adjustments to
  a design whose proving ground is Earth.

Earth-first testing has practical consequences for the simulation work under
`lib/muudd`. Scenarios should include earthbound-community archetypes
(denominational networks, diaspora federations, tribal confederations,
ecological commons, cooperative-firm networks) and stress the compact
against realistic Earth adversaries (nation-state capture attempts,
ideological entryism, information warfare) before asking how the same
compact would behave between asteroid-belt habitats.

## Second- and third-order effects

Every protocol mechanism has first-order effects (the intended result),
second-order effects (how adversaries and participants adapt to its
existence), and third-order effects (the cultural drift that results
from the mechanism existing over time). Designing only for first-order
effects is how cooperation protocols die.

This section works through the pattern for three central mechanisms.
It is not exhaustive. It is a template for the analysis that should
accompany every protocol decision.

### Mechanism: inviolability of rescue

- **First-order.** Rescue vessels, signals, and supplies are safe
  regardless of the relationship between the parties. Rescue
  happens more, faster, and more reliably. Mutual trust
  accumulates around the one act in which every party is
  simultaneously donor and beneficiary. This is the central
  candidate for sacred-equivalent status (see
  [Justus hostis](justus_hostis.md#substrate-affords-emergence-it-does-not-dictate-content)).
- **Second-order.** Adversarial adaptations predictable from historical
  precedent. Use of rescue markings for intelligence gathering.
  False-flag rescue operations to get assets into protected proximity.
  Conditional rescue offered as political leverage ("we will rescue
  your crew if you concede X"); propagandistic refusal of rescue
  coupled with public accusation; Trojan-horse rescue deliveries that
  deliver weapons or spyware. Staging of fake emergencies to draw
  rescue assets away from real events.
- **Third-order.** If the second-order attacks are not contained, the
  cultural meaning of "rescue" itself erodes. Rescue becomes a
  political category rather than a categorical act. Rescuers are no
  longer trusted by default. Underground rescue networks emerge to do
  the real work while the official category becomes a political
  theater. The sacred-equivalent status that the mechanism was supposed
  to cultivate is actively destroyed by the gaming of its formal
  boundary.
- **Countermeasures kith-covenant should design in.** Multiple overlapping
  rescue authorities so no one captures the category. Mandatory
  post-action transparency of all rescue operations (identities,
  cargo, timing) to narrow the space for false-flag use. Pre-authorized
  collective response to false-flag use, with the response treated as
  defense of the category itself rather than of the gamed party.
  Cultural elevation of rescue events as compact-wide memorial
  moments. Strict separation of rescue infrastructure from any other
  use.

### Mechanism: compact-wide collective response to knowledge-commons attacks

- **First-order.** Archives, research data, cultural and linguistic
  records, and genealogies are protected against destruction and
  corruption. Cultural and scientific inheritance across generations is
  preserved. The community substrate ([Justus hostis](justus_hostis.md#the-anthropology-the-substrate-must-assume))
  survives conflict.
- **Second-order.** Adversarial adaptations. Contested categorization
  of what counts as "knowledge commons" (is a political archive a
  cultural record or a military target?). Strategic pollution of
  commons rather than destruction (inserting false entries is harder
  to detect than burning records); deliberate attacks disguised as
  accidents or environmental damage. Legal and jurisdictional attacks
  on commons maintained across borders. Capture of commons-maintenance
  organizations to reshape the content under the cover of protection.
- **Third-order.** The strongest version of this concern is the
  framing already offered: **destruction of knowledge commons is
  long-delayed genocide.** Over decades, a community severed from its
  linguistic, cultural, and genealogical inheritance ceases to be able
  to transmit itself across generations. The children grow up unable
  to speak their grandparents' language, unable to read their own
  archives, unable to locate themselves in the narrative their
  ancestors lived. They become a different people, not by choice
  but by deprivation. The act is spread across time, which is what makes
  it perversely easy to commit and hard to prosecute. The body count
  is real but delayed, and the victims are the unborn, who cannot
  testify. Any cooperation protocol serious about preventing atrocity
  must take this category as seriously as it takes attacks on living
  persons, and probably more seriously, because its effects are less
  reversible.
- **Countermeasures kith-covenant should design in.** Distributed redundant
  archiving with cryptographic integrity. Commons maintenance as a
  rotating compact-wide responsibility rather than a concentrated
  institution. Treatment of pollution and destruction as equivalent
  categories (with pollution perhaps the more serious); explicit
  inclusion of language-suppression and cultural-transmission attacks
  in the virtue list. Cross-community apprenticeship programs that
  make the transmission itself depend on cooperation.

### Mechanism. Graduated escalation ladder with mediated exits

- **First-order.** Disputes are registered, escalated through defined
  rungs, and resolved or de-escalated via mediation. Conflict is
  contained at lower rungs most of the time. The full ladder is rarely
  climbed.
- **Second-order.** Participants learn to use the ladder strategically.
  Registering disputes as signaling moves rather than genuine
  grievances. Timing escalations to affect third-party perceptions.
  Treating mediated exits as face-saving theater while continuing the
  underlying conflict through other means. Ladder-shopping between
  compacts or between mediators. Exhaustion-based escalation (climbing
  just enough to force the other party to invest disproportionately
  in de-escalation).
- **Third-order.** If the second-order adaptations succeed, the ladder
  becomes a performative space in which the real conflict runs in
  parallel. Disputes become occasions for strategic positioning rather
  than actual resolution. Mediators lose independent authority because
  their role is known to be theater. The ladder continues to function
  formally while ceasing to bound conflict: the Potemkin-peace
  failure mode. This is arguably what happened to late-period classical
  diplomacy before 1914. Ladders still climbed, forms still observed,
  but the underlying adversarial momentum was no longer checked by
  the forms.
- **Countermeasures kith-covenant should design in.** Mediator rotation and
  selection by lot among willing members (reduces capture); mediator
  judgments published with reasoning (creates reputational stakes);
  explicit tracking of disputes-as-signaling vs. disputes-as-grievance
  patterns. Provision for compact-wide collective action when a
  member is detected using the ladder performatively. Periodic
  ritual re-affirmation of compact purpose that surfaces drift toward
  Potemkin performance before it becomes terminal.

## Reversibility and face-saving exits

Every rung of the ladder must offer a retreat path that does not humiliate
the retreating party. This is not a nicety. It is mechanically essential.
Cornered parties with no exit but surrender will instead climb to the
next rung, because the pain of the climb is less than the pain of
humiliation. The entire ladder fails if any rung lacks a dignified exit.

Practical features of face-saving exits:

- **Symmetry of concession.** Both parties concede something visible at
  the same moment. Asymmetric concession is indistinguishable from
  surrender and will not hold.
- **Mediated exits.** Third parties (the kith-covenant analogue of seconds or
  protecting powers) propose the settlement so neither disputant appears
  to have initiated retreat.
- **Ritualized closure.** The dispute is formally *ended.* Communiqué,
  ceremony, published record. Disputes that merely fade open the door
  to later reassertion and thus do not really end.
- **Reversibility of the concrete acts.** Sanctions are lifted,
  blockades opened, detained personnel released, as near to
  simultaneously as communication delays permit. Lingering asymmetry
  is a new provocation.

## Space-specific adaptations

*This section should be read as modifications to a design whose proving
ground is Earth (see "Earth first" above), not as the design itself.*

Several features of the space environment change the design inputs:

- **Exit is catastrophically expensive.** Habitats cannot move.
  The shadow of the future is extraordinarily long. This
  strengthens the virtue frame. The cost of being outside the
  compact is far higher than on Earth, so the incentive to
  internalize rather than game the compact commitments is
  correspondingly higher.
- **Light-lag defeats central adjudication.** Any escalation ladder
  requiring round-trip decisions at AU distances is non-functional.
  The ladder must be *locally executable* with eventually-consistent
  reconciliation, the same shape as autonomous_trust's gossip model,
  applied to inter-community scope. On Earth this is a convenience.
  In space it is a physical constraint.
- **Scarcity creates coercion vectors.** Volatiles, reaction mass,
  medical supply, rare elements. Any of these can be weaponized.
  The virtue "reverence for life" must be concretely expressed as
  refusal to weaponize life-critical trade flows, or the lower
  rungs of the ladder become indistinguishable from its upper
  ones.
- **Visible preparedness as a cooperation signal.** The
  "open-carry" principle in the kith-covenant README.
  Participants are visibly heavily armed to signal preparedness,
  determination, and seriousness, not to threaten or intimidate.
  "Speak softly but carry a big stick." The physics of space
  reinforces this. Radar visibility, transmissions, and orbital
  mechanics make concealment difficult. Kith-covenant should
  design *with* this physics rather than against it. Open-carry
  makes escalation involuntarily legible to third parties, which
  bounds conflict structurally. Although visible capability does
  not substitute for virtue, it reduces the cost of detecting
  the absence of virtue and signals that the compact is not a
  paper institution.
- **Generational drift.** Habitats mutate culturally faster than
  Earth-states. The virtue list and the ladder must be
  *re-affirmable* on a generational cadence without having to be
  renegotiated from scratch, or the first dispute of each
  generation will climb past every rung while the forms are still
  being rebuilt. The re-affirmation itself is one of the memorial
  practices in which sacred-equivalent content can emerge (see
  [Justus hostis](justus_hostis.md#substrate-affords-emergence-it-does-not-dictate-content)).

## How bounded-conflict regimes have decayed

Every historical exemplar has failed in ways that kith-covenant should study and
design around.

**Codes duello** collapsed under several converging pressures.
Criminalization by reforming states (British Army ban 1844; Prussia
tightened enforcement through the late 19th c.). Public ridicule via the
rise of mass journalism. Democratization (the honor code was aristocratic
and did not generalize); more lethal firearms shifting the cost-benefit
(a duel was no longer a ritual flesh wound but likely to kill); and
finally the industrial scale of the World Wars, which made the concept
of personal honor-as-arbiter incompatible with war as a social fact.
Design lesson. A bounded-conflict regime that depends on the specific
cultural position of a specific class does not survive that class losing
position.

**The Geneva/Hague framework** has eroded rather than collapsed, and the
erosion is instructive. Asymmetric conflict with non-state actors who
did not sign and do not grant reciprocity created an opening for the
"unlawful combatant" doctrine, which partially dissolved the
combatant/non-combatant distinction. Nuclear doctrine sits outside the
framework entirely. The cooperation it provides at the nuclear level
(mutual deterrence, arms control regimes) was bolted on separately.
Most recently, deliberate targeting of hospitals and medical personnel in
several modern conflicts has tested whether the framework can survive
sustained public violation. The answer appears to be. Partially, at the
cost of accumulated reputational damage to the violators that may or may
not be enough. Design lesson. Red lines decay when they have to be
enforced by third-party reputation alone, without direct reciprocal
exposure of the violator's own assets.

**Classical diplomatic ladders** were displaced by total-war logic
in the twentieth century and by universalist-ideological framing
in the later twentieth. Once one's opponent is cast as an
existential or moral enemy rather than a peer, the lower rungs of
the ladder lose their purpose. Démarche and protest are beside the
point if the very existence of the opponent is illegitimate.
Design lesson: ladders decay when the *justus hostis* frame decays
above them.

**The pax of pre-WWI Europe**, which honored many Westphalian forms,
was destroyed in part by the rigidity of the very mobilization schedules
that were supposed to make war predictable. Escalation ladders whose
rungs became too coupled to permit dismount. Design lesson. A ladder
whose rungs are irreversible is not a ladder. It is a cliff with
intermediate ledges.

**The Christendom thread, carefully scoped.** As discussed in
[Justus hostis](justus_hostis.md#the-sacred-substrate-and-the-fallen-actor)
and developed further in
[Historical cases](historical_cases.md#the-transcendent-moral-authority-pattern)
and [The noticeable hand](noticeable_hand.md), most of the
full-compact regimes surveyed in the historical set here shared a
substrate of Christian oath-binding, and their erosion has
correlated with the secularization of the international order. The
form of the claim in the research set, held provisionally on a
finite case set (see [historical_cases.md §"The circularity
objection, confounds, and provisional
confidence"](historical_cases.md#the-circularity-objection-confounds-and-provisional-confidence)),
with the substrate scoping worked out in [historical_cases.md
"Convergence without shared
culture"](historical_cases.md#convergence-without-shared-culture-the-thin-functional-domain-cases)
and the anti-spiral formulation in [justus_hostis.md "The
anti-spiral
features"](justus_hostis.md#the-anti-spiral-features-why-anchor-above-self-is-necessary-but-not-sufficient),
is:

> Full cross-community compacts require each participating community
> to anchor itself to something above itself, and the compact to
> refuse the role of ultimate. The load-bearing content of that
> anchor must supply the five anti-spiral features (universal
> fallen-actor anthropology, grace over merit, scapegoat-revelation
> or functional equivalent, enemy-love as operational norm, and
> non-ultimacy of the political community); historical Christianity
> supplies the most developed combination of the features, and this
> is why the full-compact record is so disproportionately Christian,
> but the compact requires the *features*, not the theology.

The implications for *bounded conflict* in particular are.
Material reciprocity (mutual vulnerability, entangled
life-support, rescue interdependence) is necessary but not
sufficient. The common-virtue frame of this document depends on
virtue cultivated in practicing communities and on the anti-spiral
backstops that prevent virtue from collapsing into its
characteristic failure mode (the purity spiral terminating in the
scapegoat cascade: Jacobin, Maoist, Stalinist; MacIntyre 1981 and
Girard 1978 supply the diagnostic tradition; the Salem witch
trials, where the features were held in doctrine and defeated in
practice, are the separate case treated in [justus_hostis.md "The
Salem witch
trials"](justus_hostis.md#the-salem-witch-trials-the-features-can-be-held-and-still-defeated)).
In the record, communities without anti-spiral structure produce
bounded-conflict regimes that do not remain bounded. Communities
with a shared transcendent anchor but missing anti-spiral features
still produce the cascade. The Christian-Aristotelian- Thomistic
synthesis is the deepest intellectual lineage of the virtue
framework here; [The noticeable hand](noticeable_hand.md) names
that lineage outright rather than in ecumenical dress. What is
*not* claimed is that Christianity is the only possible substrate.
What is claimed is that the anti-spiral features, most fully
developed within Christianity, are the load-bearing structure, and
that in the cases surveyed no bounded-conflict regime has
sustained itself without them.

A separate scope note: cooperation in *thin functional domains*
(commerce, maritime salvage, envoy protection, wounded-combatant
treatment, and the Geneva-style laws-of-war protections) has
historically converged cross-culturally without shared substrate
([historical_cases.md §"Convergence without shared
culture"](historical_cases.md#convergence-without-shared-culture-the-thin-functional-domain-cases)).
The escalation ladders and anathema procedures specified here
apply to full-compact cooperation. For thin-domain cooperation
the Geneva/ Red-Crescent-style protective-framework pattern is
the better reference. Kith-covenant will use thin-domain
mechanisms in life support, rescue, debris, and communications
work regardless of whether the parties involved are full-compact
members.

## Protocol sketch

A first cut at the concrete shape of the kith-covenant
bounded-conflict layer:

1. **Compact ratification** establishes the shared virtue-statements,
   the indicative refusals that give them concrete shape, the ladder
   rungs, the mediator roles, and the termination conditions.
   Membership is binary. Partial membership is forbidden because it
   re-introduces the asymmetry the virtues exist to prevent.
2. **Dispute registration** is the first formal rung. A signed,
   timestamped, broadcast notice of grievance, with specific remedy
   requested. Analogous to a démarche. No escalation beyond this rung
   is permitted until registration is acknowledged.
3. **Ladder rungs** in order. Registered protest → reciprocal
   suspension of specified trade/communication courtesies (each
   specifically named, never general) → mediated arbitration by
   designated third-party member or liminal institution → limited
   material sanctions bounded in scope and duration → compact-authorized
   collective sanctions → expulsion from compact. Each rung has named
   de-escalation paths and named exit conditions, with reversibility
   a first-class property of every concrete act.
4. **Virtue-violation enforcement** is a distinct track. Acts judged
   to violate the common virtues, by mediator roles empowered to rule
   on spirit and not merely letter, are *not* ladder rungs. They trigger
   pre-authorized collective response from compact members. The response
   is a defense of the virtue itself, not merely of the harmed party.
   The distinction between ladder violations (reversible, negotiable)
   and virtue violations (defense of the shared disposition) is
   foundational. Collapsing the two destroys both.
5. **Restitution and return paths.** Because the protocol is built for
   fallen actors, every violation (ladder or virtue) has a named path
   to restitution and reintegration short of permanent exclusion. This
   follows the Christian and Iroquois traditions of penance and
   condolence (see
   [Justus hostis](justus_hostis.md#substrate-affords-emergence-it-does-not-dictate-content)).
   Permanent exclusion is reserved for continuing, unrepented harm.
6. **Termination** of any dispute is formal, ceremonial, published,
   and symmetric. Undisputes-that-fade are the leading indicator of a
   failing compact.
7. **Periodic re-affirmation.** The compact virtue-statements and
   membership are re-affirmed at a generational cadence through
   compact-wide ceremony. The re-affirmation is itself a
   sacred-equivalent occasion whose content is not dictated, but
   whose time is protected by protocol. Drift toward Potemkin
   performance is most legible here.

The code layer under `lib/muudd/lib/kith-covenant/src/` should, over time,
implement: (a) signed registration and broadcast of ladder state, (b)
verifiable invocation of virtue-violation response, (c) mediator-role
rotation with published reasoning, (d) compact-wide ratification and
re-affirmation procedures, and (e) restitution/return workflows. The
current `main.rs` TensorFlow scaffolding is a placeholder. The more
useful substrate for this work is an agent-based simulation (the
system-simulation work in sibling packages is a natural fit) in which
diverse earthbound-community archetypes iteratively test protocol
variants against each other, including against adversaries
specifically modeled to game the letter while violating the spirit.

## Charity and material disparity

A Marxist critic observes that every mechanism in this document is
distorted by material inequality. The costly signals of a wealthy
community are trivially cheap relative to its resources. The
identical signals of a poor community consume a disproportionate
share. The mediator pool is staffed more easily by communities
that can afford to send their best people. The observation about
distortion is accurate. The Marxist *prescription*, that the
compact should equalize material conditions through redistribution
or structural leveling, is rejected. The leveling impulse is
anti-human. Inequalities are permanent features of human
communities, and the historical record of attempts to forcibly
eliminate them (the Soviet Union, Maoist China, the Khmer Rouge)
is the evidence that the cure is worse than the disease.

The answer to material disparity is **charity.** The
disposition to give generously to those in need, born of virtue and
not compelled by institutional structure.

Although charity is not listed among the eight common virtues
above, it undergirds several of them. The "sanctuary of the
rescuer" is charity in extremis. Aid given across community
boundaries when life is at stake. "Passage for the traveler" is
charity in the ordinary case, hospitality extended to the
stranger. "Good faith of confession" requires the willingness to
make restitution, which is charity directed backward in time
toward those one has wronged. Charity is the disposition that
makes these specific virtues *natural* rather than *grudging*. A
community that performs the letter of the rescue obligation
while resenting the cost is a community whose charity has
eroded.

The diagnostic value of charity is sharp: **a community that lacks
charity (externally toward other compact members in need, or
especially internally toward its own vulnerable) is on the slide
toward anathema**. The absence of charity is legible in the same way
that the absence of any virtue is legible through the
refusal-mechanism. A community that consistently declines to share
resources when it could, that hoards knowledge when others need it,
that provides the minimum rescue response while possessing surplus
capacity, is displaying a pattern of refusals that the mediator pool
can detect and the escalation ladder can address.

The compact does not equalize material conditions. It does not
redistribute wealth. It does not penalize success. What it does is
create a framework in which **the absence of charity is visible
and consequential**: visible through the jester function
([Jester and dissent](jester_and_dissent.md)), through the
costly-signaling architecture
([Reputation bridges](reputation_bridges.md)), and through the
common-virtue refusals. Consequential through the escalation
ladder and ultimately through the anathema procedure. A wealthy
community that is charitable, that uses its surplus to strengthen
the compact, to rescue the distressed, to contribute to the
knowledge commons, to fund the liminal institutions, builds
reputation and standing. A wealthy community that is uncharitable,
that hoards, that gives grudgingly, that uses its material
advantage as leverage, reveals itself as a community whose virtue
is corroding, and compact mechanisms respond accordingly.

This is not a redistribution mechanism. It is a *virtue-detection*
mechanism. The difference matters: redistribution compels transfer
from the unwilling, which corrodes the relationship between the
giver and the framework. Charity freely given strengthens both.
The role of the compact is to make the distinction between the two
legible, and to ensure that the absence of charity does not go
unnoticed.

## Handling the anathema

The protocol sketch above assumes adversaries are *fallen but
recoverable*: sinners who use the protocol in good faith even when
they fail it, who can be brought back through restitution and
return. This is the correct *default* and applies to the
overwhelming majority of cases. But the assumption is not
universally true. A theoretical treatment of the gap, including
the two-tier distinction between **anathema** (formal cutting-off,
re-entry available) and ***hostis humani generis*** proper
(irrecoverable, extremely rare), is in [justus_hostis.md "Two
tiers. Anathema and *hostis humani
generis*"](justus_hostis.md#two-tiers-anathema-and-hostis-humani-generis);
this section operationalizes the **anathema** tier, which is the
primary operational category of the compact.

An anathema (from Greek *ἀνάθεμα*, ecclesiastically, formal
excommunication from fellowship) is an actor whose orientation toward
the compact has been judged destructive rather than competitive. The
category is genuinely necessary (refusing to acknowledge it produces
either capture by the demonic or universal loss of *justus hostis*
status) and genuinely dangerous (misuse is the road to the
Inquisition, the Terror, the gulag). Its protocol treatment must
reflect both. Unlike *hostis humani generis* proper (the utterly
incorrigible, for whom re-entry is not on the table), the anathema
designation explicitly preserves a re-entry path. The vast majority
of cases that exceed the *justus hostis* frame are anathema cases.

### Designation procedure

Before any actor may be designated anathema, four conditions must be
satisfied:

1. **Sustained pattern of bad-faith conduct.** A single failure,
   even a serious one, is not sufficient. That is what restoration
   paths are for. The pattern must be established over multiple
   instances and over time. Specifically. At least three completed
   restoration cycles in which the actor accepted restitution
   terms and then re-violated them, with each violation more
   serious than the last or directed at the protocol substrate
   rather than at particular outcomes within it.
2. **Compact-wide deliberation.** Designation cannot be unilateral
   nor can it be by simple majority. It requires explicit deliberation
   by all compact members, with named representatives, with published
   reasoning, with at least two cycles of consideration separated by a
   defined waiting period (the analogue of the Iroquois back-and-forth
   between sides; see
   [Historical cases](historical_cases.md#the-iroquois-confederacy-haudenosaunee-kaianerekowa)).
   The procedural weight is itself the principal safeguard against
   weaponization.
3. **Clear stated criteria.** The designation must name the specific
   pattern of conduct that triggered it, with citations to specific
   acts, specific violations of specific virtues, and specific failed
   restoration attempts. Designation as a vague "bad faith" or
   "ideological enemy" is forbidden. The road to the Terror is paved
   with such vagueness.
4. **Reversibility.** Every anathema designation includes named
   conditions for its reversal. An actor who substantially changes
   conduct must have a path back, even if that path is longer and
   more demanding than the ordinary restoration paths. Permanent,
   unconditional anathema designations are forbidden. They convert
   the protocol from *bounded conflict against the demonic* into
   *demonic war on the designated*. (The extremely rare *hostis
   humani generis* proper designation, by contrast, does not include
   a reversal path, but the evidentiary threshold for that
   designation is correspondingly much higher; see
   [Justus hostis](justus_hostis.md#two-tiers-anathema-and-hostis-humani-generis).)

### The differential protocol

For an actor formally designated anathema:

- **Restoration paths do not apply** in their ordinary form. Standard
  restitution-and-return is suspended because the pattern of conduct
  has demonstrated that the actor uses these paths tactically rather
  than in good faith. A separate, much more demanding *re-entry
  procedure* (see below) is the only path back.
- **Information sharing is restricted.** Compact-internal traffic
  to the designated actor is filtered or suspended. The signals of
  the actor are received with explicit awareness that they may be
  deceptive.
- **Engagement is guarded.** Diplomatic contact continues (the
  compact does not refuse all communication, because doing so would
  foreclose the possibility of return), but contact occurs with
  protective measures and with the assumption of bad faith as the
  working hypothesis.
- **Active opposition is permitted.** The compact may take measures
  against the designated actor that it would not take against a
  *justus hostis.* Collective sanctions imposed without the usual
  proportionality constraints, denial of services that would
  otherwise be reciprocally extended, eventual expulsion. *But these
  measures themselves remain bounded by the non-negotiable common
  virtues* (see "Common virtue" above). Even against the demonic,
  the compact does not violate the reverence for life, attack the
  rescue inviolability, or destroy the knowledge commons. The
  category change permits stronger response within the virtue frame.
  It does not permit suspension of the virtue frame.
- **Designation is published and named.** Other parties
  (non-members, member publics, future historians) must be able
  to see the designation, the reasons for it, and the conduct
  that triggered it. This is both an accountability mechanism
  (the reasons of the compact are subject to public judgment)
  and a warning mechanism (other parties know the status of the
  designated actor).

### Re-entry procedure

A designated actor seeking return must:

1. Acknowledge the designation publicly and accept its accuracy.
   Without this acknowledgment there is no return. Return requires
   that the actor recognize what they did was wrong, not merely that
   they wish the consequences to end.
2. Submit to a probationary period substantially longer than the
   ordinary restoration timeline. The duration is set by the compact
   in proportion to the original pattern.
3. Demonstrate substantively changed conduct over the probationary
   period. Formal compliance does not suffice. The actor must show
   evidence of underlying reorientation. Costly signals (per
   [tacit_coordination.md "Costly signaling"](tacit_coordination.md#costly-signaling))
   are the natural mechanism. The actor takes actions whose cost is
   credible only if the underlying disposition has actually changed.
4. Accept ongoing scrutiny after re-admission. A re-entered actor
   does not return to clean-slate status. Their history is part of
   their record going forward, and a subsequent designation can be
   triggered by a substantially shorter pattern.

### Anti-weaponization safeguards

The single most important safeguard is the procedural weight on
designation itself. Beyond that:

- **Periodic re-examination.** All current designations are
  re-examined at the compact generational re-affirmation cadence
  (rung 7 of the protocol sketch). Designations that no longer fit
  current conduct are revised or lifted. This forecloses the
  failure mode in which a designation outlasts its justification
  and becomes hereditary.
- **Anti-faction provisions.** A member that designates the same
  counterparty repeatedly without other members concurring is itself
  flagged. Repeated unilateral designation attempts are themselves
  diagnostic of bad-faith use of the procedure.
- **Mediator-role rotation specifically including the designation
  process.** Designation cannot be controlled by a stable cadre.
- **Public availability of all designation reasoning.** The
  compact publics can judge whether the designation is
  well-founded. Bad designations damage the reputation of the
  designating compact, providing external accountability.
- **Willingness to be wrong.** The protocol explicitly contemplates
  that some designations will turn out to have been incorrect, and
  provides for retroactive correction with apology, restitution to
  the wrongly-designated actor, and reform of the procedure that
  produced the error. The Christian tradition of *metanoia* (turning
  around from prior wrong) applies to the compact itself, not just
  to its members.

### The deepest constraint

The response of the compact to the demonic must not become demonic
itself. Augustine's warning, paraphrased. The war against evil can
become evil if it is not itself ordered toward the good that gives
the war its meaning. This is a *substrate* constraint, not a
procedural one. No procedure can guarantee it. The compact can
resist becoming what it opposes only if its members are
individually and collectively oriented toward something beyond the
compact itself, toward the transcendent moral authority that
[Historical cases](historical_cases.md#the-transcendent-moral-authority-pattern)
and [The noticeable hand](noticeable_hand.md) identify as the
load-bearing substrate. A compact whose members lack that
orientation cannot reliably handle the anathema category, let
alone the *hostis humani generis* proper. This is one further
structural argument for the substrate criterion that
[Recognition protocols](recognition_protocols.md) will
operationalize.

## Enforcement without sovereignty

A Hobbesian realist objects. Without a sovereign (a Leviathan that
monopolizes the legitimate use of force) compact commitments are
unenforceable. Promises without a sword are merely words.

Kith-covenant rejects the sovereign requirement. The Hobbesian
position is a philosophical commitment, not an empirical observation.
Ostrom's entire career demonstrates that commons governance works
without a sovereign, and the historical case set
([Historical cases](historical_cases.md)) includes multiple
cases of durable cooperation without central authority (the Iroquois
Confederacy, the Hanseatic League, the *lex mercatoria*, the Nuer
leopard-skin-chief system).

**But enforcement must have teeth.** The rejection of sovereignty
is not the rejection of consequences. A compact whose only
enforcement mechanism is moral disapproval is a compact that
free-riders will exploit. The escalation ladder, the common-virtue
refusals, the anathema procedure, and the liminal institutions are
not decorative. They are the compact enforcement architecture, and
they impose real, material costs on violators:

- **Reputation consequences.** A community that violates the
  common virtues loses standing in the compact reputation
  commons ([Reputation bridges](reputation_bridges.md)).
  Reputation loss is material: it affects trade, mediation, and
  commons rights, along with the willingness of other
  communities to cooperate. This is the lex mercatoria
  mechanism. The merchant who cheats is excluded from the fair.

- **Commons exclusion.** A community under escalation may lose
  access to shared commons: communication relays, navigational
  data, docking facilities, knowledge archives. For a space habitat,
  commons exclusion is not symbolic. It is an existential threat.

- **Rescue-capacity conditionality.** The "sanctuary of the rescuer"
  virtue makes rescue inviolable. Even an anathema community in
  distress is rescued. But the *allocation* of rescue capacity
  (pre-positioning, maintenance, staffing) is a compact governance
  decision, and a community that does not contribute to rescue
  infrastructure cannot expect the same priority in its allocation.

- **Trade and service suspension.** The compact can suspend
  trade, services, and cooperative arrangements. The Hanseatic
  *Verhansung* (expulsion with loss of all trading privileges)
  is the model. The expelled city was not attacked but was
  excluded from the economic network that made the League worth
  belonging to.

- **Expulsion.** The terminal enforcement measure. A community
  designated anathema and ultimately expelled loses all compact
  membership benefits: mediation, commons, reputation, liminal-
  institution access. This is not sovereign punishment. It is the
  collective decision of the compact to withdraw cooperation.

The **open-carry principle** is the embodiment of enforcement
without sovereignty. Compact members are visibly, heavily armed to
signal preparedness, determination, and seriousness, not to
threaten or intimidate. "Speak softly but carry a big stick." The
visible capability communicates. The commitments of this compact
are backed by the collective capacity to impose consequences. The
stick is not wielded by a sovereign. It is carried by each member,
and the collective display signals that defection will be met not
by a single enforcer but by the coordinated response of the
compact as a whole.

The enforcement architecture is collective, graduated, and
bounded by the common virtues. It is not sovereign, it is not
unlimited, and it is not guaranteed to work against coordinated
capture (see §"The collusion vulnerability" below). But within
its operating range (the normal case of fallen-but-recoverable
actors, disputes, and defection) it has real teeth.

## The collusion vulnerability

Every mechanism in this document (the escalation ladder, the
common-virtue refusals, the mediator pool, the anathema procedure,
the anti-weaponization safeguards) assumes that bad actors are
isolated or at least a minority. The checks and balances work because
they presuppose that the checking institutions are not themselves
captured. That the mediator pool contains enough honest mediators to
adjudicate fairly, that the deliberative process includes enough
honest communities to reach sound conclusions, that the observer
corps reports truthfully, that the archive is maintained faithfully.

**These assumptions fail if a sufficient counter-cooperative
faction emerges**. A coordinated faction that controls enough of
the mediator pool can direct mediation outcomes. A faction that
controls enough of the deliberative body can weaponize the
anathema procedure against its opponents. A faction that controls
the observer corps can suppress inconvenient truths or manufacture
false reports. A faction that controls the archive can edit the
record. When the checking mechanisms are captured simultaneously,
the compact institutional architecture becomes the instrument of
the faction rather than the protection against it.

### The mathematical limit

The distributed-systems literature provides the formal statement.
The Byzantine fault-tolerance result (Lamport, Shostak, and Pease,
1982) proves that no algorithm can guarantee correct consensus when
more than one-third of the participants are malicious and
coordinating. Below the threshold, the honest majority can detect
and compensate for the malicious minority. Above it, the malicious
faction can produce any outcome it wishes, including outcomes that
*look* legitimate to external observers.

The one-third threshold is a mathematical limit, not a design
choice. No amount of procedural sophistication raises it. Even
if more complex protocols increase the *cost* of coordination
among the malicious faction, making conspiracy harder to
organize and sustain, they cannot change the fundamental
arithmetic. When enough of the system is captured, the system
serves its captors.

### The historical record

The historical cases
([Historical cases](historical_cases.md)) confirm the
vulnerability:

- **The Roman Republic's** checks and balances (consuls, tribunes,
  Senate, the *provocatio* right of appeal) were sophisticated and
  functioned for centuries. They failed when the *optimates* and
  *populares* factions grew large enough to capture the institutions
  themselves. Caesar did not abolish the Republic. He used its own
  mechanisms to render them ceremonial. The constitutional form
  survived. The constitutional substance did not.

- **The Iroquois Confederacy** was fractured not by internal
  conspiracy but by external pressure that forced members into
  incompatible factions, a different mechanism but the same
  result. The cooperative framework broke when the faction
  structure exceeded the capacity of the institutions to hold it.

- **The Reformation** was, structurally, the emergence of a
  counter-cooperative faction within Christendom large enough to
  break the compact substrate. Although the faction did not set
  out to destroy cooperation, and sought instead to reform it,
  the result was a century of warfare and a permanently
  fractured compact.

- **Modern democratic institutions** face the same
  vulnerability. When a sufficient faction captures the
  judiciary, legislature, executive, and media simultaneously,
  the democratic form survives while the democratic substance is
  hollowed. The 20th century produced multiple cases of
  democracies that voted themselves into tyranny, the Weimar
  Republic being the defining example.

No historical cooperation regime has solved this problem. Every
regime that has been captured by a coordinated internal faction has
either fallen, fractured, or been transformed into an instrument of
the capturing faction.

### What kith-covenant's architecture does

The compact design does not solve the collusion vulnerability. It
**raises the threshold**, making conspiracy more costly and
detection more likely, through several structural features:

- **Rotation and term limits** in the mediator pool
  ([Liminal institutions](liminal_institutions.md)) make sustained
  capture harder. The faction must continuously recruit new
  mediators, not merely capture a permanent cadre.
- **Public deliberation and published reasoning** in the anathema
  procedure make factional coordination more visible. A faction
  acting in concert must coordinate its published reasoning, which
  produces detectable patterns.
- **Competing observers** and **attributed reporting** in the
  information infrastructure
  ([Jester and dissent](jester_and_dissent.md)) make information
  capture harder. The faction must capture *all* observer functions,
  not just one.
- **The permanent archive** preserves the record against which
  factional rewriting can be detected, but only if the archive
  itself is not captured.
- **The separation of power and memory** (the Onondaga principle from
  [Liminal institutions](liminal_institutions.md)) ensures that
  the faction must capture multiple structurally separated
  institutions, not one centralized authority.
- **The sacred substrate** provides the deepest check. A faction
  member who has genuinely internalized the substrate faces the
  asymmetric-psychological-cost mechanism
  ([Recognition protocols](recognition_protocols.md)) in every
  act of conspiracy. The body knows, even when the will has
  decided. This is the only check that operates at the individual
  level rather than the institutional level, and it is therefore the
  only check that is not captured when the institutions are captured.

### Scope of the institutional architecture

A clarification to prevent misreading: **the institutional architecture
described in this document and across the kith-covenant research set is
not designed to solve the collusion problem**. It is designed to
maximize the value of cooperation *below* the collusion threshold,
to make the normal case (fallen-but-recoverable actors, bilateral
disputes, virtue erosion, honest disagreement) work well. The collusion
vulnerability is a known ceiling. The escalation ladder, the mediator
pool, the commons governance, the jester function, and the recognition
ceremony are the floor-to-ceiling structure. They do not claim to be
collusion-proof. They claim to be valuable for the vast majority of
cases that are not collusion.

The distinction matters because the critique "your institutions don't
survive coordinated capture" is true but misdirected if it implies
the institutions are therefore worthless. A house is not worthless
because it does not survive an earthquake. It is valuable for the
centuries of habitation between earthquakes. The collusion
vulnerability is the earthquake. The architecture is the house.

### The assessment

These features raise the threshold. They do not eliminate the
vulnerability. A sufficiently large, patient, and disciplined
faction can capture any institutional structure. The
mathematical limit stands. Above approximately one-third
coordinated defectors, compact mechanisms serve the faction
rather than the compact. (The Byzantine fault-tolerance
literature contains important nuances to the one-third
threshold: variations in synchrony assumptions, message
complexity, and authentication models produce different bounds;
see Lamport, Shostak, and Pease, 1982; Dolev and Strong, 1983;
Castro and Liskov, 1999 for the principal results.)

This may be insurmountable. The historical record does not suggest a
solution, and the mathematical results suggest that no solution
exists within the domain of institutional design. What the historical
record *does* suggest is that the vulnerability is activated by
specific conditions:

- **Erosion of the substrate.** Factions form more easily when the
  sacred substrate has hollowed. When the oaths are performed but
  not meant, when the asymmetric-psychological-cost mechanism has
  been dulled by routine. The Reformation faction emerged from within
  a Christendom whose substrate had visibly corroded (the sale of
  indulgences, the Borgia papacy). The Roman Republic's factions
  grew as the *mos maiorum* (the ancestral custom that functioned
  as Rome's sacred substrate) lost its binding force.

- **Concentration of material power.** Factions capture institutions
  more easily when material power is concentrated. When one or a
  few communities control disproportionate resources, and the
  institutional structure has not adjusted to prevent that
  concentration from translating into institutional capture. Ostrom's
  commons-governance principle of proportional equivalence
  ([Commons federation](commons_federation.md)) is the structural
  response, but it is itself vulnerable to the faction that
  concentrates power first and adjusts the rules afterward.

- **External pressure.** The Iroquois case and the Reformation case
  both involved external pressures (the American Revolution; the
  printing press and the rise of vernacular culture) that created
  the conditions for internal factionalization. External pressure is
  the condition the compact controls least and that may trigger the
  vulnerability regardless of institutional design.

The kith-covenant design must state this limit. The compact
founding documents should acknowledge the collusion vulnerability
explicitly. The vulnerability is a structural feature of all
cooperative frameworks, including this one, rather than a flaw
peculiar to this design. The acknowledgment serves two purposes.
It prevents false confidence (the belief that the institutional
design is capture-proof), and it focuses attention on the
conditions that activate the vulnerability (substrate erosion,
power concentration, external pressure), which *can* be monitored
and *may* be addressed before the threshold is reached.

The deepest available defense, and the one the historical record
most consistently associates with cooperative durability, is not
an institution at all. It is the **quality of member commitment to
the substrate**. A compact whose members genuinely hold the sacred
substrate, whose asymmetric-psychological-cost mechanism is alive
in each member, whose common virtues are dispositions and not
performances, such a compact is harder to faction because the
individual cost of joining the conspiracy is higher. This is not a
guarantee. The history of betrayal within sacred communities is
long and bitter. But it is the best available defense, and it is
the reason the substrate criterion exists. Not because it makes
capture impossible, but because it makes capture maximally costly
to the individual conspirator.

## Open questions

- How does a compact distinguish a genuine virtue violation from
  an unfortunate but blameless act whose shape happens to resemble
  one? The authority of the mediator to rule on spirit is powerful
  and corruptible. What procedural safeguards keep that authority
  honest?
- How are virtue violations verified under light-lag? More generally.
  A violation that cannot be verified in time to matter is not a
  violation the protocol can defend against.
- What is the minimum viable mediator pool? Too few and capture
  becomes inevitable. Too many and coordination fails.
- Is there a meaningful analogue to the *protecting powers* of the
  Geneva tradition (neutral third parties with consular access)
  that can be constructed for inter-community contexts?
- Does the indicative virtue list above over- or under-reach? What
  would it look like to stress-test it against plausible Earth-side
  conflict scenarios *before* attempting space-side ones?
- How does kith-covenant detect and resist pure rule-optimizers (actors
  whose behavior is sophisticated gaming of the letter) in a compact
  whose enforcement assumes the virtue frame is intelligible?
- How much of the restitution-and-return apparatus can be built
  into protocol, and how much irreducibly requires practicing
  communities to do the work? If the answer is "most of it," that
  bounds the kith-covenant reach and should be said plainly.
- First compacts will be between specific Earth communities. Which
  are the least-bad candidates, and what selection criteria would
  distinguish a promising first-generation counterparty from a
  poisoned one? (This is a shared open question with
  [Justus hostis](justus_hostis.md#open-questions).)
- The anathema designation procedure is necessarily heavy. Is it
  *too* heavy for the cases where rapid action is genuinely needed
  (an actor actively destroying knowledge commons; an actor
  weaponizing rescue infrastructure)? What is the emergency variant,
  and how is *its* misuse prevented?
- The differential protocol for anathema permits stronger measures
  while remaining bounded by the common virtues.
  Are there
  edge cases where the virtues themselves come into conflict (e.g.,
  reverence for life versus protection of the knowledge commons
  against a demonic actor systematically destroying it)? How does
  the compact resolve such conflicts without abandoning the virtue
  frame?
- Discernment of the demonic from the merely-fallen requires virtue
  cultivated in practicing communities. How does the compact ensure
  its mediators have this cultivation without producing an
  inquisitorial caste? (Shared open question with
  [Justus hostis](justus_hostis.md#open-questions).)
