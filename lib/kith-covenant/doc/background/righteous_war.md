# Righteous war: escalation, conduct, and return when cooperation fails

## Thesis

Kith-covenant exists to make cooperation among unconnected communities
durable. This document handles the case the rest of the corpus would rather
avoid and cannot honestly ignore, the point at which cooperation with a
particular adversary has become impossible, and force is the truthful name
for what is left. The claim here is narrow and load-bearing. Though a
compact whose substrate commands enemy-love is not disqualified from war, it
is disqualified from a certain kind of war. The same substrate that forbids
the compact to hate its enemy is what lets it fight one without becoming
one. Righteous war has three parts, and the common virtues govern all three.
The parts are entry, conduct, and ending: the grounds on which a compact may
escalate to it (jus ad bellum), the manner in which it must be fought (jus
in bello), and the terms on which it must be ended (jus post bellum). The
hardest case, the enemy for whom no return is possible, is where the
constraint bites hardest, not least.

## Can this compact wage war at all?

The objection has to be answered before the rest is worth writing. The substrate of the
compact requires enemy-love as an operational norm, the fourth anti-spiral
feature (see [neighbor.md](neighbor.md) and
[justus_hostis.md](justus_hostis.md#the-anti-spiral-features-why-anchor-above-self-is-necessary-but-not-sufficient)).
"Resist not evil" and "turn the other cheek" sit near the center of the
tradition that most fully supplies the anti-spiral features. If that teaching
forbids force outright, then a righteous-war doctrine is a contradiction, and
the compact must be either pacifist or dishonest.

The tradition has worked this tension for many centuries and produced
several answers that hold without collapsing into naive pacifism. Although
they do not all agree, they converge on a residue the compact can use. The
readings differ in ground, scope, and temper.

- **Augustine reads the command as governing the heart.** "Resist not evil"
  frees the will from hatred and vengeance, and it does not forbid every act of
  restraint. A judge or a soldier can use force without personal malice, so
  defending others is not in itself a violation. This is where the just-war
  framework begins (*Letter 138* to Marcellinus; *City of God* XIX).
- **Luther splits the person by vocation.** As a private Christian you turn the
  other cheek; as a magistrate, soldier, or parent charged with protecting
  others, you carry a duty that runs by a different logic than personal
  discipleship (*Whether Soldiers, Too, Can Be Saved*, 1526; *Temporal
  Authority*, 1523).
- **Niebuhr reads the Sermon on the Mount as an ethic of perfection.** It
  exposes human sinfulness and points to grace rather than supplying a
  political program. In a fallen world justice, which sometimes requires
  coercion, is the proximate form love has to take among groups and
  institutions (*An Interpretation of Christian Ethics*, 1935; *Moral Man
  and Immoral Society*, 1932).
- **Wink reads nonresistance as a third way, not passivity.** The Greek
  *antistēnai* denotes violent armed uprising, so Jesus is rejecting both
  violent revolt and passive submission in favor of assertive, dignity-reclaiming
  nonviolence; turning the cheek and giving the cloak were coded acts of protest,
  not doormat behavior (*Jesus and Nonviolence: A Third Way*, 2003; *Engaging the
  Powers*, 1992).
- **The scope-limiting reading notes what the examples are about.** A slap
  on the cheek, a lawsuit over a coat, a mile of forced labor are personal
  insult and petty coercion under occupation, not a lethal threat or an
  invading army. The text may therefore not be addressing collective defense
  at all (N.T. Wright, *Matthew for Everyone*, Part 1; Davies and Allison,
  *Matthew*, ICC, on 5:38-42).
- **The peace-church reading takes nonviolence as deliberate, not naive.**
  Nonresistance is read as intentionally costly, trusting that the calling of the church
  differs from that of the state, rather than as accidental suicide (Yoder,
  *The Politics of Jesus*, 1972; Hauerwas, *The Peaceable Kingdom*, 1983;
  Bonhoeffer, *Discipleship*, 1937, whose own turn toward the plot against Hitler
  shows the reading tested under extremity).

Kith-covenant does not require any member community to adopt one of these
readings over the others. It requires the structural residue they share, and
they do share one. Even the pacifist readings hold that the enemy keeps a
moral standing that hatred would deny. Even the just-war readings license
force only under right disposition, protection, and the absence of hatred.
That shared residue is feature four stated for the hard case: the enemy
remains a neighbor within the encounter of conflict. Even if it never fires
a shot, a community whose substrate lets it hate has failed feature four. A
community whose war is bounded by the standing of the one it fights passes
feature four even in war. The compact credentials the disposition, not the
doctrine, which is the same finding [neighbor.md](neighbor.md) reaches for
peace, namely practice over creed.

So the compact can wage war, under one governing condition. The war must be
ordered toward the restoration of a just peace that has room in it for the
adversary, and it must refuse the acts that would make that peace impossible.
The warning from Augustine is the hinge the whole document turns on. The war
against evil becomes evil when it stops being ordered toward the good that gave
it meaning.

## Jus ad bellum: when escalation to war is permitted

War is the top of the escalation ladder, not a separate track beside it. The
ladder in [bounded_conflict.md](bounded_conflict.md#escalation-ladders) runs from
dispute registration through formal protest, reciprocal suspension, mediated
arbitration, and limited and collective sanctions, up to the rung where force
enters. The virtue overlay of the compact adds conditions to that last step. They
restate the classical just-war criteria and sharpen them with the architecture
the compact already has.

- **Just cause, tied to the common virtues.** The cause must be the defense of
  what the virtues protect, meaning life, the sanctuary of rescue, the
  communicative commons, safe passage, the non-combatant, the knowledge commons,
  and the memory of the dead (see the list in
  [bounded_conflict.md](bounded_conflict.md#common-virtue-not-non-negotiable-rules)).
  Not gain, not offense, not the spread of a creed.
- **Right authority, held by the liminal institutions.** A compact of unconnected
  communities cannot be adjudicated by any one of them, so escalation to force
  runs through the mediator pool and the compact-wide process, never by the unilateral
  declaration of a single community (see
  [liminal_institutions.md](liminal_institutions.md) and §5d of the overview).
  This is also the central anti-capture guard, because the most dangerous moment
  in the life of the compact is exactly when one party wants to name its opponent
  an enemy of all.
- **Last resort, as a real exhaustion of the ladder.** The lower rungs must have
  been genuinely attempted, with restoration paths offered rather than performed.
  A party that skips rungs is itself signaling the bad faith the ladder exists to
  expose.
- **Proportion of ends.** The harm the war will do must be weighed against the
  harm it answers. A war that destroys more of the protected good than it
  defends fails its own justification.
- **Right intention, carried from the disposition.** The intent must be the
  restoration of a just peace, not the humiliation or erasure of the adversary.
  This is where jus ad bellum meets feature four, since the compact must be able
  to intend the eventual return of the adversary even while it fights.
- **Reasonable prospect.** Force with no prospect of securing the protected good
  is not righteous. It is expressive violence, which the virtues do not license.

The four categories of other (see
[recognition_protocols.md](recognition_protocols.md#members-neighbors-enemies-the-four-categories)
and §5c of the overview) fix who a war is against and what remains available
throughout it. Against a *justus hostis*, war is bounded conflict at its most
intense. The adversary stays a legitimate peer, restoration to non-conflict
remains structurally available, and the war is fought toward a peace that
readmits them. Against an **anathema**, ordinary restoration is suspended and
active opposition is permitted, but a re-entry path remains and the virtues still
bind. Against a *hostis humani generis*, the terms are different again, and the
final section takes that case on directly.

## Jus in bello: how a righteous war is fought

Conduct in war is where the substrate is tested hardest, because the enemy is now
in front of you and armed. The jus in bello of the compact is already written, as the
common-virtue list in
[bounded_conflict.md](bounded_conflict.md#common-virtue-not-non-negotiable-rules).
This document does not restate that list. It names how the list maps onto the
classical in-bello principles and why it is framed as virtue rather than rule.
Those principles are discrimination, proportion, and good faith.

- **Discrimination** is the innocence of the non-combatant and the child,
  together with the sanctuary of the rescuer. The category of those outside the
  conflict is real even when the conflict is just, so there is no collective
  punishment, no use of civilians as shields, and no targeting of the wounded or
  the fleeing. Rescue is prior to conflict, and there is no such thing as an
  enemy of a rescuer.
- **Proportion of means** is reverence for life, including the life of the
  adversary, so there are no attacks on life-support, no biological or ecological
  attacks on closed-loop systems, and no weaponizing of trade in life-critical
  commodities.
- **Non-destruction of the future** is the inheritance of the future and the
  memory of the dead. Destruction of the knowledge commons is long-delayed
  genocide, because it severs the unborn members of a community from the
  inheritance that would have made them who they could be. This is the
  space-specific sharpening of the doctrine. Inside a closed-loop habitat the
  line between a military target and a life-support system is thin, and the virtues draw that
  line where a rulebook would let an adversary blur it.
- **Good faith of the confession** is the requirement that a defeated or
  repentant adversary keep a path to restitution that does not demand its own
  destruction.

The reason this is a virtue list and not a rulebook matters most in war. An
adversary that optimizes against the letter of a rule while gutting its spirit
reveals, by that very behavior, that it does not share the disposition, and that
revelation is information the compact needs, because it is the slide toward the
next category. The Geneva framework is the rule-shaped shadow of this doctrine,
made necessary because the older just-war tradition had lost its community
substrate. The just-war tradition is the virtue-shaped original, rooted in
practicing communities, and rebuilding the substrate in which the virtue frame
carries the load is exactly the ambition of kith-covenant at the inter-community
scope.

## Jus post bellum: termination and return

The fallen-actor anthropology and the restitution-and-return commitment mean a
righteous war is not finished when the fighting stops. Its ending has terms of
its own, and they are readmission, restitution, and memory.

- **The peace must readmit rather than annihilate.** For a *justus hostis*, that
  is restoration to peer standing. For an anathema, it is the re-entry path
  through fresh recitation under heightened scrutiny (see
  [recognition_protocols.md](recognition_protocols.md#members-neighbors-enemies-the-four-categories)).
  The war is fought toward the return of the adversary, not toward its erasure.
- **Restitution runs both ways.** The confession virtue binds the victor as much
  as the vanquished. A compact that harmed in war and then refuses to make it
  right has failed the very virtue it demanded of its enemy.
- **Proportion holds in victory.** The temptation at the end of a war is to
  extract what the cause of the war never justified. The non-ultimacy of the
  compact, the fifth anti-spiral feature, is the guard here. The compact is not
  the thing the commitments of its members are for, so winning does not license
  it to remake the adversary in its own image.
- **Memory without the spiral.** The Salem finding in
  [justus_hostis.md](justus_hostis.md#the-salem-witch-trials-the-features-can-be-held-and-still-defeated)
  is that the work of the substrate is termination and repair rather than
  prevention.
  The post-war task is to metabolize the conflict into memory and restitution
  rather than into a grievance that seeds the next purity spiral.

## The hardest case: hostis humani generis

Everything above assumes an adversary with whom some peace is finally
conceivable. The *hostis humani generis* is the actor for whom it is not, the
utterly incorrigible, oriented toward the destruction of cooperation as such, who
uses even the restoration paths tactically rather than in good faith. Pirates
were the original Roman-law case, not because piracy is especially cruel but
because the pirate had placed himself outside every cooperative framework at
once, attacking all and protected by none. The full two-tier treatment, and the
detection problem that the demonic mimics the legitimate until examined closely,
is in
[justus_hostis.md](justus_hostis.md#two-tiers-anathema-and-hostis-humani-generis).
Against such an actor, restoration does not apply and permanent opposition is
permitted.

This is the case that most severely tests the claim that the compact can fight
without becoming what it fights, and the answer the doctrine gives is meant to
be hard.

- **The category is extremely rare, and the presumption is always anathema.** The
  vast majority of actors who look like a *hostis humani generis* are in fact
  anathema, formally cut off but able in principle to return. Mislabeling an
  anathema forecloses a return that was available, and that error is both more
  common and more dangerous than the reverse (see
  [justus_hostis.md](justus_hostis.md#design-implications)).
- **The virtues still bind, with no exception for the incorrigible.** Even against
  the demonic, the compact does not become demonic. The category change permits a
  stronger response within the virtue frame, and it does not suspend the frame.
  No target, by existing, licenses an attack on life-support, on rescue, on the
  knowledge commons, or on non-combatants. The enemy of all humankind is still
  owed the standing that hatred would deny. What changes is only that no path of
  return is held open, not that the manner of opposition is unbounded. That
  standing is owed on a ground the designated actor has never accepted and does
  not share, which is the thin functional minimum the compact has adopted rather
  than the authority of the compact itself
  ([natural_law.md](natural_law.md#the-ground-the-compact-claims-for-itself)). The ground cuts
  both ways, and this is the practical importance of adopting it. The same
  minimum that makes the designation available at all is what forbids the compact
  to fight without limit under it.
- **Designation is the most dangerous moment in the compact's life, and is
  guarded as such.** It requires compact-wide deliberation, an evidentiary
  threshold higher than anathema (the destructive orientation must be shown to be
  constitutive, not merely current), published reasoning, slow procedure,
  periodic re-examination, and a standing willingness to be wrong about past
  designations. The road these guards are built against has a name in the record, and three of
  its stations are Inquisition, Terror, and gulag. Each began by discovering an
  enemy of all and ended by becoming one.
- **Discernment here is a virtue, not a procedure, and so it requires the
  substrate.** Procedure alone cannot tell the *justus hostis* who is currently
  failing from the anathema who may yet return from the *hostis humani generis*
  who never will. The transcendent reference the substrate supplies is what makes
  that discernment possible, and a compact whose members lack it will either
  over-designate, which is capture and the Inquisition, or under-designate, which
  is being gutted by the destroyer it would not name. This is the deepest reason
  the whole doctrine sits downstream of the substrate rather than of the rules.

The sentence the document turns on is Augustine's, and it applies most sharply
right here. The war against evil becomes evil the moment it stops being ordered
toward the good that gave it meaning. Righteous war is the name for a war that
keeps that order under the worst pressure the compact will ever face. When a
compact loses that order, it has not won its war. It has changed sides.
