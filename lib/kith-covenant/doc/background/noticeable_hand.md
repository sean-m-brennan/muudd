# Noticeable Hand: The Question of Grace in Stable Cooperation

## A note on this document's posture

This document takes up a question the rest of kith-covenant's research has
been circling without addressing directly: **is it plausible that all
stable human cooperation across unconnected communities is sustained
by grace, that is, by something divinely given rather than humanly
generated?** And if so, is one religious tradition's account of that
grace closer to the truth than the others?

The earlier research documents in this set framed substrate questions
in ecumenical-secular terms. That framing was partly methodologically
defensible (kith-covenant as a working project must function for communities
with varying commitments) and partly intellectual hedging: avoiding
conclusions the evidence may actually support because they would be
uncomfortable in pluralist discourse. The hedging was itself a kind
of dishonesty and is worth naming. The present document attempts to
follow the evidence where it leads, including to uncomfortable
places, while distinguishing two questions that the earlier
documents conflated: *what is true* about the substrate of human
cooperation, and *what should kith-covenant presuppose* in its working
design. These can come apart.

The posture here is honest inquiry, not apologia and not anti-apologia.
Christianity's claims are taken seriously as propositions about
reality, not treated as cultural artifacts to be relativized. The same
courtesy is extended to other traditions. Where the evidence weighs in
a particular direction, that is reported plainly. Where it does not,
that is reported plainly too.

## A methodological refinement: voluntary peer cooperation under longevity

The first intellectual debt to repay: the case set in
[historical_cases.md](historical_cases.md) included regimes that are
*not* voluntary peer cooperation among equals. The Pax Mongolica was
an imperial framework; *dhimma* was the legal architecture of a
caliphate governing its non-Muslim subjects; Roman federations were
underwritten by Roman power; the Chinese tributary system was a
hierarchical recognition order centered on the Son of Heaven. These
are real cases of cooperation across communities, and they may carry
useful lessons, but they are not what kith-covenant is. Kith-covenant is the
protocol layer for *voluntary* cooperation among communities that do
not share a sovereign.

Applying the voluntary-peer filter, and weighting by longevity, the
case set looks substantially different:

- **The Buddhist *sangha* across kingdoms.** Voluntary monastic
  networks; ~2500 years; transmitted across India, Central Asia,
  China, Korea, Japan, Tibet, Sri Lanka, and Southeast Asia. Longest
  by a wide margin.
- **The Iroquois Confederacy (Haudenosaunee Kaianerekowa).**
  Voluntary federation of peer nations; 300 to 500+ years on most
  datings; still extant in altered form.
- **Christian Europe's voluntary peer regimes.** *Lex mercatoria*
  (~600 years from 11th-12th c. through absorption in the 18th-19th);
  the Hanseatic League (~300 years from 1356 Hansetag through 1669);
  the *jus publicum Europaeum* and Westphalian system (~270 years from
  1648 through 1914 in its working form); the Peace of God (~200 years
  as movement, longer in absorbed forms); codes duello (~300+ years).
  Multiple cases of substantial duration, all within Christendom.
- **Mali Kurukan Fuga.** Borderline. Sundiata's federation was
  established after his victory at Kirina, so it has an imperial
  origin moment, but the federated structure thereafter operated
  with substantial autonomy among constituent kingdoms. Several
  centuries of duration before fragmentation.

Imperial-framework cases (Pax Mongolica, *dhimma*, Pax Romana,
Chinese tributary system, Concert of Europe) are filtered out as
*not the kind of thing kith-covenant is*. They retain interest as
cooperation cases but do not bear directly on the question of what
makes voluntary peer cooperation among strangers stable.

### What the filter also excludes, and why that matters

The voluntary-peer-longevity filter was initially applied to the full
case set without distinguishing *full cooperation regimes* (regimes
that govern a broad envelope of a community's interactions with
other communities) from *bounded functional frameworks* (frameworks
that govern a specific domain such as commerce, maritime salvage, envoy
protection, or wounded-combatant treatment, without otherwise
regulating the relationship between the communities that share
them). The filter as applied correctly identifies the full-compact
cases but silently excludes a different and equally real category
of historical evidence, now treated explicitly in
[historical_cases.md](historical_cases.md#convergence-without-shared-culture-the-thin-functional-domain-cases):
the Late Bronze Age treaty grammar (Kadesh and its cognates),
Rhodian Sea Law's millennium-long transmission across Hellenistic,
Roman, Byzantine, Islamic, and Christian Mediterranean practice,
Mediterranean frontier norms across the Christian-Islamic boundary,
the polycentric emergence of diplomatic immunity, and the
Geneva-Convention framework with its culturally-pluralized emblems
(Red Cross / Red Crescent / Red Crystal). These are real,
well-documented, centuries-long examples of cross-cultural
cooperation among communities that did not share substrate.

The prior framing of this document treated the filter's output as
if it were the whole record, and that was a mistake. Honest
accounting requires separating two questions:

- **At the full-compact scale**, where a compact governs a broad
  envelope of inter-community life, not merely a bounded functional
  domain, the voluntary-peer-longevity filter produces the case
  set this document analyzes, and the substrate finding that emerges
  is real.
- **At the thin-functional-domain scale** (commerce, maritime
  rules, envoy protection, combatant-treatment rules), convergence
  across communities that do not share substrate is historically
  common and has the preconditions enumerated in
  [historical_cases.md](historical_cases.md#what-the-thin-domain-cases-have-in-common):
  functional (not metaphysical) domain, frontier geography, prior
  independent emergence of analogous local structures, each party
  justifying the shared rule in its own frame, legible cost of
  non-convergence, and a mediator class that lives across the
  boundary.

The two scales are not in competition; they are doing different
work. Kith-covenant proposes to operate as a full compact and is
therefore subject to the substrate finding at that scale. But
kith-covenant will *also* do thin-functional-domain work (in life
support, rescue, orbital debris, communications), and should be
honest that for that work the thin-domain preconditions are what
the historical record actually shows. Treating the full-compact
substrate requirement as if it applied at every scale would both
overclaim the evidence and underweight the genuine mechanism by
which most historical cross-cultural cooperation has in fact
operated.

## What the filtered evidence shows

After the filter, the evidence is striking but uneven.

**Christianity has an outsized share of the voluntary peer
cooperation record across unconnected communities.** Five distinct,
substantial regimes (Westphalia, Hanse, lex mercatoria, codes duello,
Peace of God), each running multiple centuries, all within
Christendom. The numerical density is hard to dismiss even
controlling for the West's documentary advantage. The cases are
well-documented Christian cases, and they are also *cases that produced
durable institutions whose effects on subsequent global history are
disproportionate*.

**The Buddhist *sangha* is a real and significant counter-case.**
Longest-running voluntary cross-community network in the dataset;
non-theistic in its philosophical core; obviously not Christian.
This complicates any narrowly Christian reading.

**The Iroquois Confederacy is the second strongest non-Christian
voluntary case.** Centuries of duration; theistic substrate with a
Creator at the apex (transcendent-moral-authority structure); deeply religious in
character though not Abrahamic.

**Beyond these, the voluntary peer cases that meet the longevity bar
are sparse.** Modern secular international institutions are too young
to count, and (per
[historical_cases.md](historical_cases.md#brief-notes-on-the-harder-cases))
fit the predicted brittleness of cooperation regimes built without a
transcendent-moral-authority substrate.

The honest summary: **the empirical record of voluntary peer
cooperation among unconnected communities is dominated by Christian
cases, with Buddhist and Iroquois cases as the principal
non-Christian counterparts.** Whatever theological reading we adopt
must account for this distribution.

## The hypothesis stated in its strongest form

Stated bluntly: **stable cooperation among unconnected human
communities is not the natural condition of human social life. It is
rare, fragile, and historically exceptional. Where it exists, it
appears to require something that humans receive rather than
generate: call it grace, call it the participation of cosmologies in
a transcendent reality, call it the substrate of moral order.
Without that received element, cooperation reverts to the
default: predation, faction, war.**

This is the hypothesis the user has asked be taken seriously. It is
a *theological* hypothesis, not a sociological one. It claims that
something divine is involved in the stable cases, not merely that
they share certain structural features. The structural features
(cosmology pointing to a transcendent moral authority, oath-binding, etc.) are then *evidence* of the
divine activity, not the divine activity itself.

The hypothesis applies at the full-compact scale where the evidence
has been filtered; it is *not* a claim about every scale of
cross-community cooperation. The thin-functional-domain cases
([historical_cases.md](historical_cases.md#convergence-without-shared-culture-the-thin-functional-domain-cases))
show that cross-cultural cooperation on bounded functional matters
converges without shared substrate when the preconditions hold.
What the strongest form of the hypothesis claims is that *full
compacts* (regimes that govern a broad envelope of inter-community
life) have historically required something more than those
preconditions: each party anchored to something above itself, and
the compact structurally precluded from claiming to be that thing.
That narrower formulation is what the evidence actually supports.

Three readings of the evidence are available. They are not all
equally well-supported, and the comparison repays careful attention.

## Three readings of the evidence

### The Christian-particularist reading

This reading holds that **Christianity is largely correct about the
nature of God, the human person, and the conditions of human
cooperation, and that the historical record is consistent with what
Christianity would predict.** It need not deny that other traditions
participate in truth (most Christian theology in fact affirms that
they do), but it holds that the Christian account is *more right*
than the alternatives in describable ways.

Several lines of evidence and argument support this reading:

- **Imago Dei as the deepest grounding of *justus hostis*.** The
  recognition of the enemy as a legitimate peer worthy of equal
  standing (the move that makes bounded conflict possible) has its
  most explicit theological grounding in the Christian doctrine that
  every human bears the image of God. Vattel and Grotius developed
  this jurisprudentially. Carl Schmitt, no friend of Christianity in
  the conventional sense, observed that the depoliticization of the
  enemy was a Christian achievement that secular successors could
  not maintain. Other traditions affirm human dignity in various
  ways; none does so with quite the same uncompromising universality
  combined with realism about human fallenness.
- **The architecture of confession, restitution, and return.** The
  protocol sketch in
  [bounded_conflict.md](bounded_conflict.md#protocol-sketch)
  requires named paths back into good standing after violation, on
  the premise that the protocol is built for fallen actors. The
  developed sacramental tradition of penance and reconciliation
  (with its specific structure of contrition, confession, satisfaction,
  and absolution) is a Christian innovation absorbed into Western
  jurisprudence (probation, parole, restorative justice) and into
  most modern conflict-resolution practice. Other traditions have
  analogues (the Iroquois Condolence ceremony, Buddhist practices of
  formal apology and renewed vows) but the Christian form is unusually
  developed.
- **Grace as gratuitous gift.** The Christian conception that grace
  is given without merit and cannot be earned is theologically
  distinctive. Other traditions emphasize merit, karma, ritual
  efficacy, or covenant fidelity. The cooperation-protocol implication
  is direct: tit-for-tat-with-forgiveness has its deepest justification
  in the Christian view that we are all undeserving of grace and yet
  receive it. This *exact* anthropology is what
  [justus_hostis.md](justus_hostis.md#the-anthropology-the-substrate-must-assume)
  argued kith-covenant must assume.
- **The sin/sinner distinction.** Hate the sin, love the sinner. This
  separation makes possible the *justus hostis* move structurally: I
  can recognize you as a peer even when I judge your acts wrongful.
  Other traditions struggle with this separation more than Christianity
  does. Most notably, ideological universalisms tend to collapse
  the distinction (the heretic *is* his heresy) and produce
  annihilationist politics in consequence.
- **Particularity within universality.** "Neither Jew nor
  Greek...male nor female...slave nor free, for you are all one in
  Christ Jesus." But the particular identities are transcended,
  not extinguished. Christianity in its catholic form is unusual in
  asserting universalism without demanding cultural assimilation.
  This is exactly the structure kith-covenant needs: cooperation across
  communities whose particularities are preserved. Strict Islamic
  universalism arguably tends toward assimilation; strict Jewish
  particularism tends toward boundary maintenance; Christianity
  uniquely supports particularity-within-universality at scale.
- **The doctrine of original sin as load-bearing realism.** The
  fallen-actor anthropology that
  [justus_hostis.md](justus_hostis.md#the-anthropology-the-substrate-must-assume)
  centers (humans as sinners, capable of evil, requiring containment
  and grace) is in its developed form a Christian doctrine
  (Augustine, Niebuhr, the entire Western tradition of Christian
  realism). Earlier drafts of kith-covenant's documentation tried to import
  this anthropology in secular dress; the Christian original is
  more coherent than the imports.

The reading further holds that the multiple long-running Christian
cooperation regimes are not coincidence: Christianity provides the
substrate that makes such regimes possible, and where the substrate
is present, the regimes recur. The Buddhist and Iroquois cases are
real but are partial participation in the same truth that
Christianity articulates more completely. (This is roughly the C.S.
Lewis position in *Mere Christianity*, or in stronger form the Karl
Barth or G.K. Chesterton line.)

### The catholic / perennialist reading

This reading holds that **there is one God who has made cooperation
possible across all human communities through whatever cosmological
substrate they have access to.** Christianity is a fuller and
clearer revelation of this God in some accounts; in others, all
transcendent moral authority religions equally participate in the same truth under
different surface forms.

Evidence supporting this reading:

- **The structural pattern across radically different traditions.**
  The same load (making oaths meaningful, making recognition costly
  to the self, making restitution real) is borne by Christianity,
  by the Haudenosaunee Kaianerekowa, by Buddhist Dharma, by Mande
  *Maa Ngala* + Islam, by classical Greek high gods. The functional
  isomorphism across surface differences is striking.
- **Tengrist tolerance as policy.** The Mongol case (filtered out as
  imperial above, but the *theology* of which is still relevant)
  *explicitly held* that worship of other peoples' high gods was
  worship of Tengri under another name. This is the catholic
  reading made into governance.
- **Christian theological precedent for the catholic reading.**
  Justin Martyr's *logos spermatikos* (the seeds of the Word
  scattered across pre-Christian philosophy) and the Church Fathers'
  appropriation of Plato and Aristotle. *Acts* 17 (Paul at the
  Areopagus, on the unknown God). *Romans* 1-2 on natural revelation
  and the moral law written on the conscience. The Christian
  tradition itself has resources for affirming that God works
  through other substrates, even while holding that one substrate
  is the fullest expression. This is not an abandonment of Christian
  particularism; it is a particular form of it.
- **The Buddhist sangha's unusual longevity.** If the Christian
  reading were strictly correct, we would expect Christianity to
  produce more durable cooperation than other traditions. The
  Buddhist sangha (non-theistic by the Christian definition of
  theism) has lasted longer than any Christian regime in the
  dataset. This is uncomfortable for narrow Christian particularism
  and easier for the catholic reading.

### The naturalist reading

This reading holds that **the pattern is entirely explained by
structural and psychological features of human cognition and group
dynamics, with no need to invoke divine activity.** Transcendent moral authority
substrates work because human psychology is shaped by certain
evolutionary and developmental constraints; oath-binding works
because shame and guilt are real psychological mechanisms; the
restitution architecture works because reciprocity is a deep human
disposition. The pattern coincides with theistic substrates because
those substrates happen to match the relevant human
psychological architecture, not because anything actually divine
is involved.

The naturalist reading has the advantage of parsimony: no extra
metaphysical commitments are required. It can point to the
substantial empirical literature on cross-cultural moralizing-gods
research (Norenzayan and others) which finds that transcendent moral authority beliefs
correlate with prosocial behavior across many cultures, and can
explain this entirely in terms of social-cognitive mechanisms.

The reading's difficulties begin where it tries to account for the
specifically *uncoerced* and *cross-community* cases. Cooperation
within a community that shares norms is sociologically expected.
Cooperation across communities that *do not* share norms, sustained
over centuries by people who would have benefited individually from
defection, is much harder to explain on purely naturalist grounds.
The cooperation costs of the cases under examination (Hanseatic
merchants honoring contracts that no court would enforce; Christian
sovereigns honoring safe-conducts to opponents in active war;
Iroquois nations giving up the right of feud) are individually
substantial, and the explanations available within naturalism
(reputation, iterated game equilibria, kin selection extended) are
not obviously sufficient. The most honest naturalist reading
probably has to admit that the theistic cases work *better* than
naturalism alone predicts.

## Comparing the readings

Three observations on which reading the evidence favors:

**First, the naturalist reading is the weakest of the three on the
filtered evidence.** It can explain a great deal of human
cooperation in general, but it has trouble explaining the specific
cases under examination: long-running, voluntary, peer-to-peer
cooperation across unconnected communities, sustained at substantial
individual cost. The structural features it points to (reciprocity,
shame, in-group dynamics) are real but seem insufficient to bear the
weight observed. This is not a refutation of naturalism in general;
it is the observation that naturalism has more difficulty than the
other readings with the specific kind of cooperation kith-covenant is
trying to enable.

**Second, the Christian-particularist and catholic readings are
both strong, and the choice between them probably cannot be made on
historical evidence alone.** Both readings can account for the
distribution of cases. Both can account for the structural features.
Both can account for the failure modes. The Christian-particularist
reading has the advantage that it explains *why Christianity in
particular* produced so many long-running regimes; the catholic
reading has the advantage that it accounts more easily for the
non-Christian cases, especially the Buddhist longevity. Choosing
between them requires philosophical and theological commitments
beyond what the historical record alone can settle.

**Third, the practical implications of the two theistic readings
converge significantly.** Whether one believes Christianity is
specifically right or that one God works through multiple substrates,
the design implications for kith-covenant are nearly identical:

- The substrate must point to a single ultimate (transcendent-moral-authority structure).
- The *nature* of the ultimate cannot be dictated by kith-covenant (first-order theology is each community's business); the *existence* of a Creator, and the pro-human and pro-cooperation orientation of the substrate, *are* required.
- A Christian member of kith-covenant will see grace at work; a Buddhist
  member will see the Dharma; a Haudenosaunee member will see the
  Creator's work; a perennialist will see all of these as one.
- Kith-Covenant's documentation can be honest about the historical pattern
  without taking sides on the ultimate question.

## Honest counterweights

Three serious difficulties for any strong Christian reading deserve
direct acknowledgment.

**Intra-Christian violence.** If Christianity provides the substrate
for cooperation, *why was the Thirty Years' War necessary in the
first place?* Why the Wars of Religion? Why the Albigensian Crusade?
Why centuries of intra-Christian persecution? The honest Christian
answer is that the substrate is given but human use of it is
fallen. Christians can refuse the grace as readily as anyone else,
and the worst Christian failures occur precisely when Christianity
is weaponized as ideology rather than received as gift. But this
move is potentially unfalsifiable; one always says "true Christianity
would have done better" while the actual Christians do worse. Kith-Covenant
should be wary of any reading that allows the substrate's failures
to be defined out of the substrate.

**The colonial frontier.** Christian-European powers committed some
of history's most spectacular violations of *justus hostis* in their
treatment of non-European peoples, even while maintaining the
*jus publicum* among themselves. If the substrate provides the
grace, why so unevenly? The honest reading is that the European
substrate was *operating selectively*, treating Christians as peers
and non-Christians as outside the moral universe. This is the
colonial blind spot of the *jus publicum* discussed in
[justus_hostis.md](justus_hostis.md#how-the-jus-publicum-fell-apart).
Whatever theological framework one adopts, it must reckon with the
fact that the Christian regimes' grace operated within a tightly
drawn boundary that excluded most of humanity for most of the
period in question.

**But this requires a further observation, which sharpens the
counterweight rather than dissolving it.** The selective operation
of the Christian substrate was an *internal Christian failure*,
a failure to follow the substrate's own scriptural mandate, not
an inevitable consequence of the substrate itself. The
foundational Christian commandment is *love thy neighbor as
thyself* (Lev 19:18, taken up by Christ in Matt 22:39, Mark 12:31,
and the Lukan version that is decisive here). The Lukan rendering
is precisely the version where the lawyer asks "and who is my
neighbor?" This is the question that the user's observation correctly
identifies as foundational. Christ's answer is the parable of the
Good Samaritan (Luke 10:25-37). The Samaritan, despised heretic
to the Jewish audience, is the neighbor, *because he showed mercy
to the stranger left for dead by the side of the road.* The
parable's point, made with the full force of Christ's authority,
is that **the category of neighbor is to be defined expansively,
across ethnic and religious boundaries, by the criterion of mercy
shown rather than the criterion of group membership shared.**

Two clarifications on the parable before drawing the implication.
First, the Samaritan's neighbor is the one *he encountered*, the
man he met on the road. The priest and Levite had also encountered
the wounded man; their failure was a failure within encounter, not
a failure to love all theoretical travelers. *The neighbor relation
is actualized by contact.* Second, the Samaritan *discerned* within
the encounter: he rescued the victim, not the robber (absent, un-deserving), not
the fellow travelers (not in need). The parable does not teach
"treat everyone the same" but "within every actual encounter,
discern the situation (who is in need, what is the risk, what is
the appropriate response) and act." The moral innovation is the
expansion of *who counts as neighbor in encounter* across ethnic
and religious boundaries, not the flattening of moral response
into undifferentiated universal embrace.

The colonial period's Christian Europe did not follow this. It
encountered non-Christian peoples face-to-face, *saw them*, and
yet refused to recognize them as neighbors within the encounter.
The colonists did not fail to love abstract humanity; they failed
to extend neighbor status to those actually before them. This was
not a direct application of the substrate. It was a *betrayal* of
the substrate by its nominal carriers. It was the same kind of betrayal
that produced intra-Christian violence (the prior counterweight),
produced by the same mechanism: the *de facto* drawing of
moral boundaries inside the in-group, in defiance of the *de jure*
universalism-in-encounter that the Christian scriptures actually
mandate.

The implication for kith-covenant is sharp and operationally specific:
*the failure mode is not inevitable, but within every encounter
with a non-member the encountered party must be recognized as a
neighbor, with discernment of the specific situation.* Kith-Covenant must
not recapitulate the colonial failure of refusing to see the party
before it. The compact's *protocols* apply to members; the
compact's *moral commitments* apply within every actual encounter,
to whomever is encountered, discerned appropriately to situation.
What kith-covenant does *not* owe is fantasy-universal love to parties it
does not encounter. No finite body can bear that burden, and
pretending otherwise produces either sentimentalism or exhaustion.
What it owes is full moral seriousness in every encounter it
actually has. This is operationalized in
[recognition_protocols.md "Members, neighbors, enemies"](recognition_protocols.md#members-neighbors-enemies-the-four-categories).

This is also where the Schmittian friend/enemy distinction becomes
important to kith-covenant, and where kith-covenant must depart from Schmitt.
Schmitt held that the political is constituted by the friend/enemy
distinction: that politics exists where there is an us-and-them
serious enough to potentially involve killing. The distinction is
real and kith-covenant acknowledges it (the anathema category in
[justus_hostis.md](justus_hostis.md#hostis-humani-generis-limits-of-the-justus-hostis-frame)
is the kith-covenant analogue). But the friend/enemy distinction *does
not exhaust the categories of moral relation*. There is also the
neighbor: the human being actually encountered, to whom one owes
recognition and appropriate response regardless of whether they are
friend or enemy or neither, discerned within the encounter. Schmitt's
political theology, by collapsing all moral relation into the
friend/enemy axis, loses the neighbor. Christian theology does not.
The colonial failure was a Schmittian failure imported into Christian
practice, a treating of the non-Christian other as neither friend
nor *justus hostis* but as *outside the political* altogether, which
on Schmitt's own logic permits any treatment whatever.

Kith-Covenant must hold the friend/enemy distinction in its proper place
(operationally important for compact membership and for the
anathema procedure) while also holding the neighbor
category above and around it (morally prior, operative within every
actual encounter). This is the structural correction the colonial
counterweight teaches.

**Buddhist longevity.** If Christianity is **uniquely** well-suited to
long-running voluntary cross-community cooperation, the Buddhist
*sangha* is a problem. It has lasted longer; it has spread further;
it has been more consistently voluntary. The counterweight is real
and is treated at length in [buddhist_sangha.md](buddhist_sangha.md),
which analyzes the sangha on the same metrics the Christian cases are
evaluated on in [historical_cases.md](historical_cases.md). That
document's findings sharpen rather than dissolve the counterweight
and also narrow its force in a specific direction.

Three findings from that analysis bear directly on the Christian
reading. **First, the sangha really is a case of long-running
voluntary cross-community cooperation** (more than 2300 years in the
Theravada regime, ~1700 years in the East Asian regime, ~1000 years
in the Tibetan Vajrayana regime) and it really does supply the five
[anti-spiral features](justus_hostis.md#the-anti-spiral-features-why-anchor-above-self-is-necessary-but-not-sufficient)
through non-Christian theoretical apparatus, strongly on four of the
five and in functional-but-not-identical form on the
scapegoat-revelation feature where Christianity's form is structurally
distinctive. **Second, the sangha is structurally a different kind of
cooperation regime than Westphalia or the Hanse.** It is a
trans-polity religious community that sustains itself across polities
that remain separate, which is closer to the Roman Catholic monastic
orders (Benedictines, Cistercians, Jesuits) than to cross-polity
cooperation frameworks among separate sovereigns. The Christian
tradition has produced both kinds of regime; the sangha evidence
shows Buddhism has produced the *first* kind in greater abundance and
longer duration than Christianity, and the *second* kind in weaker
forms (the Tibetan *yön-chö* relationship being the closest analogue).
**Third, the strong Christian-particularist reading ("only Christianity
can do the load-bearing work") is substantially weakened by the
sangha evidence**, but the moderate version ("Christianity supplies
the most complete single combination of the features, and produces
the greatest density of cross-polity cooperation *frameworks* among
separate sovereigns") survives and is in fact strengthened by the
specificity the comparison forces.

The three counterweights do not refute the Christian reading. They
mean that the strongest version of the Christian reading must include
a serious account of intra-Christian failure, of the colonial
selectivity, and of the different-kinds-of-cooperation-regime that
the Buddhist case actually instantiates. A reading that cannot do
this is not strong; it is partisan.

## What the historical evidence can and cannot settle

The historical evidence can establish:

- That voluntary peer cooperation across unconnected communities is
  rare and exceptional in the human record.
- That when it occurs and persists, it occurs disproportionately on
  substrates with transcendent-moral-authority structure.
- That the Christian record, while not exclusive, contains a
  disproportionate share of the long-running cases.
- That naturalist explanations have measurable difficulty accounting
  for the costliest forms of cross-community cooperation.

The historical evidence *cannot* establish:

- Whether the transcendent-moral-authority substrates are *actually pointing at God* or
  are merely psychologically convenient frameworks that happen to
  produce cooperation.
- Whether Christianity is the truest of the transcendent-moral-authority substrates or
  one truth among several.
- Whether the failures of Christian regimes (intra-Christian
  violence, colonial selectivity) refute the Christian reading or
  illustrate the fallenness the Christian reading presupposes.
- What kith-covenant's individual users *should* believe about God.

These last questions are genuine (they admit answers) but the
answers cannot be derived from the historical pattern alone. They
are theological and philosophical questions, and they are properly
the work of communities and persons in their own substrates.

## Implications for kith-covenant as a project

The implications converge across the three readings, with one
important nuance.

Across all three readings:

- Kith-Covenant's compact members must have internal cosmologies with
  transcendent-moral-authority structure (the conclusion of
  [historical_cases.md](historical_cases.md#the-transcendent-moral-authority-pattern)).
  This holds whether the transcendent moral authority is the Christian God specifically,
  participates in a single transcendent reality, or is merely
  psychologically convenient.
- Kith-Covenant cannot dictate the *nature* of the Creator (first-order theology is each community's own business) but does require acknowledgment of a Creator and a pro-human, pro-cooperation orientation (see [justus_hostis.md](justus_hostis.md) §"What the compact requires, what it does not, and why").
- Kith-Covenant must include the architecture of confession, restitution,
  and return, built for fallen actors who will fail and need paths
  back.
- Kith-Covenant should structure its protocols around the *imago Dei*
  recognition (or its functional equivalent in other traditions),
  treating each person as bearing equal standing regardless of their
  community of origin.

The nuance: **kith-covenant's documentation should be honest that the
historical evidence weighs in particular directions, rather than
pretending to a neutrality the evidence does not support.** The
earlier ecumenical-secular framing in
[justus_hostis.md](justus_hostis.md) and other documents can be
maintained for *design* purposes (kith-covenant must work for members
across traditions) but should be supplemented with honest reportage
of what the historical pattern shows, and with explicit
acknowledgment that the pattern is most consistent with the
hypothesis that something genuinely transcendent is at work.

A Christian member reading kith-covenant's documentation should not be
asked to pretend that the document is neutral on whether God exists
when the evidence suggests otherwise. Nor should a Buddhist or
Haudenosaunee or perennialist member be asked to read documentation
that asserts the Christian God specifically. The middle path is:
acknowledge the pattern, state the hypothesis as a hypothesis,
recommend that members hold the hypothesis in the form that fits
their own substrate, and let kith-covenant as a working project remain
*structurally* compatible with multiple readings while not
*epistemically* dishonest about the data.

## Truth-finding and design-making

This document distinguishes two questions the rest of kith-covenant's
research has tended to conflate.

**The truth-finding question:** *What is actually the case about the
substrate of human cooperation?* This document's honest answer is
that the historical evidence is most consistent with the hypothesis
that something genuinely transcendent is involved: that grace, in
some sense and from some source, is a real feature of stable
cooperation, not merely a useful fiction. Within that hypothesis,
both the Christian-particularist and catholic readings have substantial
support; the Christian-particularist reading is the strongest single
account of the evidence, with the catholic reading required to handle
the Buddhist counterexample most gracefully. The naturalist reading
is the weakest of the three on the specific evidence under
examination, though it remains a coherent overall worldview.

**The design-making question:** *What should kith-covenant presuppose in its
working architecture?* The answer is that kith-covenant must function for
members across the three readings: it cannot require Christian
commitment, perennialist commitment, or naturalist commitment from
its members. Its protocols must work whether the user believes God
the Father is sustaining cooperation, that the Dharma is, that
*Shöñgwa'ya'di:söñ* is, or that nothing transcendent is at all. The
*structural requirement* (transcendent-moral-authority substrate within member
communities) is a constraint the design can impose; the *content
requirement* is not.

These two questions can be held simultaneously. Kith-Covenant can be honest
in its documentation about what the evidence shows, while remaining
compatible with the design constraint that members do not have to
share the conclusion. This is not hedging if it is honestly stated:
it is the recognition that historical evidence has its limits and
that working software cannot adjudicate ultimate questions.

The proposition the user offered (*that what Christians call God the
Father is the answer, giving grace to the cooperative and meaning to
the virtuous*) is, on the present analysis, the strongest single
account of the evidence available. It is not the only account
consistent with the evidence; the catholic reading is also strong,
and the naturalist reading remains coherent overall while being
weakest on the specific cases. Kith-Covenant cannot adopt the proposition
as doctrine, because kith-covenant is not a church. But kith-covenant's
documentation should not pretend the proposition is implausible
when the evidence in fact supports it. The honest middle is to
state the proposition as a hypothesis with the support it has,
acknowledge where it remains contested, and let members resolve the
question for themselves within their own substrates.

## Open questions

- The Buddhist longevity case was the single sharpest challenge to the
  strong Christian reading. A direct analysis of the sangha on the
  same metrics the Christian cases are evaluated on now exists in
  [buddhist_sangha.md](buddhist_sangha.md). The result: the strong
  Christian-particularist reading ("only Christianity can do the
  load-bearing work") is weakened; the moderate version ("most
  complete combination of the features; greatest density of
  cross-polity cooperation *frameworks*") survives and is sharpened.
  The remaining open question is whether Christianity's specific
  form of the scapegoat-revelation feature is structurally more
  robust than the Buddhist ontological-disarming form; Girard's late
  work argued yes, but the question is not settled.
- The naturalist reading is weakest on the specific evidence but
  remains strong as a general worldview. What additional evidence
  (historical, ethnographic, experimental) would distinguish more
  cleanly among the three readings?
- The Christian-particularist reading must reckon with intra-Christian
  violence, colonial selectivity, and Buddhist longevity. What is
  the strongest version of the Christian reading that takes these
  difficulties seriously rather than defining them away?
- The catholic reading has Christian theological precedent (Justin
  Martyr's *logos spermatikos*, Acts 17, Romans 2). It also has
  precedent in other traditions (Mongol Tengrist tolerance as
  policy; Hindu ideas of multiple paths to the same ultimate). What
  would a substantive synthesis look like that honored both?
- Should kith-covenant's recognition protocols
  ([recognition_protocols.md](recognition_protocols.md)
  ) include explicit acknowledgment of the
  transcendent moral authority substrate criterion at compact-formation? Does this risk
  being a soft religious test that excludes secular communities,
  and is that exclusion theologically honest if the evidence
  supports it?
- The user's framing ("the *noticeable* hand of God") inverts
  Adam Smith's invisible hand. The fact of stable cooperation,
  rather than the mechanism within cooperation, is itself the
  noticeable evidence. What other domains might bear the same
  inverted-Smith analysis (fine-tuning in cosmology is the obvious
  parallel; what others)?
