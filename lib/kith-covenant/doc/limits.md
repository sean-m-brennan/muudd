# What only humans can do

This is the last chapter, and it is the one where the compact says what it cannot
do.

Two things belong here. The first is a division of labor: some of the work in
this book is procedural and cryptographic, maintainable by software with nobody
in the loop, and some of it requires bodily presence, virtue formation, or
contextual judgement that no automated system supplies. The split is not
arbitrary; it follows from what each kind of work actually is.

The second is a list of places the design is exposed, stated without softening.
None of them is a reason the project is wrong. Each is a place it is weak, and
saying so is a requirement of the anthropology the whole thing rests on. A
protocol that pretended to have answered everything would be precisely the kind
of protocol the fallen-actor claim forbids.

## The litmus test

One question sorts nearly every case.

If the work depends on *what somebody meant*, on *whether the act expressed the
virtue*, or on *whether a community is who it claims to be*, it is human work.

If it depends on *whether a signature is valid*, *whether a threshold has been
crossed*, or *whether a deadline has passed*, it is machine work.

## What software carries

Identity and credentials, being cryptographic proofs that a community authorized
an envoy, standing tokens with explicit issuance time and half-life and decay
curves, and revocation propagation with priority over unexpired tokens.

The audit commons, being an append-only log, cryptographically immutable and
distributed-redundant, with selective disclosure that lets different observers see
different layers.

The escalation-ladder state machine, being dispute registration, rung tracking,
automatic timeouts, quorum signatures, ratification thresholds, and restitution
workflows.

The decision-class router, enforcing first-class against second-class decisions
cryptographically, and self-protecting because reclassification is itself
second-class.

The commons drawdown ledger, tracking consumption, threshold proximity,
proportional-contribution accounting, and threshold-crossing alerts.

Reputation propagation, being a gossip protocol with eventual consistency,
half-life decay, and light-lag tolerance.

Selective disclosure across the boundary with the trust substrate, being
zero-knowledge proofs of internal-health properties and ring signatures for
whistleblower attestation.

The machine handshake, being envelope computation, continuous-attestation
verification, ratchet-rule enforcement, and fork-and-merge re-attestation.

Mediator-pool rotation, being deterministic selection by lot, term-limit
tracking, conflict-of-interest filtering, and apprenticeship rosters.

Periodic event scheduling, being re-affirmation cycles, the structured-fast
cadence, generational-cadence reminders, and ceremony-cost metric collection.

Statistical triangulation, being behavioral comparison of similarly-situated
principals, divergence flagging, and ensemble-baseline maintenance.

And the manufactured-event delay protocol, being a structured-skepticism state
machine that imposes delay, requires corroboration, and attaches stake disclosure
to high-charge events.

## What only humans carry

*Sincerity assessment.* Whether a recitation is hollow or alive. Whether a
principal is going through the motions. The body knows; the protocol does not.

*Discernment of category.* A peer in good-faith dispute looks superficially like
a party acting in bad faith, which looks superficially like a party outside every
framework. The distinction is virtue-cultivated rather than procedural.

*Spirit-versus-letter adjudication.* Compliance with the rule while violating the
disposition. Optimization against the letter is detectable only by a judgement
about what the act expressed.

*Substrate adequacy.* Whether the claimed pro-human, pro-cooperation orientation
of a candidate community is genuine or instrumental.

*Hollowing detection.* Forms persisting while substance erodes. No metric catches
this. Only trained observers, jesters, and witnesses with longitudinal exposure.

*Restoration adequacy.* Whether the substantively changed conduct of a designated
community is genuine repair or the tactical exhaustion of goodwill.

*De-escalation timing.* When mediation should soften pressure and when it should
hold firm. Reading the room.

*Detection at the substrate layer.* Sophisticated mimicry exceeds attestation
windows, triangulation, and envelope checks. Detection at that level is a
property of substrate-engaged communities rather than of protocol.

*The witness function in recognition rites.* The three-witness structure, being a
continuity cohort, a current cohort, and an external check, requires bodily
presence and sincerity assessment.

*Generational transmission.* Catechesis, apprenticeship, and chronicle. Mentors
shape successors through practice and example rather than through a manual.

*First-order against second and third-order adjudication.* The compact does not
adjudicate the nature of God. It does adjudicate which doctrinal positions
produce structural effects incompatible with membership. Distinguishing the two
requires discernment rather than procedure.

*The naming the jester does.* Pointing at what is becoming legible only through
the degradation of the institutions themselves.

## The structural limit

The deepest weakness belongs up front rather than in a footnote, so here it is
plainly.

When a coordinated faction exceeds roughly a third of the compact and
simultaneously controls the mediator pool, the observer corps, the archive, and
the deliberative body, the institutional architecture becomes the instrument of
that faction. No institutional design defeats this.

No historical regime solved it either. The checks of the Roman Republic fell to
it. The Iroquois fractured under external pressure that forced members into
incompatible factions. The Reformation produced a counter-cooperative faction
large enough to break the substrate. Modern democracies have voted themselves
into tyranny once the threshold was crossed.

The machinery here raises the threshold, slows the takeover, and gives the jester
a chance to name what is happening. It does not eliminate the vulnerability. The
design works in the normal case of fallen-but-recoverable actors below the
collusion threshold. Above it, the compact survives only through the kind of
substrate-level renewal the protocol can afford conditions for and cannot itself
produce.

## The rest of the exposure

*The substrate thesis is provisional rather than proven*, being abductive, drawn
from a finite and selection-weighted case set, with confounds including literacy,
trade density, imperial backing, and geography not yet controlled for on the
merits.

*Hollowing and feigned sincerity* is the deepest threat and the one most
resistant to procedural countermeasures. Substrate-deference is an appearance
instrument that the most skilled actor games rather than the sincerest.

*Asymmetric psychological cost is a weak filter at scale.* The cost of sustaining
deception is weakest exactly where the compact needs it most, in low-contact,
low-repetition dealings across interplanetary distance.

*The requirement excludes* pro-human, pro-cooperation communities that do not
acknowledge a Creator from full membership. Deliberate, and costly, and stated.

*The bootstrapping problem.* The compact protects preconditions it cannot
generate. It connects communities that already meet them and cannot make them so
where they are absent.

*Spiral speed and contagion.* A purity spiral inside a member community can
outrun the generational credentialing cadence. Only the continuous instruments,
being the jester, the observer corps, and the health indicators, operate at
spiral speed, and they are the least developed part of the system.

*Detection under light-lag* against a capable adversary with perfected mimicry is
bounded by constraining the envelope rather than by any claim of discrimination.

*The frontier asymmetry.* The four categories correct structurally for the
colonial recapitulation without claiming the frontier problem is solved. One half
has closed, since the *ground* of what the compact owes at its frontier no longer
depends on reciprocal exposure. The practice remains scoped and the asymmetry
itself is unchanged.

*No verdict on a member's interior until it touches the covenant.* The stop is a
commitment rather than a shortfall, and the compact can carry a member whose
interior life it would not defend, knowingly.

*Operational gaps.* Reconciling in-person recognition with light-lag, the
mediator training pipeline, a scheduler for the re-affirmation cadence, and above
all the absence of any pilot.

## The seven holes

The corpus tracks a standing list, and it is worth keeping the distinction
between them rather than flattening it.

Three are substantively answered. *Substrate coherence under pluralism*, which is
type-invariant and content-substantive. *Preventing engineered civil religion*,
which the jester supports and the fifth movement forecloses. And *succession
under doctrinal drift*, where the succession ceremony is itself the re-binding
and a community that cannot honestly re-affirm loses standing as the drift
becomes legible.

One is answered through a separate research artifact, being *the machine model*.

And three are scoped without being solved. *Detection under light-lag, opacity,
and mimicry*. *Colonialism and the frontier asymmetry*. And *the bootstrap
condition*, at both the human layer, where a compact must form among communities
that already meet its preconditions, and the machine layer, where machines carry
first-contact back to principals rather than authorizing anything new.

## Where this ends

Kith-Covenant is research-backed and protocol-ready. The arguments are in place,
the historical basis is drawn, the mechanisms are specified and largely built,
and the open questions are named. What remains is iterative engineering carried
forward against simulation that stresses the compact with realistic adversaries,
and then an Earth pilot, because a compact that binds where exit is one phone
call away has demonstrated something that no amount of specification can.

What the compact cannot do is make people good. It protects the communities in
which character is formed, connects communities that already meet its
preconditions, and buys time and legibility when things go wrong. Everything
above that belongs to the people running it and to whatever each of them holds
above itself.

That is the whole claim, and it is deliberately smaller than the machinery might
suggest.
