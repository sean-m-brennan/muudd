# Crossing to the compact

The compact tier does not know this tier exists, and keeping it that way is the
point of the whole arrangement. It sees communities and evidence about
communities, and it never learns where either came from.

That is achievable only if our border is narrow and speaks the vocabulary of the
compact rather than a vocabulary of its own. The border is important and still
narrow. Six kinds of artifact cross it, all of them record types the compact
already understands, and nothing else does. There is no adapter protocol, no
negotiation, and no handshake, since a polity emits a signed payload and
something on the other side appends it.

The confinement is structural as well as conventional. Exactly one crate in this
tier speaks the record types of the compact, and every other crate depends only
on the neutral base. We check that property mechanically rather than asserting
it, and we check it again whenever a crate is added.

## The six artifacts

*A community identity.* A chartered polity is handed off as a self-signed
community identity, being a root identifier and its delegation graph.
Self-signed is the correct shape here, because a community exists by its own
act. A compact is constituted by its members instead, and is co-signed
accordingly, which is a distinction the tier above draws and this one respects.

*A concern.* When our cohesion monitor grades erosion at the concern level, and
only then, we emit the pre-indictment conduct flag the compact already routes,
under a neutral bearing that names coherence erosion.

What it carries is early warning, and deliberately not a verdict about fission.
A concern is about conduct, where a split is about whether a community still
exists, which is a category a conduct record should not be made to carry. We
decline to decide a split at all, because no measure can make that judgement and
a measure pretending to would be worse than none.

*A fission.* A ratified split emits no new type. We charter the successor
polities, emitting some number of new community identities not fewer than two, a
signed successor link to each, index-aligned, and the dissolution of the
predecessor charter naming them all.

Making it N-ary rather than binary matters. A three-way split is one act rather
than two chained fissions recording an intermediate polity nobody ever lived in.
Four guards apply, being arity, distinctness, non-collision and verification.
The successors must number at least two, must be pairwise distinct, and must not
collide with the predecessor. *Every* successor must carry a verifying self-signature, absent
which a link is signed under the root of the predecessor to an identity whose
own signature does not verify.

From the far side, the compact sees several new communities seeking recognition
and an old one going quiet, which is entirely within vocabulary it already has.

Fusion is the mirror, taking some number of predecessors not fewer than two and
merging them into one community identity, with a successor link and a charter
dissolution per predecessor naming the merged polity as sole successor. It
introduces no new vocabulary, because it emits the same three record types a
fission does, so the compact simply sees several communities go quiet and one
new one seek recognition.

*An envoy delegation.* An effective envoy standing becomes the speaker
delegation of the compact, scoped as an envoy. This is the only border artifact
an office produces, and it takes the derived envoy state rather than the
archive, which keeps the crate that speaks compact vocabulary from having to
read the log.

*A member roll.* The published roll of the polity, under its root key.

*A rite.* The five-movement ceremony of the polity becomes the records the intra
rite of the compact is held to, being a reciter delegation, a witness registry,
and the recitation itself. This is the youngest artifact and the one whose
absence was the most consequential, since the ceremony that admits a community
to a compact takes a sealed intra rite as its input. A polity could therefore be
recognized in the compact and admitted to nothing.

Two rulings decide its shape. The office that presides over the rite of the
polity is the office that carries one across, so the inductor recites and the
speaker does not, a speaker being for representation. The witnesses are the
members who were present for every movement of the ceremony the polity actually
held, so the record answers to the men who were there rather than to whoever
holds a key.

The commitments travel with it. The commitment keys of the polity become the
fourth movement of the recitation unchanged, the two tiers happening to carry
the same shape for a commitment, so the compact grades its members against the
object each one actually holds rather than against a taxonomy fixed for them.

What does not travel is a signature. The two authorizations are signed under the
root key of the polity, and the recitation crosses unsigned, since sealing it
needs the key of the reciter and of every witness and our border holds neither.
Whoever holds the ceremony signs, which is the division the compact already
draws for every community under it.

## Reasons for crossing the roll

The last artifact is the youngest, and the reason for it is worth following,
because it is a case of one tier honestly recording a limit and naming the other
as the fix.

The whistleblower channel of the compact tier proves that a source is an insider
by a ring signature over the key set of the accused community. That set was the
root key plus the live speaker delegations. The compact models communities and
speakers and has no member roster, so an insider holding no delegation was not
in the ring, and a community that had delegated nobody had no anonymous channel.

The compact recorded that as an honest limit and named this tier as the feed,
since a polity is the thing that does model membership. Every member here
carries a self-resolving identifier, so each verifying key comes out of the
identifier itself and nothing else has to travel.

*What measuring first found was the larger half.* The compact recomputed the
insider set at verification time against the *current* archive tip, while
evaluating it as of the filing time, and required exact set equality. So the
accused community, the one party with a motive, held two levers. Backdate one
delegation ahead of the filing, and every filing against it stopped verifying.
Backdate a revocation, and the suspect set around a source narrowed after the
fact. We measured both, and going from three keys to four invalidated a filing
where going from three to two narrowed one.

A member roll bolted onto that would have inherited the defect and added a
second lever to it. The ring is now computed over the records preceding the
filing in insertion order, which is the principle the escalation ladder of the
compact already stated, that authorizing an earlier transition never sees a
later one, and the principle the observer channel had not applied.

It is the same failure our guardian co-signature rules name on this side of the
boundary, and the sentence generalizes past both: authority must be a property
of the log rather than of when you looked.

*Rolls are unioned and never superseded*, which is the rule of the compact tier
and the one that decides what publishing a roll here means. Once named an
insider, always an insider, because the alternative hands a polity a way to
shrink the suspect set around a member the day after he left. So our roll
shrinks on a departure and the ring there does not, and a departed member keeps
the channel he had.

One honest limit remains, and we state it rather than paper over it. A roll is
the polity speaking about itself, and the compact checks that the publisher is
who he claims to be rather than that the roll is true.

## Deliberate omissions

Naming the omissions is as useful as naming the artifacts.

No observation crosses. The evidence a monitor gathered stays on this side, and
what goes over is a graded conclusion with a bearing. The compact tier has
observer machinery of its own and needs no second stream of raw readings from
underneath it.

No reputation number crosses. Earned reputation is a measurement, conferred
standing is an adjudication, and wiring one to the other numerically is the
category error this tier was built to avoid rather than to commit at its own
border.

No office crosses except as a delegation, being the envoy who speaks and the
inductor who recites. Internal offices are internal, and the compact has no
business knowing who convenes a member community or who keeps its charter. What
crosses is the authorization rather than the office.

No archive dependency crosses. We emit serialized signed payloads that a
consumer on the far side appends, and we never depend on the archive crate of
the compact tier, which keeps the coupling to record shapes rather than to
storage.

## The end of Part III

A polity now exists in the strong sense. It has a boundary that somebody
ratified and that says what it excluded, a ground it stands under, and a record
of who withdrew if anybody did. It has offices conferred by its roll and lapsing
with it, a rite by which members actually receive what it stands for, and a
measure of whether that rite is getting cheaper. It has a monitor that can tell
the difference between quiet and stopped. It can hand all of that to a stranger
as six kinds of artifact in a vocabulary the stranger already speaks, the last
of them being the rite that makes it admissible rather than merely recognizable.

What it does not have is any relation to that stranger. Two chartered polities
on opposite sides of a ridge, or of an orbit, share no history, no founding, and
quite possibly no ground. They cannot merge, cannot submit to one another, and
cannot appeal upward, since nobody stands above both. They will nonetheless need
each other.

That is the subject of Part IV, and it is the oldest unsolved problem in this
book.
