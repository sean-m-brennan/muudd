### What was done to it, and why

Two operations, both reproducible:

1. **Cut to the first 48 seconds** with a stream copy — no re-encode:

   ```bash
   ffmpeg -y -t 48 -i happy-beats-business-moves-vol-9-by-ende-dot-app.mp3 \
       -c copy cut.mp3
   ```

   The video runs 46.0s and the bed fades to silence at 44.8s.

2. **Low-passed** at 2600 Hz:

   ```bash
   ffmpeg -y -i happy-beats-business-moves-vol-9-by-ende-dot-app.mp3 \
       -af "lowpass=f=2600" -c:a libmp3lame -q:a 3 \
       happy-beats-business-moves-vol-9-by-ende-dot-app.lowpass.mp3
   ```

   The reason is tonal rather than technical. Measured off the first render, the
   gaps between narration lines already sat 6–10 dB under the voice spans
   (voice ≈ −23 dB mean; gaps −29.8 to −33.2 dB), so the *level* posture was
   never the problem. What the level could not fix was character: this is a
   bright, cheerful business-moves cut, and next to a constitutional document
   read aloud its high end sat beside the voice rather than under it. Rolling
   the top off leaves the pulse and the bass — which is all this edit asks of
   it — and removes the brightness that was arguing with the tone.

   Both files are kept in `composition/assets/music/`. The composition plays
   the `.lowpass.mp3`; the unfiltered cut is beside it so the treatment can be
   re-judged or reversed without going back to the skill's asset tree.

The bed opens to 0.24 in the gaps and ducks to 0.11 for every voice span,
dropping to 0.09 through the refusal. Those numbers are set after the filter,
not before it.

`composition/assets/audio-data.js` — the per-frame band data the audio-reactive
layer reads — is extracted from the **filtered** file, so what the picture
breathes with is what the viewer hears. Regenerate with:

```bash
python3 scripts/make_audio_data.py \
    composition/assets/music/happy-beats-business-moves-vol-9-by-ende-dot-app.lowpass.mp3 \
    --output composition/assets/audio-data.js --seconds 46.3
```

(needs `ffmpeg` and `numpy`; `librosa` is not required.)

## Sound effects

From the bundled `/brag` skill library (`skills/brag/assets/sfx/`), source
[Kenney](https://kenney.nl/). Only `low` high-frequency-risk files were used,
per `sfx-analysis.md`:

`rollover2` (presumption cards), `bong_001` (spine cards), `click2` (the five
movements), `impactSoft_medium_003` (the refusal), `impactSoft_medium_001` (the
`kc-*` highlight and the wordmark).

Nothing rings, nothing swells, and nothing fires on a moment the voice is
already carrying.

## Narration

Synthesised voiceover, Kokoro `am_michael` at speed 0.96, generated once into
`composition/assets/voice/`. These WAVs are **not** regenerated by `build.sh` —
the render deliberately skips the TTS backend — so they are tracked as source,
not as build output.

Clip 7 is generated from the text `Ethnee.` rather than `Ethne.`: Kokoro reads
the correct spelling as *Ethan*, and `Ethnee` gives ETH-nee, which is the Greek.
The spelling on screen is `Ethne` throughout.

Transcripts were taken with `whisper.cpp` (`base.en`) and independently with
`faster-whisper` (`base.en`); the two agree. All seven clips were delivered
whole — nothing was dropped or compressed. The transcripts are in
`brag-plan.md` under "As synthesized".
