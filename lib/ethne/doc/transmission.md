# The rite

Being added to a list is not the same as receiving what the list is a list of. A
community that treats the two as one thing is a community whose members hold no
particular relation to what it stands for, and it will discover this at the
moment it most needs them to.

So we hold them apart. *Admission* puts a man on the roll, by a majority plus
his own consent. *Induction* is the distinct later step by which he acquires the
substrate, witnessed by the members who already hold it. A man can be admitted
and not yet inducted, which is a normal and legible state rather than an error.

The separation is not decorative, and measuring it is what showed as much.
Before the rule that closes this chapter, no authority rule anywhere in the tier
read the inducted flag, so members who had never received the substrate could
govern the polity that substrate constitutes. The ceremony existed and decided
nothing.

What follows is the rite itself, which has five movements, then the rules that
keep a rite from being cheap, then the renewal that recurs for the life of a
member, and lastly the one thing our whole apparatus finally gates, which is who
may decide anything.

## Two ceremonies, one record kind

An `Affirmation` record covers both ceremonies, distinguished by kind.

An *induction* happens once per member, witnessed by a majority co-signing
alongside the inductee. The ratifiers of a charter are inducted by the act of
ratifying it, which is the only sensible bootstrap, since our founders cannot be
inducted by members who do not yet exist.

A *re-affirmation* is self-signed renewal by an already-inducted member. It
recurs, and since the rule described below it carries a full re-recitation of
the five movements and is refused without one.

## The five movements

A `RiteMovementRecorded` entry carries one movement of one member of one rite,
and there are exactly five, taken from the recognition doctrine of the compact
tier, which places them at this layer and says they carry their full content
here.

They are naming, acknowledgment, recognition, commitment, and submission. In
full: the naming of the substrate, the acknowledgment of fallenness, the
recognition of others as peers, the commitment to the common virtues as
dispositions, and the submission of the polity itself, which is not the
ultimate.

Each variant carries only what its own movement can be held to. The first names
a substrate, the fourth names what is committed to, and the fifth names the
anchor. The second and third carry nothing, because no rule can read contrition
or recognition, and a field a rule cannot check is a field that invites a rule
to check the wrong thing.

The inductee recites and signs, and everyone attending co-signs. Attendees must
already hold the substrate, since a rite conducted by men who have not received
what they are transmitting transmits nothing.

## Cited, not carried

We cite movements by content identifier rather than carrying them inside the
completion record, which reverses our rule for guardian evidence in the previous
chapter.

The reason is the reason that rule exists. A citation is unsafe only when what
it points at can be pruned, and the prunable predicate in this tier matches
nothing but an observation. A movement is not an observation, so a citation to
one can never dangle.

Citing is also what puts the rite in the log as it happens, one movement at a
time, rather than as a single record assembled afterward by somebody who was
there.

## The checks, and what they deliberately omit

Order is semantic. The first movement opens a rite, and re-opening abandons an
unfinished attempt, so that a stalled ceremony cannot deadlock a member forever.

Duration is never required, because the doctrine asks that the movements happen
rather than that they take a week.

What we do check is coherence with the charter, and never content. The first
movement must name the chartered substrate, the fourth must commit to exactly
the commitments in force, and the fifth must name the chartered anchor, or name
nothing at all where the charter is bare. Fallenness and recognition are
presence-only.

That distinction runs throughout this tier. Whether a recitation coheres with
the record is checkable, where whether it is sincere is not, and a rule
pretending otherwise would be measuring the wrong thing while looking rigorous.

A witness counts only if he is present for every movement. The completion cites
the five identifiers of its rite in order and may name only the intersection of
their attendance, because five records with disjoint signers is a rite nobody
attended.

## Renewal

Re-affirmation was previously missing its rite and actively refusing one, so we
rewarded the empty form and rejected the substantive one.

Now a movement declares which ceremony it belongs to, and we check the claim
against the state of the member rather than inferring it. A renewal must recite
its own rite, an induction cannot pass one off as its own, and one re-recitation
completes one affirmation.

Attendance on a renewal is *recorded* rather than required, which differs from
the rule induction runs under. It must be exactly who was present throughout, so
a member can neither over-report nor under-report it, and it may be nobody at
all. The signer stays the member alone, because renewal is the act no one else
may condition.

A reciter may not attend his own ceremony. That rule only ever applies to
renewals, since an inductee could never attend his.

The coherence checks bind renewals too, and that is what makes renewal do work
rather than merely recur. A polity that amends its commitments and never
re-recites becomes visible at its next renewal, because the recitation will name
commitments that are no longer the ones in force.

## The substrate is required of whoever governs

This is the rule the whole chapter exists for.

Two derived quantities are the single representation every deliberative
threshold in the tier reads, being the seasoned members and the deciding weight.
An un-inducted member may not hold an office, refused at conferral and dropped
from the effective holders, lapsing and reviving exactly as the roll rule does.
An un-inducted member may not attend a rite.

The denominator is the *inducted* roll rather than the whole roll, and we chose
that deliberately. Padding the roll while carrying no weight would make
admission a way to freeze a polity, which is the worse hazard of the two
available.

The machine-majority question still reads the whole roll, because it asks what
the polity is made of rather than who decides in it.

## Reading a polity back

Three read models expose the state we create here, and although all three read
the same records, they answer different questions.

The *affirmation state* reports, for each current member, whether he is inducted
and when he last affirmed.

*Stale members* names the un-inducted and the overdue against a freshness
cadence, so a community that stops re-affirming shows erosion. That cadence is a
query parameter rather than a charter term, which keeps a judgment about how
often is often enough out of a document that cannot be amended.

The *rite state* reports each rite with its movements, its span, meaning the
cost of the ceremony in time, who attended throughout, how many attendees it
lost, and which rites were begun and never finished.

That last group of numbers is what the next chapter measures. A ceremony getting
shorter, a turnout thinning, and a rite that men drift away from partway through
are all things a polity can do to itself without anybody deciding to, and they
are visible only because we record the rite as it happens rather than
summarizing it after.
