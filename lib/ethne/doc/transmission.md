# The rite

Being added to a list is not the same as receiving what the list is a list of. A
community that treats the two as one thing is a community whose members hold no
particular relation to what it stands for, and it will discover this at the
moment it most needs them to.

So this tier separates them. *Admission* puts somebody on the roll, by a
majority plus their own consent. *Induction* is the distinct later step by which
they actually acquire the substrate, witnessed by the members who already hold
it. A person can be admitted and not yet inducted, and that is a normal and
legible state rather than an error.

The separation is not decorative, and measuring it is what showed as much.
Before the rule that closes this chapter, the inducted flag was read by no
authority rule anywhere in the tier. Members who had never received the
substrate could nonetheless govern the polity that substrate constitutes. The
ceremony existed and decided nothing.

What follows is the rite itself, which has five movements; then the rules that
keep a rite from being cheap; then the renewal that recurs for the life of a
member; and lastly the one thing the whole apparatus finally gates, which is who
may decide anything.

## Two ceremonies, one record kind

An `Affirmation` record covers both ceremonies, distinguished by kind.

An *induction* happens once per member. It is witnessed by a majority co-signing
alongside the inductee. The ratifiers of a charter are inducted by the act of
ratifying it, which is the only sensible bootstrap: the founders cannot be
inducted by members who do not yet exist.

A *re-affirmation* is self-signed renewal by an already-inducted member. It
recurs, and since the rule described below it carries a full re-recitation of
the five movements and is refused without one.

## The five movements

A `RiteMovementRecorded` entry carries one movement of one member of one rite,
and there are exactly five, taken from the recognition doctrine of the compact
tier, which places them at precisely this layer and says they carry their full
content here.

They are naming, acknowledgment, recognition, commitment, and submission. In
full, they are the naming of the substrate, the acknowledgment of fallenness, the
recognition of others as peers, the commitment to the common virtues as
dispositions, and the submission of the polity itself, which is not the
ultimate.

Each variant carries only what its own movement can be held to. The first names
a substrate. The fourth names what is committed to. The fifth names the anchor.
The second and third carry nothing at all, because no rule can read contrition
or recognition, and a field a rule cannot check is a field that invites a rule
to check the wrong thing.

The inductee recites and signs. Everyone attending co-signs. And attendees must
already hold the substrate, since a rite conducted by people who have not
received what they are transmitting transmits nothing.

## Cited, not carried

Movements are cited by content identifier rather than carried inside the
completion record, which is deliberately the opposite of the rule for guardian
evidence in the previous chapter.

The reason is the reason that rule exists. A citation is unsafe only when what
it points at can be pruned, and the prunable predicate in this tier matches
nothing but an observation. A movement is not an observation, so a citation to
one can never dangle.

Citing is also what puts the rite in the log as it happens, one movement at a
time, rather than as a single record assembled afterward by somebody who was
there.

## What is checked, and what deliberately is not

Firstly, order is semantic. The first movement opens a rite, and re-opening
abandons an unfinished attempt so that a stalled ceremony cannot deadlock a
member forever.

Second, duration is never required. The doctrine asks that the movements happen,
not that they take a week.

Lastly, what *is* checked is coherence with the charter, and never content. The
first movement must name the chartered substrate. The fourth must commit to
exactly the commitments in force. The fifth must name the chartered anchor, and
must name nothing at all where the charter is bare. Fallenness and recognition
are presence-only.

That distinction is the same one drawn throughout this tier. Whether a
recitation coheres with the record is checkable. Whether it is sincere is not,
and a rule that pretended otherwise would be measuring the wrong thing while
looking rigorous.

A witness counts only if present for every movement. The completion cites the
five identifiers of its rite in order and may name only the intersection of
their attendance, because five records with disjoint signers is a rite nobody
attended.

## Renewal

Re-affirmation was previously not merely missing its rite but actively refusing
it. The tier rewarded the empty form and rejected the substantive one.

Now a movement declares which ceremony it belongs to, and the claim is checked
against the state of the member rather than inferred from it. A renewal must
recite its own rite, an induction cannot pass one off as its own, and one
re-recitation completes one affirmation.

Attendance on a renewal is *recorded* rather than required, which is a different
rule from the one induction runs under. It must be exactly who was present
throughout, so a member can neither over-report nor under-report it. It may be
nobody at all. And the signer stays the member alone, because renewal is
precisely the act no one else may condition.

A reciter may not attend their own ceremony. That rule only ever bites on
renewals, since an inductee could never attend theirs.

The coherence checks bind renewals too, and that is what makes renewal do work
rather than merely recur. A polity that amends its commitments and never
re-recites becomes visible at its next renewal, because the recitation will name
commitments that are no longer the ones in force.

## The substrate is required of whoever governs

This is the rule the whole chapter exists for.

Two derived quantities are the single representation every deliberative
threshold in the tier reads, being the seasoned members and the deciding weight.
An un-inducted member may not hold an office, refused at conferral and dropped
from the effective holders, lapsing and reviving exactly as the roll rule does.
An un-inducted member may not attend a rite.

The denominator is the *inducted* roll rather than the whole roll, and that
choice is deliberate. Padding the roll while carrying no weight would make
admission a way to freeze a polity, which is the worse hazard of the two
available.

The machine-majority question still reads the whole roll, because it asks what
the polity is made of rather than who decides in it.

## Reading a polity back

Three read models expose the state this chapter creates.

Although all three read the same records, they answer different questions. The
*affirmation state* reports, for each current member, whether they are inducted
and when they last affirmed.

*Stale members* names the un-inducted and the overdue against a freshness
cadence, so a community that stops re-affirming shows erosion. The cadence is a
query parameter rather than a charter term, which keeps a judgement about how
often is often enough out of a document that cannot be amended.

The *rite state* reports each rite with its movements, its span, meaning the
cost of the ceremony in time, who attended throughout, how many attendees it
lost, and which rites were begun and never finished.

That last group of numbers is what the next chapter measures. A ceremony getting
shorter, a turnout thinning, and a rite that people drift away from partway
through are all things a polity can do to itself without anybody deciding to,
and they are visible only because the rite is recorded as it happens rather than
summarized after.
