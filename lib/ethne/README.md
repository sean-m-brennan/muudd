# Ethne

**Ethne** (Greek ἔθνη, "peoples") is the **polity tier**: it turns a set of cooperating agents into a *community* — a bounded, self-governing entity that stands for a substrate, acts with one voice, and persists across the turnover of its members.

It sits between a trust substrate and a federation. Below it, a substrate like [autonomous-trust](../autonomous_trust/README.md) produces *emergent* trust among nodes but no community: its groups and gateway subtrees are topological clusters, not covenant-bearing entities with a membership roll and a standing. Above it, [kith-covenant](../kith-covenant/README.md) governs relations *between* communities and presumes each one is already coherent, persistent, and able to act as a unit. Ethne is the tier that produces the entity KC presumes, out of whatever cooperation the substrate supplies.

```
   autonomous-trust        ethne                     kith-covenant
   "who cooperates?"  ──►  "what is a community —  ──►  "how do communities
                            and what keeps it one?"      treat one another?"
```

The governing discipline is **ratify emergence, do not replace it**: ethne reads the emergent structure and offers it back to the members for deliberate ratification into a charter. Emergence proposes; the polity ratifies; KC recognizes.

Ethne is a **separate tier by design**, so that kith-covenant depends on neither ethne nor autonomous-trust: ethne emits only KC-native artifacts (a `CommunityIdentity`, recognition records, and a `Concern` on a coherence breakdown), and autonomous-trust is one *pluggable* substrate provider among others.

## Status

Design decisions D1–D29 are locked and **every open question Q1–Q9 is resolved**; see **[doc/design.md](doc/design.md)** for the full architecture: the gap it fills, the decoupling rule, the five responsibilities (founding & charter, substrate articulation, internal offices, cohesion monitoring, longevity), what it reuses from KC vs. what is new, the machine-guardian model, the lifecycle, and the open questions.

Built so far — the Rust workspace and the Python surface over it (D2):

- **`en-core`** — base crate; re-exports the neutral `kc-core` primitives ethne builds on (D1: kc-core only).
- **`en-archive`** — append-only, content-addressed log, generic over the entry type (the neutral archive mechanism, forked thin from `kc-archive`; verification/DID-resolution are domain concerns for later slices). Persistence lives here as of **D17**: a versioned JSON dump/load, generic over the entry type, plus `dump_json_from(start)` / `append_json` so a host holding a cursor appends what is *new* rather than re-serializing the whole log — which is what a steadily-appended observation record needs. The store itself stays strictly append-only with no removal path; compaction is a charter-level operation that returns a new archive (see `en-charter`).
- **`en-charter`** — the polity **founding** primitive (keystone). Records (`Substrate`, `Commitment`, `FoundingProposal`, `PolityCharter`, `CommitmentAmendment`, the roll-lifecycle `MembershipChange` (`Admit` / `DepartVoluntary` / `Expel`), and the terminal `CharterDissolution`), the *ratify-not-replace* API (a `FoundingProposal` carries no authority; only `ratify` + co-signing yields a charter), signature verification (`did:key` self-resolves), state derivation (`charter_state` — the *live* roll = founding + admits − departures, plus commitments, dissolution + successors), and the authority rules: genesis-singleton, majority ratification, genesis-immutable substrate + founding commitments, asymmetric amendment (add at a majority, remove at a majority + 1), an asymmetric membership lifecycle (Admit at a majority + the joiner's consent, DepartVoluntary self-signed, Expel at a majority + 1 — all under a self-govern floor), an internal-office lifecycle (`OfficeChange` over `Office { Convener, Inductor, EnvoyAuthorizer, CharterKeeper, Other }` — Confer at a majority + the holder's consent, and in a **machine-majority** polity never on a derived member with no guardian (D15), Resign self-signed, Revoke at a majority with the holder excluded; the roll confers, offices never confer offices, so the founding roll bootstraps the first ones with no regress; a holding is effective only while its holder is on the roll), majority-of-roll dissolution — whose **named successors are checked since D25**: no duplicate (a fan-out nobody counts is a count that misleads), no self-successor (a polity that survives its own dissolution has not dissolved), and no successor drawn from its own roll (a member is a person or a machine, not a polity — the third of the closure family after D15's guardian loop and D23's substrate anchor), while naming *no* successor stays valid because that is the difference between a fission and a death — and terminal-dissolution (no charter event after a polity is dissolved). `office_state` derives who holds what — and since **D14** an office is not merely held but *used*: where an `Inductor` is effectively held, one of its holders other than the inductee must witness an induction, and the roll may proceed without them only at a majority + 1 (a *defeasible* gate — an office may raise the bar for the rite, never block it), while an `EnvoyAuthorizer` holds a *delegated power* — one holder's signature stands in for the roll's majority on an `EnvoyAuthorization` (`EnvoyKind { Authorize, Resign, Revoke }`: who may speak for the polity beyond it, in ethne's own vocabulary, with the envoy always co-signing, never as a unilateral self-appointment, and the key checked to derive the DID). Because the majority route always stays open, filling that office costs the roll nothing; `Convener` and `CharterKeeper` gate nothing, and self-signed acts are never gated. `envoy_state` derives the standings in force — effective only while the envoy is on the roll and unexpired. **The node→guardian edge is chartered** (**D15**): a `Derivation` says which members are derived (machine) parties and which human each answers to — ratified at founding, with every named guardian co-signing, because answerability for a machine is accepted and never assigned — and moved afterwards by `GuardianChange` records (`Designate` at a majority + the guardian's consent, `Rotate` with the *incoming* guardian consenting, `Release` by either the departing guardian alone or a majority recording that the human is gone). `guardian: None` is an honest chartered state, not a missing value; a `Release` clears the edge and keeps the designation, since a machine does not become human by losing its human. No machine may guard a machine, and nothing may guard itself. `guardian_state` derives the effective picture and answers `is_machine_majority`, which is what conditions the office rule above. **A claimed edge carries what showed it** — a `GuardianChange` that asserts one presents the signed `Observation` in which the substrate reported it, or declares `Unbacked { reason }`; evidence is *carried* rather than cited by CID, because D17 pruning would dangle a citation and make authority a property of when you looked. **Founding derivations are gated the same way** (**D27**), and there the asymmetry inverts: `Unbacked` is permitted and honest but earns nothing, because at founding a self-report would *buy* D23's anchor exemption — measured, one asserted edge with nothing behind it chartered a whole machine cluster with no ground, both passes green and grading `Guarded`. And the observer may be neither the polity nor anyone on its roll, since requiring a signed observation was not enough on its own: a founder signed a real one about its own cluster and bought the exemption anyway — the closure shape D23 refused for the anchor and D25 for a successor, reaching the *evidence* this time. A named guardian observing the edge it is about to accept does count: it is not on the roll, it co-signs either way, and what it asserts by observing is its own answerability — interest disqualifies praise, not an admission (**D24**). **Transmission** (the `Affirmation` record over `AffirmationKind { Induction, ReAffirmation }`): admission puts someone on the roll, while *induction* is the distinct step by which they acquire the substrate — witnessed by a majority co-signing alongside the inductee, with the charter's **ratifiers** inducted by ratifying it — and *re-affirmation* is self-signed renewal by an already-inducted member. **Since D29 the rite has movements and the substrate is required of those who govern.** A fourteenth entry, `RiteMovementRecorded`, carries one movement of one member's rite over `Movement { Substrate { names }, Fallenness, Recognition, CommonVirtue { commits_to }, NonUltimacy { names_anchor } }` — `recognition_protocols.md`'s five movements, which that document places at exactly this tier ("the five movements carry their full content at this layer"). Each variant carries only what its own movement can be held to, so a rule cannot check the wrong field. The inductee recites and signs; everyone attending co-signs, and **attendees must already hold the substrate**. Movements are **cited, not carried** — deliberately the opposite of D15/D27's guardian evidence, and for the reason that rule exists: a citation is unsafe only when what it points at can be pruned, and `is_prunable` matches nothing but an observation. Citing is also what puts the rite in the log *as it happens*. Order is semantic (movement 1 opens a rite, and re-opening abandons an unfinished attempt so it cannot deadlock a member); duration is never required, because the doctrine asks that the movements happen, not that they take a week. What *is* checked is **coherence with the charter, never content**: movement 1 must name the chartered substrate, movement 4 must commit to exactly the commitments in force, movement 5 must name the chartered anchor (and nothing where the charter is `Bare`), while fallenness and recognition are presence-only — no rule can read contrition. And **a witness counts only if present for every movement**: the completion cites its rite's five CIDs in order and may name only the intersection of their attendance, since five records with disjoint signers is a rite nobody attended (D28's arithmetic, one record over). Measured before any of it was written: an induction carried witnesses and one timestamp, so a rite held over a week with an officer presiding and one performed in a second by a majority signing a blank produced **identical record shapes**, both authorized. **The substrate is also now required of whoever governs** — `prior.inducted` had been read by exactly one rule, so a roll of three inducted founders plus six who never were let the six confer `Inductor` on one of their own, amend the commitments, expel a founder, and preside over a newcomer's induction, every pass green. `seasoned_members` and `deciding_weight` are the one representation every deliberative threshold reads; an un-inducted member may not hold an office (refused at conferral *and* dropped from `effective_holders`, lapsing and reviving as the roll rule does) and may not attend a rite. The **denominator is the inducted roll**, not the whole roll: padding it while carrying no weight would make admission a way to freeze a polity, which is the worse hazard. `is_machine_majority` still reads the whole roll, because it asks what the polity is made of rather than who decides. `affirmation_state` reports each current member's induction status and last affirmation, and `stale_members(cadence_seconds, as_of)` names the un-inducted and the overdue, so a community that stops re-affirming shows erosion (the freshness cadence is a query parameter, not a charter term). `rite_state` reports each rite's movements, its **span** — the ceremony's cost in time, which v1 could not express at all — who attended throughout, how many attendees it lost, and which rites were begun and never finished. **What a monitor saw is recorded** (**D16**): a ninth entry, `Observed(Signed<Observation>)`, carries one observer's readings at one instant — perspectival evidence, in the sense KC's observer reports are, so it asserts nothing about the polity, no majority is involved (an observation is not a decision), and the observer need not be a member, since a monitor that had to sit inside the roll could be silenced by expulsion; the one rule is that it be signed by exactly the observer it names. `observation_window(since, as_of)` reads those back as a window with a span, a per-member reading count, and each member's `unattended_span` — which honestly distinguishes *never observed* (not evaluated) from *observed but never attended*, and which is **clamped at the window's `pruned_before` horizon** (D17) so that **pruning may only ever weaken a signal, never strengthen one**. That clamp is not a precaution: `last_attested` is a max over the window's stamps, so dropping the interval holding the freshest one would start the absence earlier and make it *longer* — pruning manufacturing a Concern. And `decay_trends` derives, from the polity's own signed records alone with no provider involved, a widening affirmation gap (judged against the **median** of prior gaps), a thinning induction witness turnout (never inferred from a single point), and a roll-churn rate — the shape borrowed from `kc-reputation`'s `ceremony_cost_decay` one tier up, hedges included. **The observation record can be pruned, and only it** (**D17**): an observation decides nothing — `reconstruct` matches it and does nothing — so dropping one cannot rewrite what the polity resolved, which is what makes observations the one inert and therefore the one prunable entry class. A tenth entry, `ObservationsPruned(Signed<ObservationPruning>)`, names a horizon and carries a receipt (how many records went, and a digest over their CIDs), because a Concern crossing to kith-covenant rests on observations and evidence must not be able to vanish without a signature. One effective `CharterKeeper` among the deciders suffices — the delegated power that office had no record to exercise until now — but only beyond `PRUNE_SETTLING_SECONDS` (30 days), while the roll may prune anything at a majority: destroying evidence is the move a captured party makes, so the recent record stays out of any single custodian's reach. That constant is law rather than a tunable threshold, since a floor the party it constrains could lower is not a floor. A horizon must advance, and the receipt is verified while the dropped observations are still present and treated as beyond checking once compaction has run — an honest limit rather than a rule that would flag every historical pruning. `apply_prunings` returns a **new** archive without them; it can never reach a governing record, because the single `is_prunable` predicate the receipt, the authority check and the compaction all read matches nothing but an observation. Relatedly, an attendance stamp may not postdate the observation reporting it — a monitor cannot have seen an attestation that had not yet happened — and that rule is what makes the pruning clamp below sound. **When to prune is a read-model, not a daemon** (**D19**): `prune_recommendation(archive, polity, &RetentionPolicy, as_of)` answers "should I prune, to what horizon, and who may authorize it?" and returns `None` when there is nothing worth doing. It signs nothing — the host feeds the horizon back to the ordinary authoring path — because this tier never calls `now()` and the loop, like the storage backend, stays the host's. What is *not* the host's to reconstruct is the safety floor, and it turned out to have two halves: `live_measure_seconds` (default one day) protects the **span** a live measure reads back over, and `min_witness_watchers` (default 3) protects the **witness count** it also needs. Both are required, which was measured rather than assumed — the same absence at the same horizon grades `Concern` at a dense observation cadence and `Notice` at a sparse one, because D17's clamp preserves the span but nothing preserved the count. The count is per member, since `watchers_for` is, so a thinly-watched member can pin the horizon: the correct answer, and not a deadlock, because the roll may still prune anything by majority — what is refused is *recommending* it. `retain_seconds` defaults to `PRUNE_SETTLING_SECONDS`, the smallest retention that both preserves every observation-based measure and leaves the single-`CharterKeeper` route open (a *shorter* retention keeps more history and is therefore more expensive to authorize, not less), and `min_dropped` (default 100) exists because a pruning record is itself unprunable — measured, pruning eagerly at 1 left the log 2.9× larger than at 100. The recommendation reports every open route, cheapest first, and can only ever propose a horizon `check_authority` accepts. **A founding says what it excluded** (**D21**, resolving Q1): the genesis quorum used to take its denominator from the proposal's *own* roll, so a faction of one could charter a thirty-node cluster as a polity of itself on a single signature — passing both verification passes, with the other twenty-nine not merely outvoted but *unrecorded*. `FoundingProposal` and `PolityCharter` now carry a `BoundaryAttestation`: the `candidate_size` the boundary showed, and a reasoned `Exclusion` for everyone it showed that the roll does not name. Genesis authority gates **the accounting only** — the numbers must add up, exclusions may not repeat, and nobody may be excluded and enrolled at once — while **nothing refuses a charter for being narrow**, because a genuinely separate group of three inside a thirty-node cluster is indistinguishable from a faction capturing it without a substantive judgement no rule can make. What the rule removes is the silence, not the discretion. Since the charter's core has no amendment path, the attestation is genesis-immutable by construction, and `founding_legitimacy` reads the grade back off the charter **alone** — no provider, no cohesion input, so anyone holding it can re-run the judgement forever. It reports `consent_share`, ratifiers over everyone the boundary *showed* rather than over the roll the founders *chose*: the faction of one reads **0.033**. Its `FoundingBasis` (`Corroborated` / `Narrowed` / `SelfAttested`) is deliberately unordered and deliberately not an `ErosionLevel` — a founding is not eroding, and these are not comparable harms. This is accountability, not prevention: the attestation is the proposer's signed *claim*. **And since D22 (resolving Q5) the claim names its author.** D21 could record *that* a boundary came from a reading, never whose — an unsigned label, so flipping one field promoted a convener's bare assertion to a corroborated reading with no provider consulted and both verification passes green. The `Substrate` arm now **carries** its evidence: `Substrate { readings: Vec<Signed<SubstrateReading>> }`, each reading naming a `reader`, signed by that reader alone (D16's rule that nobody may put an observation in somebody else's mouth), and bound to the founding it is offered for — without which an honest reader's account of one cluster replays into somebody else's founding while the signature still verifies. **The rule with teeth is identity, not arithmetic:** the roll ∪ exclusions must equal some signed reading's members *as a set*, where D21 supplied both sides of its own sum. `founding_legitimacy` gains `readers` and `corroborating_readers` — deliberately not a count of readings filed, since a reader who signed a *different* boundary corroborates nothing and the gap between the two numbers is a reader contradicting the founding on its own charter, permanently. That count is a **lens and never a gate**, because `did:key` costs nothing and a captured reader can mint its own corroborators; the same stance kith-covenant took on capture move 1, with independence attestation named as the successor. **And the excluded answer back**: an eleventh entry, `BoundaryDissented`, over `DissentKind { Unnamed, ReasonRejected }` — perspectival evidence in `Observed`'s exact shape, signed by the dissenter alone, no deciders, no majority, and the dissenter need not be a member, since one required to sit inside the roll could be silenced by never being admitted. It is **the only thing in the tier that can contradict an understated `candidate_size`**, which is exactly what D21 recorded as beyond its reach: the archive holds no provider, so the excluded are the only witnesses. Authority checks coherence with the record contested and never its truth — a `ReasonRejected` dissenter must actually be among the exclusions, an `Unnamed` one must be in neither the founding roll nor the exclusions. `founding_dissent` is a separate read-model from `founding_legitimacy` on purpose, since dissent accrues over time and folding it in would destroy the charter-alone property. A dissent may be filed **after dissolution** — the only entry class that may — because otherwise founders silence the excluded by winding the polity up; and it is unprunable for free, since `is_prunable` matches only the observations D17 made forgettable precisely because they are inert. **D23 makes the substrate mean something.** Non-ultimacy is the whole reason the tier has one, and nothing checked it: `substrate.anchor` was read by no rule anywhere, so a polity whose entire ground was two empty strings chartered, as did a fresh cluster of machines with nobody behind any of them, and — the sharper hole, since Q4 was framed as an *absence* problem — one naming **itself** or a member of its own roll as its anchor. Four genesis rules now: the substrate must be **articulated** (name and statement non-empty — presence, never content, since no rule can read a tradition); an anchor may be neither the polity nor anyone on its roll (a ground inside what it grounds is no ground, the guardian loop rule one level out); a roll of nothing but unguarded derived parties **must** name an anchor, the trigger being deliberately narrower than machine-majority because a human roll's statement *is* its unkeyed anchor and a guarded machine already has a consenting human above it; and a present anchor **co-signs the founding** alongside the ratifiers and guardians, because answerability is accepted and never assigned. `founding_legitimacy` gains `grounding` — `Anchored` / `Guarded` / `Held` / `Bare`, descriptive and unordered like `FoundingBasis` — which lives there rather than in a new read-model on exactly D22's test: a genesis-immutable substrate cannot accrue, so the answer is the same forever. `Bare` is legal, and reported so that *permitted* is not mistaken for *grounded*. **And the ground may answer back**: a twelfth entry, `SubstrateDisavowed`, is the named anchor's own signed withdrawal, read by the separate `GroundStanding` (which *does* accrue). It **asserts and cannot un-charter** — nothing rewrites the substrate, reconstructs authority state or dissolves anything, since a withdrawal with that power would be a unilateral kill switch in every anchor's hands; a disowned polity keeps governing itself, on a ground that disowns it, legibly. It shares the dissent's two deliberate properties (permitted after dissolution — the second and only other such class — and unprunable for free) and is *stricter* in one: only the key the charter names may file it, where a dissent must admit anyone because an omitted party is by definition absent from the record. **D24 closes the residue D22 named as its own successor.** D22 made a reading attributable but could not tell one reader from five faces of the same actor: measured, one party minted five `did:key`s, signed five identical readings, and the charter graded `Corroborated` — the highest basis the tier awards — with both passes green, and the chorus cost **32 µs a voice** (500 readers in 16.2 ms). A thirteenth entry `ReaderIndependenceAttested`, over `IndependenceKind { Independent, Controlled }`, carries a third party's account of a named reader, on kith-covenant's move-1 shape (`InterpreterIndependenceAttestation`). Three rules: sole signer, postdates the charter, and the reader must **actually appear among the founding's readings** — a *gate* where KC keeps it a lens, on the line D22's dissent arm draws (coherence with the record spoken about is checkable; the truth of the claim never is). Who *counts* is stricter than KC: an attestation from the polity, the reader itself, or **anyone on the founding roll** does not credit independence, since the founders are precisely the interested party — but it is **accepted and reported separately** rather than refused, because destroying a claim somebody signed serves nobody where merely not crediting it costs nothing. `independent_corroboration` is then the conjunction D22 could not produce — readers who *both* agree with the ratified boundary *and* carry a disinterested voucher, so a chorus of five hundred scores `0` — and it is a separate accruing read-model rather than a field on `founding_legitimacy`, on that model's charter-alone property. The `Controlled` arm lets somebody say the lens is being fooled, and counts **from anyone**: interest disqualifies praise, not warning, so a founder breaking ranks is heard. With this there are **three** entry classes a dissolution does not close, which is enough to name the rule rather than the exceptions — *evidence about the founding* outlives the polity because the founding's legitimacy does, where governing acts die with it. **An attestation is itself unverifiable and does not pretend otherwise**: it moves the cost of faking corroboration from one key to a second party willing to be *named* for it. A lens, never a gate. **D28 gives it the other half of its job.** D15 and D27 each closed a hole in guardian evidence and each named this record as the successor for what was left — that the evidence is still one observer's perspectival reading — and measured, it did not reach: its coherence gate demanded the subject be a *reader of the boundary*, and an observer is not one. The same measurement found the sharper case, and it points **outward**: a stranger with one keypair signs an observation showing any guardian edge and the machine cluster grades `Guarded`; filing three times, the same stranger manufactured the sustained-absence `Concern` that crosses to kith-covenant as a conduct record against a community it had never met. The record is therefore **generalized rather than duplicated** — `IndependenceAttestation { polity, subject, role, … }` over `AttestedRole { BoundaryReader, Observer }`, one entry, one set of stake buckets, one survives-dissolution rule, and a coherence gate parameterized by role because each is checkable against a different part of the log (a boundary reader is fixed at genesis; **observers accrue**, so that half reads the log up to the filing). Two consequences worth naming: **a monitor must speak before it can be vouched for**, since pre-registering a witness is the sockpuppet move with a longer setup; and a key may hold both roles while **a voucher for one is not a voucher for the other**, because vouching is for a party *in a capacity* — what an outsider can speak to is a relationship, not an identity in the abstract. `observer_independence`, `independent_observation` (which also reports the **unvouched**, so that the absence of vouching is visible rather than silently read as its presence) and `independent_grounding` (how many founding edges that earned `Guarded` rest on a monitor somebody was willing to be named for) are the read-models; the last is separate and accruing rather than a field on `founding_legitimacy`, on that model's charter-alone property.
- **`en-substrate`** — the pluggable `SubstrateProvider` interface: the source of the emergent boundary a founding ratifies (`FoundingCandidate` → `propose` → `FoundingProposal`). The **guardian edges travel with the boundary** (D15): a candidate the substrate reads as a derived (machine) party becomes a proposed `Derivation`, so which human answers for which machine is ratified rather than asserted. **The candidate says what the provider *knows***, not merely what it saw (**D18**): `reputation: Option<f64>` distinguishes *unrated* from *rated low*, and `derived: bool` carries the substrate's reading of *kind* independently of the guardian edge — so a machine with nobody behind it is ratified as the unguarded `Derivation` it is, where before it proposed silently as a human. An unrated candidate does not satisfy a demanded reputation floor (a positive floor is a positive assertion, and an absent reading cannot support it); a floor of `0.0` demands nothing. **Change is streamed, not diffed** (**D16**): `SubstrateChange` deltas (a member appearing or vanishing, a reputation or guardian-edge move, an advancing attendance stamp, a boundary going opaque) drain through a `ChangeStream` trait kept deliberately **separate** from `SubstrateProvider` — an empty stream and no stream are different claims, and a convener with a list should not have to fake one. **The boundary's provenance travels with the candidate** (**D21**): `BoundarySource` says whether a boundary is an independent reading or the convener's own word, and it rides on `FoundingCandidate` rather than being stamped at `propose`, because a `ManualFounding` candidate has exactly the same *shape* as a trust service's — only the provider knows which it is. `propose` records whoever the reputation floor trims, with the reason it actually used, distinguishing "reputation 0.21 below the floor 0.30" from "unrated; a floor of 0.30 demands a reading" (D18's semantics, said where an excluded party can read them), and `propose_selecting` narrows to a named subset for one stated reason — so an honest small founding is a first-class act rather than a floor it is not. Ships `ManualFounding`, a convener-driven provider needing no substrate service (and, consistently, no stream); such a polity still charters and the charter simply never pretends it was corroborated — the same honest chartered state as an unguarded machine's `guardian: None`. **Since D22 that is a fact rather than a label** (resolving Q5): `FoundingCandidate` carries no `source` field at all, because the evidence *is* the source — `propose` takes `Vec<Signed<SubstrateReading>>`, empty meaning a convener's assertion. Keeping a separate label would have left a claim and its evidence as two things that could disagree, which is the defect D22 exists to close. `FoundingCandidate::reading()` yields the **unsigned** reading; the provider holds no keys and gains none, since signing is the host's exactly as D20 settled for every other authored act. `ManualFounding` is therefore `ConvenerAsserted` because it holds no reader key — a convener who genuinely obtains a signed reading matching their roll has real corroboration and is welcome to it; what is no longer free is *claiming* one. **Since D27 the guardian edges travel with their backing too**: `build` takes an optional `Signed<Observation>` so a live provider can present what it actually saw, and falls back to `Unbacked { reason }` when no monitor has run — the provider does not mint backing the rule would reject, so an honest bootstrap proposal is one the founders must ground rather than one that fails late.
- **`en-at`** — the `autonomous_trust` provider (D5–D8): an `AtEventSource` seam (mock + `at-ffi`-gated embed/attach coupling) folded into an `AtSubstrateProvider` that maps AT's gateway-reputation subtree to the candidate roll, the verified operator to each member's guardian edge, and AT's attended-now stamp to when that guardian was last confirmed present. It implements `ChangeStream` by diffing each folded event against the view it already holds — the provider is the one place the prior reading is in hand — and emits an `AttendanceStamped` delta only when the stamp actually *advances*, so a repeated identical stamp is not mistaken for renewed presence. The default build links no AT — KC ⟂ AT holds with the adapter present.
- **`en-cohesion`** — the polity's **coherence monitor** (a pure `kc-core`-only read-model). `cohesion_report` grades strain into an `ErosionLevel` (`None` / `Notice` / `Concern`) off the chartered roll vs. the provider's current candidate — attrition, reputation decay, floor breaches, guardians not present, transmission decay (members lapsed on re-affirming the substrate, read off `en-charter`'s `AffirmationState`), lost guardian edges (a chartered node→guardian edge the substrate no longer shows), an opaque boundary capping attrition at `Notice`. Absent guardians are capped at `Notice` outright: a snapshot cannot tell a night shift from an abandoned polity, so the signal may warn but never escalate. Transmission decay carries no such cap and may reach `Concern` — duration is already inside that measure. Nor do lost guardian edges, for a different reason: they compare what the polity *ratified* against what is observed, and only over members the provider still sees, so a vanished member is attrition rather than answerability collapsing and no cap is needed. **D16 adds the four measures that only exist over time**: a widening affirmation gap and a thinning witness turnout, both capped at `Notice` because a trend is a leading indicator whose consequence the affirmation component can already escalate (grading both would count one decay twice); roll churn, a rate rather than a trend, with a full `Notice`/`Concern` pair; and **sustained absence**, the one signal permitted past the attendance cap — a guardian unattended across the recorded window for at least `sustained_absence_seconds` *and* seen by at least `sustained_absence_watchers` **distinct observers** reaches `Concern`. **Watchers, not filings, since D28**: until that was measured the component counted *observations*, so one key filing three times cleared a floor whose entire justification was that somebody had been watching — and a stranger with one keypair could manufacture the escalation this component alone is trusted with. Counting distinct observers is arithmetic rather than a judgement about who they are; whether the watchers are independent *of each other* is the `watchers` lens (`distinct` / `vouched` / `contested`), reported beside the escalation it qualifies and read by no component. That asymmetry is deliberate: gating an escalation on the monitor being vouched for would let a captured polity vouch for nobody and thereby become unwatchable, and an unvouched monitor is the ordinary state of an honest new one. Those two conditions do two different jobs: **the span is the absence; the count is the evidence that somebody was watching while it lasted.** What lifts the snapshot cap is therefore not a looser threshold but a record. **D18 adds the eleventh component and fixes a conflation the other ten rested on**: both reputation measures are now computed over the members the provider actually *rated*, reported as `rated_members`, and say nothing when nobody is rated — before this, an unrated roll was indistinguishable from a failing one and a freshly founded polity graded at `Concern` for being new. The unrated share itself is graded, capped at `Notice` by construction for the attendance reason (a young cohort and a provider that has stopped rating are the same picture), and exists so the two reputation components cannot go *silently* dark. **D23 adds the twelfth, and it is unlike the other eleven**: whether the polity's chartered substrate anchor has withdrawn. Every other measure here grades how far something has drifted; this one grades a single signed act by the one key entitled to make it, so it reaches `Concern` alone and takes **no threshold** at all — event-triggered, like kith-covenant's `contraction_detected`, and it cannot decay back, since there is no withdrawal of a withdrawal. **D29 adds the rite's own cost, which no earlier measure could reach**: a *cheapening ceremony* (the latest completed rite shorter than the first — against the first rather than the median, because a uniform drift moves its own recent history, `gap_widening_since_founding`'s lesson one measure over) and *attrition within a rite* (the polity that turned out for the naming of the substrate and had drifted away by the submission of the polity itself), both capped at `Notice` like the other trends. Abandoned rites are reported and deliberately **not** graded: nothing in the doctrine says how long a catechumenate may take. And `nobody_holds_the_substrate` reaches `Concern` alone with no threshold — a polity that has expelled its way there can take no act at all, since every majority is of the members who hold it. The report's read-models travel in one `CohesionInputs` struct of `Option<&T>`, which puts *not evaluated* in the type rather than in a convention. It never decides a split (see D4/Q2).
- **`en-uplift`** — the **upward interface** to kith-covenant (D4/D9/D10) and the *one* crate holding the confined KC-record dependencies (`kc-conduct`, plus `kc-identity` since D14). `community_identity` hands a chartered polity off as a self-signed `CommunityIdentity`; `coherence_concern` emits KC's `Concern` (neutral `Other("coherence-erosion")` bearing) only at `Concern`-level erosion; `fission` emits a ratified split — **N (≥ 2) successor identities since D25**, a signed `SuccessorLink` to each (index-aligned) and the predecessor's `CharterDissolution` naming them all, so a three-way split is one act where it used to be two chained fissions recording an intermediate polity nobody lived in; it carries fusion's four guards in the other direction (arity, pairwise-distinct successors, no collision with the predecessor, and `verify_self_signed` on **every** successor — before D25 fission had *none* of them, and would sign a link under the predecessor's root to an identity whose own signature did not verify); `fusion` emits the mirror ratified merger — N (≥ 2) predecessors → one merged `CommunityIdentity`, with a `SuccessorLink` and a `CharterDissolution` (naming the merged polity as sole successor) per predecessor. `envoy_delegation` turns an *effective* envoy standing into KC's `SpeakerDelegation` scoped `Envoy` — the fourth border artifact and the only one an office produces, taking the derived `EnvoyState` rather than the archive.

- **`en-store`** — **durable storage** for a log that can be *compacted* (**D20**), the backend D17 and D19 both left owed. An `ArchiveStore` trait generic over the entry type (so this crate needs no `en-charter` dependency), with `FileStore` — one segment envelope per line, append is write + `fsync`, and a full rewrite goes temp + `fsync` + `rename`, which POSIX makes atomic — and `SqliteStore` behind a `sqlite` feature, storing *the same* segments as rows so the two are differentially testable; SQLite earns its place on the rewrite, which is one transaction rather than a temp-file dance. **The whole crate exists for one branch**: *same generation ⇒ append a segment; generation changed ⇒ rewrite in full*. The store owns the cursor, because the hazard it guards is exactly what happens when a caller keeps one — a host that appends past a compaction loses the `ObservationsPruned` record and keeps the observations it dropped, and both verification passes still say the reloaded log is fine. A torn tail left by an interrupted write is *reported*, never skipped; `recover()` truncates it as a deliberate act, because silently dropping an unparseable last line would discard a signed entry without saying so. No clock: the loop stays the host's.
- **`en-host`** — **what a host does on a cadence, and when** (**D20**). `HostCycle::tick(archive, candidate, as_of)` returns the `DueAction`s owed — persist, a graded `CohesionReport`, `RaiseConcern` when erosion reaches that level, a `PruneRecommendation` when the observation log is due — assembling *every* read-model the grade can use, since a missing input makes its component report nothing rather than complain. **`tick` decides and computes; the host signs and does I/O**: it takes `as_of` like every other cadence in this tier (so a year of host behaviour is unit-testable in microseconds) and authors nothing, because raising a Concern and pruning both need keys the host holds. It covers the cadenced duties and only those — membership, induction, office conferral and envoy authorization are deliberate acts of the polity, not scheduled ones.
- **`src/rust/examples/polity_host`** — a runnable **reference host** (`[[bin]]`, nothing depends on it), mirroring kith-covenant's `two_communities_demo`. Its single `time::now()` is the only clock read in ethne outside a test, apart from `en-pyo3`'s `*_at=None ⇒ now` binding convenience. Runs a simulated timeline by default so it terminates and can be verified; `--follow` is the real loop.

- **`en-pyo3`** + **`src/python/ethne/`** — the **Python surface** (D2): an `ethne._core` extension module built by maturin, and a Pythonic package over it in the tier's own four parts — `ethne.polity` (the `Archive` charter log, keys, and the five derived read-models), `ethne.authoring` (a `Signer` plus one call per charter record: found, amend, admit / depart / expel, confer / resign / revoke an office, designate / rotate / release a guardian edge, authorize / resign / revoke an envoy, induct / re-affirm, record an observation, prune the observation log, dissolve), `ethne.monitor` (a `FoundingCandidate`, `manual_founding`, and `cohesion_report`, whose `as_of` fixes the moment guardian attendance is judged fresh against, and which now takes the `DecayTrends` and `ObservationWindow` read-models, plus `sign_observation` — the pre-archive counterpart of `record_observation`, since a founding derivation's evidence must be signed before the charter that will carry it exists), and `ethne.uplift` (the four artifacts that cross to kith-covenant, as JSON a KC-side consumer appends verbatim). The ratify-not-replace discipline survives the crossing: `propose_charter` returns only JSON, and a charter exists only once `ratify_charter` has a majority of the roll co-sign it. `en-at` has no bindings yet — its real coupling is behind the `at-ffi` gate — but Python can drive the same path through `founding_candidate`.

The build needs a per-component **conda** env, consistent with the rest of sustainable-space: `environment.yaml` pins `python==3.13.*`, `rust`, `rust-src`, `maturin`, and `pytest` from conda-forge (`conda env create -f environment.yaml && conda activate ethne`). `run-tests.sh` errors early with an instruction if any of them is missing.

The decoupling is concrete and machine-checked (`cargo tree`): every core, monitor and host crate (`en-core`, `en-charter`, `en-substrate`, `en-at`, `en-cohesion`, `en-store`, `en-host`) depends only on the neutral `kc-core` — no `autonomous_trust`, no kith-covenant governance crate. The two KC-record couplings needed to speak KC's own vocabulary — `kc-conduct` for the `Concern`, `kc-identity` for the envoy delegation — are confined to `en-uplift` and go no further (and the second widens nothing: `kc-conduct` already reached `kc-identity` through `kc-ceremony`, so D14 only made an existing edge direct); ethne never depends on `kc-archive` (it emits serialized `Signed<T>` payloads a KC-side consumer appends). `en-substrate`'s end-to-end test founds a polity through the manual provider with neither AT nor KC present. The one C dependency in the tree, `rusqlite` behind `en-store`'s `sqlite` feature (**D20**), is off by default and absent from the default workspace graph — also `cargo tree`-checked.
