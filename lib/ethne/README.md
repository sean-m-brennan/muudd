# Ethne

**Ethne** (Greek ἔθνη, "peoples") is the **polity tier**: it turns a set of cooperating agents into a *community* — a bounded, self-governing entity that stands for a substrate, acts with one voice, and persists across the turnover of its members.

It sits between a trust substrate and a federation. Below it, a substrate like [autonomous-trust](../autonomous_trust/README.md) produces *emergent* trust among nodes but no community: its groups and gateway subtrees are topological clusters, not covenant-bearing entities with a membership roll and a standing. Above it, [kith-covenant](../kith-covenant/README.md) governs relations *between* communities and presumes each one is already coherent, persistent, and able to act as a unit. Ethne is the tier that produces the entity KC presumes, out of whatever cooperation the substrate supplies.

```
   autonomous-trust        ethne                     kith-covenant
   "who cooperates?"  ──►  "what is a community —  ──►  "how do communities
                            and what keeps it one?"      treat one another?"
```

The governing discipline is **ratify emergence, do not replace it**: ethne reads the emergent structure and offers it back to the members for deliberate ratification into a charter. Emergence proposes; the polity ratifies; KC recognizes.

Ethne is a **separate tier by design**, so that kith-covenant depends on neither ethne nor autonomous-trust: ethne emits only KC-native artifacts (a `CommunityIdentity`, recognition records, and a `Concern` on a coherence breakdown), and autonomous-trust is one *pluggable* substrate provider among others.

## Status

Design decisions D1–D23 are locked and **every open question Q1–Q9 is resolved**; see **[doc/design.md](doc/design.md)** for the full architecture: the gap it fills, the decoupling rule, the five responsibilities (founding & charter, substrate articulation, internal offices, cohesion monitoring, longevity), what it reuses from KC vs. what is new, the machine-guardian model, the lifecycle, and the open questions.

Built so far — the Rust workspace and the Python surface over it (D2):

- **`en-core`** — base crate; re-exports the neutral `kc-core` primitives ethne builds on (D1: kc-core only).
- **`en-archive`** — append-only, content-addressed log, generic over the entry type (the neutral archive mechanism, forked thin from `kc-archive`; verification/DID-resolution are domain concerns for later slices). Persistence lives here as of **D17**: a versioned JSON dump/load, generic over the entry type, plus `dump_json_from(start)` / `append_json` so a host holding a cursor appends what is *new* rather than re-serializing the whole log — which is what a steadily-appended observation record needs. The store itself stays strictly append-only with no removal path; compaction is a charter-level operation that returns a new archive (see `en-charter`).
- **`en-charter`** — the polity **founding** primitive (keystone). Records (`Substrate`, `Commitment`, `FoundingProposal`, `PolityCharter`, `CommitmentAmendment`, the roll-lifecycle `MembershipChange` (`Admit` / `DepartVoluntary` / `Expel`), and the terminal `CharterDissolution`), the *ratify-not-replace* API (a `FoundingProposal` carries no authority; only `ratify` + co-signing yields a charter), signature verification (`did:key` self-resolves), state derivation (`charter_state` — the *live* roll = founding + admits − departures, plus commitments, dissolution + successors), and the authority rules: genesis-singleton, majority ratification, genesis-immutable substrate + founding commitments, asymmetric amendment (add at a majority, remove at a majority + 1), an asymmetric membership lifecycle (Admit at a majority + the joiner's consent, DepartVoluntary self-signed, Expel at a majority + 1 — all under a self-govern floor), an internal-office lifecycle (`OfficeChange` over `Office { Convener, Inductor, EnvoyAuthorizer, CharterKeeper, Other }` — Confer at a majority + the holder's consent, and in a **machine-majority** polity never on a derived member with no guardian (D15), Resign self-signed, Revoke at a majority with the holder excluded; the roll confers, offices never confer offices, so the founding roll bootstraps the first ones with no regress; a holding is effective only while its holder is on the roll), majority-of-roll dissolution, and terminal-dissolution (no charter event after a polity is dissolved). `office_state` derives who holds what — and since **D14** an office is not merely held but *used*: where an `Inductor` is effectively held, one of its holders other than the inductee must witness an induction, and the roll may proceed without them only at a majority + 1 (a *defeasible* gate — an office may raise the bar for the rite, never block it), while an `EnvoyAuthorizer` holds a *delegated power* — one holder's signature stands in for the roll's majority on an `EnvoyAuthorization` (`EnvoyKind { Authorize, Resign, Revoke }`: who may speak for the polity beyond it, in ethne's own vocabulary, with the envoy always co-signing, never as a unilateral self-appointment, and the key checked to derive the DID). Because the majority route always stays open, filling that office costs the roll nothing; `Convener` and `CharterKeeper` gate nothing, and self-signed acts are never gated. `envoy_state` derives the standings in force — effective only while the envoy is on the roll and unexpired. **The node→guardian edge is chartered** (**D15**): a `Derivation` says which members are derived (machine) parties and which human each answers to — ratified at founding, with every named guardian co-signing, because answerability for a machine is accepted and never assigned — and moved afterwards by `GuardianChange` records (`Designate` at a majority + the guardian's consent, `Rotate` with the *incoming* guardian consenting, `Release` by either the departing guardian alone or a majority recording that the human is gone). `guardian: None` is an honest chartered state, not a missing value; a `Release` clears the edge and keeps the designation, since a machine does not become human by losing its human. No machine may guard a machine, and nothing may guard itself. `guardian_state` derives the effective picture and answers `is_machine_majority`, which is what conditions the office rule above. **Transmission** (the `Affirmation` record over `AffirmationKind { Induction, ReAffirmation }`): admission puts someone on the roll, while *induction* is the distinct step by which they acquire the substrate — witnessed by a majority of the roll co-signing alongside the inductee, with founding members inducted by ratifying the charter — and *re-affirmation* is self-signed renewal by an already-inducted member. `affirmation_state` reports each current member's induction status and last affirmation, and `stale_members(cadence_seconds, as_of)` names the un-inducted and the overdue, so a community that stops re-affirming shows erosion (the freshness cadence is a query parameter, not a charter term). **What a monitor saw is recorded** (**D16**): a ninth entry, `Observed(Signed<Observation>)`, carries one observer's readings at one instant — perspectival evidence, in the sense KC's observer reports are, so it asserts nothing about the polity, no majority is involved (an observation is not a decision), and the observer need not be a member, since a monitor that had to sit inside the roll could be silenced by expulsion; the one rule is that it be signed by exactly the observer it names. `observation_window(since, as_of)` reads those back as a window with a span, a per-member reading count, and each member's `unattended_span` — which honestly distinguishes *never observed* (not evaluated) from *observed but never attended*, and which is **clamped at the window's `pruned_before` horizon** (D17) so that **pruning may only ever weaken a signal, never strengthen one**. That clamp is not a precaution: `last_attested` is a max over the window's stamps, so dropping the interval holding the freshest one would start the absence earlier and make it *longer* — pruning manufacturing a Concern. And `decay_trends` derives, from the polity's own signed records alone with no provider involved, a widening affirmation gap (judged against the **median** of prior gaps), a thinning induction witness turnout (never inferred from a single point), and a roll-churn rate — the shape borrowed from `kc-reputation`'s `ceremony_cost_decay` one tier up, hedges included. **The observation record can be pruned, and only it** (**D17**): an observation decides nothing — `reconstruct` matches it and does nothing — so dropping one cannot rewrite what the polity resolved, which is what makes observations the one inert and therefore the one prunable entry class. A tenth entry, `ObservationsPruned(Signed<ObservationPruning>)`, names a horizon and carries a receipt (how many records went, and a digest over their CIDs), because a Concern crossing to kith-covenant rests on observations and evidence must not be able to vanish without a signature. One effective `CharterKeeper` among the deciders suffices — the delegated power that office had no record to exercise until now — but only beyond `PRUNE_SETTLING_SECONDS` (30 days), while the roll may prune anything at a majority: destroying evidence is the move a captured party makes, so the recent record stays out of any single custodian's reach. That constant is law rather than a tunable threshold, since a floor the party it constrains could lower is not a floor. A horizon must advance, and the receipt is verified while the dropped observations are still present and treated as beyond checking once compaction has run — an honest limit rather than a rule that would flag every historical pruning. `apply_prunings` returns a **new** archive without them; it can never reach a governing record, because the single `is_prunable` predicate the receipt, the authority check and the compaction all read matches nothing but an observation. Relatedly, an attendance stamp may not postdate the observation reporting it — a monitor cannot have seen an attestation that had not yet happened — and that rule is what makes the pruning clamp below sound. **When to prune is a read-model, not a daemon** (**D19**): `prune_recommendation(archive, polity, &RetentionPolicy, as_of)` answers "should I prune, to what horizon, and who may authorize it?" and returns `None` when there is nothing worth doing. It signs nothing — the host feeds the horizon back to the ordinary authoring path — because this tier never calls `now()` and the loop, like the storage backend, stays the host's. What is *not* the host's to reconstruct is the safety floor, and it turned out to have two halves: `live_measure_seconds` (default one day) protects the **span** a live measure reads back over, and `min_witness_readings` (default 3) protects the **witness count** it also needs. Both are required, which was measured rather than assumed — the same absence at the same horizon grades `Concern` at a dense observation cadence and `Notice` at a sparse one, because D17's clamp preserves the span but nothing preserved the count. The count is per member, since `readings_for` is, so a thinly-watched member can pin the horizon: the correct answer, and not a deadlock, because the roll may still prune anything by majority — what is refused is *recommending* it. `retain_seconds` defaults to `PRUNE_SETTLING_SECONDS`, the smallest retention that both preserves every observation-based measure and leaves the single-`CharterKeeper` route open (a *shorter* retention keeps more history and is therefore more expensive to authorize, not less), and `min_dropped` (default 100) exists because a pruning record is itself unprunable — measured, pruning eagerly at 1 left the log 2.9× larger than at 100. The recommendation reports every open route, cheapest first, and can only ever propose a horizon `check_authority` accepts. **A founding says what it excluded** (**D21**, resolving Q1): the genesis quorum used to take its denominator from the proposal's *own* roll, so a faction of one could charter a thirty-node cluster as a polity of itself on a single signature — passing both verification passes, with the other twenty-nine not merely outvoted but *unrecorded*. `FoundingProposal` and `PolityCharter` now carry a `BoundaryAttestation`: the `candidate_size` the boundary showed, and a reasoned `Exclusion` for everyone it showed that the roll does not name. Genesis authority gates **the accounting only** — the numbers must add up, exclusions may not repeat, and nobody may be excluded and enrolled at once — while **nothing refuses a charter for being narrow**, because a genuinely separate group of three inside a thirty-node cluster is indistinguishable from a faction capturing it without a substantive judgement no rule can make. What the rule removes is the silence, not the discretion. Since the charter's core has no amendment path, the attestation is genesis-immutable by construction, and `founding_legitimacy` reads the grade back off the charter **alone** — no provider, no cohesion input, so anyone holding it can re-run the judgement forever. It reports `consent_share`, ratifiers over everyone the boundary *showed* rather than over the roll the founders *chose*: the faction of one reads **0.033**. Its `FoundingBasis` (`Corroborated` / `Narrowed` / `SelfAttested`) is deliberately unordered and deliberately not an `ErosionLevel` — a founding is not eroding, and these are not comparable harms. This is accountability, not prevention: the attestation is the proposer's signed *claim*. **And since D22 (resolving Q5) the claim names its author.** D21 could record *that* a boundary came from a reading, never whose — an unsigned label, so flipping one field promoted a convener's bare assertion to a corroborated reading with no provider consulted and both verification passes green. The `Substrate` arm now **carries** its evidence: `Substrate { readings: Vec<Signed<SubstrateReading>> }`, each reading naming a `reader`, signed by that reader alone (D16's rule that nobody may put an observation in somebody else's mouth), and bound to the founding it is offered for — without which an honest reader's account of one cluster replays into somebody else's founding while the signature still verifies. **The rule with teeth is identity, not arithmetic:** the roll ∪ exclusions must equal some signed reading's members *as a set*, where D21 supplied both sides of its own sum. `founding_legitimacy` gains `readers` and `corroborating_readers` — deliberately not a count of readings filed, since a reader who signed a *different* boundary corroborates nothing and the gap between the two numbers is a reader contradicting the founding on its own charter, permanently. That count is a **lens and never a gate**, because `did:key` costs nothing and a captured reader can mint its own corroborators; the same stance kith-covenant took on capture move 1, with independence attestation named as the successor. **And the excluded answer back**: an eleventh entry, `BoundaryDissented`, over `DissentKind { Unnamed, ReasonRejected }` — perspectival evidence in `Observed`'s exact shape, signed by the dissenter alone, no deciders, no majority, and the dissenter need not be a member, since one required to sit inside the roll could be silenced by never being admitted. It is **the only thing in the tier that can contradict an understated `candidate_size`**, which is exactly what D21 recorded as beyond its reach: the archive holds no provider, so the excluded are the only witnesses. Authority checks coherence with the record contested and never its truth — a `ReasonRejected` dissenter must actually be among the exclusions, an `Unnamed` one must be in neither the founding roll nor the exclusions. `founding_dissent` is a separate read-model from `founding_legitimacy` on purpose, since dissent accrues over time and folding it in would destroy the charter-alone property. A dissent may be filed **after dissolution** — the only entry class that may — because otherwise founders silence the excluded by winding the polity up; and it is unprunable for free, since `is_prunable` matches only the observations D17 made forgettable precisely because they are inert. **D23 makes the substrate mean something.** Non-ultimacy is the whole reason the tier has one, and nothing checked it: `substrate.anchor` was read by no rule anywhere, so a polity whose entire ground was two empty strings chartered, as did a fresh cluster of machines with nobody behind any of them, and — the sharper hole, since Q4 was framed as an *absence* problem — one naming **itself** or a member of its own roll as its anchor. Four genesis rules now: the substrate must be **articulated** (name and statement non-empty — presence, never content, since no rule can read a tradition); an anchor may be neither the polity nor anyone on its roll (a ground inside what it grounds is no ground, the guardian loop rule one level out); a roll of nothing but unguarded derived parties **must** name an anchor, the trigger being deliberately narrower than machine-majority because a human roll's statement *is* its unkeyed anchor and a guarded machine already has a consenting human above it; and a present anchor **co-signs the founding** alongside the ratifiers and guardians, because answerability is accepted and never assigned. `founding_legitimacy` gains `grounding` — `Anchored` / `Guarded` / `Held` / `Bare`, descriptive and unordered like `FoundingBasis` — which lives there rather than in a new read-model on exactly D22's test: a genesis-immutable substrate cannot accrue, so the answer is the same forever. `Bare` is legal, and reported so that *permitted* is not mistaken for *grounded*. **And the ground may answer back**: a twelfth entry, `SubstrateDisavowed`, is the named anchor's own signed withdrawal, read by the separate `GroundStanding` (which *does* accrue). It **asserts and cannot un-charter** — nothing rewrites the substrate, reconstructs authority state or dissolves anything, since a withdrawal with that power would be a unilateral kill switch in every anchor's hands; a disowned polity keeps governing itself, on a ground that disowns it, legibly. It shares the dissent's two deliberate properties (permitted after dissolution — the second and only other such class — and unprunable for free) and is *stricter* in one: only the key the charter names may file it, where a dissent must admit anyone because an omitted party is by definition absent from the record.
- **`en-substrate`** — the pluggable `SubstrateProvider` interface: the source of the emergent boundary a founding ratifies (`FoundingCandidate` → `propose` → `FoundingProposal`). The **guardian edges travel with the boundary** (D15): a candidate the substrate reads as a derived (machine) party becomes a proposed `Derivation`, so which human answers for which machine is ratified rather than asserted. **The candidate says what the provider *knows***, not merely what it saw (**D18**): `reputation: Option<f64>` distinguishes *unrated* from *rated low*, and `derived: bool` carries the substrate's reading of *kind* independently of the guardian edge — so a machine with nobody behind it is ratified as the unguarded `Derivation` it is, where before it proposed silently as a human. An unrated candidate does not satisfy a demanded reputation floor (a positive floor is a positive assertion, and an absent reading cannot support it); a floor of `0.0` demands nothing. **Change is streamed, not diffed** (**D16**): `SubstrateChange` deltas (a member appearing or vanishing, a reputation or guardian-edge move, an advancing attendance stamp, a boundary going opaque) drain through a `ChangeStream` trait kept deliberately **separate** from `SubstrateProvider` — an empty stream and no stream are different claims, and a convener with a list should not have to fake one. **The boundary's provenance travels with the candidate** (**D21**): `BoundarySource` says whether a boundary is an independent reading or the convener's own word, and it rides on `FoundingCandidate` rather than being stamped at `propose`, because a `ManualFounding` candidate has exactly the same *shape* as a trust service's — only the provider knows which it is. `propose` records whoever the reputation floor trims, with the reason it actually used, distinguishing "reputation 0.21 below the floor 0.30" from "unrated; a floor of 0.30 demands a reading" (D18's semantics, said where an excluded party can read them), and `propose_selecting` narrows to a named subset for one stated reason — so an honest small founding is a first-class act rather than a floor it is not. Ships `ManualFounding`, a convener-driven provider needing no substrate service (and, consistently, no stream); such a polity still charters and the charter simply never pretends it was corroborated — the same honest chartered state as an unguarded machine's `guardian: None`. **Since D22 that is a fact rather than a label** (resolving Q5): `FoundingCandidate` carries no `source` field at all, because the evidence *is* the source — `propose` takes `Vec<Signed<SubstrateReading>>`, empty meaning a convener's assertion. Keeping a separate label would have left a claim and its evidence as two things that could disagree, which is the defect D22 exists to close. `FoundingCandidate::reading()` yields the **unsigned** reading; the provider holds no keys and gains none, since signing is the host's exactly as D20 settled for every other authored act. `ManualFounding` is therefore `ConvenerAsserted` because it holds no reader key — a convener who genuinely obtains a signed reading matching their roll has real corroboration and is welcome to it; what is no longer free is *claiming* one.
- **`en-at`** — the `autonomous_trust` provider (D5–D8): an `AtEventSource` seam (mock + `at-ffi`-gated embed/attach coupling) folded into an `AtSubstrateProvider` that maps AT's gateway-reputation subtree to the candidate roll, the verified operator to each member's guardian edge, and AT's attended-now stamp to when that guardian was last confirmed present. It implements `ChangeStream` by diffing each folded event against the view it already holds — the provider is the one place the prior reading is in hand — and emits an `AttendanceStamped` delta only when the stamp actually *advances*, so a repeated identical stamp is not mistaken for renewed presence. The default build links no AT — KC ⟂ AT holds with the adapter present.
- **`en-cohesion`** — the polity's **coherence monitor** (a pure `kc-core`-only read-model). `cohesion_report` grades strain into an `ErosionLevel` (`None` / `Notice` / `Concern`) off the chartered roll vs. the provider's current candidate — attrition, reputation decay, floor breaches, guardians not present, transmission decay (members lapsed on re-affirming the substrate, read off `en-charter`'s `AffirmationState`), lost guardian edges (a chartered node→guardian edge the substrate no longer shows), an opaque boundary capping attrition at `Notice`. Absent guardians are capped at `Notice` outright: a snapshot cannot tell a night shift from an abandoned polity, so the signal may warn but never escalate. Transmission decay carries no such cap and may reach `Concern` — duration is already inside that measure. Nor do lost guardian edges, for a different reason: they compare what the polity *ratified* against what is observed, and only over members the provider still sees, so a vanished member is attrition rather than answerability collapsing and no cap is needed. **D16 adds the four measures that only exist over time**: a widening affirmation gap and a thinning witness turnout, both capped at `Notice` because a trend is a leading indicator whose consequence the affirmation component can already escalate (grading both would count one decay twice); roll churn, a rate rather than a trend, with a full `Notice`/`Concern` pair; and **sustained absence**, the one signal permitted past the attendance cap — a guardian unattended across the recorded window for at least `sustained_absence_seconds` *and* seen by at least `sustained_absence_readings` observations reaches `Concern`. Those two conditions do two different jobs: **the span is the absence; the count is the evidence that somebody was watching while it lasted.** What lifts the snapshot cap is therefore not a looser threshold but a record. **D18 adds the eleventh component and fixes a conflation the other ten rested on**: both reputation measures are now computed over the members the provider actually *rated*, reported as `rated_members`, and say nothing when nobody is rated — before this, an unrated roll was indistinguishable from a failing one and a freshly founded polity graded at `Concern` for being new. The unrated share itself is graded, capped at `Notice` by construction for the attendance reason (a young cohort and a provider that has stopped rating are the same picture), and exists so the two reputation components cannot go *silently* dark. **D23 adds the twelfth, and it is unlike the other eleven**: whether the polity's chartered substrate anchor has withdrawn. Every other measure here grades how far something has drifted; this one grades a single signed act by the one key entitled to make it, so it reaches `Concern` alone and takes **no threshold** at all — event-triggered, like kith-covenant's `contraction_detected`, and it cannot decay back, since there is no withdrawal of a withdrawal. The report's read-models travel in one `CohesionInputs` struct of `Option<&T>`, which puts *not evaluated* in the type rather than in a convention. It never decides a split (see D4/Q2).
- **`en-uplift`** — the **upward interface** to kith-covenant (D4/D9/D10) and the *one* crate holding the confined KC-record dependencies (`kc-conduct`, plus `kc-identity` since D14). `community_identity` hands a chartered polity off as a self-signed `CommunityIdentity`; `coherence_concern` emits KC's `Concern` (neutral `Other("coherence-erosion")` bearing) only at `Concern`-level erosion; `fission` emits a ratified split — two successor identities, a signed `SuccessorLink` to each, and the predecessor's `CharterDissolution`; `fusion` emits the mirror ratified merger — N (≥ 2) predecessors → one merged `CommunityIdentity`, with a `SuccessorLink` and a `CharterDissolution` (naming the merged polity as sole successor) per predecessor. `envoy_delegation` turns an *effective* envoy standing into KC's `SpeakerDelegation` scoped `Envoy` — the fourth border artifact and the only one an office produces, taking the derived `EnvoyState` rather than the archive.

- **`en-store`** — **durable storage** for a log that can be *compacted* (**D20**), the backend D17 and D19 both left owed. An `ArchiveStore` trait generic over the entry type (so this crate needs no `en-charter` dependency), with `FileStore` — one segment envelope per line, append is write + `fsync`, and a full rewrite goes temp + `fsync` + `rename`, which POSIX makes atomic — and `SqliteStore` behind a `sqlite` feature, storing *the same* segments as rows so the two are differentially testable; SQLite earns its place on the rewrite, which is one transaction rather than a temp-file dance. **The whole crate exists for one branch**: *same generation ⇒ append a segment; generation changed ⇒ rewrite in full*. The store owns the cursor, because the hazard it guards is exactly what happens when a caller keeps one — a host that appends past a compaction loses the `ObservationsPruned` record and keeps the observations it dropped, and both verification passes still say the reloaded log is fine. A torn tail left by an interrupted write is *reported*, never skipped; `recover()` truncates it as a deliberate act, because silently dropping an unparseable last line would discard a signed entry without saying so. No clock: the loop stays the host's.
- **`en-host`** — **what a host does on a cadence, and when** (**D20**). `HostCycle::tick(archive, candidate, as_of)` returns the `DueAction`s owed — persist, a graded `CohesionReport`, `RaiseConcern` when erosion reaches that level, a `PruneRecommendation` when the observation log is due — assembling *every* read-model the grade can use, since a missing input makes its component report nothing rather than complain. **`tick` decides and computes; the host signs and does I/O**: it takes `as_of` like every other cadence in this tier (so a year of host behaviour is unit-testable in microseconds) and authors nothing, because raising a Concern and pruning both need keys the host holds. It covers the cadenced duties and only those — membership, induction, office conferral and envoy authorization are deliberate acts of the polity, not scheduled ones.
- **`src/rust/examples/polity_host`** — a runnable **reference host** (`[[bin]]`, nothing depends on it), mirroring kith-covenant's `two_communities_demo`. Its single `time::now()` is the only clock read in ethne outside a test, apart from `en-pyo3`'s `*_at=None ⇒ now` binding convenience. Runs a simulated timeline by default so it terminates and can be verified; `--follow` is the real loop.

- **`en-pyo3`** + **`src/python/ethne/`** — the **Python surface** (D2): an `ethne._core` extension module built by maturin, and a Pythonic package over it in the tier's own four parts — `ethne.polity` (the `Archive` charter log, keys, and the five derived read-models), `ethne.authoring` (a `Signer` plus one call per charter record: found, amend, admit / depart / expel, confer / resign / revoke an office, designate / rotate / release a guardian edge, authorize / resign / revoke an envoy, induct / re-affirm, record an observation, prune the observation log, dissolve), `ethne.monitor` (a `FoundingCandidate`, `manual_founding`, and `cohesion_report`, whose `as_of` fixes the moment guardian attendance is judged fresh against, and which now takes the `DecayTrends` and `ObservationWindow` read-models), and `ethne.uplift` (the four artifacts that cross to kith-covenant, as JSON a KC-side consumer appends verbatim). The ratify-not-replace discipline survives the crossing: `propose_charter` returns only JSON, and a charter exists only once `ratify_charter` has a majority of the roll co-sign it. `en-at` has no bindings yet — its real coupling is behind the `at-ffi` gate — but Python can drive the same path through `founding_candidate`.

# Python surface

The build needs a per-component **conda** env, consistent with the rest of sustainable-space: `environment.yaml` pins `python==3.13.*`, `rust`, `rust-src`, `maturin`, and `pytest` from conda-forge (`conda env create -f environment.yaml && conda activate ethne`). `run-tests.sh` errors early with an instruction if any of them is missing.

The decoupling is concrete and machine-checked (`cargo tree`): every core, monitor and host crate (`en-core`, `en-charter`, `en-substrate`, `en-at`, `en-cohesion`, `en-store`, `en-host`) depends only on the neutral `kc-core` — no `autonomous_trust`, no kith-covenant governance crate. The two KC-record couplings needed to speak KC's own vocabulary — `kc-conduct` for the `Concern`, `kc-identity` for the envoy delegation — are confined to `en-uplift` and go no further (and the second widens nothing: `kc-conduct` already reached `kc-identity` through `kc-ceremony`, so D14 only made an existing edge direct); ethne never depends on `kc-archive` (it emits serialized `Signed<T>` payloads a KC-side consumer appends). `en-substrate`'s end-to-end test founds a polity through the manual provider with neither AT nor KC present. The one C dependency in the tree, `rusqlite` behind `en-store`'s `sqlite` feature (**D20**), is off by default and absent from the default workspace graph — also `cargo tree`-checked.
