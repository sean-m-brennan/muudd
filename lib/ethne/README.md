# Ethne

**Ethne** (Greek ἔθνη, "peoples") is the **polity tier**: it turns a set of cooperating agents into a *community*, a bounded, self-governing entity that stands for a substrate, acts with one voice, and persists across the turnover of its members.

It sits between a trust substrate and a federation. Below it, a substrate like [autonomous-trust](../autonomous_trust/README.md) produces *emergent* trust among nodes but no community: its groups and gateway subtrees are topological clusters, not covenant-bearing entities with a membership roll and a standing. Above it, [kith-covenant](../kith-covenant/README.md) governs relations *between* communities and presumes each one is already coherent, persistent, and able to act as a unit. Ethne is the tier that produces the entity KC presumes, out of whatever cooperation the substrate supplies.

```
   autonomous-trust        ethne                     kith-covenant
   "who cooperates?"  ──►  "what is a community,    ──►  "how do communities
                            and what keeps it one?"      treat one another?"
```

The governing discipline is **ratify emergence, do not replace it**: ethne reads the emergent structure and offers it back to the members for deliberate ratification into a charter. Emergence proposes; the polity ratifies; KC recognizes.

Ethne is a **separate tier by design**, so that kith-covenant depends on neither ethne nor autonomous-trust: ethne emits only KC-native artifacts (a `CommunityIdentity`, recognition records, and a `Concern` on a coherence breakdown), and autonomous-trust is one *pluggable* substrate provider among others.

The Rust workspace, and the Python surface over it (D2):

- **`en-core`**: base crate; re-exports the neutral `kc-core` primitives ethne builds on (D1: kc-core only).
- **`en-archive`**: append-only, content-addressed log, generic over the entry type (the neutral archive mechanism, forked thin from `kc-archive`; verification and DID-resolution are domain concerns, not archive concerns). Persistence lives here (D17): a versioned JSON dump/load, generic over the entry type, plus `dump_json_from(start)` / `append_json`, so a host holding a cursor appends what is *new* rather than re-serializing the whole log, which is what a steadily-appended observation record needs. The store is strictly append-only with no removal path; compaction is a charter-level operation that returns a new archive (see `en-charter`).
- **`en-charter`**: the polity **founding** primitive, and the keystone of the tier. It holds fourteen record kinds, the ratify-not-replace API, the authority rules, and the read-models that derive state from the log.

  *Founding and the charter.* `Substrate`, `Commitment`, `FoundingProposal`, `PolityCharter`, `CommitmentAmendment`, the roll-lifecycle `MembershipChange` (`Admit` / `DepartVoluntary` / `Expel`), and the terminal `CharterDissolution`. A `FoundingProposal` carries no authority of its own: only `ratify` plus co-signing yields a charter. Signatures verify against self-resolving `did:key` identities. `charter_state` derives the live roll (founding + admits − departures), the commitments in force, and any dissolution with its successors.

  *Authority rules.* Genesis-singleton and majority ratification. A genesis-immutable substrate and founding commitments. Asymmetric amendment: add at a majority, remove at a majority + 1. An asymmetric membership lifecycle: Admit at a majority plus the joiner's consent, DepartVoluntary self-signed, Expel at a majority + 1, all under a self-govern floor. Dissolution at a majority of the roll, and it is terminal: no charter event follows it. A dissolution's named successors are checked (D25) for duplicates (a fan-out nobody counts is a count that misleads), for self-succession (a polity that survives its own dissolution has not dissolved), and against its own roll (a member is a person or a machine, not a polity, which is the third of the closure family alongside D15's guardian loop and D23's substrate anchor). Naming *no* successor is valid, because that is the difference between a fission and a death.

  *Internal offices, and their use.* `OfficeChange` over `Office { Convener, Inductor, EnvoyAuthorizer, CharterKeeper, Other }`: Confer at a majority plus the holder's consent, and in a **machine-majority** polity never on a derived member with no guardian (D15); Resign self-signed; Revoke at a majority with the holder excluded. The roll confers, and offices never confer offices, so the founding roll bootstraps the first ones with no regress; a holding is effective only while its holder is on the roll. An office is not merely held but *used* (D14). Where an `Inductor` is effectively held, one of its holders other than the inductee must witness an induction, and the roll may proceed without them only at a majority + 1: a *defeasible* gate, since an office may raise the bar for the rite and never block it. An `EnvoyAuthorizer` holds a *delegated power*, where one holder's signature stands in for the roll's majority on an `EnvoyAuthorization` (`EnvoyKind { Authorize, Resign, Revoke }`), which is how the polity says who may speak for it beyond itself, in ethne's own vocabulary, with the envoy always co-signing rather than self-appointing and the key checked to derive the DID. Because the majority route always stays open, filling that office costs the roll nothing. `Convener` and `CharterKeeper` gate nothing, and self-signed acts are never gated. `office_state` derives who holds what; `envoy_state` derives the standings in force, effective only while the envoy is on the roll and unexpired.

  *The node→guardian edge is chartered* (D15). A `Derivation` says which members are derived (machine) parties and which human each answers to, ratified at founding with every named guardian co-signing, because answerability for a machine is accepted and never assigned. It moves afterwards by `GuardianChange` records: `Designate` at a majority plus the guardian's consent, `Rotate` with the *incoming* guardian consenting, `Release` by either the departing guardian alone or a majority recording that the human is gone. `guardian: None` is an honest chartered state rather than a missing value, and a `Release` clears the edge while keeping the designation, since a machine does not become human by losing its human. No machine may guard a machine, and nothing may guard itself. `guardian_state` derives the effective picture and answers `is_machine_majority`, which is what conditions the office rule above.

  *A claimed edge carries what showed it.* A `GuardianChange` that asserts an edge presents the signed `Observation` in which the substrate reported it, or declares `Unbacked { reason }`. The evidence is *carried* rather than cited by CID, because D17 pruning would dangle a citation and make authority a property of when you looked. Founding derivations are gated the same way (D27), and there the asymmetry inverts: `Unbacked` is permitted and honest but earns nothing, because at founding a self-report would *buy* D23's anchor exemption. The observer may be neither the polity nor anyone on its roll, since a founder signing a real observation about its own cluster is the same closure shape D23 refuses for an anchor and D25 for a successor, reaching the *evidence* one level out. A named guardian observing the edge it is about to accept does count: it is not on the roll, it co-signs either way, and what it asserts by observing is its own answerability, since interest disqualifies praise rather than an admission (D24).

  *Transmission, and the rite.* The `Affirmation` record over `AffirmationKind { Induction, ReAffirmation }`. Admission puts someone on the roll; *induction* is the distinct step by which they acquire the substrate, witnessed by a majority co-signing alongside the inductee, with the charter's **ratifiers** inducted by ratifying it; *re-affirmation* is self-signed renewal by an already-inducted member. The rite has movements (D29): a `RiteMovementRecorded` entry carries one movement of one member's rite over `Movement { Substrate { names }, Fallenness, Recognition, CommonVirtue { commits_to }, NonUltimacy { names_anchor } }`, which are `recognition_protocols.md`'s five movements, a document that places them at exactly this tier ("the five movements carry their full content at this layer"). Each variant carries only what its own movement can be held to, so a rule cannot check the wrong field. The inductee recites and signs, everyone attending co-signs, and **attendees must already hold the substrate**. Movements are **cited, not carried**, deliberately the opposite of the guardian evidence above, and for the reason that rule exists: a citation is unsafe only when what it points at can be pruned, and `is_prunable` matches nothing but an observation. Citing is also what puts the rite in the log *as it happens*. Order is semantic (movement 1 opens a rite, and re-opening abandons an unfinished attempt so it cannot deadlock a member); duration is never required, because the doctrine asks that the movements happen, not that they take a week. What *is* checked is **coherence with the charter, never content**: movement 1 must name the chartered substrate, movement 4 must commit to exactly the commitments in force, movement 5 must name the chartered anchor and nothing at all where the charter is `Bare`, while fallenness and recognition are presence-only, since no rule can read contrition. **A witness counts only if present for every movement**: the completion cites its rite's five CIDs in order and may name only the intersection of their attendance, because five records with disjoint signers is a rite nobody attended.

  *The substrate is required of whoever governs.* `seasoned_members` and `deciding_weight` are the one representation every deliberative threshold reads. An un-inducted member may not hold an office, refused at conferral *and* dropped from `effective_holders`, lapsing and reviving as the roll rule does, and may not attend a rite. The **denominator is the inducted roll** rather than the whole roll: padding the roll while carrying no weight would make admission a way to freeze a polity, which is the worse hazard. `is_machine_majority` still reads the whole roll, because it asks what the polity is made of rather than who decides. `affirmation_state` reports each current member's induction status and last affirmation, and `stale_members(cadence_seconds, as_of)` names the un-inducted and the overdue, so a community that stops re-affirming shows erosion; the freshness cadence is a query parameter rather than a charter term. `rite_state` reports each rite's movements, its **span** (the ceremony's cost in time), who attended throughout, how many attendees it lost, and which rites were begun and never finished.

  *What a monitor saw is recorded* (D16). `Observed(Signed<Observation>)` carries one observer's readings at one instant: perspectival evidence, in the sense KC's observer reports are, so it asserts nothing about the polity, no majority is involved (an observation is not a decision), and the observer need not be a member, since a monitor that had to sit inside the roll could be silenced by expulsion. The one rule is that it be signed by exactly the observer it names. `observation_window(since, as_of)` reads those back as a window with a span, a per-member reading count, and each member's `unattended_span`, which distinguishes *never observed* (not evaluated) from *observed but never attended*, and which is **clamped at the window's `pruned_before` horizon** (D17) so that **pruning may only ever weaken a signal, never strengthen one**. That clamp is load-bearing rather than precautionary: `last_attested` is a max over the window's stamps, so dropping the interval holding the freshest one would start the absence earlier and make it *longer*, which is pruning manufacturing a Concern. Relatedly, an attendance stamp may not postdate the observation reporting it, since a monitor cannot have seen an attestation that had not yet happened, and that rule is what makes the clamp sound. `decay_trends` derives, from the polity's own signed records alone with no provider involved, a widening affirmation gap (judged against the **median** of prior gaps), a thinning induction witness turnout (never inferred from a single point), and a roll-churn rate, borrowing the shape of `kc-reputation`'s `ceremony_cost_decay` one tier up, hedges included.

  *The observation record can be pruned, and only it* (D17). An observation decides nothing (`reconstruct` matches it and does nothing), so dropping one cannot rewrite what the polity resolved, which is what makes observations the one inert and therefore the one prunable entry class. `ObservationsPruned(Signed<ObservationPruning>)` names a horizon and carries a receipt, being how many records went plus a digest over their CIDs, because a Concern crossing to kith-covenant rests on observations and evidence must not be able to vanish without a signature. One effective `CharterKeeper` among the deciders suffices, which is the delegated power that office otherwise has no record to exercise, but only beyond `PRUNE_SETTLING_SECONDS` (30 days), while the roll may prune anything at a majority: destroying evidence is the move a captured party makes, so the recent record stays out of any single custodian's reach. That constant is law rather than a tunable threshold, since a floor the party it constrains could lower is not a floor. A horizon must advance; the receipt is verified while the dropped observations are still present and treated as beyond checking once compaction has run, an honest limit rather than a rule that would flag every historical pruning. `apply_prunings` returns a **new** archive without them, and can never reach a governing record, because the single `is_prunable` predicate that the receipt, the authority check and the compaction all read matches nothing but an observation.

  *When to prune is a read-model, not a daemon* (D19). `prune_recommendation(archive, polity, &RetentionPolicy, as_of)` answers "should I prune, to what horizon, and who may authorize it?", and returns `None` when there is nothing worth doing. It signs nothing, the host feeding the horizon back to the ordinary authoring path, because this tier never calls `now()` and the loop, like the storage backend, belongs to the host. The safety floor is not the host's to reconstruct, and it has two halves: `live_measure_seconds` (default one day) protects the **span** a live measure reads back over, and `min_witness_watchers` (default 3) protects the **witness count** it also needs. Both are required, because D17's clamp preserves the span and nothing preserves the count: without the second, the same absence at the same horizon grades `Concern` at a dense observation cadence and `Notice` at a sparse one. The count is per member, since `watchers_for` is, so a thinly-watched member can pin the horizon. That is the correct answer rather than a deadlock, because the roll may still prune anything by majority; what is refused is *recommending* it. `retain_seconds` defaults to `PRUNE_SETTLING_SECONDS`, the smallest retention that both preserves every observation-based measure and leaves the single-`CharterKeeper` route open, a *shorter* retention keeping more history and therefore being more expensive to authorize rather than less. `min_dropped` (default 100) exists because a pruning record is itself unprunable: pruning eagerly at 1 leaves the log 2.9× larger than at 100. The recommendation reports every open route, cheapest first, and can only ever propose a horizon `check_authority` accepts.

  *A founding says what it excluded* (D21, resolving Q1). `FoundingProposal` and `PolityCharter` carry a `BoundaryAttestation`: the `candidate_size` the boundary showed, and a reasoned `Exclusion` for everyone it showed that the roll does not name. Without it the genesis quorum takes its denominator from the proposal's *own* roll, so a faction of one charters a thirty-node cluster as a polity of itself on a single signature, with the other twenty-nine not merely outvoted but *unrecorded*. Genesis authority gates **the accounting only**: the numbers must add up, exclusions may not repeat, and nobody may be excluded and enrolled at once. **Nothing refuses a charter for being narrow**, because a genuinely separate group of three inside a thirty-node cluster is indistinguishable from a faction capturing it without a substantive judgement no rule can make. What the rule removes is the silence, not the discretion. Since the charter's core has no amendment path, the attestation is genesis-immutable by construction, and `founding_legitimacy` reads the grade back off the charter **alone**, with no provider and no cohesion input, so anyone holding it can re-run the judgement forever. It reports `consent_share`, ratifiers over everyone the boundary *showed* rather than over the roll the founders *chose*: the faction of one reads **0.033**. Its `FoundingBasis` (`Corroborated` / `Narrowed` / `SelfAttested`) is deliberately unordered and deliberately not an `ErosionLevel`, since a founding is not eroding and these are not comparable harms. This is accountability rather than prevention: the attestation is the proposer's signed *claim*.

  *The claim names its author* (D22, resolving Q5). The `Substrate` arm **carries** its evidence: `Substrate { readings: Vec<Signed<SubstrateReading>> }`, each reading naming a `reader`, signed by that reader alone (D16's rule that nobody may put an observation in somebody else's mouth), and bound to the founding it is offered for, without which an honest reader's account of one cluster replays into somebody else's founding while the signature still verifies. An unsigned label cannot do this work: with one, flipping a field promotes a convener's bare assertion to a corroborated reading with no provider consulted. **The rule with teeth is identity, not arithmetic**: the roll ∪ exclusions must equal some signed reading's members *as a set*, where an accounting rule supplies both sides of its own sum. `founding_legitimacy` carries `readers` and `corroborating_readers`, deliberately not a count of readings filed, since a reader who signed a *different* boundary corroborates nothing, and the gap between the two numbers is a reader contradicting the founding on its own charter, permanently. That count is a **lens and never a gate**, because `did:key` costs nothing and a captured reader can mint its own corroborators; this is the stance kith-covenant takes on capture move 1, with independence attestation as its successor.

  *The excluded answer back.* `BoundaryDissented`, over `DissentKind { Unnamed, ReasonRejected }`, is perspectival evidence in `Observed`'s exact shape: signed by the dissenter alone, no deciders, no majority, and the dissenter need not be a member, since one required to sit inside the roll could be silenced by never being admitted. It is **the only thing in the tier that can contradict an understated `candidate_size`**, which is precisely what the accounting rule cannot reach: the archive holds no provider, so the excluded are the only witnesses. Authority checks coherence with the record contested and never its truth, so a `ReasonRejected` dissenter must actually be among the exclusions and an `Unnamed` one must be in neither the founding roll nor the exclusions. `founding_dissent` is a separate read-model from `founding_legitimacy` on purpose, since dissent accrues over time and folding it in would destroy the charter-alone property. A dissent may be filed **after dissolution**, because otherwise founders silence the excluded by winding the polity up, and it is unprunable for free, since `is_prunable` matches only the observations D17 makes forgettable precisely because they are inert.

  *The substrate has to mean something* (D23). Non-ultimacy is the whole reason the tier has a substrate, so four rules hold at genesis. The substrate must be **articulated**, name and statement non-empty, which is presence and never content, since no rule can read a tradition. An anchor may be neither the polity nor anyone on its roll, a ground inside what it grounds being no ground, which is the guardian loop rule one level out. A roll of nothing but unguarded derived parties **must** name an anchor, the trigger being deliberately narrower than machine-majority, because a human roll's statement *is* its unkeyed anchor and a guarded machine already has a consenting human above it. And a present anchor **co-signs the founding** alongside the ratifiers and guardians, because answerability is accepted and never assigned. `founding_legitimacy` carries `grounding` (`Anchored` / `Guarded` / `Held` / `Bare`, descriptive and unordered like `FoundingBasis`), which lives there rather than in an accruing read-model on exactly the charter-alone test: a genesis-immutable substrate cannot accrue, so the answer is the same forever. `Bare` is legal, and reported so that *permitted* is not mistaken for *grounded*.

  *The ground may answer back.* `SubstrateDisavowed` is the named anchor's own signed withdrawal, read by the separate and accruing `GroundStanding`. It **asserts and cannot un-charter**: nothing rewrites the substrate, reconstructs authority state or dissolves anything, since a withdrawal with that power would be a unilateral kill switch in every anchor's hands. A disowned polity keeps governing itself, on a ground that disowns it, legibly. It shares the dissent's two deliberate properties, being permitted after dissolution and unprunable for free, and is *stricter* in one: only the key the charter names may file it, where a dissent must admit anyone because an omitted party is by definition absent from the record.

  *Independence attestation* (D24, generalized by D28). A signed reading is attributable but does not distinguish one reader from five faces of the same actor: one party minting five `did:key`s and signing five identical readings grades `Corroborated`, the highest basis the tier awards, at a cost of **32 µs a voice** (500 readers in 16.2 ms). `IndependenceAttestation { polity, subject, role, … }` over `AttestedRole { BoundaryReader, Observer }` carries a third party's account of a named subject, on kith-covenant's move-1 shape (`InterpreterIndependenceAttestation`). One entry covers both roles rather than one per role, with one set of stake buckets, one survives-dissolution rule, and a coherence gate parameterized by role, because each is checkable against a different part of the log: a boundary reader is fixed at genesis, while **observers accrue**, so that half reads the log up to the filing. Three rules: sole signer, postdates the charter, and the subject must **actually appear** in the log as what it is claimed to be, a *gate* where KC keeps it a lens, on the line the dissent arm draws (coherence with the record spoken about is checkable; the truth of the claim never is). Two consequences are worth naming: **a monitor must speak before it can be vouched for**, since pre-registering a witness is the sockpuppet move with a longer setup; and a key may hold both roles while **a voucher for one is not a voucher for the other**, because vouching is for a party *in a capacity*, and what an outsider can speak to is a relationship rather than an identity in the abstract. Who *counts* is stricter than in KC: an attestation from the polity, the subject itself, or **anyone on the founding roll** does not credit independence, since the founders are precisely the interested party, but it is **accepted and reported separately** rather than refused, because destroying a claim somebody signed serves nobody where merely not crediting it costs nothing. The `Controlled` arm lets somebody say the lens is being fooled, and counts **from anyone**: interest disqualifies praise rather than warning, so a founder breaking ranks is heard. The read-models are `independent_corroboration` (readers who *both* agree with the ratified boundary *and* carry a disinterested voucher, so a chorus of five hundred scores `0`), `observer_independence`, `independent_observation` (which also reports the **unvouched**, so the absence of vouching is visible rather than silently read as its presence), and `independent_grounding` (how many founding edges graded `Guarded` rest on a monitor somebody was willing to be named for). Each is separate and accruing rather than a field on `founding_legitimacy`, on that model's charter-alone property. **An attestation is itself unverifiable and does not pretend otherwise**: it moves the cost of faking corroboration from one key to a second party willing to be *named* for it. A lens, never a gate.

  *Three entry classes outlive a dissolution*, which is enough to name the rule rather than the exceptions: *evidence about the founding* outlives the polity because the founding's legitimacy does, where governing acts die with it.
- **`en-substrate`**: the pluggable `SubstrateProvider` interface, the source of the emergent boundary a founding ratifies (`FoundingCandidate` → `propose` → `FoundingProposal`). The **guardian edges travel with the boundary** (D15): a candidate the substrate reads as a derived (machine) party becomes a proposed `Derivation`, so which human answers for which machine is ratified rather than asserted. **The candidate says what the provider *knows***, not merely what it saw (**D18**): `reputation: Option<f64>` distinguishes *unrated* from *rated low*, and `derived: bool` carries the substrate's reading of *kind* independently of the guardian edge, so a machine with nobody behind it is ratified as the unguarded `Derivation` it is, where before it proposed silently as a human. An unrated candidate does not satisfy a demanded reputation floor (a positive floor is a positive assertion, and an absent reading cannot support it); a floor of `0.0` demands nothing. **Change is streamed, not diffed** (**D16**): `SubstrateChange` deltas (a member appearing or vanishing, a reputation or guardian-edge move, an advancing attendance stamp, a boundary going opaque) drain through a `ChangeStream` trait kept deliberately **separate** from `SubstrateProvider`: an empty stream and no stream are different claims, and a convener with a list should not have to fake one. **The boundary's provenance travels with the candidate** (**D21**): `BoundarySource` says whether a boundary is an independent reading or the convener's own word, and it rides on `FoundingCandidate` rather than being stamped at `propose`, because a `ManualFounding` candidate has exactly the same *shape* as a trust service's; only the provider knows which it is. `propose` records whoever the reputation floor trims, with the reason it actually used, distinguishing "reputation 0.21 below the floor 0.30" from "unrated; a floor of 0.30 demands a reading" (D18's semantics, said where an excluded party can read them), and `propose_selecting` narrows to a named subset for one stated reason, so an honest small founding is a first-class act rather than a floor it is not. Ships `ManualFounding`, a convener-driven provider needing no substrate service (and, consistently, no stream); such a polity still charters and the charter simply never pretends it was corroborated: the same honest chartered state as an unguarded machine's `guardian: None`. **That is a fact rather than a label** (D22, resolving Q5): `FoundingCandidate` carries no `source` field at all, because the evidence *is* the source: `propose` takes `Vec<Signed<SubstrateReading>>`, empty meaning a convener's assertion. A separate label would leave a claim and its evidence as two things that could disagree, which is the defect D22 closes. `FoundingCandidate::reading()` yields the **unsigned** reading; the provider holds no keys and gains none, since signing is the host's exactly as D20 settled for every other authored act. `ManualFounding` is therefore `ConvenerAsserted` because it holds no reader key. A convener who genuinely obtains a signed reading matching their roll has real corroboration and is welcome to it; what is not free is *claiming* one. **The guardian edges travel with their backing too** (D27): `build` takes an optional `Signed<Observation>` so a live provider can present what it actually saw, and falls back to `Unbacked { reason }` when no monitor has run: the provider does not mint backing the rule would reject, so an honest bootstrap proposal is one the founders must ground rather than one that fails late.
- **`en-at`**: the `autonomous_trust` provider (D5-D8). An `AtEventSource` seam (mock + `at-ffi`-gated embed/attach coupling) folded into an `AtSubstrateProvider` that maps AT's gateway-reputation subtree to the candidate roll, the verified operator to each member's guardian edge, and AT's attended-now stamp to when that guardian was last confirmed present. It implements `ChangeStream` by diffing each folded event against the view it already holds (the provider is the one place the prior reading is in hand), and emits an `AttendanceStamped` delta only when the stamp actually *advances*, so a repeated identical stamp is not mistaken for renewed presence. The default build links no AT: KC ⟂ AT holds with the adapter present.
- **`en-cohesion`**: the polity's **coherence monitor** (a pure `kc-core`-only read-model). `cohesion_report` grades strain into an `ErosionLevel` (`None` / `Notice` / `Concern`) off the chartered roll vs. the provider's current candidate: attrition, reputation decay, floor breaches, guardians not present, transmission decay (members lapsed on re-affirming the substrate, read off `en-charter`'s `AffirmationState`), lost guardian edges (a chartered node→guardian edge the substrate no longer shows), an opaque boundary capping attrition at `Notice`. Absent guardians are capped at `Notice` outright: a snapshot cannot tell a night shift from an abandoned polity, so the signal may warn but never escalate. Transmission decay carries no such cap and may reach `Concern`: duration is already inside that measure. Nor do lost guardian edges, for a different reason: they compare what the polity *ratified* against what is observed, and only over members the provider still sees, so a vanished member is attrition rather than answerability collapsing and no cap is needed. **D16 adds the four measures that only exist over time**: a widening affirmation gap and a thinning witness turnout, both capped at `Notice` because a trend is a leading indicator whose consequence the affirmation component can already escalate (grading both would count one decay twice); roll churn, a rate rather than a trend, with a full `Notice`/`Concern` pair; and **sustained absence**, the one signal permitted past the attendance cap: a guardian unattended across the recorded window for at least `sustained_absence_seconds` *and* seen by at least `sustained_absence_watchers` **distinct observers** reaches `Concern`. **Watchers, not filings** (D28): counting *observations* instead would let one key file three times and clear a floor whose entire justification is that somebody has been watching, so a stranger with one keypair could manufacture the escalation this component alone is trusted with. Counting distinct observers is arithmetic rather than a judgement about who they are; whether the watchers are independent *of each other* is the `watchers` lens (`distinct` / `vouched` / `contested`), reported beside the escalation it qualifies and read by no component. That asymmetry is deliberate: gating an escalation on the monitor being vouched for would let a captured polity vouch for nobody and thereby become unwatchable, and an unvouched monitor is the ordinary state of an honest new one. Those two conditions do two different jobs: **the span is the absence; the count is the evidence that somebody was watching while it lasted.** What lifts the snapshot cap is therefore not a looser threshold but a record. **D18 adds the eleventh component and fixes a conflation the other ten rested on**: both reputation measures are now computed over the members the provider actually *rated*, reported as `rated_members`, and say nothing when nobody is rated. Before this, an unrated roll was indistinguishable from a failing one and a freshly founded polity graded at `Concern` for being new. The unrated share itself is graded, capped at `Notice` by construction for the attendance reason (a young cohort and a provider that has stopped rating are the same picture), and exists so the two reputation components cannot go *silently* dark. **D23 adds the twelfth, and it is unlike the other eleven**: whether the polity's chartered substrate anchor has withdrawn. Every other measure here grades how far something has drifted; this one grades a single signed act by the one key entitled to make it, so it reaches `Concern` alone and takes **no threshold** at all. It is event-triggered, like kith-covenant's `contraction_detected`, and it cannot decay back, since there is no withdrawal of a withdrawal. **D29 adds the rite's own cost, which no earlier measure could reach**: a *cheapening ceremony* (the latest completed rite shorter than the first, measured against the first rather than the median, because a uniform drift moves its own recent history, `gap_widening_since_founding`'s lesson one measure over) and *attrition within a rite* (the polity that turned out for the naming of the substrate and had drifted away by the submission of the polity itself), both capped at `Notice` like the other trends. Abandoned rites are reported and deliberately **not** graded: nothing in the doctrine says how long a catechumenate may take. And `nobody_holds_the_substrate` reaches `Concern` alone with no threshold: a polity that has expelled its way there can take no act at all, since every majority is of the members who hold it. The report's read-models travel in one `CohesionInputs` struct of `Option<&T>`, which puts *not evaluated* in the type rather than in a convention. It never decides a split (see D4/Q2).
- **`en-uplift`**: the **upward interface** to kith-covenant (D4/D9/D10) and the *one* crate holding the confined KC-record dependencies (`kc-conduct` and `kc-identity`, D10 and D14). `community_identity` hands a chartered polity off as a self-signed `CommunityIdentity`; `coherence_concern` emits KC's `Concern` (neutral `Other("coherence-erosion")` bearing) only at `Concern`-level erosion; `fission` emits a ratified split of **N (≥ 2) successor identities** (D25), a signed `SuccessorLink` to each (index-aligned) and the predecessor's `CharterDissolution` naming them all, so a three-way split is one act rather than two chained fissions recording an intermediate polity nobody lived in; it carries fusion's four guards in the other direction (arity, pairwise-distinct successors, no collision with the predecessor, and `verify_self_signed` on **every** successor, absent which a link is signed under the predecessor's root to an identity whose own signature does not verify); `fusion` emits the mirror ratified merger, N (≥ 2) predecessors → one merged `CommunityIdentity`, with a `SuccessorLink` and a `CharterDissolution` (naming the merged polity as sole successor) per predecessor. `envoy_delegation` turns an *effective* envoy standing into KC's `SpeakerDelegation` scoped `Envoy`, the fourth border artifact and the only one an office produces, taking the derived `EnvoyState` rather than the archive.

- **`en-store`**: **durable storage** for a log that can be *compacted* (**D20**), the backend D17 and D19 both left owed. An `ArchiveStore` trait generic over the entry type (so this crate needs no `en-charter` dependency), with `FileStore` (one segment envelope per line, append is write + `fsync`, and a full rewrite goes temp + `fsync` + `rename`, which POSIX makes atomic) and `SqliteStore` behind a `sqlite` feature, storing *the same* segments as rows so the two are differentially testable; SQLite earns its place on the rewrite, which is one transaction rather than a temp-file dance. **The whole crate exists for one branch**: *same generation ⇒ append a segment; generation changed ⇒ rewrite in full*. The store owns the cursor, because the hazard it guards is exactly what happens when a caller keeps one: a host that appends past a compaction loses the `ObservationsPruned` record and keeps the observations it dropped, and both verification passes still say the reloaded log is fine. A torn tail left by an interrupted write is *reported*, never skipped; `recover()` truncates it as a deliberate act, because silently dropping an unparseable last line would discard a signed entry without saying so. No clock: the loop stays the host's.
- **`en-host`**: **what a host does on a cadence, and when** (**D20**). `HostCycle::tick(archive, candidate, as_of)` returns the `DueAction`s owed (persist, a graded `CohesionReport`, `RaiseConcern` when erosion reaches that level, a `PruneRecommendation` when the observation log is due), assembling *every* read-model the grade can use, since a missing input makes its component report nothing rather than complain. **Since D31 the report says so itself**: `omitted_readings` names the readings a grade was not given, `silent_measures()` the measures that therefore went quiet, and `blind_to_concern()` whether any of them could have escalated, so `erosion == None` and *nobody looked* are finally distinguishable. That closes the hazard properly rather than in two tests of this crate: `grade` is now pinned to omit nothing, and a caller off this path is at least legible. **`tick` decides and computes; the host signs and does I/O**: it takes `as_of` like every other cadence in this tier (so a year of host behaviour is unit-testable in microseconds) and authors nothing, because raising a Concern and pruning both need keys the host holds. It covers the cadenced duties and only those: membership, induction, office conferral and envoy authorization are deliberate acts of the polity, not scheduled ones.
- **`src/rust/examples/polity_host`**: a runnable **reference host** (`[[bin]]`, nothing depends on it), mirroring kith-covenant's `two_communities_demo`. Its single `time::now()` is the only clock read in ethne outside a test, apart from `en-pyo3`'s `*_at=None ⇒ now` binding convenience. Runs a simulated timeline by default so it terminates and can be verified; `--follow` is the real loop. **Three monitors, rotated (D31)**: D19's retention floor counts distinct watchers per member since D28, so the single monitor this example used to hold never cleared it and its whole compaction path was dead, 400 observations to zero compactions, while the restart section printed a compaction count as though it had run one. `src/python/examples/polity_host.py` is the mirror of this file, and writing it is what found that.

- **`en-pyo3`** + **`src/python/ethne/`**: the **Python surface** (D2). An `ethne._core` extension module built by maturin, and a Pythonic package over it in the tier's own five parts: `ethne.polity` (the `Archive` charter log, keys, and the five derived read-models), `ethne.authoring` (a `Signer` plus one call per charter record: found, amend, admit / depart / expel, confer / resign / revoke an office, designate / rotate / release a guardian edge, authorize / resign / revoke an envoy, induct / re-affirm, record an observation, prune the observation log, dissolve), `ethne.monitor` (a `FoundingCandidate`, `manual_founding`, and `cohesion_report`, whose `as_of` fixes the moment guardian attendance is judged fresh against, and which now takes the `DecayTrends` and `ObservationWindow` read-models, plus `sign_observation`, the pre-archive counterpart of `record_observation`, since a founding derivation's evidence must be signed before the charter that will carry it exists), `ethne.host` (**D31**: `HostConfig`, `DueAction` and `HostCycle` bound whole, so a Python host drives the same cadence a Rust one does instead of reimplementing it, and `HostCycle.grade` is the only call that supplies every reading), and `ethne.uplift` (the four artifacts that cross to kith-covenant, as JSON a KC-side consumer appends verbatim). The ratify-not-replace discipline survives the crossing: `propose_charter` returns only JSON, and a charter exists only once `ratify_charter` has a majority of the roll co-sign it. `en-at` has no bindings yet, since its real coupling is behind the `at-ffi` gate, but Python can drive the same path through `founding_candidate`.

```bash
cd lib/muudd/lib/ethne

# Rust tier (en-pyo3 is a cdylib with no test harness of its own)
cargo test --workspace --exclude en-pyo3   # 437 tests (archive + charter + substrate + at + cohesion + uplift + store + host)
cargo test -p en-store --features sqlite    # 29, +4 over the default 25 (the optional SQLite backend, incl. a differential check vs FileStore)

# Python surface
maturin develop                            # builds en-pyo3 into ethne._core
pytest src/python/tests/                   # 233 tests
python src/python/examples/polity_demo.py  # a polity's life, end to end
python src/python/examples/polity_host.py  # the reference host: the loop, and the one clock

./run-tests.sh                             # all of the above in order
```

The build needs a per-component **conda** env, consistent with the rest of sustainable-space: `environment.yaml` pins `python==3.13.*`, `rust`, `rust-src`, `maturin`, and `pytest` from conda-forge (`conda env create -f environment.yaml && conda activate ethne`). `run-tests.sh` errors early with an instruction if any of them is missing.

The decoupling is concrete and machine-checked (`cargo tree`): every core, monitor and host crate (`en-core`, `en-charter`, `en-substrate`, `en-at`, `en-cohesion`, `en-store`, `en-host`) depends only on the neutral `kc-core`: no `autonomous_trust`, no kith-covenant governance crate. The two KC-record couplings needed to speak KC's own vocabulary (`kc-conduct` for the `Concern`, `kc-identity` for the envoy delegation) are confined to `en-uplift` and go no further (and the second widens nothing: `kc-conduct` already reached `kc-identity` through `kc-ceremony`, so D14 only made an existing edge direct); ethne never depends on `kc-archive` (it emits serialized `Signed<T>` payloads a KC-side consumer appends). `en-substrate`'s end-to-end test founds a polity through the manual provider with neither AT nor KC present. D31 added `en-host` to `en-pyo3` and the property was re-checked rather than assumed: `en-host`'s own tree is `kc-core`-only, so the binding gained no governance crate, and its `kc-conduct` / `kc-identity` edges are still `en-uplift`'s alone. The one C dependency in the tree, `rusqlite` behind `en-store`'s `sqlite` feature (**D20**), is off by default and absent from the default workspace graph: also `cargo tree`-checked.
